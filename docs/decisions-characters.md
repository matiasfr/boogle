### Characters and story

- <a id="d-2026-11-04-remove-the-boogler-s-relationship-progression"></a>**2026-11-04, remove The Boogler's relationship progression now** (owner: "he stays in the game and story and his copy stays, but remove the progression mechanic now and clean up; lines that become inaccessible are kept, tidily. Narrative comes later."). This implements the Design Table call: "The JRPG-style relationship system gets refactored into plenty of fun dialogue that isn't gated on it". Every thread opens from the first talk, with chapters still ordered within it. Remove points, levels, hearts, trust gates and nosiness; keep conversation memories, presents and the short-lived comic grudge after riding. First-ever talk uses the intro; later greetings and farewells draw from the other former tiers, including his over-familiar "my best friend and future business partner". Preserve every line, parking inaccessible copy outside the player bundle for the later narrative pass.

- <a id="d-2026-11-01-nail-the-bare-hero-base-before"></a>**2026-11-01, nail the bare hero base before outfits.** Clothes are separate swappable parts (`wear_<slot>_<item>`; slots head, body, hands, feet) and only need to prove the slots work until the base is right. On 2 November the owner chose a separate-head figure, proportioned closer to Ratchet or a Bricktron than lanky, and leans towards no mouth.
- <a id="d-2026-11-02-the-controls-and-motion-are-right"></a>**2026-11-02, the controls and motion are right on the mannequin; only the animation polish and the look remain.** The owner "really, really" likes how `?proto=hero` controls: today's direct controller (the modern-Mario one's inertia felt less in control), the closer camera, and hand-made clips (Quaternius's CC0 library) on a humanoid skeleton. Code-built figures and agent-authored run cycles didn't satisfy, and the quick test figures on these clips (cute, ears, stout, bib) didn't land either. Next: previz of properly modelled characters that can play these clips.
- <a id="d-2026-11-01-the-hero-starts-as-a-bare"></a>**2026-11-01, the hero starts as a bare base** (owner): settle the base body's shape and proportions first; outfits are swappable parts (head, body, hands, feet) built on it.
- <a id="d-2026-11-02-the-buddy-is-the-default-character"></a>**2026-11-02, the Buddy is the default character** (owner: "Let's ship it"): every avatar in normal play, with its camera. The capsule's code stays behind `?body=capsule`, and is the fallback until the Buddy loads. The mannequin is gone from the clip asset. Shipped before measuring on a device. The owner then ran `?buddies=8` on a phone: 60 fps after about the first second.
- <a id="d-2026-11-02-the-hero-camera-is-5-2"></a>**2026-11-02, the hero camera is 5.2 m with 5° of sprint widening** (owner, comparing 5.2, 5.8 and 6.5 m and 0°, 3° and 5° live, `docs/research/third-person-camera.md`). Horizontal follow stays locked to the body.

- <a id="d-2026-11-02-the-buddy-s-look-from-the"></a>**2026-11-02, the Buddy's look, from the owner's review in the viewer:** no rim light. Hats read as a railway conductor's rather than a police officer's: a straight stiff drum and brass trim, not a flared navy crown.
- <a id="d-2026-11-02-the-player-s-direction-is-the"></a>**2026-11-02, the player's direction is the Buddy** (`assets/src/buddy.py`, `?proto=hero&body=buddy`, `/character/`): a simple, smooth, slightly plump figure on the mannequin's clips, in the player's colour, with bead eyes and blush and no mouth, hair or clothes yet. The Tracklayer below stays one fixed design, with no customization; whether it's still the player or a separate character is open.
