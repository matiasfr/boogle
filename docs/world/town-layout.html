Home's town: today and three layouts to pick from

You asked for "a pass on the town layout so it's more like a proper town. Maybe there's like a radial part to it." This page draws the town as it is and three ways to lay it out, to scale from the game's own numbers. Each option starts small, as you said: "maybe only a couple buildings exist, so you get the satisfaction of building up." Pick a tab, then step through the three moments.

North is up. The station is drawn as the one-station Home you picked on 9 November, with the east bay for the passenger train. The ground is the game's own height grid: the town sits on a flat top about 130 m by 200 m, so none of the options needs earth moved. Black bars are the curio shelf (in the hall) and the finders' bay; the yellow dot in the hall is the ticket window.

Today: why it does not read as a town

  • Seven buildings, seven angles. Their doors face 63°, 88°, 94°, 99°, 117°, 124° and 151°. None is square to the station (70°) or to the street. They were each turned to look good from the old spawn point.
  • No street has two sides. Only 2 of the 7 front doors are within a few steps of a path. Fine Prices is 11 m from the nearest one; a cottage is 12 m.
  • No centre. The "square" is where three paths meet, 4 m in front of the hall. It has no edges and nothing in the middle.
  • Nothing to walk round. The paths are a Y: dock, camp, quarry. There is no loop anywhere in town.
  • A ring nobody can see. Five of the seven buildings already sit about 20 m from one point, but Pip's house and a cottage stand in the middle of it and no door faces it.
  • Bare ground. The flat top is about 26,000 m²; the buildings cover about 540 m² of it, and no tree stands within 57 m of the middle of town.
  • Coming up from the dock you meet the blank sides of two cottages. The path bends between them, and the station stays hidden behind Pip's house until the last few metres (about a sixth of the hall shows from the top of the path). The walk is 127 m, 23 seconds.

Stills of all of this are in the plan (docs/plans/town-layout.md).

1 · The Cross: two straight streets

A classic railway town. Station Street runs dead straight from the top of the dock path to the hall's front, along the same line as the platforms, so from the dock you look down the street at the station. A second street crosses it, with the museum at one end and Fine Prices at the other. The clock stands where they cross. Back lanes behind the houses make blocks you can walk round. No radial part.

  • On arrival: the hall, Pip's house on the square, one cottage at the top of the dock path, the forge as a frame.
  • Every building faces one of two ways (square to the station), plus the two that face the quarry road.
  • Walks: dock to the Cross 93 m (17 s); dock to the hall door 121 m (22 s); platform to the Cross 54 m (10 s).
  • A rider from the far cottage walks 60 m to the ticket window, then 29 m to the train: about 53 s at a little guy's pace.
  • Weak points: houses hide each other along a street, so from the hall you cannot see which plots are staked; the only open space is a strip in front of the hall.

2 · The Round: a green with a ring road (recommended)

The radial part you mentioned. A round green, 28 m across, sits in front of the hall with a road round it. Houses stand in a ring facing the green, 30° apart, the same spacing as the Roundhouse's stalls. The hall takes the ring's east side, so the green is the station square. Four ways leave the ring: to the station, the dock, the camp and the quarry. The clock stands in the middle.

  • On arrival: the hall, Pip's house (by the camp road, where you are sent first), one cottage beside the dock street, the forge as a frame, and the green with its ring road already there. The empty ring shows where the town will go.
  • Buying a plot fills a gap in the ring. From the green or the hall's front you can see nearly every door and every staked plot at once.
  • Walks: dock to the clock 97 m (17 s); dock to the hall door 120 m (22 s); platform to the clock 50 m (9 s); once round the ring 107 m (19 s).
  • A rider from the far cottage walks 48 m across the green to the ticket window, then 29 m to the train: about 45 s. Riders from every house cross the same green, so a crowd reads as a crowd.
  • From the dock path you see the gate cottage, then the clock with the hall and the forge's chimney behind it, on one line.
  • Costs: Pip's house moves 17 m, Fine Prices 15 m (its balcony goes with it), the museum only turns. The ring holds seven buildings, so the last plots go along the quarry and camp roads.

3 · The Fan: streets radiating from the station

The other radial answer. A half-round forecourt in front of the hall, and streets fanning out from it: Dock Street on the station's line, Museum Street with the museum closing its far end, and the camp and quarry roads as the fan's outer edges. A curved back lane joins them. Every street ends at the station.

  • On arrival: the hall, Pip's house (2 m from where it is now), one cottage at the top of Dock Street, the forge as a frame.
  • Least to move: Fine Prices stays where it is and only turns; today's buildings already sit close to these lines.
  • Walks: dock to the forecourt 113 m (20 s); dock to the hall door 120 m (22 s); platform to the forecourt 33 m (6 s); loop by the two streets and the lane 89 m (16 s).
  • A rider from the far cottage walks 54 m down Dock Street to the ticket window, then 29 m: about 49 s.
  • Weak points: the only place to gather is the forecourt, which also holds the shelf, the way in and everyone arriving; the blocks are wedges, so plots are awkward shapes; from the dock the forge is hidden.

The three moments, in every option

On arrival The station hall, Pip's house, one cottage, the forge's frame. Boards mark where Fine Prices and the museum will rise. The first two plots are staked, each with a price sign.
Mid-game (trains running, about two hours in) The forge is finished, roof and chimney on, the moment you finish building it, and you can walk in. Fine Prices and the museum stand (they rise free when the first path opens, as today). The clock is up. Three bought houses stand; two more plots are staked.
Full town Eight bought buildings, twelve in all, plus the station, the forge and the Roundhouse. A busy town, not a city.

Recommendation

2 · The Round. It is the one that gives the town a middle: a place to meet, something to walk round, and one spot from which you can see what has been built and what is still staked. Filling a ring a house at a time is the clearest picture of "building up", and the round green echoes the Roundhouse's turntable across the station. The Cross is the fallback if you would rather have plain streets; the Fan is the cheapest and keeps Fine Prices where it is, but it has no room to gather.

The same in all three

  • The forge stays where it is (the railway is tied to it). Its door moves from the south wall, where the hall would crowd it, to the west wall on the camp road. It finishes with its roof and chimney when you finish building it.
  • The ticket window is inside the hall's north end, as you decided. Every rider's walk ends there, then goes out to the platforms.
  • The finders' bay moves beside the hall's south end, facing the square, in line with the shelf. Where it stands now its back would face everyone coming up from the dock.
  • Plots: eight to buy. Three are the cottages that no longer stand on arrival; five are new houses. Two are staked at a time, nearest the station and the dock first.
  • Other towns get the same parts, not the same shape: a station on the town's edge, one centre the land suggests, streets that end at something, plots staked on the centre's edge first. Home is the only round one.

Questions for you

1. Which layout? Recommended: 2, the Round.

2. What stands on arrival? Recommended: the hall, Pip's house and one cottage; Fine Prices and the museum still rise free when the first path opens; the other three cottages are bought. Other choices: a second cottage stands from the start (plot 4), or Fine Prices and the museum are bought too.

3. Move the finders' bay beside the hall's south end? Recommended: yes. It was placed "for now".

4. The clock in the middle: free when the first trains run, or bought? Recommended: free, so one thing still grows because a train came.

5. Are the first two plots staked from the very start, before there are any fares to spend? Recommended: yes, with their price signs, so the plan of the town reads on the first walk in.

What I could not confirm

  • None of the three has been seen in the game. They are drawn from the layout numbers; the level check passed with each option's positions put in, but it does not know streets, roofs or the green.
  • Whether a player with the second jump can already hop from a cottage roof to Fine Prices' roof and on to the glider's balcony. Today a cottage roof is 5.8 m from the shop's; every option puts the nearest one at least 11 m away.
  • What the town costs to draw. The figures in the plan are counted from the building kit, not measured.
