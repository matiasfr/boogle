Where does the bought passenger train stand at Home?

The express comes in two stages: first the ordinary train with a headboard, later a passenger train the room buys, "kept ready at each big station". On 10 November you ruled that at Home it does not stand in the Roundhouse: "the Roundhouse stays the upgrade screen only; find it a bay elsewhere". This page draws Home to scale from the layout code, with three answers. Pick a tab.

track (thin: line's second track) the option's track engine and one coach, 8.5 m longest set, 12.7 m walk, at 5.6 m/s kept clear for a bigger Roundhouse

North is up. Grey bars on the platform roads are service trains standing at their marks. The blue wedge is what a player in the hall's back doorway sees of the train.

A · East bay: off the end of the island

A short straight bay in the open ground where the two lines part, past the island's end. The train stands on its own track with its own small platform, and leaves straight onto line 2 without using a platform road.

Where it leaves and ends A dead-straight 26 m track across the open ground past the end of the island. It ends at buffers 10 m beyond the island's end ramp, and joins line 2's outbound track 54 m out along line 2, where line 2 has just become double. It is that outbound track carried straight back toward the station.
Points and curves, against the track rules No new curve and no new points to bend through. The bay is the straight; the curve that already swings platform 2's road onto the outbound track (18 m radius, leaving at up to 16.7°) becomes the points. That curve misses the 7° rule for every train at Home today; the bay adds nothing to it. No kink. It ends, so it gets buffers and an end board: a marked terminal.
Where the train stands Engine and one coach (8.5 m): from 0.3 m off the buffers, the coach at the buffer end beside its platform, the engine facing out. Longest that fits: 12.7 m, the longer-boiler engine and two coaches (eight seats), with 3.0 m between track centres at its nose (the level check calls under 2.3 m too close). An end-game engine (10.7 to 14 m) with a coach does not fit.
Walk to its door From the hall: 40 m, 7 s, straight down the island and off its end ramp. From the Roundhouse's open stall: 31 m, 6 s across line 2's track, or 35 m, 6 s round the island's end.
What you see from the hall Standing under the line map you look straight down the island. The train is dead ahead, 43 m away, side-on in the gap between the two platforms' trains, its doors toward you. It is the thing at the end of the view.
What it does to the running lines Leaving, it rolls straight onto the outbound track. It never stands at a platform or on the single track in front of them, so it can wait with its doors open for the full 20 s while service trains come and go beside it. It takes the next gap on the outbound track; if a service train is already pulling out, it follows it. Coming home it arrives at platform 2 like any train, lets riders off on the island, then backs into the bay from the outbound track (15 to 20 s, my estimate). It should never hold a service train longer than any train ahead would. Line 1 is untouched; the set cannot reach Camp from here.
What it displaces The square, the shelf, the wagon's roll-out and the ground kept clear for a bigger Roundhouse: nothing (its nearest point is 23 m from the table, on the far side of line 2). It does take two things. The cart's old track crosses it, so that track is lifted once the cart has stopped running. And Q2's old delivery frame stands 2.5 m from the bay's centre line, so it is cleared or moved about 2 m.
What it costs to build 26 m of new track, one buffer stop, one short low platform (about 12 m, the island's own parts) and a ramp, and up to 0.9 m of fill under the buffer end, where the ground dips. No world reset of its own: the track is only drawn, and where the set stands rides the reset the passenger train itself needs.
Alone and together Alone: a 7 s walk, sit down, Go now. Together: the 20 s is always whole, and a friend who arrives late on a service train steps off on the island 10 to 20 m from the bay.

B · The cart's track: no new track

The cart's link between the two lines falls idle when the cart retires. The train is kept there and rolls into platform 2 when called.

Where it leaves and ends No new track. The set stands on the cart's track: the 38 m link the cart uses from line 1 (54 m out) round into platform 2's road (26 m out). The cart stops running after Q6, long before a passenger train is bought. The link has no buffers and needs none: it runs through, joined at both ends.
Points and curves, against the track rules Nothing new. The link is a 15 m curve, the same as the engine spur: far under the 60 m rule for running lines (and the 40 m one for slow loops), like everything else in Home's station. No kink.
Where the train stands On the curve, 17 to 25.5 m along the link: the only stretch far enough from both lines (its first 16 m run within 3.3 m of line 1). The 12.7 m set fits too (to 29.7 m, 4.0 m from line 2). It stands bent round a 15 m curve with no platform beside it: you do not board it there.
Walk to its door You board at platform 2 on the island: 21 m, 4 s from the hall, the shortest of the three. From the Roundhouse's open stall: 20 m, 4 s across the track (not through a standing train), or 37 m, 7 s round the island's end.
What you see from the hall The same view down the island: the train is parked 44 m away past the island's end, side-on. When you call it, it rolls 23 m round the curve into platform 2 beside you (about 6 s): a small arrival of its own.
What it does to the running lines It loads at platform 2, so for the roll-in, the wait and the pull-out (about 35 s) it holds line 2's only platform road and the single track in front of it. The express rule ends the wait early rather than hold a train, so with three trains on the ring the full 20 s will often be cut short (my estimate: to 10 to 15 s; not simulated). It never holds a service train, but it has no place of its own to wait. Line 1 is untouched, and from here the set could also leave for Camp.
What it displaces Nothing. The square, the shelf, the wagon's roll-out and the Roundhouse's ground are all clear, and the cart's track stays as planned.
What it costs to build No new track and no platform. No world reset of its own.
Alone and together Everyone is already on the island where it loads, so the walk is the best of the three. But the wait for friends is the least certain: it shrinks whenever a service train is due.

C · No bay at Home

The honest do-nothing: the passenger train lives at West landing, and from the hall the first stage's express is all there is.

Where it leaves and ends Nothing is built at Home. The passenger train's Home berth is West landing, the ring's other end, where two terminals with buffers are already planned side by side.
Points and curves, against the track rules Nothing at Home. West landing is not drawn yet, so its bay can be drawn to the rules from the start (a 40 m bay road, 65 m of parallel track, 7° points).
Where the train stands At West landing. Any length its bay is drawn for.
Walk to its door From the hall: 122 to 131 m through the village, 22 to 23 s in a straight line (longer by the real path, which is not laid out). From the Roundhouse: about 150 m, 27 s.
What you see from the hall Nothing. The hall's board either sends you on foot ("from West landing") or gives you the first stage's express: the next service train with a headboard, at platform 2.
What it does to the running lines Nothing changes at Home. From the hall the only express is the service train with a headboard, its wait cut short by traffic, exactly as before the purchase.
What it displaces Nothing.
What it costs to build Nothing at Home. West landing's bay is part of drawing that station (stage 6 of the map), so the purchase may show nothing near Home until then.
Alone and together Friends at Home have nothing of their own to gather round: the train they bought is a 22 s walk away, out of sight.

Looked at and ruled out

  • In the Roundhouse, out by the engine spur. Your ruling. Measured as well: the track behind the spur's points is 8.1 m and an engine with one coach is 8.5 m, so it could not have turned back there without moving the buffers into the hall.
  • A bay beside the hall, south of platform 2's road. It would be 5 m from the hall, but the engine spur swings across that ground 16 m along, and joining the two would need a 7 m curve or a moved table. It also sits on the ground kept free to relay the spur wider when the Roundhouse grows, with Q1's frame in the way.
  • North of line 1's road. That is the forge's goods side: the boiler stand is 3.4 m from the track, with the dock and crane beside it.
  • A through track across the gap, line 1 to line 2's outbound track, no buffers. It can be drawn at about 55 m radius, near the 60 m rule, but only about 7 m of it is far enough from both lines to stand a train on, and the set is 8.5 m.
  • The rule for designed stations (a 40 m bay road with 65 m of parallel track). Home's two roads are parallel for 22 and 25 m before they curve apart. Home is the exception whichever option you pick.

Recommendation

A, the east bay. It is the only place at Home where the train can stand with its doors open for the full 20 s without sitting on a running line, in plain view down the island from the hall, and it touches nothing kept for the Roundhouse.

B is the fallback if the cart's track has to stay. C is not competitive: it leaves the hall with nothing to see or board after the purchase.

What I could not confirm

  • Whether the cart's track stays as track after the cart retires. The plans say the cart stops serving after Q6; they do not say the rails are lifted.
  • What becomes of Q2's delivery frame once its job is done.
  • That outbound trains really run on the left-hand track from 54 m out. The layout draws it there (the double-track work); I read the drawing, not how trains are posed on it. A depends on it.
  • Every time above that is marked as an estimate. They come from distances; no simulation runs a train that belongs to no line yet.
  • West landing's layout. It is not drawn; its distances are straight lines to the blockout's two halts.
  • The ground under the bay is from the terrain data (level within about 0.9 m of the rails), not from a look in the game. The level check does not know the spur, the cart's track or a bay.
  • If the joining curve on line 2 is later eased over 30 m instead of 20 (the rail map's proposal), A's join moves from 54 m to 64 m out. The bay still works, but where a standing train clears the next track should be measured again.
