Where it is, and what the pictures are

Left: the real Roundhouse table at Home in layout B (?home=b), from about the planned garage camera, in the game as it is today. The village, the crane and the sky are bright behind it, which is why the garage stages the frame: a dark stall, a warm key light, a cool rim light and the table as a plinth.

  • Every picture in the garage is a render of the game's own train parts in their game paint: engine A's nodes (assets/src/engine.py), the passenger car's fittings, the place kit's flatbeds and wheels, the cargo car's stakes and boards with kit logs and rails. Staged with dev/look/garage-showroom.ts.
  • Yellow parts are the previewed nodes themselves, drawn translucent in the capture (the longer boiler's engine_*_long nodes, the car's car_upgrade).
  • Stand-ins: the table's disc and edge are drawn to the game's size and colours, not taken from it; the footing wagon is roundhouse-view.ts's boxes with simple footings; the teal paint recolours the engine's red in the capture. Plough and headlamp, Six seats, Bigger cradle, Brass bands and the whistles have no model yet and show today's train.
  • The Cars, Looks, Cargo and Whistle pictures show today's boiler even after the longer one is fitted.

Answered, 10 November

  1. The engine on the table is a works copy the Roundhouse keeps, wearing every fit the room has; real trains take a fit at their next Home stop.
  2. A new resource's first car is free at its unlock; the screen shows it owned and sells better ones.
  3. The Roundhouse stays 9.6 m now; growing the table and stall is itself an upgrade, planned with the end-game engine. The map keeps the ground for it clear.
  4. The Roundhouse holds the garage only; the Home hall's line map and the halt boards grow into the map and a small logistics readout.
  5. Cars are bought per line: every train on a line runs the same cars, bought once.
