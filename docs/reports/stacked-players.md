## What moves the local player

The local body is dynamic. The remote is kinematic. Both have foot and head spheres of radius 0.45 m. The remote mask excludes scenery and caves but includes the player's group (`src/physics/physics.ts:29-30, 63-73`). The remote entity teleports the proxy to the initial target and first sampled pose, then chases it before each fixed step. Same-deck co-riders already suppress proxy contacts (`src/entities/remote-player/index.ts:236-237, 249-257, 267-271`). Rapier applies collider groups and solves contacts at `src/physics/backend-rapier.ts:160, 343`.

A headless A/B used the production bodies on a level 10 m square platform. After 60 settling steps, I inserted a stationary proxy and ran 120 fixed steps without input. Exact center coincidence moved the local body down 0.112 m with zero horizontal travel. Shifting the proxy just 0.01 m horizontally from center sent the local body 10.82 m sideways and below the platform. With proxy mask zero, the local body stayed still in both cases. This isolates collision response and explains why the direction and magnitude of the historical shove vary. It does not require a moving train or a server correction.

## Recommended change

Built the client-side placement fix for off-world fall respawn and Get unstuck past the Ring Island gap. Both use the existing jump-in candidate order (center, then six points 1.4 m away). Fall respawn checks local terrain height and physics-body clearance in the shared browser/headless handler. Pier recovery checks visible peers and probes support under each candidate using the jump landing policy, including the downward ray at a narrow platform edge. It remembers the selected point. If every candidate is unavailable, recovery uses its original destination. The jump entry keeps its previous behavior. Pause already dispatches to this recovery handler.

**P3:** Flatbed uncoupling at the depot still uses its original point. The train view has neither the arrival policy nor its support probe. A later change can share that policy and verify the uncoupled car cannot itself count as platform support.

The remote-proxy contact gate remains a proposal for unseen or simultaneous arrivals. It needs independent multiplayer physics review of separation, normal shoulder contact, co-riders and objects touching a proxy.

## Reproduction and limits

The original assertion in `timeout 60 node --test test/occupied-respawn.test.ts` failed before the change. It passed afterward, along with the full-ring fallback check. In the two-player Pier proof, the first player recovered to (1405.90, 25.85, -349.09) and the second to (1405.90, 25.85, -347.69). The placement assertions passed, 1.40 m apart. The look subsequently failed its warp-clock capacity check before capture, so there is no fixed-build still to inspect. The probe measures placement, not how either player walks away afterward.

The bounded weekly arrival run passed the existing checks through the returning-railway card, then ended without a lap verdict. The historically failing lap-only checks were removed from the weekly scenario because this fix preserves jump-in behavior and there is no verified pass for them.
The worktree had no attached broker. The local look harness acquired the shared bot queue and launched WebGPU Chromium. Two-player warp used speed 2. The Camp run ended with a clock-capacity failure after both positions were logged. No timing, crash or queue timeout counts as a gameplay regression. not tested on a device.
