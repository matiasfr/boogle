# Town interiors: doors worth opening, little guys worth meeting

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Round 4's house, shop and museum: third-person indoor cameras, seamless thresholds, small surprising rooms, short dialogue and babble, comic money, collective exhibits, rumours and two visitors. Research and proposed experiments, not implemented features or new owner decisions._

**Verification.** **[V]**: relevant source text fetched and read; developer testimony establishes what its author says, not access to the game's implementation. **[T]**: developer testimony in translation. **[P]**: conference abstract only; the recording was not watched. **[C]**: code inspected at `e402350a`. **[H]**: Boogle hypothesis, interpretation or proposed number, untested. Live web searches were performed for this report. No reference game was newly played, prototype rendered, camera benchmark run or multiplayer behaviour tested. All proposed dimensions, timings, dialogue and shop rules below are **[H]**, including tables and examples.

## The bet and the boundaries

**A building should give you something the street cannot: someone's peculiar private life, a new way to look, or a spectacle you can bring a friend to see.** Three genuinely different rooms are a stronger round-4 test than a street of furnished boxes. The reward is entering and discovering; talking adds personality rather than permission to enjoy it.

The binding brief is [round-4.md, The run 6–7](../plans/round-4.md), read with [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), [decisions.md](../decisions.md) and the latest round-3 feedback in [playtests.md](../playtests.md). The owner wants Pokémon-like residences, a shop, a museum and residents who plant rumours about The Boogler. Money is a comic score; his prices are a gag. His story beyond rumours waits. The latest playtest says crates and little guys were pleasant but insufficient reasons to continue, and an objective box obscured the game. Consequently, a renamed crate, a longer conversation or another persistent checklist is an inadequate interior payoff.

This serves DESIGN principles **1, Toys before chores; 3, Side activities are optional; 4, Cooperation is automatic; 6, Communal world, personal rewards; 7, Telegraph everything; 8, Drop-in by default; 9, Leave something for next time**. Residents are welcoming, never an antagonist or a toll collector for essential progress. The approved round-4 brief advances beyond the earlier mechanics-only restrictions in [find-the-fun.md](../plans/find-the-fun.md); this report neither implements its town checkbox nor completes the combined research-and-pictures checkbox.

Read alongside [third-person-camera.md](third-person-camera.md), [comedy-in-games.md](comedy-in-games.md), [little-guys.md](little-guys.md), [growing-towns.md](growing-towns.md), [npc-babble-voices.md](npc-babble-voices.md), [ui-patterns.md](ui-patterns.md), [maximal-rewards.md](maximal-rewards.md) and [personal-rewards.md](personal-rewards.md). Do **not** silently import older som spending/knacks, a prohibition on round-4 helpers, reward-per-hour targets or unapproved Boogler ceremonies. M1 Max is the average target; M4 iPad Pro remains the floor.

## What the references actually offer

### Pokémon: an ordinary door can contain an unexpected person

The original Nintendo Pokémon Red/Blue manual's Pallet Town introduction directs the player to explore, visit the rival and Professor Oak, and talk to people; houses and the lab are distinct destinations. **[V]** ([Nintendo manual, archival mirror](https://gbdbstorage.s3.amazonaws.com/manuals/dmg/DMG-APEE-USA_En_0.pdf), Pallet Town section.) The useful reference is **Pokémon town residences as a dense sequence of optional encounters**, not a prescription to reproduce tile counts, top-down cameras or its transitions.

**Boogle interpretation [H]:** a modest facade can conceal one oddly specific occupation, one incongruous object and one rewarding interaction. The door promises a person, not a quest. A resident need not issue work, explain the railway or pay out an item to justify the visit. Give every enterable building a visible invitation—warm doorway, recognizable sign, someone inside—and make ornamental closed doors visibly different. Otherwise exploring turns into testing identical locked handles.

### Animal Crossing: a shop changes your expression; a museum changes the place

Nintendo describes Able Sisters as a place to try clothing and leave wearing it, Nook's Cranny as a furniture/item shop, and friends visiting each other's shops and museum collections. **[V]** ([Nintendo's New Horizons overview](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/animal-crossing-new-horizons-switch/).) The New Leaf manual describes donated specimens filling exhibition spaces, fossil appraisal and eventual personal exhibition rooms. **[V]** ([Nintendo manual](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_3ds_2/animal_crossing_new_leaf_2/ElectronicManual_Nintendo3DS_AnimalCrossingNewLeaf_EN.pdf), museum section.) A museum turns acquisition into a place someone can inhabit, rather than just adding an inventory entry.

Nintendo's multiplayer FAQ also makes a useful distinction: the representative starts the museum tent, then residents can contribute donations; museum progress belongs to the island. **[V]** ([official FAQ](https://www.nintendo.com/au/support/articles/save-data-and-multiplayer-support-faq-animal-crossing-new-horizons/).) **Boogle [H]:** retain collective display and personal souvenirs, but omit a representative gate, scarce shop stock, opening hours and mandatory donation errands. A friend who did none of the hauling should still get the full visit.

### Zelda houses, Breath of the Wild stables and Wind Waker towns: recognizable functions

Nintendo's Breath of the Wild guide groups villages around shops, accommodation, cooking and information; stables combine horse services with rest, merchants and encounters. **[V]** ([villages and stables](https://www.guiasnintendo.com/2c-switch/zelda-breath-of-the-wild/guia-zelda-breath-of-the-wild/lugares-aldeas.html).) The licensed Piggyback guide describes buying and furnishing Link's Hateno house, and physically approaching a displayed product to buy it. **[V]** ([official guide preview](https://www.piggyback.com/online-guide/the-legend-of-zelda/es/), houses and shops.) These are complementary patterns: a house holds personal life; a stop on a route combines a practical purpose with incidental people. Boogle can borrow that distinction without introducing sleep, horse management or rent.

Nintendo's Wind Waker guide describes Windfall's sail merchant, a dancer near a doorway, and Tingle's confined room with a switch concealed behind pots and a resulting gift. **[V]** ([Windfall Island guide](https://www.guiasnintendo.com/2_GAMECUBE/Zelda_Wind_Waker/zeldawindwaker_sp/islataura.html).) The reusable structure is **person + place + prop + response**. **[H]** Boogle's resident, shopkeeper and curator should be recognizable from their silhouette and activity before the dialogue panel appears. Keep their doorway on the street players actually use. Do not turn all three into a fetch quest, or hide basic interaction behind a secret switch.

### A Short Hike and Untitled Goose Game: curiosity at walking scale

Adam Robinson-Yu's site offers a choice of trails or backcountry, fellow hikers and hidden treasures; his GDC abstract describes shaping a small open world under a four-month initial development constraint. **[V/P]** ([creator's site](https://ashorthike.com/), [Crafting a Tiny Open World](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026613/).) Neither source supplies a minimum room dimension. **[H]** Borrow self-directed detours: the resident can point at an already-built curiosity without placing a new task in the HUD. A satisfying visit should survive being interrupted by a friend calling from outside.

House House's Panic interview recalls that making the goose and a person look at each other immediately created a funny relationship. **[V]** ([developer transcript](https://podcast.panic.com/episodes/s01e01/transcript/).) Jake Strasser's GDC abstract describes using real locations to construct Goose's village. **[P]** ([Google Maps, Not Greyboxes](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027183/Google-Maps-Not-Greyboxes-Digital).) **[H]** First stage a believable little activity—filing an enormous leaf, polishing one button—then let the resident notice the visitor. The joke should remain legible with sound and text off. Do not require theft, punishment or complicated AI to get a reaction.

### Stardew, Yakuza, EarthBound and Psychonauts: authored specificity, cheap revisits

ConcernedApe's 1.6 changelog explicitly adds dialogue responding to events and gifts, shop-arrival bubbles and reactions involving items sold to shops. **[V]** ([developer changelog](https://www.stardewvalley.net/stardew-valley-1-6-update-full-changelog/).) **[H]** A resident noticing the newly opened path makes town growth personal. Daily schedules and friendship grind are not prerequisites. React to a visible fact, not an invisible progression number.

Like a Dragon/Yakuza developers Ryosuke Horii and Hirotaka Chiba describe allocating substory slots by place and emotional tone, repeatedly rewriting weak stories and cutting failures. They distinguish light town conversations from character backstory requiring closer scenario coordination. **[T]** ([AUTOMATON developer interview, Japanese](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/20240906-309567/), substory and party-chat sections; paraphrased here.) **[H]** Use tiny self-contained human concerns with warm resolutions. Do not make every resident a punchline machine, or expand a small amusing visit into a multi-stage substory simply because Yakuza does bigger ones. This also supports keeping collaborator-written rumours separate from Boogler canon.

Hobonichi's EarthBound/MOTHER script project includes dialogue, system/menu text and item descriptions, with distinct responses to yes/no choices. **[V]** ([Itoi's publisher](https://www.1101.com/mother_project/items/book_motherscripts_en.html).) **[H]** Personality can inhabit a shelf label or a single optional answer. Boogle should borrow that attention to ordinary words, not EarthBound's full script volume or copy its jokes. A small choice can reveal someone's earnest oddness without becoming a morality or friendship test.

Double Fine programmer Anna Kipnis explains how Psychonauts starts from world and character, and how repeated fixed rants made Boyd feel mechanical; authored phrase combinations addressed that particular character. She also emphasizes testing scratch dialogue in context and managing overlapping voices. **[V]** ([developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/q-a-double-fine-s-anna-kipnis-digs-into-the-mechanics-of-good-dialog).) **[H]** Boogle needs contextual selection and silence before it needs a combinatorial dialogue engine. Randomly combining rumours risks accidentally asserting a plot. Test lines in the room, with the actual pacing and camera, rather than approving a spreadsheet of isolated jokes.

## Indoors without the walls eating the camera

### Compare the actual options

All choices below are proposed Boogle policies **[H]**, not claims about the internal rendering techniques of the reference games.

| Approach                                  | Good at                                                             | Failure to watch                                                                            | Round-4 use                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ordinary orbit plus camera-arm retraction | Large halls with generous sightlines; familiar controls             | Tiny rooms push the lens into the avatar; conversation turns can pump distance              | Keep as the clearance foundation, insufficient alone for the house                        |
| Roof and camera-facing wall cutaway       | Shows a compact room and its people without moving controls         | Missing walls can look like missing collision; careless masks expose adjacent rooms         | **First candidate:** authored shell sections, a visible low wall/plinth and doorway frame |
| Transparent wall fade                     | Retains enclosure cues while exposing people                        | Layer sorting, glass behind glass, ghostly silhouettes, overdraw, noisy dithering           | Compare only if a clean cutaway fails; never globally fade every obstructing object       |
| Fixed elevated room angle                 | A strong composed tableau; small camera implementation for one room | Can reverse camera-relative movement at the threshold, hide corners, frustrate inspection   | One comparison variant; same orientation through the doorway, no shot/reverse-shot cuts   |
| Literal roof lift                         | Charming opening gesture and clear room reveal                      | Roof can fill the sky, cross other buildings, cast moving shadows; extra motion every visit | A construction/opening flourish if later desired, not the routine visibility solution     |

**First candidate [H]:** keep manual orbit and the player's current yaw; offer a modestly elevated indoor pitch/distance only as a gentle, interruptible suggestion. Remove the active room's roof and only the wall sections between camera and the useful room view. Keep far walls, floor, doorposts and a low wall edge. Decide wall visibility from the camera's side of the room, with hysteresis around corners; do not depend only on a ray to the avatar's feet. Both a speaker and a central exhibit must remain legible. Start with a fully hidden roof and whole near-wall sections before testing a circular hole shader.

Membership comes from the **player's room volume**, not the camera passing through a roof. Start entering the presentation zone about 0.75 m before the threshold; confirm indoor membership when the player crosses inward; release after the player is 0.75 m clear outside. Those margins and a roughly 0.2–0.35 s reveal are hypotheses. Preserve the player's latest manual view on exit; don't snap to a stale saved yaw. Under reduced motion compare an immediate cutaway and stable view, not a moving roof or forced orbit.

### Standard engines versus Boogle

| Engine documentation **[V]**                                                                                                                        | What it provides                                                                                               | Boogle decision **[H]**                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Unity Cinemachine 3.1 Deoccluder](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineDeoccluder.html)                   | Post-processes camera position to preserve sightlines; collider filters, camera radius and obstruction damping | Separate desired composition from collision/visibility. Do not introduce a full shot-scoring system for three authored rooms.         |
| [Unreal Spring Arm, 4.27 documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-spring-arm-components?application_version=4.27) | Collision probing retracts an arm; lag is independently configurable                                           | Boogle already has this basic arm. A deliberate shell cutaway is a separate visibility policy, not a reason to disable all collision. |
| [Godot SpringArm3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/spring_arm.html)                                                            | Sweeps a shape; a direct child camera supplies a near-plane-related shape; a ray fallback is less robust       | Retain Boogle's lens-sized sphere and physics facade rather than substituting a single visibility ray.                                |

Cutaway walls remain solid to players, held objects and the ball. Only the local camera query may ignore the **specific, currently cut-away shell sections**. It must still respect terrain, far walls, furnishings and neighbouring buildings. The present `sweepSphere` interface exposes a mask, not a per-room ignored-collider set. A builder must first see whether existing grouping can express the distinction; otherwise a small reviewed query-filter extension is necessary. Never put every building into one ignored layer to avoid that work. No physical collider is removed to make the view convenient.

The [camera entity](../../src/entities/camera/index.ts) skips its normal arm solve when a `camConstraints` callback returns true. A new interior callback must not accidentally take that escape hatch and discard clearance. Compose room framing and conversation intent, then resolve permitted camera clearance once; verify both the radial arm and intervening orbit motion near corners. [conversation.ts](../../src/player/conversation.ts) updates later than the camera and currently writes yaw/pitch during its introduction. Prefer an opt-in framing policy for new indoor talks that supplies a safe focus and yields to manual look; preserve existing Boogler behaviour and dialogue. Explicitly close or settle the talk if the player is displaced out of reach. Do not hide these issues with a very narrow FOV.

### WebGPU implementation shape, including shadows

Three's [NodeMaterial documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/NodeMaterial.html) provides `maskNode` for fragment rejection, `opacityNode` and separate shadow masking; `maskNode` and `maskShadowNode` are also present in the installed source. **[V/C]** Author roof/wall sections separately. A hard section cut is the cheap baseline; a TSL height/plane mask can retain a low wall and feather the transition through a stable dither if needed. Extend a material through Boogle's `patchNode`, composing its existing mask rather than overwriting another effect. Build the node graph once and update uniforms; never recompile the material every frame.

A camera-specific wall mask must not accidentally remove the building's sun shadow and flood the room with outdoor light. Keep a stable enclosure shadow, using an explicit shadow-mask policy or a simple shadow proxy where appropriate; inspect the actual result in the pinned renderer. Do not assume colour-pass discards and shadow-pass behaviour match. Equally, a mesh hidden with `visible = false` cannot serve as that shadow caster. Avoid adding shadow-casting point lights per room: start with existing illumination, authored ambient colour and emissive display accents.

Scope uniforms per building/section, or deliberately index them for instanced geometry. A shared material opacity change must not erase every cottage. Keep cutaways Local: another client's view can retain this roof. Transparent blending is a fallback, because three's [renderer documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Renderer.html) notes that transparent sorting cannot solve every configuration. **[V]** A roof reveal needs no new post pass, depth-copy pipeline or WebGL fallback. Measure the three-room addition on an otherwise idle M1 Max if it adds substantial mesh/light cost; verify the scaled result on the iPad floor. There are no measured performance claims in this report.

## Doors with no load screen

**Keep these three rooms in their actual buildings in the same world [H].** Continuous floors, actual thresholds, ordinary movement and an already-ready interior are simpler and more social than teleporting to off-map duplicates. Two friends can stand on opposite sides of the same doorway. A door is not a room-server transition; do not reconnect, respawn the player or pause the simulation.

Unity's [additive scene loading](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/SceneManagement.LoadSceneMode.Additive.html) retains existing scenes, while Godot's [background-loading documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/background_loading.html) warns that retrieving a not-yet-ready resource still blocks. **[V]** Boogle has plain modules, one scene and a small authored town: keep the distinction between loading and readiness, but there is no reason to reproduce a general scene-streaming framework for this test.

Prepare the shell, floor, colliders, resident, dialogue and materials before a building becomes enterable, ideally during its existing construction sequence. Reuse the queued [precompile helper](../../src/render/precompile.ts) and warm all three modest rooms before visitors can arrive. Its caught warnings mean a resolved promise is **not** proof that every pipeline compiled successfully. Inspect warnings and first-entry completed frames. Test direct reload inside the town as well as the normal construction route. A small complete room is preferable to a doorway that displays an unexplained wait while assets arrive.

For round 4, leave enterable doors propped open. Approaching the threshold blends room ambience and roof visibility while movement remains live; the ordinary interact button belongs to people and objects. If a hinged door is later added, it must open early, never push or trap a visitor, and stay open while anyone occupies the threshold. Synchronizing that door is additional scope. A screen fade can disguise a transition, but it neither guarantees readiness nor makes a teleport seamless; it is unnecessary for these rooms.

## How little room is enough?

**Hypothesis:** one person, one visual contradiction and one response can make a house worth entering. Floor area is determined by movement and staging, not dialogue length. The current [player constants](../../src/constants.ts) give a 0.45 m radius, 0.8 m head offset and 2.4 m interaction reach **[C]**. Two bare player diameters total 1.8 m; a 2.4 m doorway leaves only 0.6 m combined spare width **[H, calculation]**. Loads, turning, trim and the actual model still need inspection. The existing cottage's 1.5 m opening is therefore a poor two-way starting point.

| Proposed usable interior, excluding wall thickness **[H]** | Clearances and composition                                                       | What earns the space                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| House: **6 × 6 m**, about **3.5 m** ceiling clearance      | 2.4 m clear door; 3 × 3 m central turning area; furniture on two edges           | A resident's home office and one odd desk activity              |
| Shop: **8 × 6 m**, about **3.5 m** clearance               | 2.4 m door/aisle; two independent browsing positions; no counter in the entrance | Three physical cosmetic displays and a fitting spot             |
| Museum: **10 × 8 m**, about **4.5 m** clearance            | 2.4 m door and circulation; two viewing spots around the centerpiece             | One room-scale visual reveal plus a small record of town growth |

These are starting envelopes, not measurements of Pokémon or Animal Crossing. A 4 × 4 m house is a comparison only after the 6 × 6 m version works: if two players plus the resident obscure the joke or cannot pass, it is too small. No upstairs, narrow corridor, functional bathroom or second bedroom is needed to establish a residence. A bed alcove, one personal possession and a work surface do it. Leave jump headroom, use a flush entrance, and give tall hats/bundles space; a cartoon house need not use realistic domestic dimensions.

Put the first discovery to one side of the entrance so the player takes a few steps before recognizing it. Keep the exit identifiable throughout. The museum's centerpiece can be visible only as light and a silhouette from the street, then become legible inside; the reward is a better view, not an arbitrary locked camera. Export door, talk and viewing points as reachable places for [level-check](../../dev/level/). Its present POI-collider gap means a passing reachability report cannot prove the finished room is navigable.

## Residents: short, situational, and allowed to be quiet

The Animal Crossing sound team's original interview describes fitting stylized speech to the subtitle rhythm rather than relying on realistic speech. **[T]** ([translated developer interview](https://shmuplations.com/animalcrossing/).) Boogle already has the needed separation: the displayed words carry meaning; synthesized babble carries mood. Reuse [npc-voice.ts](../../src/render/npc-voice.ts). Create a new `VoiceSpec` per resident and adjust rhythm, vowels and brightness as well as pitch. Keep one foreground voice per local client, stop on skip/close/replacement, and retain instant/silent presentation and readable text without audio. Ambient residents need not babble continuously.

**Writing target [H]:** one thought in roughly **8–20 words**, usually one panel; at most two short panels for a first visit. One optional answer can earn one extra line. These are editing targets, not truncation rules. Never trim existing Boogler lines to satisfy them. A greeting should answer “who lives here?”; a second line can reveal the mismatch between their serious concern and our silly world. Avoid everyone speaking in the same whimsical cadence. A proud clerk, a literal curator and an optimistic newcomer need distinct priorities.

Use the established [conversation/input contract](../../src/player/conversation.ts) and [NPC guide](../game-design/npcs.md), including keyboard E and the registered device prompts. Do not hard-code a new pad button from an older report. First confirm finishes typing; a later confirm advances or chooses. Let the player exit immediately. Display the actual cost before a shop confirmation. Keep the doorway, resident and one important prop visible above/beside the panel at phone and iPad sizes. The world and the other visitor continue normally.

### Growth changes a relationship, not just the random seed

Proposed resident data **[H]**: stable resident ID, home/role, voice, authored lines with stable IDs, availability predicate, priority and repeat policy. Predicates read **committed facts already used to draw the town**. A cosmetic selector may be Local; it must never advance shared growth or consume another player's unseen line.

| Selection order **[H]**            | Example trigger                       | Repeat policy                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Directly requested topic or action | Try a hat, inspect a lamp             | Give the requested response immediately                                             |
| First introduction                 | This player has not met this resident | Once per player; establishes their role                                             |
| Fresh, visible town change         | A path/shop/exhibit actually exists   | Once per player per relevant milestone; newest useful fact, not an old backlog      |
| Optional rumour                    | Player chooses “The Boogler?”         | Sparse, curated, never required for a reward                                        |
| Ordinary personality line          | No new relevant fact                  | Small rotation avoiding the immediately previous line; an ordinary greeting is fine |

Freeze the selected text while a panel is open. If growth happens mid-sentence, finish that sentence; next interaction can notice it. A late joiner gets a current welcome and relevant present fact, not twelve construction announcements. No daily reset, real-world opening hours, friendship meter or “talk to everyone every stage” reward. Start with roughly **six to eight lines per resident**, including responses, then cut weak ones in the actual scene. Silence plus a nod is better than hearing the same punchline on every pass. Save seen IDs only as personal convenience; losing that local preference must never lose an earned cosmetic.

## Shop, museum and rumours: three different contracts

### A shop when money is a joke

The owner has chosen comic money, not an exact accounting rule. **Proposed round-4 rule [H]: money remains a non-spendable comic score; absurd asking prices resolve visibly to a real price of zero before confirmation.** The price board can be ridiculous; the interaction must be clear. Example display: “List price: 40,000,000. Neighbour discount: 40,000,000. You pay: 0.” The confirmation is “Take hat — free”; it cannot secretly debit a score, take resources or create debt. This is a testable proposal, not an assertion that the current game has this economy.

Sell the experience of **choosing, trying on and leaving different**: a paper work hat, a button badge, a cosmetic colour choice. Use only the slots and wearables the builder actually supports; recolouring a test hat is cheaper than implementing a new wardrobe. First shelf available on arrival; a later town milestone adds a new item/colour and a clerk reaction. All are personal and inexhaustible. Show a clear milestone condition on unavailable items; never imply the player must grind up to the absurd price. Keep the round-4 axe/carry/depot/ability ladder on its intended progression path, not behind a joke shop economy.

The shop can also surface missed personal prizes once their existing eligibility is met, matching [round-4.md](../plans/round-4.md). Reuse the personal reward identity rather than minting a second copy. The first test need not solve the complete reward-save system. The Boogler's own price jokes remain his existing authored copy; the sample board and clerk below are new characters' editable material, not new quotes, contracts or business history attributed to him. If visitors cannot tell what they actually pay, change the presentation before inventing more currency rules.

### A museum that rewards the eyes

An exhibit must offer a **new view, motion, scale or relationship** that was unavailable before the museum existed. An enlarged copy of a gift crate with a paragraph beside it fails that test. Start with one beautiful, readable centerpiece, a bench/viewing patch for two and one optional label. Build the space around where people stand, not a long corridor of empty completion slots.

Use shared completed work to change the display: the first walking connection adds its miniature span; a later actual train unlock adds a moving miniature carriage. Do not show a train as completed before the plan's late unlock. Everyone sees the same completed exhibits; nobody must donate their only tool, hoard rare drops or obtain the last-hit credit. Personal souvenirs can be offered to every eligible visitor, independently, including a friend arriving late. The centerpiece must work even when neither visitor claims one.

For round 4, the main exhibit below is a **new staged view of a cut-open crystal specimen**, not a new collection or geology system. Its visual treatment is a hypothesis, and must be judged in WebGPU. Exhibit labels explain what the player can see; they do not establish ancient lore or a secret Boogler connection. One visibly changing miniature is enough to test whether a museum makes returning worthwhile. Avoid promising unbuilt galleries with a wall of silhouettes.

### Rumours without committing the story

Rumours can characterize **the speaker's impression of The Boogler** without establishing his past, motives or future. Use a subjective comparison, a mundane uncertainty, or an admission that the resident is guessing. Avoid reported dialogue attributed to him: it would invent his voice. Avoid hidden rooms, dates, crimes, family, secret organizations, collectible evidence and “come back later to learn the truth.” Those are plot promises even when prefaced with “I heard.”

Each candidate gets an editorial note: **speaker; observable anchor; what it asserts; whether it obliges a later payoff**. Accept only flavour that survives removal without breaking anything. One rumour per optional conversation branch; no journal entry, quest marker, rumour counter or reward for exhausting them. Repeated contradictory impressions may be funny, but do not construct a mystery whose answer the owner has not chosen. Keep The Boogler's existing lines verbatim, including lines about retired systems; list potential conflicts for the owner rather than rewriting them.

## Two players indoors

**One building, independent visits [H].** No party pull, instance selection, shared dialogue cursor or merchant mutex. Each player has a Local roof mask, camera, panel, babble and seen-line history. Both can talk to the same resident; the resident's physical root stays in its authored spot. For round 4, a seated or behind-counter resident avoids inventing wander authority. Local gaze/face animation may acknowledge the viewer without turning a shared collider differently on each peer. A walking resident later needs the Host/owner model described in [little-guys.md](little-guys.md), not independent random routes.

| Data                                               | Proposed ownership and boundary                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Room geometry and fixed resident address           | Same authored layout on every client; solid matching colliders                                                                                                                     |
| Camera, cutaway, dialogue, babble, fitting preview | Local; no presence field just to say a roof is hidden                                                                                                                              |
| Which buildings/exhibits exist                     | Derive from the prototype's existing shared progression/snapshot through [coop.ts](../../src/proto/place/coop.ts); production persistence follows the existing world-rule contract |
| Cosmetic claim/equipped appearance                 | Personal claim; share only the supported equipped appearance through the existing avatar route; do not consume global stock                                                        |
| A public bell or exhibit action, if added          | One-off validated/rate-limited room message if everyone should see/hear it; a physical movable shared object instead requires owner sync                                           |

The smallest test has no shared loose exhibit prop or closable door. The crystal exhibit's decorative motion can follow a common available phase, while the displayed milestone set must agree. Don't synchronize every lamp flicker. If an action is advertised as public, implement it as public instead of letting each visitor silently see a different outcome.

Check one player inside/one outside; both entering in opposite directions; both talking to the same resident; one browsing while the other leaves; growth during a talk; and a late join or authority departure. A local cutaway must not erase the roof for the outside player, alter either player's colliders or reset the other's camera. Keep the talk positions out of the circulation lane so a stationary reader is not a doorstop. At the room cap, a small house may be crowded: never trap visitors, reserve enough entrance apron, and test an eight-player pile-up before promoting the layout even though the round-4 gut test is two people.
