A · recommended next

Split-rock Works

A bright stone bowl making comically large bridge feet.

Crack a wall. Open a permanent haul cut.

The rail turns a folded walk into a direct freight route. The first load changes the village you just returned to.

Export: precast footings → home ramp + sea-deck supports
Co-op, first access & risk

Together: scout the upper cut while a friend stocks the lower frame. Every job works sequentially alone.

Reach first: existing southern footpath; no hook, glide or new material needed. Existing logs, stone and forge rails build the approach.

Risk: T2 already has stone. Without a good breach action and useful export, this is another rock chore.

Bound: one bowl, one breach, one works, two installations. No terrain destruction or curing timer.

Play target: 35–50 min
Effort: 3–5 proof / 8–14 total person-days
B · the next big payoff

Ring Island

The impossible shape on the horizon becomes a working place.

Brace an aperture. Let the first load through.

Build the crossing from the reachable shore. A steel works at the ring's foot pays back the entire railway.

Export: steel kits → longer spans + a known train's upgrade
Co-op, first access & risk

Together: supply the advancing workfront, then regroup for the first ride. One scouts the gallery while another parks the brace.

Reach first: A's footings plus existing timber and rails. Never require far-island steel to reach the island.

Risk: loading/recovery at the far shore, repeated sea errands, and a landmark promising more than the area delivers.

Bound: one landing and ring yard, one aperture, one home fitting. No boat, fuel economy or mandatory new movement power.

Play target: 50–75 min
Effort: 5–8 proof / 16–26 total person-days
C · later vertical freight

High Gorge

A mountain station above the railway you remember building.

Install a counterweight. Lift the next build.

Use a broad walking approach, then raise freight to the upper terrace. The rail spans the gorge beyond.

Export: copper hoist fittings → home gantry + high loading terrace
Co-op, first access & risk

Together: one loads below while another receives above. Alone, call the lift from either end and ride between jobs.

Reach first: earlier steel and cement, with an ordinary walking route. High movement challenges stay optional.

Risk: moving-platform carrying, camera occlusion and steep rail grades. Another gorge needs more than extra height.

Bound: one lift, two yards, one contour line. No growing crystal supports or electrical-grid simulation.

Play target: 50–70 min
Effort: 5–9 proof / 18–30 total person-days
D · strongest alternative

Canopy Town

A working glasshouse under huge trees, with a train in its aisle.

Shift the shutters. Thread a giant spindle.

Keep the great trees alive. The railway passes under the canopy and through a permanently opened loading hall.

Export: tough fibre → carry sling + crane straps + awnings
Co-op, first access & risk

Together: a friend guides the spindle while you change the opening. Park the load, swap jobs or finish alone.

Reach first: ground path and existing timber/rails. Hook/glide side routes use earned moves; they are not assumed after T3.

Risk: dense foliage and a costly new town kit. Convenience must be visible: a strap removes an actual manual transfer.

Bound: one street and loading aisle, two shutters, two fittings. Use rigid bundles; no cloth simulation.

Play target: 45–65 min
Effort: 4–7 proof / 14–22 total person-days

Forest alternative: Canopy Town replaces High Gorge; fibre replaces the proposed copper-lift dependency. Re-budget the port rather than requiring both. If today's starting point is already the completed pier, choose Ring Island next and establish its near-bank supplies first.

Smallest useful proof

One quarry. Two useful shipments.

Quarry kits→Home ramp+Sea deck

Prove a short breach-and-return loop, then the two installations. Keep the cove and T1–T3 intact. Check solo, a helper leaving, and a late visitor using the result.

Owner question: “Did bringing that back make you want to set off again?” Then stop for the verdict.

Why this order

The second source must repay the first line.

A tests that with existing geography. B then pays off the horizon. C or D adds a different kind of work after the export loop has earned its place.

Time targets: demo + A = 65–80 minutes; add B = 115–155. These are hypotheses, not an eight-hour campaign. If the good actions take less time, keep the shorter area.

  • House House: Big Walk FAQ — its required-group model differs from Boogle's solo/drop-in promise.
  • Nintendo: Kirby and the Forgotten Land — forgiving actions, quiet space and readable geography.
