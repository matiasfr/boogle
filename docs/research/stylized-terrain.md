# Beautiful land: smooth ground, authored cliffs

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Stylized terrain for Boogle's Sunshine-like archipelago: broad cliff planes without hand-painting every surface; heightfields, cliff kits and volumetric surfaces; TSL materials, collision and level validation; per-island authoring and a 1–4 km world on an M4 iPad Pro. Live primary-source research and source inspection at `76f20fee`. Recommendations and hypotheses, not an approved implementation plan. No game changes, runtime tests or device benchmarks were performed._

## Answer and evidence

**The best first investment is smooth-normal heightfield land plus a small, deliberately shaped cliff kit, sharing one opaque procedural material family.** Keep the heightfield for lawns, beaches, paths and broad slopes. Give the gorge its own rim, large rock faces, corners, shelves and occasional overhang. A finer height grid cannot provide those forms; a world-wide volume would solve a much larger problem than the current game asks for. **Hypothesis H1:** this hybrid will close more of the remaining gap to the painting per authoring day than either more shader detail or a full remesh.

**Per-island terrain modules are a good authoring boundary, provided they share surface, material and lifecycle contracts.** An island may contain several terrain chunks and a cliff kit; it should not acquire its own renderer, physics world or incompatible ground API. Separate the island's identity from both its sampling grid and its streaming units. **Hypothesis H2:** this makes a varied archipelago substantially easier to author and stream than enlarging today's global grid.

The principles served are [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)'s **Telegraph everything** (land and paths communicate where to go), **Designed for drop-in, drop-out** (a pleasant solo place), **Small and readable**, and **Code first, images by approval**. The [owner's decision log](../decisions.md) supplies the important constraints: the rail route and distant discoveries matter; solid-looking things must be solid; the M4 iPad is a target, not a reason to make the art timid. This report does not change the day/night decision or today's island layout.

## What the references actually teach

### Mario Sunshine and Odyssey: author the land as a playable object

In the translated **2002 Nintendo Dream developer interview**, Tezuka describes Koizumi's team making a clay island, and explains that tall buildings supported wall kicks. This is direct evidence of composing place and movement together, not a description of terrain shaders. [Sunshine developer interviews](https://shmuplations.com/mariosunshine/). **[V]**

For Boogle, take **Sunshine's tactile, sunlit masses**, warm cliffs against cool water, and readable terraces. Give the gorge a few memorable shoulders and ledges rather than uniformly detailed noise. Calling Sunshine “all hand-painted” or assigning it a particular cliff blend without production evidence would be speculation. **[I/U]**

**Odyssey** is valuable because its kingdoms deliberately have different material and shape languages. Nintendo's [official travel guide](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Super-Mario-Odyssey-1173332.html) presents Seaside's central Glass Tower and resort, Cascade's waterfall landscape and Luncheon's contrasting volcanic setting. It establishes authored destinations; it does not establish a universal heightfield, voxel or triplanar implementation. **[V/U]** Extract Seaside's strong coast silhouette, Cascade's large broken shelves and Luncheon's bold shape simplification as **art hypotheses**, not a single shader recipe. One reusable Boogle cliff grammar can change proportions and palette per island without making every island geologically identical. **[I]**

### Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom: readable layers, then useful voids

Nintendo's [BotW GDC 2017 session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024562/Change-and-Constant-Breaking-Conventions) identifies art, technical and design directors discussing the game's convention changes; only its abstract was checked here. [TotK's developer interview, part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-3/) is more specific: cave entrances make familiar cliffs worth investigating again, and too many sky islands made the sky cluttered. The [CEDEC 2024 session abstract](https://cedec.cesa.or.jp/2024/session/detail/s661ccb22afa0f/) confirms that seamless surface, sky, caves and depths required both technology and production workflows. **[V]** None of those abstracts/interviews specifies the shipped cliff shader or proves the whole world uses one volumetric representation. **[U]**

**Art interpretation:** BotW/TotK terrain reads through large hill silhouettes, broad rock colour, restrained strata, grass caps and atmospheric separation. Smooth hill lighting can coexist with sharp cliff breaks; “cel shaded” does not mean every rock should get the character's hard lighting bands. For Boogle, try warm, low-contrast rock faces with dark creases and cool open shade, keeping variation below the strength of the real face lighting. An overhang earns its geometry when it frames a route, shelters a path or makes an opening visible. **Hypothesis H3:** a handful of such authored breaks matters more than dense normal-map detail at the current follow camera. This is a Boogle hypothesis, not a recovered Nintendo algorithm. **[I]**

### Genshin Impact: separate composition, terrain colour and rock treatment

Haoyu Cai's [GDC 2021 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2021/2021GDC+_+Haoyu+Cai+_+presentation+file.pdf) were downloaded and pages **22–25 rendered and inspected**. Page 22 juxtaposes concept composition and the resulting landscape; page 23 explicitly distinguishes **Terrain Tint** and **Grass-filling**, with a tint-painting interface; page 24 shows large fractured cliff forms and a texture treatment labelled **Blur Slope**. It demonstrates authored composition plus reusable surface treatment, rather than unique painted detail on every face. **[V]** The slides alone do **not** prove a specific runtime mesh-to-ground alpha blend, triplanar mapping or the cliff-generation algorithm. **[U]**

Take the separation: composition in geometry, regional tint in a cheap field, grass colour coordinated with the ground, reusable rock variation. Boogle can express tint and coverage as generated vertex attributes or small masks and keep all albedo in one shared material graph. It does not need Genshin's asset count. **[I]**

### Sable and A Short Hike: editing detail is part of the technique

Gregorios Kythreotis's [Sable GDC abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027721/The-Art-of-Sable-Imperfection) explicitly connects small-team constraints, a handmade appearance and readability. In his transcribed statements in [Game Developer's talk report](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability), he explains that the desert allowed concentrated destinations, and that lighting, shadows and biome-tuned distant fog restored depth to flat colours. These developer statements support the art decisions; they do not identify the outline implementation. **[V, developer statements reproduced in coverage]** Take quiet surfaces and distinct distant layers. Sable's ink treatment is optional and does not match the accepted Boogle painting closely enough to drive this work. **[I]**

Adam Robinson-Yu's own [A Short Hike account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/) explains how low resolution led to cohesive flat shading, no antialiasing and soft outlines; it also describes making the landscape inviting to explore. Its [GDC abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A) places the initial release inside four months. **[V]** The lesson is a consistent level of detail and a compact, shaped experience. Boogle should borrow the discipline, while keeping the accepted clean resolution and smooth ground. These sources do not establish A Short Hike's complete terrain mesh pipeline. **[I/U]**

### Astroneer and Valheim: choose topology for the game, not the screenshot

System Era's Brendan Wilson [describes Astroneer's terrain directly](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/what-i-astroneer-i-s-devs-learned-while-leaving-early-access): signed voxel density, marching cubes, generated collision and rebuilding affected chunks. He also describes the difficulty of the system and the loss of offline optimization opportunities when everything is deformable. **[V, interview transcript]** That cost serves Astroneer's central digging toy. Boogle's fixed gorge does not currently need runtime sculpting, density edits, remeshing or density synchronization. Its useful inheritance is that render and collision derive from the same surface. **[I]**

Iron Gate's [Valheim FAQ](https://www.valheimgame.com/faq/) explicitly describes low-resolution textures and sparse polygons combined with modern materials, lighting and post effects. **[V]** This supports a tiny-texture budget; it does not prove that textures are absent, or that low-poly geometry automatically runs cheaply. Borrow the broad material/palette coherence and atmospheric depth, not its survival terrain requirements or pixelated texture treatment. Do not infer Valheim's exact heightmap resolution or cliff pipeline from this FAQ. **[I/U]**

### Sea of Thieves and Tiny Glade: a small kit needs strong assembly rules

Rare environment artist Joachim Coppens [describes shaping Sea of Thieves' islands and populating them with team-produced assets](https://www.joachimcoppens.com/work/sea-of-thieves-shores-plenty), including the Smuggler's Bay rock formations. Hannah Knowles's [Molten Sands production account](https://hlsmith.artstation.com/projects/3oLwLJ) separately names terrain sculpting, texture application, foliage, prop placement and texture balancing. **[V]** These support a composed ground-plus-assets workflow; neither specifies a universal seam shader. The useful reference is large rock masses arranged into a distinctive island silhouette, with vegetation clustered around their meeting points. For Boogle, build a few ledge/corner families and expose intentional placement controls. **[I]**

In the [Tiny Glade developers' interview](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games), Anastasia Opara explains that they author the procedural rules and that the early wall generator developed into the game. Elevating buildings for bridges created further requirements for supports and intersections. Tomasz Stachowiak describes their custom Vulkan rendering path. Opara's [SideFX talk abstract](https://www.sidefx.com/learn/talks/believability-in-procedural-modelling/) describes complexity emerging from layered simple rules. **[V]**

Borrow **context-aware joins**, not their whole renderer: a wall ending becomes a corner, a grass rim caps a rock face, a path crossing the rim leaves a deliberate ramp opening, and a shelf receives a few coherent chips. Generate these from a handful of semantic curves and kit pieces, offline or once at load. Tiny Glade is evidence that carefully written rules can look authored; it is not evidence that arbitrary noise or a universal procedural generator will do the art direction. **[I]**

## Compare the three terrain choices

Effort below is relative engineering/authoring effort, not a schedule estimate. All rankings are **[I]**.

| Choice                                          | Beauty it buys                                                                     | What it cannot buy alone                                                                              | Effort and runtime implications                                                                                                                                     | Boogle verdict                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Smooth-normal heightfield + procedural material | Continuous grassy hills, readable paths, broad palette variation; least disruption | Vertical surfaces with zero horizontal thickness, overhangs, arches; authored cliff-plane silhouettes | Low effort; existing geometry/collision. Fragment detail can still become expensive.                                                                                | **First baseline**, sufficient for most traversable ground.      |
| Heightfield + authored/generated cliff kit      | Large planes, sharp rims, ledges and overhangs where the player sees them          | Automatically seamless arbitrary intersections; multi-level ground queries                            | Medium effort concentrated in a small kit and seam/collision contract. More visible triangles/draws, but static, cullable and reusable.                             | **Preferred production direction**, tested on one gorge segment. |
| Local volumetric remesh                         | Continuous cave/arch/undercut surfaces across irregular joins                      | Good art direction, crisp planes or cheap LOD by itself                                               | Medium-to-high setup; density sampling, polygonization, normals, simplification, collision generation. Can be baked to ordinary static meshes.                      | Useful **local alternative** for an awkward cave mouth or arch.  |
| Whole-island/world volume                       | Arbitrary topology everywhere and possible future deformation                      | A reason to make every lawn volumetric                                                                | Highest effort; sparse storage, LOD transitions, collision residency and surface querying all become project work. Runtime editing adds persistence and networking. | Do not make this the prerequisite for beautiful cliffs.          |

Marching cubes triangulates the chosen scalar field; it does not automatically create the painting's big planes. Smooth-union noisy fields tend toward rounded lumps; hard rock needs deliberately constructed fields and controlled normals or authored meshes. Increasing voxel resolution may merely produce more small triangles. Ryan Geiss's [GPU Gems chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu) explains the density-to-mesh approach and triplanar mapping. Eric Lengyel's [Transvoxel description](https://transvoxel.org/) explains why unequal-resolution voxel chunks need transition geometry. **[V/I]**

There is a useful fourth production tactic within the hybrid: construct a local cliff from SDFs, remesh and simplify it **offline**, then ship a static kit mesh plus collision data. That avoids paying the authoring representation's runtime cost. **Hypothesis H4:** this helps unusual arches, but hand-shaped low-poly planes remain quicker for the first gorge wall. Keep one equal-size comparison, not a new engine project. **[I]**

## Make cliff meshes meet ground in three separate ways

### 1. Geometry closes the gap

For the smallest gorge section, author **one rim polyline and one foot polyline**, a few face-plane directions, and exclusions for the existing path and bridge landing. Sample the existing ground along the rim; generate a narrow transition apron and a thick grass cap from those same samples. Put the visible rock wall between rim and foot, with deliberate breaks wider than a grid cell. Proposed first sizes: a **24–32 m section**, **4–8 m broad faces**, **0.3–0.8 m rim/apron width**, and **0.25–0.5 m buried visual overlap**. These are starting values, not measured tolerances. **[I]**

Use a six-piece vocabulary: straight face, inside corner, outside corner, ledge, grass cap and foot/talus piece. A path notch is an assembly constraint, not a random missing rock. Vary length, lean and chipped corners within limits; do not scale every axis arbitrarily or repeat the same prominent notch every six metres. Keep a deliberate hard normal break at the lip and selected rock joints, while sharing/blending normals across a genuinely smooth apron. Weld coincident vertices only where their shading and topology should be continuous.

For a cosmetic face placed outside an already solid slope, bury its back in solid ground and keep every newly accessible ledge collidable. For a genuine undercut, **remove the obstructing heightfield surface in that patch**, not just its pixels. Since Boogle's facade has no hole mask today, the smallest robust experiment is to partition a bounded patch: unaffected rectangles remain heightfields; a replacement static triangle mesh covers the cut region, apron, face and floor. Build its render and collision topology from one description. Do not lower hidden heights and assume the resulting steep cells are harmless. **[I]**

The cave collars are a useful existing visual precedent, but the cave's occupancy-based collision-group switch is a special solution. A generic outdoor cliff should not disable all scenery collision when the player walks under its lip. The actual seam should remain solid in every approach direction. **[L/I]**

### 2. Material coordinates and normals close the shading gap

Ground and cliff share palette, roughness, coordinate origin, macro variation, material masks and light response. At a grass-to-grass apron, both sides use the same colour function and compatible normals. At a grass-to-rock rim, a sharp but irregular material boundary is intentional. A common procedural function prevents a different tint on each instance; it does not hide a crack or fix a silhouette.

Start opaque. If a joint remains visible, adjust the actual cap, foot wedge or material mask. Depth-faded transparent skirts, terrain redrawn in a second pass and screen-space blending create grazing-angle, shadow and overdraw problems. An engine's virtual-texture terrain/mesh blend can transfer surface attributes, but cannot resolve an incorrect physical boundary. Boogle does not need a runtime virtual-texturing system for a small reusable cliff kit. **[I]**

### 3. Contacts close the physical gap

Use fixed trimeshes for concave cliff patches and simple primitives only where they fit the visible shape. [Rapier's collider guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders/) documents these representations and the restrictions of triangle meshes on dynamic bodies. **[V]** Keep the player's walkable rim and ledges at full collision fidelity; remove tiny purely visual chips from collision only when they cannot alter a step, handoff or camera clearance. A proposed **≤2 cm error on walkable joins** is a test target, not a Rapier guarantee. Give solid rock sufficient thickness and do not rely on double-sided rendering to make it physically solid.

Build a continuous collider across a walked-on join where possible. Buried overlap is useful visually, but two exposed overlapping collision skins can produce duplicate contacts and snagging. Check both travel directions, ball sliding, jumping onto the cap, walking beneath the lip and camera retreat into the wall. The backend's internal-edge flags help a single mesh; a physical seam still needs inspection. **[L/I]**

## Grass, rock, sand and paths in TSL

**Hypothesis H5:** broad semantic colour and correct normals will supply enough texture at Boogle's camera; one tiny filtered noise texture is a useful fallback, not the starting dependency. All numeric controls in this section are proposed. **[I]**

### A texture-free first version

Give the geometry a `vec4` material-weight attribute for grass/rock/sand/path, plus a separate small contact/crease attribute if needed. These are semantic values, independent of display colour. Bake the base weights from authored path distance, shore distance/height, slope and rim masks; normalize and retain a fallback material. An authored path wins over grass on a walking ramp; a cliff face cannot become grass simply because a smoothed rim normal points upward. Keep gameplay surface tags separately in headless data: gameplay must never read a shader colour.

Use **geometric world-space steepness** for material classification and intentionally authored normals for illumination. On the heightfield, the current per-fragment facet normal avoids misclassifying the cliff rim, but should not dictate every rock's lighting forever. On a cliff kit, per-face normals come from the authored large planes; interpolate only over bevels/smooth patches. `normalNode` expects view-space output. `normalFlat` is already view-space in the installed three.js source; do not compare its `.y` directly with world up. Convert deliberately, as `look.ts` does, or pass a world-space classification normal.

Suggested shared material ingredients:

| Surface | Colour/coverage                                                                                                                                           | Normal/detail policy                                                                                                              |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grass   | Two restrained greens, varied over **20–60 m** and gently over **4–10 m**; path/shore masks carve it away. Start less yellow than the current lime grass. | Smooth terrain normals; a few opaque tufts near the player use the ground's tint and masks, rather than a full alpha-card carpet. |
| Rock    | Warm tan/ochre face colour, slightly cooler/darker feet and creases; sparse strata over **2–5 m**, not a line in every cell.                              | Large authored planes and short bevels. Keep normal-map detail off in the first comparison.                                       |
| Sand    | Broad pale shelf and subdued wet edge, driven by actual sea/contact geometry; a height rule alone must not paint every low inland floor as a beach.       | Smooth, high roughness; no glitter competing with water.                                                                          |
| Path    | Warm dirt controlled by the existing `PATHS` distance/ribbon, with an irregular **0.2–0.5 m** edge.                                                       | Preserve walking grade; detail changes albedo, not collision. A path notch through the cliff is geometry too.                     |

A handful of smooth, low-frequency functions is enough for the zero-texture candidate. Avoid deep fBm or per-pixel Voronoi stacks just to claim “no textures.” High-frequency procedural patterns need derivative-aware fading as they become subpixel; textures get that filtering from mipmaps. No camera-space grain, random changes each frame or unfiltered narrow strata. Keep colour linear and roughness near the existing **0.9–0.95** starting point. Put custom colour in `colorNode`, shading normals in `normalNode`, and conservative cavity response in `aoNode` or the existing contact attribute convention, without counting the same darkening twice. Preserve normal lighting, fog and the single output transform. **[I]**

The implementation footholds are [groundMaterial](../../src/proto/place/look.ts), [paintVertex](../../src/island/terrain.ts), [stamps.ts](../../src/island/stamps.ts) and [material-patch.ts](../../src/render/material-patch.ts). Share the proven graph from `src/render/` when promoted; do not make features import the place prototype. Use `patchNode` when extending an existing material. TSL derivatives and texture samples stay outside divergent branches, and `smoothstep` edges stay ordered per the [render contract](../../src/render/README.md). **[L/I]**

### If a tiny texture wins

Try one original **128² RGBA data texture with mipmaps**, packing low-contrast variation/edge disturbance/height cues. At 8 bits per channel that is about **85 KiB uncompressed including mipmaps**, before driver allocation; 256² is about **341 KiB**. These are calculated footprints, not download sizes. Treat it as `NoColorSpace`; tint with linear palette colours. A colour texture would instead need sRGB decoding. Follow the existing image-approval and asset pipeline if an authored/generated image becomes a shipped asset. **[I]**

Use planar x/z mapping for ordinary ground and **triplanar only where useful on rock**. The standard construction is three samples at yz/zx/xy, weighted by normalized powers of the absolute geometric normal. NVIDIA's [GPU Gems treatment](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu) and [Godot's material docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html#triplanar-mapping) document the idea. **[V]** It removes projection stretch; it does not remove geometry striping.

The installed `three/src/nodes/utils/TriplanarTextures.js` exports `triplanarTexture`/`triplanarTextures`; it performs **three texture samples**, defaults to **local** position/normal and uses normalized absolute-normal weights. Pass a consistent world or island coordinate frame explicitly to align separate meshes. Changing the weight sharpness requires a custom helper; it is not an argument to that implementation. The [TSL API](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/TSL.html) provides the nodes. **[L/V]** Start with colour/mask sampling only: tangent-space normal maps need per-projection reorientation before blending, not a direct RGB mix. **[I]**

For four surfaces, do not sample four complete albedo/normal/roughness stacks on every pixel. Reuse the packed pattern and explicit weights; split broad material classes into spatial batches only if measurements justify the draw tradeoff. A tiny texture reduces memory, not necessarily the cost of sampling it across millions of pixels.

### Height blending means microrelief, not world elevation

World height/slope chooses where materials belong. Material **height blending** changes how their small surface structures win at a boundary. A texture-free cliff can skip it entirely. For a two-material edge, or the packed texture experiment, this is a proposed algebraic sketch rather than compiled TSL:

```text
b_i = normalized authored coverage, sum(b_i) = 1
s_i = b_i + strength * (detailHeight_i - 0.5)
m   = max(s_i for layers with nonzero coverage)
q_i = b_i * max(s_i - m + transitionWidth, 0)
w_i = q_i / sum(q_i), or b_i if the sum is too small
colour = sum(w_i * palette_i)
```

Positive `transitionWidth`, a nonzero base layer, and the explicit fallback prevent empty pixels. Zero-coverage layers must not participate in the maximum. Normalize any blended normal with a safe geometric-normal fallback; do not feed a zero vector into lighting. Unreal's [height-blend documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-materials-in-unreal-engine#landscapelayerblendissues) documents the all-zero failure. **[V/I]** A narrow material edge will still look coarse if it exists only at 2 m vertices: refine the local boundary strip or sample a local mask, not the entire island. A **256² mask over 64 m** gives 0.25 m texels; the same mask across 4 km gives 15.625 m texels and cannot describe a path edge.

## Keep collision and level-check honest

The necessary architectural change is **one headless description of physical surfaces**, not a new ground shader masquerading as collision. Extend the existing island/blueprint pattern so authoring yields terrain triangles, cliff/structure collision pieces, surface tags, exclusion cuts and intended reach points. Render geometry may add small nonphysical detail; it must retain the shared boundaries. A build manifest should identify source/version and bounds for each result. **[I]**

Replace universal assumptions about `groundHeight(x,z)` gradually:

- Keep a cheap height query for a known single-valued terrain patch, with **triangle-aware interpolation matching the actual mesh diagonal**. Verify Rapier's diagonal and axis ordering against the rendered patch; do not assume the bilinear helper or an engine default is equivalent.
- Introduce a proposed `surfaceBelow(x, y, z, context)` query returning height, normal, material/surface ID and validity for multi-level places. A query from beneath an overhang must not teleport to its grass roof. Explicitly select the relevant floor for spawning, decorating and fall recovery. This is a proposed interface, not an API already in the game.
- Give `reach` points elevation/surface identity and clearance, alongside x/z. Validate approach routes to the rim, undercut floor and bridge landing separately. `groundProbe.under`/`netted` and the camera arm must consume compatible physical surfaces; a normal ground safety net must not hoist a player out of a valid undercut.
- Retain today's cheap grid flood for simple land, but mark its coverage honestly. At multi-level patches, derive walkable spans/surfaces from the collision description and connect only those passing slope, capsule clearance and transition checks. A 2D blocked footprint alone incorrectly blocks a tunnel or permits a walk through its ceiling.
- For the smallest spike, add the gorge's plain geometry to the existing headless tooling and exercise the real player/ball through the physics facade. The current [sim harness](../../dev/sim/README.md) does not automatically load place or all POIs. Likewise, a passing `npm run level-check` on today's island says nothing about a new gorge manifest until the checker actually loads it.

This closes a larger gap than terrain alone, but should start with the **one changed gorge patch**, not a general-purpose navigation rewrite. The [rail-slice plan](../plans/rail-slice.md#terrain-and-look) already requires solid structures and acknowledges missing POI collision coverage. Keep rail elevation authored; sampling the gorge floor would move the rail off its trestle. Static terrain is shared content, not Owner physics or per-frame network state. Its gameplay inputs must be identical on server/clients, through the existing world-input generation and stable IDs. Physics remains outside lockstep. **[L/I]**

## Should the grid change?

**Not globally for this look.** Keep today's 1.25 m main ground and 2 m place control while comparing shapes. Preserve visual/collider agreement if refining geometry locally; subdividing a triangle without changing its plane does not add detail, while resampling/smoothing positions changes the physical surface. Increasing 2 m to 1 m everywhere quadruples quad count and still provides no overhang. An optional **0.5–1 m local apron/path patch** should earn its resolution by silhouette or contact error. **[I]**

Separate four scales: terrain source sampling, render LOD, collision fidelity and gameplay addressing. They need not use one resolution. In particular, visual LOD must not move the floor under a stationary player, and finer rock shading must not renumber saved cells. A heightfield grid can remain convenient inside one island without being the address space for the whole archipelago.

For scale, assuming **1–4 km means total world span**, not 1–4 km² of land, these full-square allocations are deliberately pessimistic comparisons. Each assumes positions, normals and colours as float32 vec3 (**36 B/vertex**) and uint32 indices (**24 B/quad**), with no LOD, sea omissions, compression, duplicate borders or masks. Formula: `36 * (L/c + 1)^2 + 24 * (L/c)^2` bytes. **[I, calculated]**

CPU arrays, upload staging, collider acceleration structures, masks, far meshes and the rest of the game are additional. A sea-dominated world should not allocate ground at lawn resolution beneath every crossing. These figures are not claims about current residency or a device limit.

Do not stretch `GRID_SIZE` with `GRID_RES` fixed: that silently loses playable detail and changes cell meanings. Audit all `cellOf`, saved cell, source-ID and bounds users before a production transition. Follow `WORLD_NS` clean-break/version rules rather than silently remapping data. `Int16Array` centimetres also limits a zero-offset terrain height to roughly **±327.68 m**; taller islands need a declared vertical origin or wider storage, not overflowing samples. **[L/I]**

## Each island as a module

**Recommend island-local authoring, shared world services.** A proposed module exports a stable ID/version, world transform and bounds, height patches, cliff placements/cuts, surface metadata, route connection sockets, POI/reach data and a far silhouette. A shared compiler/builder produces the views and collision data. Keep these exports plain TypeScript data/functions; create and dispose runtime views through the existing entity lifecycle when promoted. The prototype can remain in its current scope while the experiment is evaluated. **[I]**

This is finer-grained than making an island one huge mesh and more consistent than giving every island custom ground hooks. Let one island use smooth hills and another a more extensively meshed rock plateau, while both answer the same surface queries and connect to the same rail graph. Stable `(islandId, local feature/cell ID)` addresses should be independent of render LOD and world placement. Any introduction into wire/save state must follow the namespace/version policy; this research does not authorize that migration.

The engine comparison matters. [Unreal World Partition](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine) keeps a persistent world and streams cells around sources. Unity connects neighboring terrain tiles, and Godot separates mesh resources from scene instances. **[V]** Borrow those separations. **Island is the content boundary; chunk is the residency boundary; room is the shared-world boundary.** Do not create separate multiplayer rooms or physics worlds merely because a train crosses the sea.

Use two levels of spatial lookup: coarse island bounds first, then local terrain/mesh patches. Empty sea returns an explicit no-land result, never a clamped edge of home. Keep global rail endpoints and island sockets in a single coordinate convention. If two land modules actually touch, they share boundary samples, normal neighborhoods, material coordinates and LOD edge rules; water-separated islands need no artificial shared grid edge. Floating-origin machinery is not the first task at 4 km, but local mesh coordinates make it possible later; measure precision before adding it. **[I]**

Test the contract with **two instances of the same gorge strip at different origins**, one nearby and one about 1 km away, rather than authoring a second island. Check all physical queries, material scale, source IDs and rail socket positions. This is a future diagnostic for the architecture hypothesis, not a new owner-facing destination.

## M4 iPad Pro: spend on visible shape and bound residency

No source establishes that a particular Boogle 4 km world will run at 60 fps. **The credible candidate is a mostly static hybrid with bounded nearby collision, spatial render batches, simple far silhouettes and one opaque terrain material family.** A large coordinate extent is cheap compared with rendering and colliding with every metre of it. **[I]**

Apple's [WWDC25 WebGPU transcript](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/236/) recommends smaller/compressed storage and fewer command buffers, passes and resource changes; its [WWDC20 Apple-GPU guidance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/) explains hidden-surface removal and bandwidth. **[V]** Opaque static cliffs exploit that pipeline well, but vertex processing, binning, shadow draws and collision are still real work. Three samples from a tiny triplanar texture can cost more than a few broad arithmetic variations, or less than elaborate procedural noise: measure both. **[I]**

Render LOD needs compatible borders: shared edge vertices, transition strips or a baked low-detail boundary. Skirts can conceal an unseen outer edge; they cannot repair a traversed gap or an opening. Use fixed collision resolution near contact and switch it only while the region is unoccupied. For world travel, predict the camera/player/train route rather than relying solely on distance behind the camera. **[I]**

Prefetch distance is at least `speed * (load + decode + build + upload + collision-install time + margin)`, using measured slow-path times. At place's **12 m/s**, a hypothetical 2 s total requires 24 m; at **60 m/s**, 120 m. Those are arithmetic examples, not load benchmarks or a chosen future train speed. Occupied train decks, jump destinations and nearby Owner toys keep collision pinned. On a failed load, stop at the last safe support/arrival boundary and show a loading state; never remove the floor underneath a rider. Distant players do not imply rendering every distant island at full detail. **[I]**

Check cold arrival and repeated travel, then **20 minutes on the actual M4 iPad Pro**, recording OS/browser, device memory variant, actual render-target size, median/p95 frame time, main-thread/physics cost, available GPU timing, draw/shadow counts and resource growth. Do not add overlapping pass timers as though they were necessarily elapsed GPU frame time. If timing is unavailable or unreliable, label that ceiling unverified. Adaptive resolution is tested afterward in normal play. The old iPad report's device/OS claims are historical context, not measurements of this terrain.

## Recommended pipeline

**Brief → authored land data → shared surface build → material assignment → physical/visual checks → device check → promotion.** This follows the existing code-first and asset pipeline, not a new terrain editor.

1. **Brief the actual views.** Use the current follow camera and the selected painting to identify the rim silhouette, broad face rhythm, visible destination and walking/rail clearances. Keep the prototype's existing route and accepted light as controls; art changes should not accidentally redesign the journey.
2. **Author the large shapes.** Island-local height patches form broad land; curves mark rim, foot, path and shore. A reusable code-built kit is first; use Blender for sculpted pieces once it demonstrably improves their forms. Keep named nodes, pivots and normals consistent with [asset-pipeline.md](../plans/asset-pipeline.md). Do not require a unique texture unwrap/painting for each placement.
3. **Compile one source into several products.** Bake terrain/cliff render meshes, shared transition boundaries, simplified collision, semantic masks, reach/clearance data and far silhouettes. Keep the export reproducible and source IDs stable. Generate gameplay inputs with the existing world-input workflow; render assets can compress independently of authoritative IDs and coordinates.
4. **Shade and dress at the right scales.** Use the shared TSL graph and palette; add only deliberate contact darkening. Place trees and sparse grass after paths, clearance and sightline masks. Try the tiny texture only if the texture-free comparison loses. Add LODs and spatial batches before duplicating the kit across an island.
5. **Validate the one patch end to end.** A real patch-aware level check, render/collision overlay, player/ball/camera exercise, fixed stills and real-time motion establish the local result. Source inspection and a default hill screenshot cannot do that. Up to three visual iterations, per AGENTS.md; keep uncertainty behind the prototype flag.
6. **Scale only the proven parts.** Measure real iPad arrival and sustained play, then update the relevant game-design/atlas/prototype records when implementation lands. Preserve the current prototype while it is in use. A research commit checks no implementation boxes in `find-the-fun` or `look-pass`.
