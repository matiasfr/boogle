# Narrative arc: familiar faces along one railway

> **Verdict:** A small recurring cast changed by the railway, one material promise per area.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Give each big area a material promise, a particular beneficiary and a visible lasting consequence. 2. Reuse a small cast through recognition, changed activity and payoff. 3. Build The Boogler's arc through optional rumours and commercial traces while preserving every line.
> **Status:** open. No decision cites it yet.

_Research report, 5 November 2026. Story and character structure for Boogle's roughly eight-hour, material-led journey: The Boogler, residents, little guys, the original-island ending, and storytelling during uninterrupted co-op play. Live web research and focused repository inspection at `68ee082d`. Research and proposals only; no copy, gameplay, approved plan or design decision changed._

**Recommendation [H]:** make the railway change the lives of a small recurring cast. Each major area offers a visible material capability, someone who has a particular use for it, and a consequence that remains after departure. Small stops offer discovery and breathing room. Carry The Boogler through contradictory rumours and ordinary commercial traces until the original-island meeting resolves them. His entrepreneurial reveal is the joke; the useful world friends built is the sincere payoff.

**Evidence.** **[V]** means fetched primary text, including direct developer interviews; **[T]** means a translated developer interview whose original language was not independently checked; **[A]** means a fetched critical analysis of a shipped game, not developer testimony; **[R]** means inspected repository evidence; **[H]** means a Boogle proposal or inference. GDC session abstracts were checked, not their videos. No reference game or Boogle build was played or timed. All proposed timings, cast sizes and emotional effects are unvalidated. Not tested on a device. Sources are inline; ending spoilers appear below.

## Seven references: useful mechanisms and their limits

### A Short Hike: one clear destination, many small relationships

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu describes a summit that offers rest and a new perspective, designing for players who take the opposite route, and optional activities that interrupt the hike pleasantly. His writing became easier when he treated dialogue like messages to friends. The [developer account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/) explicitly pairs light story with personally meaningful concerns. The [GDC postmortem abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026613/) describes the small scope and four-month initial production constraint; it supplies no measured dialogue-duration rule.

**[H] Transfer:** keep an intelligible long-distance invitation while local encounters earn their own interest. A person can want something small and specific without becoming a quest gate. The final viewpoint should let players recognize what they crossed. Boogle's small stops can change pace without adding a new antagonist or a full character arc each time.

**Limit:** a solo miniature world does not demonstrate eight-hour retention or separated-party comprehension. Borrow the clarity and conversational intimacy, not its campaign size as a production estimate.

### Wind Waker: places and their people are designed together

**[V]** Nintendo describes island and resident ideas developing together, the expressive style supporting eccentric bodies and gestures, and Link's gaze becoming a hint. The same [Iwata Asks interview](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But-/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But--807230.html) acknowledges that the original's later portion dragged despite a strong beginning.

**[T]** In the [Nintendo Dream #83 developer interview](https://goodblood.games/wind-waker-interview), available through Good Blood's credited translation, the developers explain choosing the kidnapped sister to make the adventure personally motivating. They discuss grandmother's recovery and the distinctive Medli, Makar and merchant characters. This is translated developer testimony, not a fresh playthrough or an independently verified translation.

**[H] Transfer:** make an area's resident legible through its machinery, posture and everyday concern. Return encounters should change what that person is doing, not merely add text. A visible place and someone with a concrete stake give two independent reasons to travel there.

**Limit:** Boogle needs no kidnapping, royal identity twist, escort dungeon or late-game collection tour. Give an ordinary positive reason to connect places, and reserve production effort for the final third rather than assuming the opening's charm will carry it.

### Spiritfarer: infrastructure becomes meaningful through its beneficiaries

**[V]** Creative director Nicolas Guérin describes resource gathering and boat improvement as means of caring for particular passengers. Personal anecdotes and preferences give those people specificity; they also teach activities. This is a claim about [the team's design intent](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/08/18/spiritfarer-the-cozy-management-game-about-dying-sets-sail-today/), not evidence that every care task avoids becoming a chore.

**[V]** The [Farewell Edition account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/12/13/spiritfarer-farewell-edition-launches-today-on-ps4) says Lily was added to clarify Stella's story and close its narrative loop. It also describes visually restoring Overbrook. Even a character-led game needed to repair insufficient ending clarity.

**[H] Transfer:** show who benefits from a material upgrade: the same little guy who handled a bundle can now watch the loader do that job, then do something else. A resident's concern can motivate an already-useful connection without a separate favour currency. Let the improvement remain visible for the friend who arrives later.

**Co-op caution [V/H]:** Thunder Lotus's [known-issues page](https://thunderlotus.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/25965988245268-Known-Issues-for-Spiritfarer) documents a quest step that fails to advance when Daffodil buys the seeds instead of Stella. This is a specific documented issue, not a claim about all co-op quests. Boogle's narrative acceptance checks should include either friend performing the meaningful action. Nobody becomes the designated protagonist while everyone else supplies labour. Do not import mood upkeep, feeding schedules or compulsory grief.

### Animal Crossing: familiar residents connect different visits

**[V]** Katsuya Eguchi explains that asynchronous family life inspired a shared experience through messages and traces of play. He describes responsive objects and villagers encouraging self-directed curiosity. His [GDC 2006 interview](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/interview/11229/animal-crossing-interview) also distinguishes a game without a conventional ending from Zelda-like stories; the two forms can coexist in a developer's work.

**[H] Transfer:** a familiar resident can bridge sessions by noticing a visible change. The returning player's useful information is that the loader is working and the next place is reachable; they need not receive every missed conversation. A miniature railway, new awning and different resident routine can tell the same history at different distances.

**Limit:** Boogle is a finite expedition into a persistent hangout. Daily appointments, friendship grinding and endless dialogue production would consume the eight-hour arc. Use a handful of authored changes tied to progress rather than imitating the scale of an indefinitely running village. Never make a resident guilt players for absence.

### Untitled Goose Game: routine, interruption, reaction, return

**[V]** Nico Disseldorp describes finding the core comedy by iterating on stealing from one character, using readable attention states, and selecting gags to fit a setting and interaction premise. The goose is a performable body even when the player is its only audience. [House House's interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-) supplies a useful order: place and interaction first, fitting character and joke next.

**[V]** Adding the second goose required choosing whom NPCs react to; distraction then allowed complementary actions. [Disseldorp's co-op postmortem](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/09/22/how-house-house-added-multiplayer-to-untitled-goose-game/) demonstrates that a comic reaction designed for one actor does not automatically read with two.

**[A]** Mary Andrea Arroz's [GDC narrative analysis](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/GameNarrativeReview/MaryArroz-UntitledGooseGame-paper.pdf), pp. 4–5, reads the bell's return through earlier spaces as a reprise of the player's history; the pile of bells reframes the goose's behaviour. This is critical analysis, not a House House explanation of intent.

**[H] Transfer:** make a little guy's concern readable before the interruption, hold the reaction long enough to notice, then let ordinary life resume. Reuse a familiar prop at the end with a different meaning. Do not require a compulsory reverse trip through every area or let a second player repeatedly restart somebody else's gag.

### Outer Wilds: questions lead outward; recognition brings the ending together

**[V]** Kelsey Beachum's [GDC narrative abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027368/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity) describes progression driven by curiosity rather than assigned missions. The speaker is **Kelsey**, not Alex as the older ending report attributes this particular session. The talk's slide PDF could not be fetched; no detailed slide claims are used.

**[V]** In a [2015 narrative development post](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/a-very-narrative-update), the team says earlier hostile townsfolk made the player feel ostracized. It revised them toward friends and mentors who cared about the launch. This is development history, not a description of every shipped line. Composer Andrew Prahlow describes the travellers' separate instruments being conceived as one band and the eventual campfire bringing them together. He also explains leaving exploration quiet so music retains significance. [Composer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-hauntingly-beautiful-music-of-i-outer-wilds-i-).

**[H] Transfer:** seed an interesting question about the next place, then let a material demonstration answer it. At the finish, bring recognizable people, objects or motifs into a shared context. The Boogler can be ridiculous without the entire cast treating players as fools.

**Limit:** a knowledge-gated mystery is especially vulnerable to friends arriving with different information. Boogle's physical gates must not require personally hearing rumours, and the entrepreneur punchline should work even when somebody predicts it. Avoid a lore-reading dependency chain or a cosmic reset.

### Journey: share a view without scripting the other player

**[V]** thatgamecompany describes a [visible mountaintop and encounters with companions](https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/). Jenova Chen's [GDC abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017700/Designing) describes extensive iteration on an emotional arc. In the [CONFLUENCE interview transcript](https://cnam.com/confluencearchive/jenova-chen-full-interview/), around 00:54:42 onward, Chen explains the difficulty of creating a bond between actual players: early physical helping actions also enabled pushing one another into danger; naming and chat affected trust. The transcript contains transcription errors, so this report paraphrases the clear passage rather than quoting it.

**[H] Transfer:** frame places where friends can independently pause and share a view. Give the final approach a quieter rhythm so the meeting feels different from another worksite. A companion pointing, waiting or doing something silly can carry more emotional weight than an NPC narrating friendship.

**Limit:** Boogle is explicitly goofy play among known friends. Keep names, player agency and harmless mischief. Do not transplant Journey's anonymity, solemnity or restricted communication. Author the view and opportunity to linger, not an obligation to feel reverent together.

## A journey structure to test, not a new itinerary

**[H] Three promises travel together:** “the next place lets us do something new,” “someone there has a life worth noticing,” and “what is The Boogler actually up to?” The first must work without the other two. The second supplies warmth; the third supplies anticipation and a comic release.

The following **480-minute allocation is a paper budget**, not measured content, a deadline, or a reason to inflate material bills. It overlays the proposed three-big-area route in [progression](../brainstorms/progression.md). Story beats occur during the allotted play; they do not add a second campaign of errands.

**Reason to go is more than dialogue [H].** Each major area needs a visible invitation, a material benefit demonstrable there, and a beneficiary whose routine makes the benefit understandable. For a small stop, a strange shape, a short movement problem and a view can suffice. Do not add a resident request merely to justify every island. A player who skips all optional text should still understand the next useful destination and see a change when it is connected.

**Do not turn return visits into tolls [H].** The resident may acknowledge the new awning, but visiting them does not activate its benefit. A glance from a train or a later voluntary walk is enough. Avoid a mandatory home debrief after every export.

## Cast structure: recurrence through changed activity

**[H] Start with three recurring roles, using existing people:** one home anchor, one traveller drawn from the cast, and one recognizable little-guy pair. Other residents can remain locally memorable without joining a party. Add a local specialist only when an approved area's activity needs them. This is a scope hypothesis, not a headcount requirement.

Keep a trace of his fussiness. | A small visible role at the hangout pays off his preparation. Exact job and new lines stay for collaborators; he need not become a competent manager. |

| Believer / Wren                    | He interprets something ordinary as evidence of powers.                              | New impressions conflict without becoming a detective quest.                                                                 | One understated reaction can finish the misinterpretation. | Supply pair / existing little guys | A clear, repeated physical job and a distinctive posture or prop establish identity. | Improved loading removes the old effort; they inspect, ride or take a break. Use **recognition → changed context → payoff** as a minimum authored arc. A second meeting should expose a different facet of the same concern. New lines alone do not establish development. A changed scarf alone does not establish a changed life.

Keep geographic continuity plausible. A resident travels only along an open connection; do not show the same named person simultaneously at two distant stops to guarantee a callback. If persistent travel is not available, use an anchored resident and their delivered object or changed room. Authoring a few meaningful encounters is cheaper than promising a continuously simulated society.

### The Boogler: a reveal, not a replacement personality

**[H] Use three kinds of setup, each independently understandable:**

1. **A person's interpretation.** Existing prophet/mystic rumours establish exaggerated expectations. Different speakers should express their own concerns, not repeat an exposition paragraph.
2. **An ordinary trace.** A recurring price-board style, packaging or business emblem suggests a mundane explanation. These are candidate new props requiring approval; no invented quote is attributed to him.
3. **The encounter.** At the old island, familiar commercial objects and his actual behaviour reveal the enterprise. Optional talk gives texture; the physical arrangement carries the joke for a non-reader.

Do not conceal every commercial clue until the last minute. Being suspicious can be fun: the final pleasure is seeing the familiar absurdity embodied, not winning an unspoiled guessing contest. Conversely, avoid calling him a businessman in every early rumour, which spends the whole setup immediately.

The work must still have helped the islands. His claim to the project can be funny while the visible benefits belong to the friends and residents. An ending that says their effort was worthless would undermine [DESIGN](../../DESIGN.md)'s communal world and toys-before-chores principles.

## Storytelling while friends keep playing

**[H] Use three levels of attention, with essential meaning in the first:**

| Attention             | What it carries                                                                        | Delivery rule                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Passing through       | Destination silhouette, a resident's job, a changed routine, exported material in use. | Keep movement and camera control. Stage the result where ordinary work/travel already passes it, not behind a cinematic.                                                                             |
| Brief voluntary look  | One reaction, rumour, small visual reversal or inspection of a prop.                   | A nearby speaker, one current thought, and room to leave. Initial staging target: roughly 3–8 seconds of readable physical action, not a forced wait.                                                |
| Optional conversation | Personal texture, contradictions, retells and The Boogler's existing longer stories.   | Local to the listener; immediate exit and bookmarks where supported. New resident lines can follow [town interiors](town-interiors.md)' short-writing target; never truncate protected Boogler copy. |

Do not auto-open a story panel when someone else finishes construction. Do not steer every camera toward the worker who delivered last. An earned device's existing presentation is a separate product decision; this recommendation concerns **new narrative scenes**, not an unsolicited rewrite of reward cards.

For separated players, **one event can have different points of discovery**. One friend sees the crane start; another later sees its loaded wagon and the worker resting beside it. Both understand the change. This is preferable to replaying an old construction animation through a busy site merely so everyone receives an identical scene.

| Co-op case                                | Narrative acceptance target [H]                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One player talks; another keeps hauling   | No room pause, exclusive speaker lock or required dialogue vote; the second player retains a useful task.                                                |
| Two players trigger nearby activity       | A character's physical reaction remains intelligible; repeated interaction cannot indefinitely restart the beat. Optional talks may remain individual.   |
| A friend returns after a major area       | Show present capability and a familiar changed routine. Offer the newest relevant comment, not a queue of missed chapters.                               |
| A newcomer has heard no rumours           | The old-island entrepreneur and the useful shared world are still understandable. They are welcomed without invented prior friendship or founder credit. |
| A friend misses the final arrival         | The place remains finished and visitable; The Boogler's ordinary business and optional talk still convey the reveal. Attendance does not control access. |
| Voice chat is busy, muted, or unavailable | Gestures and props carry the beat; optional text carries exact wording. No puzzle requires hearing babble or another player's verbal explanation.        |

**Implementation boundary:** [ending-and-after](ending-and-after.md) already distinguishes lasting completion from its presentation, and [production-core](../plans/production-core.md) owns durable progress. This report adds acceptance cases, not a second quest engine, event log or save schema. The current [Boogler memory](../game-design/boogler.md#known-issues-and-limits) is per browser and shared across rooms, so it is not evidence of a room's campaign completion or who witnessed a finale.

## Comedy across the journey

**[H] Author a readable setup and a durable aftermath.** Let friends choose timing where possible. The sources support a craft approach, not a universal joke frequency or a physics mandate for every punchline.

| Beat                | Setup → change → payoff                                                                                                                                             | Failure to avoid                                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Work becomes easier | A recognizable little guy performs an awkward existing task → the useful loader takes it over → the worker solemnly inspects a now-unnecessary tiny tool.           | Faking material delivery, reducing player control or adding a new workload just to manufacture the joke.              |
| Pip's preparation   | His desk is conspicuously ready → the town genuinely gains activity → a very small piece of real business reaches his enormous administrative setup.                | Resolving him only through a mandatory dialogue chain, or changing his protected first impression in this research.   |
| Growing prices      | An existing absurd number occupies a board → its presentation becomes physically more elaborate → the old-island enterprise makes the preoccupation understandable. | A joke price resembling a real access requirement. The player must know what is actually paid.                        |
| A changing rumour   | Different residents interpret the same recognizable evidence → friends compare impressions voluntarily → a mundane activity answers them.                           | A required set of clues, a hidden “rumours collected” count, or every resident sounding equally foolish.              |
| Final interruption  | The entrepreneur is seriously occupied with his enterprise → friends remain free to move or mess about → his established character provides optional reaction.      | A long speech that must finish, a repeated charge, or deleting an existing line because it interrupts the new script. |

Use quiet to preserve contrast. A view, a competent little guy or an affectionate exchange does not need a punchline. Let an ordinary greeting replace escalation on repeat rides. New reactive lines can have a repeat policy; existing Boogler copy is not retired by an automated gag lifecycle. Avoid promising a particular laugh before testing it on someone who did not write the setup.

## The old-island payoff

**[H] Give the arrival three short functions, not three separate finales:**

1. **Recognition:** a silhouette/approach introduced earlier becomes a place players can inhabit. Familiar fittings and residents show that this is connected to their journey. Players new to Boogle cannot feel nostalgia for the classic build merely because its developers do; seed recognizable parts within this campaign.
2. **Reframing:** ordinary business reveals what the rumours exaggerated. The charging gag belongs here, following the owner decision. Recommend a clearly optional comic purchase or service, with the cost visible and a free way to remain and leave. Its exact economy remains an owner choice; no compulsory balance, debt or toll should gate the finish.
3. **Belonging:** let friends use the hangout immediately. A recognizable little guy off duty and a resident's small payoff can supply warmth without announcing it. Some activities may be co-op; arrival, return travel and pleasant solo use must work alone.

The lasting state must communicate that the journey is complete even if the arrival's transient flourish was missed. Retell or revisit the reveal without erasing progress, replaying bills, demanding the whole party or awarding a new completion reward each time. Keep the railway and the hangout usable afterward; there is no obligation to manufacture another progression ladder.

Do not stack an obligatory world tour, resident roll call, long speech, museum debrief and credits scene. The payoff is the ordinary absurdity of the person and the extraordinary fact that friends reached him together. A quiet optional return past what they built can exist without becoming an eight-hour campaign's final commute.

## Numbered recommendations for Boogle

All recommendations are **[H]**, downstream of current playtest/production gates. They serve **Show, don't tell**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, **Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated**, **Leave something for the next visitor**, and **Slapstick over precision**; none approves a new mechanic.

1. **Give each big area a material promise, a particular beneficiary and a visible lasting consequence.** Add these three fields to the candidate area briefs in [progression](../brainstorms/progression.md); use [next destinations](next-destinations.md) for spatial/material work. **Smallest proof:** the proposed first material improves two things, and a player who skipped talk can identify who benefited. Small stops need discovery, not compulsory resident errands.
2. **Reuse a small cast through recognition, changed activity and payoff.** Begin with a home anchor, one possible traveller and the existing supply pair, drawing on [town-lines.ts](../../src/entities/town/town-lines.ts), [residents.ts](../../src/entities/town/residents.ts) and [folk.ts](../../src/place/folk.ts). **Smallest proof:** recognize the same resident after an intervening task and notice a changed routine without a new nameplate or explanatory speech.

## What to prototype first

**Disposition: bounded experiment proposed; research completed, no build performed.** The useful material demonstration and export are the gameplay prerequisite; the [progression brief](../brainstorms/progression.md#shape) places them after production core and the replay. No evidence here grants an exception. A full new story or gameplay feature also takes the owner's brainstorm workflow before building.

**First compare continuity, not more dialogue [H].** Use one already-approved material task, its two useful fittings, one recognizable resident and the existing little-guy pair. Allow roughly **5–8 minutes** for the test, compressing travel for inspection rather than claiming this models campaign pacing. Both variants have the same work, benefit, geometry and rewards:

- **A:** a resident is visible before the work and returns afterward in the improved routine.
- **B:** different anonymous workers occupy those two moments, performing the same actions.

Add no new Boogler dialogue, relationship meter, material bill or special quest. The same optional existing rumour may be available in both variants; it must not explain the improvement. Let the participant do another short activity between the two encounters so recognition has something to overcome.

**What's new:** “The same little guy now gets to enjoy the improvement you made.” **Owner's one gut question:** “Did coming back to that little guy make the connection matter more?” Use fresh observers or counterbalance variants to reduce familiarity effects; no survey is needed for the owner.

**Agent checks before handoff:** solo completion; a partner leaving; either partner completing the useful action; one reading while another works; muted audio; skipped talk; a visitor arriving only after the upgrade; repeated interactions; accessible exit and view at phone framing. Use the repository's look workflow when implementation exists. These checks establish behaviour, not comedy or attachment. Record recognition and voluntary lingering as observations, not an automated emotional score.

**Stop condition:** if the owner does not notice or care about the recurrence, revise that one staging beat before adding biography or more NPCs. If the material benefit itself is weak, return to the progression proof; story must not prop up an unfun task.

**After a positive verdict:** test a small old-island arrival separately with the same familiar prop/resident, an optional commercial gag and a usable hangout. Only a later full campaign with uneven attendance can establish that callbacks survive several evenings, the final third holds interest, and the entrepreneur reveal feels like a payoff. Actual laughter, attachment, pacing and an eight-hour ending remain unverified.
