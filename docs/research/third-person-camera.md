# Third-person cameras: direct control, a readable island, and comfortable rides

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Live web research and a source audit of Boogle at `83b88195`: walking, jumping, carrying, co-op, trains and the soapbox on mouse, gamepad and touch. Read alongside [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), the owner's [Boogle 2.0 decisions](../proposals/boogle-2.md), [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md), [cross-device-input.md](cross-device-input.md) and [coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md](coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md). Follows the research index and the character-controller/level-design reports' evidence-and-recommendations format. Recommendations only; this report changes no camera behavior or owner decision._

**Evidence.** **[V]** means fetched primary documentation, a developer's own account, or inspected repository code. **[P]** means partial access, such as a talk's official abstract without its recording. **[O]** means a published first-hand observation of a shipped game, not an engine specification. **[D]** means calculated from stated assumptions. **[R]** means a proposed Boogle experiment. Every new tuning number below is **[R]**, unless explicitly marked otherwise. No commercial game was instrumented or frame-counted in this research; no metres or damping constants are inferred from promotional screenshots. In particular, an attractive game is not evidence that every version of its camera is universally liked.

## The recommendation in brief

Keep the hero camera's **immediate horizontal follow**, make jumping move the character within a steadier view, and give manual looking clear priority. Start with the existing **5.2 m arm, 62° vertical FOV and −12.6° pitch**. Compare distance before adding more motion. The next substantial improvement is reliable obstruction handling: protect the lens with a swept volume, pull inward when geometry requires it, and recover outward gently. Treat riding, carrying and seeing friends as framing problems with their own constraints, without turning walking into a cinematic chase.

This serves **Toys before chores**, **Telegraph everything**, **Slapstick over precision**, and **Small and readable** in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md). The owner already liked direct hero control and disliked perceived inertia; the [decision record](../proposals/boogle-2.md) also keeps the compact, steady back bundle and excludes a forced side-on train camera. Those are stronger evidence for Boogle's default than another game's fashionable spring settings.

### The existing geometry is already a useful numerical reference

`PLAYER_R = 0.45` in [constants](../../src/constants.ts). On flat ground, treating the body's lowest point as the feet, the aim anchor is **1.80 m above the feet**, not 1.35 m. At pitch −0.22 rad, camera height is `1.80 + distance × sin(0.22)` and horizontal arm reach is `distance × cos(0.22)` **[D]**.

| Flat-ground baseline, without constraints                   |     Default |        Hero |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------: | ----------: |
| Arm distance **[V]**                                        |       6.5 m |       5.2 m |
| Horizontal arm reach **[D]**                                |      6.34 m |      5.07 m |
| Camera height above feet **[D]**                            |      3.22 m |      2.93 m |
| Downward pitch **[D]**                                      |       12.6° |       12.6° |
| Vertical FOV / equivalent horizontal at 16:9 **[V/D]**      | 62° / 93.8° | 62° / 93.8° |
| Approximate upright 1.6 m character height in frame **[D]** |         19% |         23% |
| Approximate head / feet screen Y, top = 0 **[D]**           |   52% / 71% |   53% / 76% |

These last two rows project a vertical 1.6 m segment at the body's X/Z; they are **not measurements of the actual animated avatar**. Skin proportions, hats, jumping, terrain clearance and camera corrections change them. They explain why 5.2 m can feel more connected without adding inertia: the character becomes roughly a quarter larger.

FOV values are otherwise easy to compare incorrectly. Use `horizontalFov = 2 × atan(aspect × tan(verticalFov / 2))`. Boogle's 62° vertical gives about **77.4° horizontal at 4:3**, but only **37.3° at 9:16** **[D]**. A portrait phone has much less lateral context at the same distance. FOV labels in another game's menu are not interchangeable with three.js degrees unless the axis and aspect convention are known. To measure a reference later, record build/platform, viewport, camera mode, FOV setting, avatar pixel bounds, horizon and at least one known world scale; a single screenshot cannot uniquely solve distance, FOV and target height.

## What the named games establish, and what they do not

### Precision platformers and readable characters

**Super Mario Odyssey.** Nintendo's own [options guide](https://www.guiasnintendo.com/2c-switch/super-mario-odyssey/guia-super-mario-odyssey/menu-pausa.html) documents five camera sensitivity choices and independent normal/reversed horizontal and vertical directions **[V]**. [Prima's controls guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/super-mario-odyssey-eguide/a-kingdom-travelers-guide/control-schemes) describes right-stick look and L/R camera reset, with contextual exceptions **[O]**. The transferable contract is free inspection plus an explicit way to recover a useful view. Neither source supplies orbit metres, FOV, jump dead zones or recenter timers. Do not label a Boogle spring “Mario's camera” without a timed comparison. **Boogle inference:** keep landings and the path visible while the body turns freely; an avatar reversing direction need not spin the world 180°.

**Astro Bot.** Team Asobi's [development interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/09/09/astro-bot-how-team-asobi-created-a-unified-vision-for-fun/) explains its gameplay-first prototyping and the development of the back-mounted Handy-D monkey ability **[V]**. It does not publish camera gains or dimensions. The useful comparison is a small expressive character with an attachment whose interaction still has to read immediately. **Boogle inference:** evaluate the final rounded character and maximum bundle together, at gameplay size; a close beauty shot is insufficient evidence. Astro's authored challenges also do not prove that one scripted view can cover an open co-op island.

**A Hat in Time.** [Gears for Breakfast's developer interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/interview-a-hat-in-time-dev-talks-five-year-development-switch-port-and-whats-next/) explicitly names Mario Sunshine, Wind Waker, Banjo-Kazooie and Psychonauts as foundations while describing its own different approaches **[V]**. The [developer-published store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/253230/A_Hat_in_Time/) confirms its exploratory 3D platforming and shared/split-screen co-op. The [official manual](https://hatintime.com/public/AHatinTime_Manual_Official.pdf) exceeded the retrieval tool's size limit **[P]**. I could not verify its camera-assist/distance options or a default FOV from primary material; console-command values circulating online are not a reliable shipped default. **Boogle inference:** use it as a comparison for free exploration and camera customization, not as a source of copied constants. A follow-up play capture should explicitly compare assist on/off and downward inspection near ledges.

**Fall Guys.** Mediatonic's [Summer Breeze update](https://www.fallguys.com/news/the-future-of-fall-guys-and-summer-breeze-update) explicitly added the ability to look up, responding to players and increasingly vertical rounds **[V]**. This is unusually direct evidence that a friendly, crowded platformer still needs vertical camera agency. No exact pitch or distance was published there. **Boogle inference:** retain enough upward view to find friends above, and downward view to judge a deck edge; do not “simplify touch” by locking pitch. A crowd is also a reason to keep the local avatar distinguishable, not to fit every player into the frame.

### Action, exploration and carrying

**Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart.** [Insomniac's accessibility documentation](https://support.insomniac.games/hc/en-us/articles/46716208107795-What-Accessibility-options-does-Ratchet-Clank-Rift-Apart-feature) offers camera sensitivity/inversion, shake control, motion-blur adjustment, disabling visual effects and HUD parallax, and a persistent center dot **[V]**. Its value here is the separation of camera control, combat assistance and visual comfort. That document supplies no orbit metres or follow time constants. **Boogle inference:** borrow the separable options; do not inherit a permanently close aiming composition for ordinary running with logs.

**Fortnite.** Epic's [April 2019 competitive development update](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-competitive-development-update?lang=en-US) specified an **80° default FOV** and discussed visibility, target readability and motion sickness across viewing distances **[V, historical; axis unspecified in that article]**. Current [Epic support](https://www.epicgames.com/help/c-34254770/c-37371353/a17762816) documents minimum/maximum FOV settings **[V]**. Therefore “Fortnite is permanently locked to 80°” is not a sound current premise, and 80 should not be pasted into Boogle's vertical-FOV property. **Boogle inference:** a shoulder view and readable teammates can coexist with manual looking; sustained high-speed travel deserves a bounded, optional FOV response. Exact current sprint curves were not verified.

**The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild.** Nintendo's [controls guide](https://www.guiasnintendo.com/2c-switch/zelda-breath-of-the-wild/guia-zelda-breath-of-the-wild/controles.html) describes freely moving the camera with the right stick to inspect routes/climbs and using ZL to fix the view or focus a nearby enemy **[V]**. Its documented separation of looking and moving matters more than an unverified FOV estimate. **Boogle inference:** when the player stops to inspect a ridge or watches a friend, leave their view alone. Treat an explicit focus/recenter action differently from continuous automatic yaw.

**The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom.** Nintendo's [Beginning Your Journey guide](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/zelda/totk/guide/en/index.html) explicitly asks players to stop and study surroundings, documents ZL focus, and discusses constructing/traversing with objects **[V]**. It publishes no camera fit algorithm for arbitrary vehicles or giant fused objects. **Boogle inference:** a player's current task can change the necessary visible bounds; preserve manual inspection while handling unusual loads and deck geometry. Do not infer that the camera must frame the full extent of every construction. BotW/TotK metres, pitch, FOV and start/stop/jump smoothing remain unverified here; no ROM or extracted game data was used.

**Palworld.** Pocketpair's [store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1623730/Palworld/) establishes the relevant combination of third-person co-op, creatures, traversal and construction **[V]**. Accessibility specialist Arevya's [first-hand settings inspection](https://www.arevya.tv/post/accessibility-settings-in-palworld-1-0) reports **70–90 FOV**, **0.5–2 ride-camera distance**, camera shake/motion blur switches and independent input settings **[O]**. The page was fetched; its linked screenshot could not be retrieved. The FOV axis and ride-distance units are unspecified: **0.5–2 is a settings range, not verified metres**. No damping or automatic recenter behavior was established. **Boogle inference:** separate mounted and walking preferences, especially when the mount or load is much larger than the player.

**Death Stranding.** The official [TGS gameplay breakdown](https://blog.playstation.com/2019/09/12/death-stranding-new-tgs-gameplay-video/) discusses balancing cargo, terrain, speed equipment, carriers and vehicles; Kojima Productions' [Director's Cut beginner guide](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/index.php/en/death-stranding-directors-cut-beginners-guide) covers transport tools and shared assistance **[V]**. These establish a deliberate relationship between what is carried, how one moves and what lies ahead; they do not publish a camera offset formula. **Boogle inference:** test the loaded silhouette against the walking route and landing area, including when the camera retracts. Borrow that visibility problem, not balance inputs, towering cargo or load-induced camera sway: the owner explicitly rejected those for Boogle.

### Social framing and authored cameras

**Sackboy: A Big Adventure.** Sumo design director Jack Houghton's [developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/sackboy-a-big-adventure-remains-cute-as-a-button-in-next-gen?lang=en-US) says the single shared camera viewpoint shaped levels, animation and costumes, and describes one-to-four-player play **[V]**. This is a useful counterexample: shared framing is a level-design commitment. **Boogle inference:** players with their own browser views do not need a shared centroid camera or a leash that makes a departing friend shrink everyone else. Preserve personal framing; make nearby friends and shared actions legible through world composition.

**Journey and John Nesky.** [Nesky's own site](https://johnnesky.com/) credits his work on Journey's avatar control and dynamic camera **[V]**. His GDC 2014 [50 Camera Mistakes](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020460/50-Camera) abstract covers direction, distance, line of sight, simulation sickness and balancing the player's needs against storytelling; the [official GDC recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7307qRmlMI) was uploaded in 2015 **[P: abstract/description, not a watched transcript]**. This is the Journey-related talk used here, not two independent camera specifications. I do not attribute specific gains or every circulating “50 rules” paraphrase to it. A developer's concrete adoption of this approach is Daedalic's article below. **Boogle inference:** a memorable landscape view should remain useful to the person steering; scenic rides should not silently seize their camera.

**Big Walk.** House House's [official FAQ](https://bigwalk.game/faq/) explicitly discusses **first-person-game** motion sickness and lists motion-reduction overlays, PC/Mac FOV adjustment and crosshair choices **[V]**. It is a social/comfort reference, not evidence for a third-person follow rig. PlayStation's [hands-on account](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/03/09/big-walk-hands-on-report-cooperative-chaos-exploring-a-puzzle-filled-open-world/) describes gestures, local communication and tools for finding one another **[O]**. **Boogle inference:** letting players look at each other's actions is more useful than always recentering toward travel. No published follow-camera dimensions were found, and the FAQ's comfort options have no numeric ranges.

Boogle's analogue can remain a few typed, Local modules. Respect that migration; do not introduce another entity framework, frame loop, physics engine or network camera state. The proposed camera data flow is:

```text
interpolated body/deck pose + device look intent
  -> stable follow anchor and player orbit
  -> selected walking/vehicle composition and optional assists
  -> desired position, aim and one FOV value
  -> hard clearance + separate visibility handling
  -> one final camera write before render
```

Any later positional effect must also satisfy clearance. Cave knowledge remains an explicit constraint; it should not accidentally skip comfort-state cleanup. A small mode owner and ordered stages are enough; no generic shot-scoring solver is justified yet.

## Tight and soft should mean different things on different axes

The following is a Boogle policy proposal **[R]**, informed by the documented independent axes and manual-priority mechanisms above. It is not a claim that Mario, Astro or Journey use these exact curves.

| Action           | Follow tightly                                    | Follow softly / leave alone                                                 | Why                                                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start running    | X/Z anchor in the body's interpolated frame       | No automatic yaw; no distance extension                                     | The first frame of movement should read as moving the character, not dragging the camera.                 |
| Sustain a sprint | Same X/Z; chosen distance                         | Optional small FOV transition                                               | Show speed without shifting the control reference. Use speed relative to the supporting deck.             |
| Turn / reverse   | Body translation and manual look                  | Avatar facing may turn independently; default camera yaw stays put          | With camera-relative movement, chasing facing can rotate the movement basis and create unwanted arcs.     |
| Stop             | X/Z stops with body                               | Height settles; optional FOV returns gradually; yaw stays where placed      | Avoid a second camera movement that reads as continued body inertia.                                      |
| Jump / fall      | Horizontal position and manual orbit              | Vertical anchor follows with bounded lag; no automatic yaw or landing shake | Let the character rise in the frame while preserving destination context; long falls still need catch-up. |
| Land             | Support-frame change is continuous                | Blend the vertical policy without resetting its state                       | A grounded/airborne switch should not cause a one-frame horizon jump.                                     |
| Walk on a deck   | Interpolated deck translation plus local movement | No train-speed-dependent walking FOV; no inherited pitch/roll               | A passenger should feel attached to the deck while retaining the ability to look around.                  |

For time constants, use `alpha = 1 - exp(-dt / tau)`; `tau = 0.20 s` means about 63% convergence in 0.20 s, **not** “fully caught up in 0.20 s.” Define this explicitly rather than confusing it with an engine's differently named damping slider. At constant vertical speed, lag is approximately `speed × tau` before the cap **[D]**. On a train at 20 m/s, a 0.20 s world-position filter would leave the camera roughly **4 m behind**. This is why smoothing the entire position is the wrong default.

### Device authority and recentering

**Mouse:** manual yaw/pitch should be immediate; default automatic yaw off. Pointer-lock movement is a displacement, so do not multiply it by frame time. Preserve independent unlocked drag and trackpad paths. A user who turns to look sideways while running is expressing a valid intent.

**Stick:** rotation is a rate and must use frame time. Retain the current squared response/dead zone initially; offer independent axes and sensitivity. Optional recentering can help when the thumb must leave the stick to jump/interact, but should wait after manual look, cancel immediately on new input, and stay off while idle, aiming, airborne or deliberately moving sideways/backward. Store input intent separately from resulting body velocity: feeding camera-relative velocity back into camera yaw can make the controller steer itself.

**Touch:** the right-hand drag should win from contact until release; buttons must not leak drag deltas. Normalize optional sensitivity against CSS viewport size, not device pixels, then validate phone versus iPad. Test a gently assisted preset because the right thumb also presses actions, but keep full manual looking available. A single shared “last look” timestamp must include mouse, wheel, touch and stick. Track the **active input device**, not just whether the machine has a coarse pointer; an iPad can use a trackpad or gamepad.

An explicit recenter should restore **yaw only** by default: keep a deliberate up/down view. Do not steal R3 silently; it already means camera distance. Use the controls registry and a proposed cross-device remapping or visible action, preserving a free pad slot. Recenter settings belong in the pause settings regardless of whether a new action fits. Nintendo's controls and the Daedalic article support explicit recovery/manual priority; all waits and speeds in recommendation 5 are Boogle experiments.

### Look-ahead and screen composition

Put the character below center so the route and friends occupy the space ahead. The present hero calculation already puts the body around the lower-middle and the feet near 76% of height on flat ground. Keep that first, then inspect actual pixels with the final model. Do not continuously reframe left/right whenever the stick changes direction: that introduces another motion and can make steering feel indirect.

Velocity look-ahead is optional on foot. Moving both camera position and aim by a small filtered offset preserves viewing direction; moving aim alone adds camera rotation. These are different experiments. Stop/reversal should remove or reverse the offset without an overshoot. Prefer the zero-look-ahead baseline for the owner's direct feel. Vehicles have a clearer need to see the future path, so they get larger, separately tuned look-ahead. On slopes, preserving lens clearance is mandatory; automatically pitching toward a landing spot is optional and must yield to manual inspection.

## Trees, walls and steep slopes need different obstruction policies

Separate **physical camera clearance** from **whether a useful amount of the player/path is visible**. A trunk may briefly cover the character without requiring a huge zoom, but the lens should not move through the trunk. Leaves can dither without dragging the camera. A cliff or building generally needs retraction. This distinction is supported by Godot's volume sweep and Unity's separate visibility controls, rather than by a claim that one commercial game has a universal algorithm.

For Boogle, sweep from a valid subject-side anchor toward the desired camera position through terrain and solid scenery; exclude the local body, sensors and carried presentation. Use a sphere or near-plane volume through [engine.ts](../../src/physics/engine.ts) and [backend-rapier.ts](../../src/physics/backend-rapier.ts), not raw Rapier calls in a feature. The current facade exposes ray queries, so this is more work than a tune. Handle a probe that starts overlapping, and also sweep/check the previous-to-next lens motion during orbit; a radial arm check alone can cut a corner between frames.

**Proposed start:** radius at least **0.25 m**, expanded for near-plane coverage; about **0.05 m** additional clearance. The near-plane half-diagonal is `r = near × tan(vFov/2) × sqrt(1 + aspect²)` **[D]**. At 62°, 16:9 it is about **0.123 m** with near 0.1, but **0.306 m** with crossing's near 0.25. A sphere centered at the lens must also cover the axial near distance: use `max(0.25, sqrt(near² + r²) + 0.05)` as a conservative starting radius, approximately **0.25 m normally / 0.45 m in crossing**. Revisit at widest allowed FOV/aspect. A near-plane-shaped cast can fit tighter than this enclosing sphere.

Resolve an actual hard obstruction immediately to the safe side; then hold the nearest safe distance for roughly **0.12 s** and recover with **0.25–0.40 s** time constant **[R]**. A smooth proposed position must still be clamped to current safe reach every frame. Do not impose a minimum arm length that overrides a nearer wall. If the camera must become very close, fade the local avatar/bundle and preserve a useful aim. Smoothing outward and ignoring fleeting soft occlusion prevent repeated in/out “pumping”; smoothing inward cannot guarantee safety against a suddenly appearing obstacle.

Terrain clearance must apply along the arm and around the near plane, not just at the final endpoint. Start with fixed player-selected pitch; on a hill, resolve geometry without automatically orbiting sideways. Only if downhill landings remain hidden, compare an optional **up to 5°** downward assist after **0.3 s** of sustained descent, blended over **0.4 s**, canceled by look input **[R]**. Do not add it before the basic collision experiment, or a camera pop will be impossible to attribute.

## Loads, moving platforms and co-op

**A back bundle is part of the silhouette, not the camera's tracking target.** Keep the compact no-sway arrangement from the owner's decision record. Compare empty, full and largest held objects at normal distance, near a wall, while looking up/down, and while a friend crosses the foreground. If the full bundle blocks the route, first try a small fixed shoulder/height adjustment or local dither; do not expand distance with every collected log. Use hysteresis for a “large object” mode so pickup/drop does not produce repeated zooms. For a large object held in front, preserve the object’s contact/destination area and a visible route to it; shoulder offset should be selectable rather than automatically swapping sides around trees.

The existing [props fade](../../src/render/props.ts) offers a TSL-based precedent. A load/self fade must be scoped to the local instance: [haul-stack.ts](../../src/features/haul-stack.ts) contains shared/instanced presentation, so changing a shared material's opacity can hide other people's loads. Avoid blending the whole world, changing the renderer, or adding a post pass to solve one foreground obstruction.

**Train/deck:** compute camera position in the same interpolated reference frame as the rendered rider and platform. Keep the horizon upright. World-stable yaw is useful while sightseeing; deck-heading inheritance is useful when walking along a turning carriage. Compare these explicitly, with manual input priority, rather than parenting the lens to every train rotation. [platforms.ts](../../src/physics/platforms.ts) already has interpolated frame conversion, and [remote-player/index.ts](../../src/entities/remote-player/index.ts) uses deck-relative presentation. Boarding, changing decks and jumping off should preserve world pose at the transition, then change the support frame. The upcoming sitting pose does not authorize reviving the cut side-on camera.

**Soapbox:** it needs visibility into bends and beyond the cart. Keep a separate chase mode with a bounded direction lead, but allow a deliberate manual glance to persist. Smooth vehicle bounce in the anchor; do not inherit wheel/suspension roll. Restore the player's previous distance, pitch policy and FOV when exiting, including a wipeout, respawn and flag teardown. The current three-ray visibility heuristic and positive minimum distance must not become the general hard collision solver.

**Friends:** keep one Local camera per player. Do not rotate or zoom whenever another peer joins, leaves or runs away. Nearby friends are readable through a modestly wide view, clear silhouettes, unobstructed shared interaction space and existing labels/pings; a person across the island is a navigation problem, not a request to pull the camera hundreds of metres back. An optional farther social distance can help with a build site or crowded deck. Sackboy's shared-camera design and Big Walk's communication affordances solve different versions of this problem; neither requires a multi-target follow rig for Boogle.

## Motion sickness and accessibility

[Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) recommends control over camera movement and FOV, including automatic movement and distracting effects **[V]**. Insomniac's published controls show how separate motion blur, shake, parallax and a center dot can be offered without disabling ordinary camera control. These are options, not a guarantee that a particular preset is comfortable for everyone.

Hemmerich, Keshavarz and Hecht's 2020 [Visually Induced Motion Sickness on the Horizon](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2020.582095/full) tested a moving starfield with different reference cues **[V]**. Only the earth-fixed horizon produced a significant, modest reduction; an earth-fixed cross and a head-yoked line did not. This is experimental evidence for a stable orientation cue, **not** a browser-game camera-speed limit or proof that a center dot cures sickness. The paper discusses visual motion without matching physical motion and individual/contextual variation.

**Boogle implications [R]:** prevent involuntary roll, repetitive bob, abrupt collision zoom, forced yaw, repeated FOV pulsing and render/physics jitter. A camera can be mathematically smooth yet unpleasant if it continually moves after the player stops. Low or uneven frame delivery also deserves investigation alongside camera tuning; a stronger spring does not repair frame pacing. Do not prescribe “wider is always better”: field of view, viewing distance, display size and avatar readability interact.

Offer independent X/Y inversion and sensitivity; manual/assisted recenter policy; camera distance; vertical FOV; sprint/vehicle FOV effect strength; shake strength including zero; and an optional fixed center dot. Keep blur/roll/bob off in the proposed comfort baseline. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` as an initial preference and observe changes, while allowing explicit in-game settings. Keep stabilizing vertical follow available even when motion effects are disabled. Do not snap the camera back behind someone who has paused to rest or inspect the world. Settings should describe their visible effect, not expose spring coefficients to players.

## Numbered recommendations for Boogle

All values here are **[R] starting points**, except those explicitly described as existing. Order reflects expected return; the cheapest, most visible experiment is **1**, followed by **2** and the FOV-off comparison in **3**. Collision (**6**) is the most valuable larger task. Each recommendation is Local and names its implementation surface; this report authorizes none of the implementation.

1. **Compare distance while preserving direct control.** Behind the existing hero flag, compare **5.2 / 5.8 / 6.5 m**, holding **62° vertical FOV, −0.22 rad pitch, +1.35 m body-relative anchor and +0.55 m shoulder** constant. Keep X/Z lag **zero**. Start at 5.2, with 6.5 as the old-camera reference; reconcile the R3 cycle so it does not silently lose the selected prototype distance. **Touches:** [hero/camera.ts](../../src/proto/hero/camera.ts), [input.ts](../../src/player/input.ts), [protos.ts](../../protos/protos.ts). **Cost/visible gain:** tiny / high; serves direct control and readable slapstick.

2. **Keep separate, bounded jump-height smoothing.** Retain existing **0.10 s grounded / 0.20 s airborne / ±1.4 m** as the baseline; compare **0.08 / 0.16 s** only if repeated jumps feel detached. Preserve filter state on landing, reset on explicit teleport/respawn and mode entry, and keep this stabilizer available under reduced motion. Test long falls and moving-deck takeoff separately. **Touches:** [hero/camera.ts](../../src/proto/hero/camera.ts), stable body/deck presentation in [player.ts](../../src/player/player.ts) and [platforms.ts](../../src/physics/platforms.ts). **Cost/gain:** small / high.

3. **Give FOV one owner and make speed effects optional.** Compare **+0 / +3 / +5°** on top of 62°; use actual sprint intent plus horizontal speed relative to support, starting over **6.5 m/s** and reaching full strength at **9.5 m/s**. Try **0.25 s widening / 0.40 s return**. A stationary train passenger gets zero walking boost. Restore the chosen base lens on exit; modes request an offset rather than each writing `camera.fov`. **Touches:** [hero camera](../../src/proto/hero/camera.ts), [soapbox](../../src/proto/soapbox/index.ts), [crossing](../../src/proto/crossing/index.ts), camera owner in [entity plan step 12](../plans/entity-architecture.md). **Cost/gain:** tiny for A/B, medium for ownership / high clarity.

4. **Keep the avatar low enough to see ahead; defer walking look-ahead.** Start with the current anchor and shoulder: target roughly **20–25%** avatar screen height and **75–80%** feet Y in flat-ground neutral views. Compare shoulder **0.30 / 0.55 m** with the actual final model and bundle. Default foot look-ahead **0**; only then try **0.10 s of relative velocity, capped at 0.6 m**, filtered over **0.15 s**, moving position and aim together. **Touches:** `updateCamera` in [player.ts](../../src/player/player.ts), [hero camera](../../src/proto/hero/camera.ts), [avatar loadFit](../../src/player/avatars.ts). **Cost/gain:** small / visible; omit look-ahead if it recreates perceived inertia.

5. **Make manual look authoritative on every device.** Default mouse recenter **off**; offer stick assistance after **1.5 s**, touch assistance after **1.2 s**, starting at **45°/s maximum** with a **0.35 s** response. These assisted presets are experiments, not mandatory defaults. Require sustained forward travel, suppress at idle/in air/sideways/backward, and cancel on manual input immediately. An explicit recenter may blend yaw over **0.25 s** while preserving pitch. **Touches:** [input.ts](../../src/player/input.ts), [gamepad.ts](../../src/features/gamepad.ts), [controls.ts](../../src/player/controls.ts), shared Local camera intent. **Cost/gain:** medium / high on touch and pad; solve the occupied R3 mapping before adding an action.

6. **Add volume-based camera clearance with gentle recovery.** Start with sphere radius **max(0.25 m, lens-to-near-plane-corner distance + 0.05 m)**, about **0.25 m normally / 0.45 m in crossing**; clamp inward to safe reach immediately, hold **0.12 s**, recover with **0.30 s** time constant. Validate radial and intervening lens motion, initial overlaps, terrain and cave mouths. Separate fleeting foliage visibility from solid collision; no minimum-distance override through a wall. **Touches:** [engine facade](../../src/physics/engine.ts), [Rapier backend](../../src/physics/backend-rapier.ts), [camera constraints](../../src/player/player.ts), [caves](../../src/pois/caves/index.ts), [soapbox constraint](../../src/proto/soapbox/index.ts). **Cost/gain:** medium–large / highest reliability gain; keep standard engine separation of desired pose and collision solution.

7. **Solve load obstruction without making hauling sway the view.** Preserve the compact bundle; try the existing **0.55 m** shoulder before **0.70 m** or an added **0.10–0.20 m** anchor height. If a retracted camera is obscured, compare local avatar/bundle dithering starting within **1.0 m**, strongest by **0.5 m**, transitioning over **0.12 s**; tune against actual bounds. For large held objects, use a stable object-class preset, not per-item-count zoom. **Touches:** [haul-stack.ts](../../src/features/haul-stack.ts), [avatars.ts](../../src/player/avatars.ts), [props.ts](../../src/render/props.ts), camera composition. **Cost/gain:** small for offsets, medium for instance-safe fade / high near walls; keep remote loads visible.

8. **Anchor the train camera to the rendered support frame.** Use interpolated deck transforms, **zero horizontal lag** and **zero inherited roll/pitch**. Compare world-stable yaw against current deck-turn inheritance; manual looking wins in both. Preserve world pose when boarding/jumping off, and blend any optional framing change over **0.25 s**. Keep normal **5.2 m** and optional **6.5 m** views rather than introducing a side-on seat camera. **Touches:** [platforms.ts](../../src/physics/platforms.ts), [player.ts](../../src/player/player.ts), [crossing](../../src/proto/crossing/index.ts); coordinate with the real train work in [boogle-2.md](../proposals/boogle-2.md). **Cost/gain:** medium / high ride stability.

9. **Give the soapbox a comfortable chase mode and a real manual override.** Retain the existing **8.5 m** distance as control; compare **0.20 s / 2 m maximum** look-ahead against **0.30 s / 4 m**. Try steering yaw lead capped at **8°** versus today's roughly **14°**, with **0.30 s** blending; optional FOV cap **+5°** versus +12°, roll **0**. Hold the player's manual view for **2 s** after input before resuming chase assistance. **Touches:** [soapbox/index.ts](../../src/proto/soapbox/index.ts), shared look/FOV policy, [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md). **Cost/gain:** medium / high at speed; apply recommendation 6's hard clearance first.

10. **Keep co-op framing personal, with an optional farther view.** Offer **6.5 m**, then test **7.5 m** only if a full deck/build site remains cramped; do not zoom based on room population. Preserve existing friend labels/pings and manual observation of gestures; no all-player centroid or camera networking. **Touches:** [camera distance input](../../src/player/input.ts), [remote-player/index.ts](../../src/entities/remote-player/index.ts) for visibility review, [ping.ts](../../src/features/ping.ts), [co-op platform research](coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md). **Cost/gain:** small / useful in crowded moments; judge at phone scale as well as desktop.

11. **Expose comfort controls without removing stabilizers.** Start with vertical FOV **50–80°** around **62°**, walking distance **4–8 m**, per-device sensitivity **0.25–2×**, X/Y inversion, recenter off/assisted, FOV effects **0–100%**, shake **0–100%**, and a center-dot toggle. Preserve mode-specific vehicle distance separately. Reduced motion initially chooses zero cosmetic shake/roll/FOV effects, not zero vertical filtering. **Touches:** [pause feature](../../src/features/pause.ts), [input](../../src/player/input.ts), [gamepad](../../src/features/gamepad.ts), [constants](../../src/constants.ts), camera settings owner. **Cost/gain:** medium / broad access; validate extreme FOV against collision radius and narrow screens.

12. **Use one staged Local camera owner and validate one variable at a time.** Keep update order **−200** after interpolated presentation, with explicit lifecycle/reset and ordered mode, comfort and clearance work; follow [entity-architecture step 12](../plans/entity-architecture.md). First compare recommendations **1–3**, then **6–7**, then **5/8/9**. Keep experiments behind a named entry in [protos.ts](../../protos/protos.ts), with one gut question per comparison. **Touches:** [player.ts](../../src/player/player.ts), [loop.ts](../../src/loop.ts), relevant prototype adapters and, when implemented, the camera's [game-design documentation](../game-design.md). **Cost/gain:** medium / prevents contradictory behavior; no new sync system, dependency or general camera framework.

## Smallest useful validation pass

For a future implementation, use the existing `npm run look` harness and open every still/motion sheet; do not add UI unit tests or a scenario for tuning alone. A human comfort trial is valuable after the agent has checked the actual output; it is not a substitute for that check. This research-only change requires no browser or runtime behavior test.

| Pass            | Setup                                                                                                    | What would reject the experiment                                                                                             |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Directness      | Flat run, sprint, immediate stop and 180° reversal; same route at 5.2/5.8/6.5                            | Perceived continued movement after stopping, or automatic yaw changing the intended path.                                    |
| Height          | Repeated jumps, stairs, ridge crest, steep descent, long fall, respawn                                   | Horizon snap, feet/destination disappearing, or a long camera swoop after teleport.                                          |
| Clearance       | Circle a trunk, pass under leaves, back into a wall, orbit a corner, enter/exit a cave                   | Near-plane clipping, unsafe smoothing through solids, repeated zoom pumping or unexpected yaw.                               |
| Cargo/co-op     | Empty/full bundle, large held object, friend crossing, several players building                          | Own cargo hides the destination; fading hides everyone else's load; camera reacts to peer arrivals.                          |
| Vehicles        | Board/ride/turn/jump off a train; soapbox drift, manual glance, collision and exit                       | Deck jitter, train speed triggering sprint FOV, forced roll, manual view stolen or settings not restored.                    |
| Devices/comfort | Mouse, stick, touch phone and iPad; 16:9, 4:3 and portrait; reduced motion; 30/60/120 Hz where available | Frame-rate-dependent input, trapped touch gestures, insufficient side context or comfort mode removing useful stabilization. |

Record distance, pitch, anchor height, vertical FOV, aspect and active mode with captures. Keep a short unchanged-baseline clip beside each variant. Ask “Does starting and stopping still feel entirely under your control?” before asking which view looks more cinematic. A useful second question is “Could you see where you were going while watching your friend?”

## Evidence limits and follow-up sources

- **Exact commercial-game camera constants are the main gap.** Apart from the historically published Fortnite FOV and Palworld's first-hand menu ranges, this research did not verify the requested games' distance, height, pitch or FOV numbers. Engine examples and Boogle-derived geometry are explicitly separated from them. No precise commercial-game acceleration, recenter delay or jump filter is claimed.
- **Nesky's talk merits a full viewing during implementation.** The official abstract and upload metadata were accessible; the recording/transcript was not inspected. The report uses primary engine documentation and Daedalic's implementation account for technical recommendations instead of presenting third-party notes as a transcript. [The Cameras of Uncharted 3](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015514/The-Cameras-of-Uncharted) is another primary GDC lead for camera layering **[P: abstract]**, not proof of a particular moving-platform solver.
- **A Hat in Time is the least verified camera comparison.** Its official manual could not be fetched in full. It remains a named play-capture target, not a source of invented FOV/distance values. Palworld's first-hand settings report is useful but should be checked against the exact platform/build before copying UI ranges.
- **No runtime defects were demonstrated here.** Competing lens writers, short-circuit constraints and ray/minimum-distance limitations were found in source. Reproduce the relevant flag/mode combination before calling any one of them a shipped bug. Existing research reports may describe older controller/physics behavior; this report's Boogle findings come from the checked-out Rapier code.

All external sources are linked beside the claims they support. The proposed camera policy is a synthesis for this particular island, character, control preference and device mix; it is not a claim that the reference games share one hidden formula.
