# Carrying and logistics as play

*Research report, 1 November 2026. How games make carrying, hauling and moving goods fun, and what they do when it turns into a chore: Death Stranding 1 and 2, Pikmin, Moving Out, Overcooked 2, Human Fall Flat, Totally Reliable Delivery Service, Peak, Untitled Goose Game, Lethal Company, Valheim, Factorio, Satisfactory, Palworld, Deep Rock Galactic, Sons of the Forest, Animal Crossing, Minecraft and Spiritfarer. Builds on [0.1/7-resource-loop.md](0.1/7-resource-loop.md) (why a body stack, Pikmin carry, Katamari scale, the pacing table) and does not repeat it. Context: the first solo haul playtest replaced the swaying log tower with a plain back bundle ([boogle-2.md](../proposals/boogle-2.md), Decided); wheelbarrow and toss variants are being built. Recommendations only, no decisions.*

*Gap-fill pass, 1 November 2026 (later the same day).* This pass filled four gaps: R.E.P.O.'s cart, Tears of the Kingdom's Ultrahand, Lethal Company's two-handed scrap, and Death Stranding's balance. Nintendo's Ask the Developer and its GDC 2024 talk gave first-party material on Ultrahand. R.E.P.O.'s cart and Lethal Company's two-handed scrap are covered only by wikis and guides. Death Stranding's balance design still has no primary source. Recommendations 2, 3, 6 and 9 gained evidence; none changed direction. New sections are marked *(gap-fill)*.

**Markers.** **[V]** fetched this round and the wording matched. **[S]** from a search summary, wiki or guide only. **[K]** well-known mechanic, not re-checked this round. The web search budget ran out partway through; what wasn't found is listed at the end.

## Summary

1. **Keep the bundle boring and reliable; put the slapstick in optional tools.** Human Fall Flat's charm was clumsiness, but the clumsy thing was always optional to grab; Totally Reliable Delivery Service made the *carry itself* chaotic and split its reviews.
2. **The beloved part of Death Stranding's hauling was building what ends it.** Players loved building highways, and Kojima says that is where DS2's monorail came from. For Boogle: the rail is the reward for hauling, so it should visibly take hauling over early.
3. **Today's chore is probably the gathering, not the carrying.** A wood section is 60 logs at 4 per E-press, so 15 presses at about 8 palms per section, then one walk. Count actions per section before tuning carry feel.
4. **Throwing exists to kill dead walking.** Overcooked 2 added throwing to fix idle players; Sons of the Forest's log sled auto-stores logs thrown into it. A toss should shorten the last few metres, never add aim pressure.
5. **A wheelbarrow pays only if it holds clearly more and is itself a toy.** Sledges and carts hold roughly 4–6× what you can carry by hand (Sons of the Forest: 8–12 logs against a handful), and Valheim's are "delicate and awkward" on purpose. At Boogle's 60-log back cap, a barrow must hold far more or be fun to push, or nobody fetches it.
6. **Read the load at a glance and make every pickup audible.** Pikmin's carry sound scales with carrier count; Untitled Goose Game gives every prop a grab, release, impact and drag set, pitched by impact speed.
7. **Weight as slowness, stamina, fragility or balance is pressure** (Death Stranding, Lethal Company, Peak, TRDS). It reads as challenge in games built around it and as a chore anywhere else. The owner has already cut sway; keep speed penalties tiny or zero.
8. **Delegation is the industry's chore remover:** Palworld's carrier pals, Deep Rock's Molly that follows you, Stardew's Junimos, Factorio's belts. Boogle's helper hut is already this; make it visible and early.
9. **Group carrying works when it's legible and solo-safe:** Pikmin's "needed / carrying" fraction and smooth speed-up as hands join. For Boogle, keep one player able to move anything, just slower.
10. **Opt-outs are normal even for believers.** Valheim's hauling rule is "controversial" yet later gained a world setting that turns it off; Moving Out's Assist Mode lightens objects and makes them vanish on delivery. Plan the off-switch early.

## Findings by source

### Death Stranding (2019) and Death Stranding 2 (2025)

- **The system.** Cargo packing and total weight "affect Sam's ability to navigate" [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Stranding)]. Guides describe a full skill layer: stack height and weight raise the chance of losing balance, both triggers grip the straps, and you counter a lean with the opposite trigger [S, [GameSpot guide](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/death-stranding-guide-tips-on-how-to-walk-in-the-w/1100-6471246/), [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/death-stranding-walking-gameplay-immersion-experience/)]. Critics who liked it argue the game adds deliberate effort only to the parts of walking that wouldn't be automatic in real life [S, [Gary Kings, Medium](https://garyjkings.medium.com/tread-carefully-death-stranding-and-walking-in-games-7fb361edcea4)]. Edge's 2019 preview called cargo a pain [S, [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20191107/281560882591986)].
- **The sequel softened the start.** Kojima: "it was a delivery game that no one had seen before, so people may have felt frustrated with that", and "You can use vehicles and bikes – it's more accessible now" [V, [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/hideo-kojima-thinks-on-the-beach-could-be-death-stranding-2s-mgs2-moment-and-i-agree/)]. Delivery stays the core, but the sequel adds freedom on top of it.
- **What players loved was the infrastructure.** Per VGC, the monorail idea came from players who enjoyed building highways in the first game [V, same]. Highways were shared projects: anyone could add materials, and the top contributor's name was shown [S, [MP1st on the TGS 2019 reveal](https://mp1st.com/news/death-stranding-multiplayer-details-revealed-at-tgs-2019)]. A road turns an hour of careful walking into a minute of driving, and you built it.
- **Cargo as a frame for everything.** DS2's designer Shinkawa: "we needed to treat weapons as cargo that players carried around" [V, [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/06/25/death-stranding-2-on-the-beach-launch-interview/)]. One idea, carried things, holds the whole game together.
- **The balance layer's intent, as far as it's documented** *(gap-fill)*. Kojima wanted the opening to feel like "a baby learning to walk" [S, Vulture interview as quoted by [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/death-stranding-kojima-players-babies-learning-to-walk/); Vulture refused the fetch]. Contemporary coverage of the TGS 2019 demo and Kojima's Game Informer interview describes the balancing as realism Kojima was after, but in the writers' words, not his [S, [Kakuchopurei](https://www.kakuchopurei.com/2019/09/kojima-responds-to-death-strandings-walking-simulator-criticism/)]. Still no Kojima Productions source on the trigger controls or the tipping numbers. As documented, the clumsiness was meant as a beginner's stage you grow out of (exoskeletons, vehicles, roads), not a permanent tax.
- **For Boogle:** the owner has already said not to over-index on Death Stranding's carrying. The transferable lesson is the arc, not the balance layer: hauling by hand is the cost, and the road (Boogle's rail) is the payoff that removes it.

### Pikmin

- **Carrying is readable.** Each object has a weight and a maximum number of carriers. Too few carriers stand and try to lift it until enough join. Two numbers float above it, read as a fraction in Pikmin 3 and later. In Pikmin 3 and 4 the speed changes smoothly as carriers join or leave; in the first two games joining briefly halted the carry [V, [Pikmin wiki, Carry](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Carry)].
- **The sound counts the carriers.** The Pikmin 4 sound team "adjusted the sound of Pikmin carrying objects to reflect the number of Pikmin", their type and whether they're on land or in water [V, [Nintendo, Ask the Developer, Pikmin 4 part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-10-pikmin-4-part-3/)].
- **For Boogle:** group carrying is legible because of the fraction and the sound, not because of physics. The stall (too few carriers stand and strain) is a soft wait, never a failure.

### Moving Out

- **The fun is awkward shapes, not distance.** The idea came from a real move: sizing a couch to get it round a hallway choke point [S, [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/ph-en/smg-studios-moving-out-interview)]. Ringrose: "even just getting a bed through a door in the game, it was like, 'This happened to us!'" [V, [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/114440/moving-out-interview-blending-real-moving-experiences-with-games)]. He calls it "much more physics and chaos based than rules and recipes" [S, Red Bull].
- **Mechanics.** Some heavy objects need two people; anything can be thrown; some items break [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_Out_(video_game))].
- **Assist Mode** can add time, lighten objects, remove obstacles, make items vanish on delivery, or skip levels [V, same].
- **For Boogle:** "vanish on delivery" is Boogle's pour ring already. The other assist knobs (lighter objects, more time) are the shape of an opt-out that doesn't remove the activity.

### Overcooked 2

- "To address issues where players were left idle during map transitions, they introduced a throwing mechanic" [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcooked_2)]. A preview noted throwing also feeds the chaos: people throw blindly at each other when time runs short [S, [Digitally Downloaded](http://www.digitallydownloaded.net/2018/06/our-first-look-at-overcooked-2.html)].
- **For Boogle:** throwing was a fix for dead time, not a skill test. The toss variant should do the same thing: cut the walk, not add aiming.

### Human Fall Flat

- **Clumsy on purpose.** It began as a prototype with "independent control of each arm" [V, [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/)]. Sakalauskas's son "did everything possible not to solve puzzles, but to parkour or walk around", so the puzzles became "not really watertight" [V, same].
- **The sequel lost it.** The first Human Fall Flat 2 build was scrapped: "It was too stiff. Too polished" [V, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/-human-fall-flat-2-is-cancelled-we-are-making-human-fall-flat-3-no-brakes-games-founder-looks-back-on-a-defining-decade)].
- Its levels are built around tools you choose to grab: carts, catapults, wrecking balls, wheelie bins [S, [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/) summary].
- **For Boogle:** clumsiness is the charm, but the owner found it irritating on the thing you always carry. The two fit together if the clumsiness moves into things you choose to use (a barrow, a toss) and the bundle stays steady.

### Totally Reliable Delivery Service

- Ragdoll couriers, one object per hand, packages that explode on hard impacts, plus vehicles, trampolines and rockets [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_Reliable_Delivery_Service)]. Producer Keith Muelas: "wacky physics that are either helping you or causing trouble" [V, [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/totally-reliable-delivery-service-is-a-wacky-physics-title/)].
- Reviews split: IGN called it "shambolic by design"; Nintendo Life couldn't "imagine anyone genuinely enjoying it" [V, Wikipedia].
- **For Boogle:** chaos in the *control* of carrying divides players. It also makes solo play lonely: the comedy needs someone watching.

### Peak

- Aggro Crab's Nick Kaman: "you can't get items out of your backpack on your own. You need a friend to do it", "one more layer of friction that means you have to interact socially" [V, [Game Developer, GDC talk](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/peak-co-developer-aggro-crab-shares-lessons-in-friendslop)]. Carried weight eats the stamina bar, and the useful items are the heavy ones [S, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game)), guides].
- **For Boogle:** friction as a social prompt only works when friends are present. Boogle is solo-first, so it doesn't transfer; weight-as-stamina is pressure.

### Untitled Goose Game

- Every prop shares "a grab, release, and impact set, along with a drag for some of them"; impact sounds change pitch and volume with the velocity of each hit, tuned per prop, with per-prop cooldowns. Sound designer Em Halberstadt wanted prop sounds "easy to listen to and understated", so the honk stands out against them [V, [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/untitled-goose-game-sound/)].
- **For Boogle:** a small, fixed sound set per material (grab, land in bundle, drop, pour thunk), scaled by speed or count, is cheap and does most of the "feels good to pick up" work.

### Lethal Company

- Scrap has weight in pounds (a cash register 84 lb, an Easter egg about 3) that "affects the player's movement speed and stamina consumption" [V, [Lethal Company wiki](https://lethal.miraheze.org/wiki/Scrap)]. Two-handed scrap gives "Hands Full", blocking other items and ladders [S, same wiki and guides].
- *(gap-fill)* The wikis flag only a few large items as two-handed (the Apparatus and the Painting, both weight 1.3 on Fextralife's scale) [S, [Fextralife](https://lethalcompany.wiki.fextralife.com/Lethal_Company_Items)]. Players note that the company vehicle lets you haul two-handed items, which changes how worth taking they are [S, [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/598512477964147999/)]. Still no statement from Zeekerss on the design.
- **For Boogle:** weight here is the horror: it slows you while something chases you. Without the threat it would just be slow. Boogle has no threat, so a speed penalty would be a pure tax. "Hands full" is the gentler lever: a big piece occupies you, rather than slowing you, and a vehicle frees your hands again.

### Valheim

- Ore and metal bars can't go through portals, so they are carried by cart or ship. Iron Gate's CEO said the rule "has changed many times during development" [S, [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/ore-portal)]. A design essay defends it: "accomplishments can't be free", and it makes you "think about travel and trade routes"; it also calls the hauling vehicles "delicate and awkward" and the rule "controversial" [V, [Bottom Feeder, Design Deep Dive: Valheim](https://bottomfeeder.substack.com/p/design-deep-dive-valheim)].
- A world-generation Portals setting now exists, and Casual lets every item through [S, [Valheim wiki guide](https://thevalheim.wiki/guides/portals-and-teleportable-items)].
- **For Boogle:** even a team that believed in hauling as a cost ended up shipping an off-switch. Hauling as a cost belongs in a survival game; Boogle wants hauling as a toy.

### Factorio and Satisfactory

- Factorio calls belts the core of every factory from the early hours to the very late game [S, [FFF #447](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-447)]. On belt stacking, the team worried that "you might never need to use more than 1 or 2 belts worth of throughput", which would "reduce the logistics challenge drastically", so they gated it late; stacked items on belts "look absolutely psycho in a very good way" [V, [FFF #393](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-393)].
- Coffee Stain's Snutt Treptow calls Satisfactory's early stretch, before faster belts let you lay out a real factory, an annoying grind [S, [GodisaGeek interview](https://godisageek.com/2024/02/get-rid-of-the-detail-on-the-rocks-satisfactory-interview-with-snutt-treptow-part-1/)].
- **For Boogle:** in factory games logistics is the puzzle, and hand-carrying is the phase players want to leave. Showing quantity physically on the conveyor (stacks you can see) is part of the satisfaction.

### Palworld, Deep Rock Galactic, Stardew Valley: delegation

- **Palworld.** Pals with the Transporting skill carry items from production sites and ground drops to storage, from 2 items a trip at level 1 to 500 at level 10. Natural nodes drop single items, so those go one at a time at any level [V, [Palworld wiki](https://palworld.wiki.gg/wiki/Transporting)].
- **Deep Rock Galactic.** Molly, the M.U.L.E., follows the team, holds the team's minerals, walks to a called marker across walls and ceilings, and can't be attacked [V, [DRG wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/M.U.L.E.)]. You mine, deposit nearby and keep going; you never walk back to base.
- **Stardew Valley.** A Junimo Hut sends Junimos to harvest crops around it into the hut [K].
- **For Boogle:** the genre's standard fix for a carrying chore is something else does the walking. Boogle's helper hut already sends freight; the cheapest win is making that delegation visible and early.

### Sons of the Forest

- Its log sled (added in a May 2023 patch) holds 8 logs (12 for the advanced version, or 40 rocks). You can load it by hand or throw logs into it and they store themselves; the companion Kelvin can fill it; you push it by the rear handlebar [S, [Sons of the Forest wiki](https://sonsoftheforest.wiki.gg/wiki/Log_Sled), [EIP patch notes](https://eip.gg/sotf/news/patch-05/)]. On foot you carry only a couple of logs on your shoulder [K].
- **For Boogle:** this is the nearest shipped precedent for "wheelbarrow + toss". The vehicle is worth fetching because it holds several times what you carry, and throwing into it is generous.

### R.E.P.O.: the C.A.R.T. *(gap-fill)*

- **The cart.** Every level starts with a cart outside the truck, which you load with loot and push to extraction [S, [beginner tutorial](https://www.repo-game.org/en/repogame-how-to-play), [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3561257961)]. A built-in display shows the running value of what's inside [S]. Loot breaks and loses value when handled roughly, but guides say items in the cart are protected from impacts [S]. A smaller "Pocket C.A.R.T." takes small valuables, leaving the big cart for big ones [S].
- **Handling.** You grab with the mouse, scroll to push the held object away or pull it closer, and rotate it to fit through doors or stack it on the cart [S, [controls guide](https://www.repo-game.org/en/repogame-controls)]. One player can push the cart, and a second pulling from the front steers it faster through corridors [S]. Riding the cart at about sprint speed without spending stamina is a known trick [S, [a player's blog](https://substack.com/home/post/p-161818009)].
- **Online.** The host simulates all physics objects, so a client feels a round trip on every grab, and carts are named among the laggy objects ([coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md](coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md), R.E.P.O.).
- **For Boogle:** the cart works as a toy and as a safe: inside it, loot stops being fragile, and its own display counts the haul. It suits recommendation 6's barrow: what's in the barrow is safe and visibly counted, two people make it go better, and riding it is an allowed joke. R.E.P.O.'s fragility belongs to a game with a threat. Boogle's loads never break.

### Tears of the Kingdom: Ultrahand *(gap-fill)*

- **Origin.** Fujibayashi built vehicles from the previous game's parts and presented them; prototypes included a cog-wheel car, a paddle steamer and a tank [V, [Nintendo, Ask the Developer Vol. 9, ch. 4](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-chapter-4/)].
- **Predictable by hand-tuning.** With so many combinations, staff made "special adjustments to each item" so results never feel unexpected [V, same]. The freedom reads as physics, but behind it each object is tuned.
- **The glue is the feedback.** The bulging glue looked odd to Aonuma at first. It was kept as the clearest way to show that objects are joined, and it stands for "the fun of sticking things together" [V, same]. Sound director Wakai used one "attaching sound" for every combination, because players hear it constantly, and he preferred unrefined, tactile sounds to abstract ones [V, same].
- **Two kinds of player.** The team aimed to serve people who build elaborate things and people who build only what they need, and Fujibayashi says they were "very conscious of finding the right balance" [V, same].
- **Physics underneath.** At GDC 2024 the physics lead said scripted, non-physics objects caused "daily problems" under Ultrahand (a stone thrown onto a cart launched cart and horse), so they made everything physics-driven [V, [Shacknews recap](https://www.shacknews.com/article/139210/zelda-totk-gdc-2024-panel-recap)]. The sound team's system made sounds from wheels and materials rather than from a wagon or boat sound, which the director called "basically a physics engine for sound" [V, [Zelda Dungeon recap](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/tears-of-the-kingdom-developers-discuss-the-challenges-of-programming-a-physics-based-world/)]. I found no developer description of how a held object follows the hand.
- **For Boogle:** three things transfer. One distinct, satisfying sound for the most repeated carrying action. Visible proof that something is attached (the bundle on your back, logs inside the barrow). And per-object tuning, so a system that looks physical never surprises the player.

### Animal Crossing, Minecraft, Spiritfarer

- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons.** Pockets start at 20 slots and upgrade to 30 and 40 for Nook Miles. A full pocket offers drop, bury or swap rather than blocking, and gifts you can't hold are mailed [V, [Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Pockets)].
- **Minecraft's bundle** took about four years from its announcement to release, mostly over UI that worked badly on touch screens [S, [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/how-minecraft-bundles-changed-since-first-announcement-four-years-ago)]. Inventory UI is expensive on touch; Boogle's "no inventory, you wear it" sidesteps that.
- **Spiritfarer.** Farming was planned much larger, Stardew-like, and Thunder Lotus scaled it back "so resource collecting would take less time"; materials come from character-linked mini-activities instead [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritfarer), paraphrasing the creative director].

## What this means for Boogle

Current numbers (from [docs/game-design/haul.md](../game-design/haul.md)): 60-log back cap, 4 logs per E on a bundle, 2 charges per bundle, 36 palms, 60 logs per wood section, 36 per helper hut, pour 2 logs every 0.3 s (a full stack in 9 s), and a 45 s machine run.

1. **Count the chore before tuning the carry.** A section costs 15 Take-logs presses across about 8 palms, then one walk and a 9 s pour. That is a gathering loop, not a carrying one. Log per section, solo: presses, palms visited, metres walked, seconds from first take to "Start the machine". **Start from:** at most 6–8 deliberate actions and one trip per section, and under about 2 minutes of hauling before a 45 s run, for the first section. If it is above that, cut presses (8 logs per take, or a bundle that empties in one press) before adding a vehicle.
   *Smallest test:* add the counters to the haul kit panel (Local only) and play one section in each of two settings: 4 logs per take and 12 logs per take.

2. **Keep the back bundle as the steady default; put any clumsiness in things you choose to use.** Human Fall Flat's clumsiness was charming and optional; TRDS's chaotic carry split players; the owner cut sway for the same reason. Wobble, tipping and spills, if they come back at all, belong to the barrow and the toss. *Gap-fill:* Tears of the Kingdom points the same way. Nintendo hand-tuned each item so results never feel unexpected, and Death Stranding's clumsiness, as far as it's documented, was a beginner's stage you outgrow.

3. **Make every pickup and pour feel like something, with sound first.** Following Untitled Goose Game and Pikmin, each material gets four sounds: grab, land on the bundle, drop, and pour thunk. Pitch rises slightly with the count, and the thunk is varied (about ±8% pitch, 3–4 samples). Add one character squash per pickup (about 80–120 ms, 5–8% vertical) and a short settle when the bundle reaches a new visual step.
   *Smallest test:* sounds and squash only, `?proto=haul`, then ask whether picking up feels better with them than without.
   *Gap-fill:* Tears of the Kingdom used one "attaching sound" for every Ultrahand join because it is heard so often, and preferred tactile sounds to abstract ones. So the "land on the bundle" sound, the most repeated, is the one to get right first. Keep it the same across materials, with only pitch and count variation.

4. **Make the load readable without the chip.** The bundle already fills out to 22 drawn logs. Add three or four clear silhouette steps (for example a quarter, half, three-quarters and full) so another player, or you in third person, can read "nearly full" at a glance. A full bundle could also tilt the character slightly forward, as a pose, not a speed change. **Start from:** no walk-speed penalty; at most −5% at a full 60 if a sense of weight is wanted (Lethal Company and Peak show bigger penalties read as pressure).

5. **Toss: shorten the last metres, never test aim.** Following Overcooked 2's reason for throwing and Sons of the Forest's auto-storing sled: a toss throws one handful (start at 4 logs) along the existing scripted flight, 6–10 m. Anything landing within the ring radius plus 1 m counts as poured, and a miss lands as an ordinary pile, never lost. Tossing into a parked barrow counts the same way. Hold-to-repeat should empty a full bundle in about the 9 s a walk-in pour takes, so tossing saves walking, not time at the ring.
   *Smallest test:* the toss variant with a measured split: in one solo section, how many logs went in by toss and how many by walking into the ring? If walking wins anyway, the toss isn't solving anything.

6. **Wheelbarrow: worth fetching, and a toy in itself.** Sledges and carts in shipped games hold several times what you carry. At a 60-log back cap, a barrow worth fetching needs one of two things:
   - **(a) It holds a lot:** 2–3 sections (120–180 logs), parked near the palms and pushed to the site once.
   - **(b) Pushing it is fun:** a light tilt and roll on slopes, a tip-over only on steep ground (spilling as a pile), and a ride-in comedy beat.

   **Start from:** (a), at about 85% of walking speed on flat ground, loaded by walking your bundle into it or tossing. It stays where you leave it, so it is a place on the island. Brief 7 (Loop D) already lists the cost: a second Owner physics object.
   *Gap-fill:* R.E.P.O.'s cart adds three things worth copying. Its own running total (a count on the barrow, not only in the HUD). Anything in it is safe. And a second player at the front helps it go faster. Riding it is a tolerated joke there and could be one here. For sync, draw the load from the barrow's pose, never as separate objects ([coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md](coop-physics-vehicles-netcode.md), recommendation 14).
   *Smallest test:* a Local-only barrow (no sync) in a `proto-` room, solo. Does the tester go back for it in the second section without being told?

7. **Let the rail take hauling over, visibly and early.** This is Death Stranding's highways and Kojima's monorail, Palworld's carriers, Factorio's belts and Deep Rock's Molly. The helper hut's freight should be something you see arrive (the wagon carrying a stack of logs and tipping it into the ring), not a counter. **Start from:** the first helper hut is affordable within the first section (36 logs is already 0.6 of one), and its first delivery lands within about 30 s of being built, so the payoff comes while the player is still there.
   *Smallest test:* in `?proto=wagon`, have the existing wagon carry visible logs from a pile at the first stop to the open site's Track ring every run. Does the tester stop gathering to watch it, and do they notice when it shortens a section?

8. **A follower instead of a vehicle, if pushing is the chore.** Molly shows that a carrier that walks behind you removes trips without taking you out of the world. A Boogle version would be a small barrow or critter that trails you, takes your bundle when full, and walks it to the open site along the path. This is a big step (Host or Owner state, pathing), so only pursue it if both the barrow and the toss fail.
   *Smallest test:* a Local-only follower that teleports logs into the ring when it arrives, solo.

9. **Group carrying later, legible and solo-safe.** For heavier later-era pieces (girders, sacks), use Pikmin's model: a fraction over the object ("1/2"), speed rising smoothly as people join, and a sound that thickens with each carrier. To stay solo-first, one player can always move it. **Start from:** one carrier at 40% of walking speed, two at 100%, three at 110%, capped there.
   *Smallest test:* one girder prop in a `proto-` room, pushed by one player, then two.
   *Gap-fill:* R.E.P.O.'s cart (one pushes, one steers from the front) and Lethal Company's two-handed items both make a big piece occupy you, not slow you. Carrying a girder could fill your hands (no taking logs while carrying) rather than cutting speed.

10. **Leave out balance, stamina, fragility and timers.** Death Stranding, Lethal Company, Peak and TRDS each build these in deliberately, and each reads as pressure outside its genre. The current design already has none. Keep spills from costing anything: a spill is always a pile anyone can take back.

11. **Plan an opt-out for players who only want to play.** Valheim shipped a switch for its own hauling rule; Moving Out has Assist Mode. Boogle's version could be cosmetic-free and progress-neutral, such as a magnet radius that grows with era (already in brief 7) or a bundle you can empty into a nearby pile with one press. Hauling stays ignorable (DESIGN.md, "every system is ignorable"); helper freight already lets progress happen without you.

12. **Show quantity as a physical heap wherever it piles up.** Factorio's stacked belts and Katamari's growth (brief 7) both show amounts as stuff you can see. The Track ring's sleepers, a barrow's load and the wagon's freight should look fuller as they fill, so the counter only confirms what you can already see.
