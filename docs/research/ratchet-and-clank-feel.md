# Ratchet & Clank feel: bolts, loot, movement and juice

*Research report, 1 November 2026. What Insomniac's Ratchet & Clank games (Rift Apart, 2021, and the series back to 2002), plus its sister game Sunset Overdrive (2014, Insomniac's grinding game), do for collection feel, rewards, movement and constant feedback, and what Boogle can borrow: a bolt-style magnet for the haul, a counter that ticks with each piece, grinding on the laid rails and a few controller tweaks. Builds on [character-controller-feel.md](character-controller-feel.md) (jump shape, squash), [procedural-animation.md](procedural-animation.md) (springs), [carrying-and-logistics-play.md](carrying-and-logistics-play.md) (pickup squash and thock, rec 3), [ui-patterns.md](ui-patterns.md) (the `?proto=uisfx` count tick), [comedy-in-games.md](comedy-in-games.md) (hit-stop) and [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md) (trauma shake, chase camera) without repeating them. Boogle's side was read from `main` at f6e6d91: [src/features/haul-stack.ts](../../src/features/haul-stack.ts), [src/adapters/piles.ts](../../src/adapters/piles.ts), [src/proto/haul/pickup-feel.ts](../../src/proto/haul/pickup-feel.ts), [src/player/haul-feel.ts](../../src/player/haul-feel.ts), [src/features/feel.ts](../../src/features/feel.ts), [src/render/particles.ts](../../src/render/particles.ts), [src/render/motion.ts](../../src/render/motion.ts), [src/render/audio.ts](../../src/render/audio.ts). Nothing was played or measured frame by frame: **Insomniac has never published its bolt numbers** (magnet range, curve, speeds), and no developer talk or breakdown found this session gives them. Every number under "What this means for Boogle" is a starting value derived from Boogle's own constants, not a measurement of Ratchet. Recommendations only; nothing here is a decision.*

**Markers.** **[V]**: the page was fetched and the point matches it. **[S]**: seen only in a search summary, a review or a fan wiki; check before quoting. **[C]**: computed here from Boogle's code. Findings are paraphrased.

## Summary

1. A bolt pickup is three beats: the bolts **burst out** (a crate breaks, they spill and bounce), they **notice you** inside a pickup radius and curve in, and **each one lands with its own sound**. Reviewers describe the burst as individual clicks rather than one blob [S]; Insomniac's own line is that every bolt makes a sound as it flies into your hands [V].
2. The radius is a tunable stat in the series: the Bolt Grabber item extends it (fans say about double) [S], and a Rift Apart player felt the auto-collect range had shrunk and the pickups were less exciting for it [S]. Range is a feel dial, not a constant.
3. Boogle already has two of the three beats: pieces fly to your back when you walk into a pile ([haul-stack.ts](../../src/features/haul-stack.ts)), and `?proto=haul` adds a squash and a thock. What's missing is **reach** (you must walk within 1.3 m [C]), **stagger and spin** in the flight, and **one sound per piece tied to its arrival** (the `?proto=uisfx` count ticks toward the host's confirmed count about half a second later, not with the pieces [C]).
4. Escalating pitch is the cheapest way to make repetition exciting: PopCap's Peggle steps peg hits up a diatonic scale that fits the music's current harmony [V]. Boogle already has a hook for this, `onBeat(t, freq)` and `setNoteSnap` in [audio.ts](../../src/render/audio.ts).
5. Rewards in Rift Apart never stop play: weapons level up automatically from use (cap 5 on a first run) and spend raritanium on a per-weapon grid [S]; armour pieces give a collection bonus without being worn (5 %, 10 %, then 20 % for a set) [S]; each of 25 gold bolts unlocks something at once, often cosmetic, including replacement looks for the bolts themselves [S].
6. Movement is a chain, not a set of moves: the creative director describes swingshot into Phantom Dash into wall-run into rift tether flowing together, a lesson from Sunset Overdrive and Spider-Man [S]. Feedback carries traversal: Ratchet barely moves during a rift tether, yet it feels like a rush because of the feedback [S].
7. Hoverboots were expanded *because of* haptics: a haptic experiment led to the pumping mechanic and its high speeds [V]. Feel tests can create mechanics, not just polish them.
8. Insomniac's grinding rules come from Sunset Overdrive: grinds at least 8 m long, reverse direction instantly at will, keep surfaces out of the 26–45° band, and "shooting while grinding was fun" merged two systems [V]. Ratchet's grind rails add hopping between parallel rails and trick jumps by tapping jump [S].
9. Haptics in Rift Apart are a second channel the player can read during chaos (a slow weapon is ready again) [S]. On the web, pads may rumble (`vibrationActuator`, uneven support [S]); iPad touch gets none ([ui-patterns.md](ui-patterns.md)). Sound and squash must carry the feel alone.
10. Ratchet's comfort settings are worth copying in spirit: game speed 70/50/30 % on a shortcut, hoverboot auto-pump, auto-glide, simplified traversal on one button [S].

## 1. Bolt collection

### The three beats

- **Burst.** Crates break into bolts; Rift Apart's crates explode into bolts the same way as the 2016 remake [S, [Kotaku review](https://kotaku.com/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-the-kotaku-review-1847047303)]. Bolts also drop from enemies; a special crate type doubles bolts and breaks on touch [S, [fan wiki via search](https://ratchetandclank.fandom.com/wiki/Crates)].
- **Notice and pull.** Within a radius, bolts move toward you and are collected [S, [a fan's Unity devlog](https://medium.com/@christer_bjoerk/ratchet-clank-bolt-multiplier-3d-devlog-2-d5f69b0b41b4)]. In the 2016 remake they take wider, longer paths to reach you [S, [fan wiki, Bolt Grabber](https://ratchetandclank.fandom.com/wiki/Bolt_Grabber)]: the pull curves rather than flying straight, which spreads a burst out in time and space.
- **Land.** "Every bolt makes a sound as it flies into your hands" [V, [TheGamer on the DualSense](https://www.thegamer.com/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-dualsense-features-2/)]; a review describes them clicking individually, mirrored by the DualSense [S, [Jump Dash Roll](https://www.jumpdashroll.com/article/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-review)]. Players notice when the pickup sound is too quiet to enjoy [S, [GameFAQs thread](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/292801-ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart/79518492)].

### Range is a feel dial

- The original game's Bolt Grabber greatly increases the radius of attraction; later games' versions double it [S, [Ratchet Galaxy](https://ratchet-galaxy.com/en/games/ps2/ratchet-and-clank/inventory/items/bolt-grabber), [fan wiki](https://ratchetandclank.fandom.com/wiki/Bolt_Grabber)]. Size Matters sells it for 5,000 bolts [S]. A reach upgrade is an option that makes the same action feel better, which fits boogle-2's knacks ("options, never power").
- A Japanese player's review of Rift Apart found bolt collection less exhilarating than before and guessed the auto-collect range had narrowed [S, [note.com](https://note.com/papalonia/n/n63bcd829216a?hl=en)]. Whether or not that's true, players feel the radius.
- How Ratchet stays satisfying after thousands of pickups is not documented by Insomniac. What the sources support: each bolt is individual (sound, flight), bursts vary in size, and the currency always has somewhere to go (shops, upgrades). Peggle shows the standard audio answer: the same sound, stepped up a scale [V, [Audio Gang, Peggle 2](https://www.audiogang.org/peggle2-sonic-joy/)]; its sequel's team found escalating pitch on one sound "a big win" for variety without new assets [S, [Audiokinetic, Peggle Blast](https://blog.audiokinetic.com/en/peggle-blast-peg-hits-and-the-music-system)].

### Rarer variants

- **Raritanium**: blue crystals found by exploring, spent on each weapon's upgrade grid [S, [gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/ratchet-clank-rift-apart/character-development-weapon-upgrades/z4e960)].
- **Gold bolts**: 25, none missable, every planet can be revisited; each unlocks a reward immediately (wrench skins, *replacement looks for the bolts*, gallery, then cheats and gold weapons) [S, [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-gold-bolt-locations/1100-6492498/), [Ginx](https://www.ginx.tv/en/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-what-cheats-do-gold-bolts-unlock)]. A review singles out that immediate payoff as a strength [S, [One More Game](https://onemoregame.ph/2021/06/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-review/)].
- The bolt-replacement reward is the pattern to note: a rare find changes **how the common pickup looks and sounds**, not how much it's worth.

## 2. Loot and rewards

- **Level-ups come from use.** Each weapon earns XP when it hits enemies and levels up automatically, to 5 on a first run (10 in Challenge Mode) [S, [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-what-the-max-weapon-level/), [gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/ratchet-clank-rift-apart/character-development-weapon-upgrades/z4e960)]; Wikipedia lists "automatic weapon and health upgrades" beside manual raritanium upgrades [V, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_%26_Clank:_Rift_Apart)]. The player never stops to claim them.
- **Armour is a collection bonus.** Sets of helmet, chest and boots, mostly found in optional pocket dimensions; one piece gives 5 % of the set's bonus, two give 10 %, all three double it to 20 %, and you don't have to wear them [S, [Digital Trends via search](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=2718667), [Rift Apart armor, fan wiki](https://ratchetandclank.fandom.com/wiki/Rift_Apart_armor)]. Looks and benefit are separated, so you can wear what you like.
- **Tuned by watching.** The 2002 postmortem: four focus tests, more than 200 players, and the recorded data used to tune prices and "monetary rewards"; about three months spent prototyping 35+ weapons, many cut (one joke weapon's humour "lasted for about three seconds") [V, [Ted Price, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/postmortem-insomniac-games-i-ratchet-clank-i-)].
- No source found gives Rift Apart's level-up or reveal timings (pop size, duration, sound). That would need frame-by-frame capture.

## 3. Movement feel

- **The series is a platformer first.** The 2016 remake's directors call the original "a platformer first and a shooter second"; young testers ignored the right stick, so levels avoid sharp turns [V, [2016 postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-ratchet-clank-2016-i-postmortem)].
- **Chains.** Rift Apart adds dashing and wall-running [V, Wikipedia]. Marcus Smith describes Hook Shot into Phantom Dash into wall-run into rift tether flowing together, learned from Sunset Overdrive and Spider-Man [S, an interview quote seen in search results; the page was not fetched]. Spider-Man's creative director says they wanted Sunset's "sense of flow" [V, [Critical Hit](https://www.criticalhit.net/gaming/insomniac-used-experience-sunset-overdrive-make-spider-mans-movement-fluid/)].
- **Wall-run** works only on arrow-marked plates: jump at one and hold jump; far-apart walls are linked with the double jump [V, [gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/ratchet-clank-rift-apart/walls-how-to-wall-run/z8e883)]. It is placed, readable and forgiving, not a free-form system.
- **Phantom Dash** makes you briefly not exist, negating damage, and doubles as traversal [S, interview summaries via search].
- **Rift tether**: a near-instant pull through a portal; Mike Daly calls it one of the things he's proudest of, and says that although Ratchet is technically still, the feedback gives the rush of traversal [S, [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-devs-look-back-on-showcase-game-for-ps5/) and panel quotes via search]. The SSD made the dimension hops possible [V, Wikipedia].
- **Hoverboots**: hold to activate, alternate two buttons to pump up to top speed; an accessibility option pumps for you [S, [Insomniac support](https://support.insomniac.games/hc/en-us/articles/46716208107795-What-Accessibility-options-does-Ratchet-Clank-Rift-Apart-feature), [Can I Play That?](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/05/20/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-accessibility-features-revealed/)]. Daly: they expanded the boots *because of* haptics; that test "gave us the nudge" for pumping and high speeds [V, [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-devs-look-back-on-showcase-game-for-ps5/)]. The boots' sound is several layers combined, played partly from the controller [V, TheGamer].
- **Grind rails (series).** Grind boots ride rails, bars, train lines; you hop between parallel rails with stick plus jump to dodge mines and trains; some set pieces have three parallel rails and staying on one loops you back [S, fan wiki pages via search]. Rift Apart adds trick jumps by tapping jump on a rail [S, [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-trick-jump-do-how/)].
- **Grinding (Sunset Overdrive)**, Insomniac's own rules [V, [Wikipedia, development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Overdrive)]: you can reverse direction on a rail at any moment; the city is built from 4×4 blocks; surfaces avoid 26–45°; grinds are at least 8 m long (longer where enemies are, to give time to choose); a style meter rises for grinding without touching the ground. The level-design talk adds that the team scrapped two whole cities before one supported fast traversal without leaving players lost [V, [Liz England, GDC 2015 summary](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-cities-for-high-speed-traversal-in-i-sunset-overdrive-i-)].
- **Animation.** Insomniac's write-up mentions procedural locomotion fitted to uneven terrain (Glitch's legs adjusting on walls) and crowds in vertex animation rather than rigs [V, [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/09/02/deconstructing-the-impeccable-animation-of-ratchet-clank-rift-apart/)]. Lindsay Thompson's GDC 2022 talk on the game's animation exists, but no written summary of its blending or frame counts was found [V, [Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2022/11/15/the-animated-world-of-ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart/)].
- **Responsiveness numbers.** The only published number found: the 120 Hz display mode cut input latency by about 8 ms without changing frame rate; the 40 fps fidelity mode followed [S, [ResetEra on Digital Foundry](https://www.resetera.com/threads/digital-foundry-ratchet-and-clank-rift-aparts-40fps-fidelity-mode-is-a-big-deal-for-consoles.460662/page-2), [PSU](https://www.psu.com/news/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-update-1-002-adds-120hz-display-mode-40-fps-target-for-fidelity-graphics-mode/)]. Jump heights, run speeds and blend times are not published.
- **Camera.** No Insomniac camera talk was found. Chad Dezern (co-op Ratchet) describes an ideal camera distance at which you can track your character in busy scenes, plus colour-coded signals when the camera has to pull further out [S, [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/interview-insomniac-s-dezern-and-the-local-co-op-appeal)].

## 4. Juice systems

- **Haptics as information.** Insomniac found players still notice haptic feedback when a lot is happening on screen, so it signals when a slow weapon is ready again [S, interviews via search]. Each weapon gets its own trigger resistance and buzz (a shotgun resists, then thumps both hands) [V, TheGamer]. One weapon's charge-up music plays through the haptic motors [S, [Glitched](https://www.glitched.online/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-creates-music-using-ps5-haptic-feedback/)]. Daly hadn't expected haptics to matter so much [V, GamesBeat].
- **Audio from the hands.** Weapon and gadget sounds come partly from the controller speaker so they seem to come from what you're holding [V, TheGamer]; Marcus Smith on 3D audio: you can hear where things are in the world [S].
- **Impact.** Weapon hits knock, freeze or convulse enemies; Goons take a selfie after landing a hit [V, PlayStation Blog]. Reactions themselves are feedback, and funny.
- **Hit-stop and shake** are covered in [comedy-in-games.md](comedy-in-games.md) (Sakurai) and [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md) (trauma²); nothing Ratchet-specific was found that changes them.
- **Comfort and readability.** Game speed 70/50/30 % on a shortcut (traversal timing and busy fights were the reasons), hold or toggle for swing and aim, auto-glide, flight assist, screen-effect and HUD options, controller vibration and trigger options, and one-button simplified traversal [S, [Sony Interactive](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/news/blog/inside-the-accessibility-features-of-ratchet-clank-rift-apart/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-accessibility-options-game-speed-174718726.html)].
- **Without haptics on the web.** `Gamepad.vibrationActuator` is listed from Safari 16.4 but `playEffect` support on Apple platforms is unclear [S, [web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/gamepad-haptics.json)]; [cross-device-input.md](cross-device-input.md) already says to feature-detect and treat it as a bonus. Touch on iPad has no web haptics [S, ui-patterns.md]. Ratchet's own fallback is the same: a sound per bolt.

## What this means for Boogle

Numbers are starting values for a playtest. Where Boogle's code sets the context it is cited: walk 5.6 m/s, sprint 9.5, gravity 18 m/s², jump 7.6 m/s ([player.md](../game-design/player.md)); pickup reach `PICK_R = 1.3` m and the take is not distance-checked by the host, so reach is a Local dial ([src/adapters/piles.ts](../../src/adapters/piles.ts)) [C]; fly-ins last `flyMs = 380` ms, at most 6 drawn, staggered by 8 % of the flight each, with a 1.2 m hop ([haul-stack.ts](../../src/features/haul-stack.ts), [haul-feel.ts](../../src/player/haul-feel.ts)) [C].

1. **A bolt-style magnet for piles.**
   - *Reach:* 3.0 m instead of 1.3 m. At sprint that's about 0.18 s earlier, at walk about 0.3 s [C]: enough for pieces to visibly lift and come to you instead of being stepped on. Try 2.2 and 4.0 as the other two settings.
   - *Pull, per piece:* a 50–70 ms "notice" (lift 0.3–0.5 m, start spinning), then home on a moving target (the top of your bundle, not where you were) with speed rising from 2 m/s at 60 m/s² to a cap of 16 m/s, about 1.7× sprint so pieces always catch a sprinting player [C]. Add a sideways bow that fades as they close (offset 0.6 m × sin(πu), side alternating by piece) so they curve like the 2016 bolts rather than fly straight.
   - *Stagger:* 40 ms between pieces (today it's about 30 ms [C]), drawn fliers up to 8; a 6-piece pickup lasts about 0.5 s end to end.
   - *Spin:* 2–3 turns/s around a random axis, damped to the bundle's orientation over the last 0.15 m; the piece shrinks 100 → 75 % (the bundle's scale, `RS`) as it lands.
   - *Smallest prototype:* `?proto=haul&v=magnet` (a third value in [haul-variant.ts](../../src/player/haul-variant.ts)), with `&reach=` to compare 2.2, 3.0 and 4.0 in one session. Question: do you start steering *at* piles to sweep them up?

2. **One sound per piece, on arrival, stepping up a scale.**
   - Tie the tick to each flying piece reaching your back, not to the host's confirmation; let the chip's number advance with the ticks and reconcile silently if the host disagrees (it nearly never will: the take is checked only against the pile's count).
   - *Pitch:* a short wooden tick (the pickup-feel thock, shortened to 40–60 ms) that climbs a major pentatonic scale one degree per piece from about 520 Hz, caps after 10 steps (two octaves) and then alternates its top two notes. The run resets after 700 ms with no pickups. Detune ±2 % at most (it must stay in tune), and pan alternate pieces ±0.25.
   - *Layering:* every 5th piece adds the low body of the thock; a burst of 4 or more ends with a two-note "chord" (root and fifth) 80 ms after the last tick.
   - *Music:* pass each tick through `onBeat(t, freq, 16)` ([audio.ts](../../src/render/audio.ts)), so with `?proto=groove` the ladder fits the current chord, as Peggle's pegs do.
   - *Smallest prototype:* inside the magnet variant above (no extra flag), with the chip's number driven by arrivals; compare against `?proto=uisfx`'s confirmed-count tick on a 4-log Take and a 30-log pile.

3. **Spill and settle when logs come from a bundle.** Take logs (E on a palm's bundle) today flies 4 logs straight to your back. Instead: they pop out of the bundle in a fan (up 4–5 m/s, out 1.5–2.5 m/s, spinning 6–10 rad/s, Boogle's 18 m/s² gravity), bounce once at 0.3 restitution, rest 120–200 ms, then the magnet takes them. About 0.8 s from E to the last tick [C]. A small dust puff per landing from `emit` (2–3 particles, life 0.3 s). *Smallest prototype:* the same `v=magnet` variant, in [log-sources.ts](../../src/features/log-sources.ts)'s fly call. Question: is the pop worth the extra half second, or does it feel like a delay?

4. **Rare looks, same value.** The gold-bolt lesson: a rare find changes how the common pickup looks and sounds. One log in about 30 (picked from the piece's id, so everyone sees the same one) is a "knotted" log with a glint and a sparkle chord on arrival; it counts as one log. Fits brief 9's rarity done kindly and boogle-2's "no prize piles". *Smallest prototype:* a `&rare=30` flag on the magnet variant; watch whether players mention it unprompted.

5. **Grind the laid rails.** A movement toy on the track the island builds (DESIGN.md 1 and 10).
   - *Enter:* land on a laid rail section while falling, within 0.7 m of the track centreline and with your horizontal heading within 50° of it; snap to the track over 0.08 s.
   - *Speed:* keep the along-track part of your entry speed, at least 6 m/s; ease toward 9 m/s (sprint speed) at 4 m/s², plus gravity along the grade; cap 13 m/s. Pull back on the stick to reverse at any moment, as Sunset allowed.
   - *Leave:* jump any time (rail velocity plus the normal 7.6 m/s jump); at the railhead you fly off with your speed, and the existing tumble flop (landing above 3 m/s off a moving deck) gives the punchline. Meeting the wagon on the rail is a bonk flop.
   - *Feel:* stand the capsule sideways to the rail (a skater's stance), lean into curves with the existing flop spring (max 0.35 rad), 20–40 orange sparks per second at the feet (life 0.25 s), a band-passed metal hiss whose pitch follows speed, a clack at every rail joint, camera dropped 0.5 m and looking 3 m ahead along the rail.
   - *Level design:* Sunset's minimum of 8 m per grind; Boogle's first laid section is longer than that already.
   - *Smallest prototype:* `?proto=wagon&grind=1` on the laid sections the wagon prototype already has. Questions: do you ride the rail back to the palms instead of walking, and do you race the wagon?

6. **A second jump in the air.** Ratchet's double jump is the glue between moves. Try a second jump while airborne that sets vertical speed to max(current, 0) + 6.0 m/s (about 80 % of the first, so it never stacks into a super-jump), with a bigger stretch on the flop spring and a puff. Optional: hold jump while falling caps the fall at 4 m/s (Ratchet's glide, Boogle-size). Same button, no new pad slot. *Smallest prototype:* `?jump2=1` and `?glide=1` flags read by the controller, compared on the mushroom bounces and at the lighthouse.

7. **Reward pops that don't stop play.** When your contribution tier changes, or a section you poured into is laid: the haul chip scales 1 → 1.25 → 1 over 350 ms with `outBack`, plays a three-note rising arpeggio, shows the tier name for 2.5 s, and a ring of 12 sparkles circles your avatar once (0.6 s). Nothing pauses and nothing needs dismissing, like Ratchet's automatic level-ups. *Smallest prototype:* under `?proto=uisfx`, with a debug button that grants the next tier.

8. **A reach knack, later.** The Bolt Grabber shows that a bigger pickup radius is a satisfying upgrade that adds no power: it makes the same walk feel better. If knacks need a first example, "Long Arms" (reach 3 → 4.5 m) fits boogle-2's "options, never power". *Smallest prototype:* none needed beyond `&reach=` in rec 1; ask playtesters whether 4.0 feels like a treat or like cheating.

9. **Keep the mix clean under constant ticking.** Put pickup ticks on the `ui` bus; cap pickup voices at 6 (drop the oldest, not the newest); about −18 dBFS per tick so a 20-piece burst doesn't clip; with more than 12 pieces in one burst, ticks after the 12th run at a fixed 45 ms cadence (a slot-machine run) and the rest are counted silently. *Smallest prototype:* the magnet variant on a 60-log pile, listened to with the groove mixer on.

10. **Feedback without haptics.** Every pickup and grind effect must work with sound and squash only (iPad touch). Where a pad exposes `vibrationActuator`, add 20–40 ms of weak `dual-rumble` (weak 0.15, strong 0) per tick, no more often than every 60 ms, and a low continuous 0.1 while grinding. Feature-detect; never required. *Smallest prototype:* behind the grind and magnet flags, tested on the Mac with a pad.

11. **Readable bursts.** Keep fliers small and alike (the bundle's 0.75 scale), sparkles to 2 per piece with a 0.2 s life, no screen shake for pickups (shake stays for impacts, per [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md)). A 60-log pile at 2 sparkles a piece is 120 particles, well under the pool's 720 slots and 256 per frame ([particles.ts](../../src/render/particles.ts)) [C].

12. **A slow-down shortcut for comfort.** Rift Apart's game speed (70/50/30 %) answers traversal timing trouble. Boogle's Local feel (fly-ins, grind speed, a solo wagon prototype) could take a 70 % "relaxed" setting in the pause view; the shared sim can't slow down, so this only fits solo prototypes. Low priority; listed so it's on record.

### The five to try first

1. **Magnet pickup** (rec 1): `?proto=haul&v=magnet&reach=3`.
2. **Arrival ticks up a scale** (rec 2): in the same variant, with the chip driven by arrivals.
3. **Grind the laid rails** (rec 5): `?proto=wagon&grind=1`.
4. **Spill and settle from bundles** (rec 3): in the magnet variant.
5. **Second jump** (rec 6): `?jump2=1` (and `?glide=1`).
