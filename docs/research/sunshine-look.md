# Sunshine's look: cool water, warm stone, readable light

> **Verdict:** Cool water, warm stone, readable light; make the sea the hero.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Make the crossing's sea the hero. 2. Lock the daytime palette under Neutral. 3. Filter glitter before increasing it.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [plan look-pass](../plans/look-pass.md), [plan rail-slice](../plans/rail-slice.md).

_Research report, 1 November 2026. Super Mario Sunshine's visual construction, the crossing beauty board's selected shots, and a practical translation to Boogle's WebGPU renderer on an M4 iPad Pro. Recommendations only; no renderer changes or new design decisions._

## Findings

### What makes Sunshine feel beautiful

The useful abstraction is **cool, inviting water beneath hot, readable solid forms**. Large cyan/blue areas let sparse white sparkle feel intense; warm walls and timber make those blues feel cooler. Clean silhouettes, generous sky, open shadows and visible shore contacts make the place comprehensible. Heat distortion and glare reinforce that temperature, but cannot establish it on their own. This is an aesthetic interpretation, not a claim that Nintendo used physically based shading. **[I]**

There is primary evidence for the intended sensation: in the 2002 Nintendo Dream interview, Yoshiaki Koizumi relates the water play to childhood sensations, and Takashi Tezuka connects tall buildings with wall-jump play. The team also describes a clay island model. It supports a tactile, playable holiday place, not a shader specification. **[V, developer interview through a translation]** [Super Mario Sunshine development interview](https://shmuplations.com/mariosunshine/).

### Hardware tricks and their modern equivalents

GameCube's TEV is a staged texture/colour combiner, not an unrestricted modern fragment shader. Indirect texturing can offset another texture lookup. These capabilities are documented by the author of the homebrew [tevsl compiler](https://github.com/crtc-demos/tevsl). **[R, hardware implementation evidence; not proof Sunshine used every capability]** Dolphin's developers explain the embedded framebuffer, external framebuffer and copy operations in [Hybrid XFB](https://dolphin-emu.org/blog/2017/11/19/hybridxfb/). **[V]**

## Recipe for shot 2

### 1. One Local sea graph; no new simulation

Move shared colour, reflection, normals and glint construction into a small renderer module consumed by [water-render.ts](../../src/render/water-render.ts) and [crossing/sky.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sky.ts). Keep different mesh extents and depth providers. Use `Fn`, `uniform`, `texture`, `positionWorld`, `cameraPosition`, `normalNode` only where actually consumed, and `colorNode`/`opacityNode`; a Basic material needs the normal explicitly in its colour calculations. Extend other materials with [patchNode](../../src/render/material-patch.ts). All motion is **Local**, never read by physics or lockstep. **A**

Use world `xz` for texture coordinates, shared time and sun direction, and no per-frame random seed. The four outside quads and home plane must agree on the rendered water level at their seam. Start with a flat common far surface and normal-only waves; preserve local player ripple behaviour. The 5 m overlap and differing existing levels deserve a seam look before choosing exact geometry changes.

### 2. Depth and view angle have separate jobs

Keep the existing height source near home. For an outlying island, provide a small world-space bathymetry tile with explicit bounds/validity. Outside valid tiles, choose open water deliberately; never clamp the home height texture across the entire crossing.

Start with vertical depth `d = max(waterY - groundY, 0)`. Blend shallow→body using `smoothstep(0, 2.5, d)`, then body→deep using `smoothstep(2.5, 9, d)`. These retain existing tuning points while changing the colours. Bathymetry measures the bottom, not the pier top. **E near home / A for shared graph and new tiles**

Compute `V = normalize(cameraPosition - positionWorld)`, `N` from filtered waves, `NoV = clamp(dot(N,V),0,1)`. Reflect `-V` about `N`, evaluate the same horizon/top gradient used by the sky at that direction, and blend with `F = 0.02 + 0.98 * pow(1-NoV,5)`. Start with an **art-directed cap of 0.60** on reflection weight to preserve blue at the horizon; this cap is intentionally not physical Fresnel. Clouds may remain absent from the reflection in the first pass. **A**

This gives turquoise looking down and more sky looking across. It is not SSR, and will not mirror the town. Keep the current shallow transparency while checking swimmers and submerged props; `alpha 0.5→0.97` over `0.2→5 m` is the initial control. Do not simultaneously add absorption, multiply by a dark depth ramp and lower alpha: that can count depth three times. A Beer–Lambert transmission model is an optional later replacement, not another multiplier.

### 3. Sun glitter that survives 20 m/s

The current high-power procedural lobe can alias even though MSAA smooths geometry edges. World locking prevents swimming textures, but **does not prevent temporal aliasing**: at 60 fps the camera advances about 0.33 m/frame. Tiny bright details crossing that footprint must broaden or fade.

Start with an original 128² repeatable normal texture sampled at **8 m and 2 m repeat lengths**, rotated differently, scrolling at **0.03 and 0.017 m/s**. Limit combined slope amplitude to approximately **0.10–0.16**. Two samples replace the high-frequency analytic detail; retain only slow broad motion. Data textures use `NoColorSpace`; create proper mipmaps. Do not sample white noise into a threshold to invent glitter. **A**

Calculate the world footprint from `dFdx(positionWorld.xz)` and `dFdy(...)` in uniform fragment control flow. Fade the fine contribution when its useful features shrink below **2–3 pixels**. Use derivatives of the final texture coordinates for rotated/scaled sampling. TSL's texture gradient sampling can increase the footprint along projected camera travel; an initial conservative floor is the **0.33 m/frame** travel footprint at 20 m/s/60 Hz, scaled by measured frame interval. Treat this motion-aware filter as a **spike**, not a proven drop-in cure. Do not force every sample to one manually chosen mip: retain anisotropic footprint information.

Normal mipmapping alone does not filter the nonlinear specular lobe. Toksvig's paper derives a correction from the length `l` of the averaged, **not yet normalized** normal: `f = l / (l + s * (1-l))`, exponent `s' = f*s`, lobe amplitude factor `(1+s')/(1+s)`. **[V]** [Mipmapping Normal Maps](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/whitepapers/2006/Mipmapping_Normal_Maps.pdf). Start `s = 96` rather than today's 140, normalizing only after measuring `l`.

For this method, the custom mip chain must preserve averaged XYZ length; a normal-map pipeline that renormalizes every mip, or reconstructs unit Z from two channels, loses that information. Validate one normal layer first; combining variance from two layers is **S**, not “just add Toksvig.” If that spike is disproportionate, use one filtered normal layer for glints and keep the second only for broad colour/reflection distortion. **A for a wider lobe; S for variance-preserving filtering**

Use `H = normalize(V + sunDir)` and a smooth `pow(max(dot(N,H),0),s')` lobe, multiplied by the correction and warm HDR sun colour. Start peak gain **0.6–0.9**, without thresholding it into dots. A broad reflected-light envelope can leave a broken lane rather than uniform sparkles everywhere. For sunset, use a larger effective sun lobe before increasing brightness. Mask glints when the sun is below the horizon; the moon is a separate, dimmer source. Bloom stays off during filtering tests.

The following is a **TSL construction sketch, not compiled implementation**. Inputs are supplied nodes/textures; the normal texture is authored with world-up Y and preserves averaged XYZ in its mip chain. This isolates the one-layer filtering test before adding a second layer, motion-expanded gradients, foam or the existing ripple field:

```ts
// Inside the shared Fn: all texture/derivative work is outside divergent branches.
const p = positionWorld.xz;
const waveUv = p.div(8).add(vec2(0.03, 0).mul(uTime).div(8));
const avgN = texture(waveTex, waveUv).grad(dFdx(waveUv), dFdy(waveUv)).rgb.mul(2).sub(1);
const l = clamp(length(avgN), 0.001, 1);
const N = normalize(avgN);
const V = normalize(cameraPosition.sub(positionWorld));
const R = reflect(V.negate(), N);
const F = min(float(0.02).add(pow(float(1).sub(clamp(dot(N, V), 0, 1)), 5).mul(0.98)), 0.6);
const reflectedSky = mix(uHorizon, uSkyTop, pow(max(R.y, 0), 0.6));
const d = max(waterY.sub(groundY), 0); // validated tile; choose open colour outside it
const shelf = mix(uShallow, uBody, smoothstep(0, 2.5, d));
const body = mix(shelf, uDeep, smoothstep(2.5, 9, d));
const s = float(96);
const f = l.div(l.add(s.mul(float(1).sub(l))));
const filteredS = s.mul(f);
const energy = filteredS.add(1).div(s.add(1));
const H = normalize(V.add(uSunDir));
const glint = pow(max(dot(N, H), 0), filteredS)
  .mul(energy)
  .mul(0.75)
  .mul(uSunVisibility);
const rgb = mix(body, reflectedSky, F).add(uSunColour.mul(glint));
// Return rgb to colorNode; derive opacityNode separately. No tone mapping here.
```

The installed [TextureNode implementation](../../node_modules/three/src/nodes/accessors/TextureNode.js) exposes `.grad(dx, dy)`; `dFdx`/`dFdy`, `reflect`, `Fn` and arithmetic come from `three/tsl`. This is a new graph within the existing pass, not a new rendering architecture.

### 4. Foam belongs to contacts

Retain the home shore's **0–0.6 m depth** band as a cheap first step. Remember that vertical depth produces a wider band on a shallow beach than on a cliff; a constant-width artistic rim requires horizontal shore distance. Start pale blue-green foam at **0.5–0.8 opacity**, broken gently by the same low-frequency wave field, with no binary threshold. **E/A**

Piers require another signal: use the distance to their **horizontal waterline footprint**, not water depth. For the first pier, an analytic box SDF gives a **0.2–0.45 m outer band** with **0.05–0.10 m** oscillation. Then combine repeated static footprints into a contact atlas or instanced local foam strips. Do not loop over every pier in every sea fragment.

For an atlas, start **256² over a 64 m local tile** (0.25 m/texel), with padded edges, explicit validity and a stationary world origin. A 256² mask across the whole 1.2 km crossing is too coarse for this band. Test whether 512² or analytic strips are needed near the camera. Tile updates follow scenery changes, not every frame. A depth/contact lookup plus two normal samples is the intended water sampling envelope; extra bathymetry and mask channels can be packed where their scale agrees. **A for one analytic pier; S for tiled atlas integration**

Fade subpixel foam coverage smoothly rather than widening all distant piers into white rings. Moving splashes use the existing [particle pool](../../src/render/particles.ts); persistent wakes are outside this brief.

### 5. Palette and tone mapping

Keep **Neutral** initially. Khronos designed it to preserve base colour better under neutral lighting while compressing bright highlights; its [specification](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md) and [interactive comparison](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/tone-mapping) support that choice. **[V]** It is not “no desaturation”: the installed three.js implementation compresses highlights above a post-offset peak of 0.76 and moves the brightest colours toward white. **[L: `node_modules/three/src/nodes/display/ToneMappingFunctions.js`]** This is useful for sun glitter, provided the whole sea is not driven into that shoulder.

Use exposure **1.0**, no saturation grade initially, matte roughness **0.85–0.95** on stone/wood. Keep the existing sun intensity **2.5** and hemisphere **1.2** as an A/B starting point, then judge lit surfaces, since water's Basic shader and lit Standard materials respond differently. Do not multiply a fully authored sea colour by the sun's intensity as though it were diffuse albedo.

## Cost, architecture and validation

The target is **about 1.44 million actual rendered pixels and at most 10 ms GPU time**, for example a 1600×900 target. It is not a claim about native iPad resolution. Record render-target dimensions and fix [adaptive resolution](../../src/features/adaptive-res.ts) during comparisons; otherwise a faster frame may simply contain fewer pixels. `?dpr=fixed` stops adaptation but does not by itself select 1.44 MP; configure the capture viewport/render scale and verify the actual target size.

The [visual-target report](../proposals/visual-target.md) contains historical Mac measurements and an inferred M4 relationship, not measurements of this proposed sea on an M4 iPad. Do not scale its entire frame by pixel count or quote its high preset as a validated iPad cost. Browser queue throughput, rAF time and summed overlapping pass timers are also not interchangeable with elapsed GPU frame time.

The following are **rough engineering cost estimates and proposed acceptance allowances**, assuming sea covers 50–70% of the frame, warm shaders, no competing renderer and stored 4× MSAA. They are unmeasured planning ranges, not benchmark results or a guarantee. If the present scene is already above its allowance, fix that before spending the remainder.

| Increment or allocation                                    | Status | Proposed GPU allowance at 1.44 MP | Work added                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Existing scene, shadows, MSAA and output                   | E      | Reserve **≤5.5 ms**               | Must measure the actual baseline; no pass added               |
| Palette, shared depth/view colour, analytic sky reflection | E/A    | **+0.05–0.20 ms**                 | Arithmetic, existing depth sample; no scene copy              |
| Two filtered wave samples and stable specular              | A/S    | **+0.20–0.60 ms**                 | Two texture samples, derivatives/lobe math; no history target |
| Contact foam                                               | E/A/S  | **+0.05–0.20 ms**                 | One local mask sample or a small instanced draw               |
| Added original kit, palm sway and local contact treatment  | A      | **+0.20–0.60 ms**                 | Instancing, modest vertex math; caster count matters          |
| Restricted height haze                                     | L/A    | **+0.05–0.15 ms**                 | Integrate into existing shading, no fullscreen volume         |
| Optional reduced-resolution glow                           | L/S    | **+0.25–0.60 ms**                 | Extra target/pass work; leave off initially                   |
| Optional heat shimmer                                      | S      | **+0.15–0.35 ms**                 | Scene sampling/composite work; leave off initially            |

The core additions' upper allowances total **1.75 ms**; with a 5.5 ms baseline that leaves **2.75 ms** under the ceiling. Even both optional effects must leave roughly 1.8 ms reserve. Those are allocation sums, not evidence that Apple schedules all work additively. Avoid filling the reserve with AO, TRAA and cascades before measuring.

For scale, one full-resolution RGBA16F read plus write is nominally **23.04 MB/frame**, or **1.38 GB/s at 60 Hz**, before MSAA, extra samples, cache behaviour or tile stores. Apple's [WWDC20 transcript](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/) explains why pass structure and memory traffic matter on its tile-based GPUs. **[V]** Native Metal recommendations do not authorize bypassing WebGPU semantics or reinstating Boogle's previously broken discard path.

### Why this architecture, compared with standard engines

- **Unity HDRP:** its [version 16 water system](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@16.0/manual/WaterSystem-Overview.html) separates wave bands, surface appearance and masks. **[V]** Borrow those independent art controls; Boogle needs Local surface shading, not that simulation/rendering system. The cited version's restrictions are not claims about every current Unity release.
- **Unreal:** [Single Layer Water](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine) uses a dedicated composite with scattering/absorption and reflection work; its reduced path drops expensive features. **[V]** Boogle deliberately approximates body colour and sky reflection inside its existing material. It is not an equivalent implementation of Unreal's volume model.
- **Godot:** [screen-reading shaders](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/screen-reading_shaders.html) document scene-copy timing, transparent-object omissions and depth reconstruction. **[V]** A world-height/contact tile avoids those screen-space limits for Boogle's long, grazing views. Screen depth remains useful for a later refraction spike, not the first foam dependency.

These choices serve **Small and readable** and **8. Designed for drop-in, drop-out** in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md): better water makes an ordinary solo ride enjoyable without adding gameplay state, player work or a second renderer.

## What would fight the look or the budget

- **Never import Nintendo assets, extracted textures, ROM data or Delfino's level layout.** Study Sunshine by name and build our own forms and patterns.
- **No per-frame random sparkle, hard specular thresholds or deliberately unfiltered subpixel foam.** MSAA and bloom cannot repair their temporal instability.
- **No compulsory full-scene planar reflection, SSR, FFT ocean, volumetric cloud/heat field, SSGI or multiple shadow cascades for this target.** They answer problems absent from shot 2 and spend the budget before the art improves.
- **No wholesale high look preset.** AO/TRAA/bloom/grade/shadows together hide which layer helps and can exceed the budget. TRAA is not the prerequisite for readable water.
- **No filmic desaturation fix, hard cel bands across the sea, heavy outlines, chromatic aberration, vignette or depth-of-field over the driving view.** They weaken its clear, saturated, playable distance.
- **No full-screen heat wobble or glowing white sky.** They obscure the town and walkway. Heat shimmer is the last optional experiment.
- **No dense alpha-card palms, leaf microgeometry, photographic plaster noise or tiny roof tiles.** Spend geometry on silhouette, eaves and openings.
- **No fake shore shelf along the entire bridge; no white foam carpet; no global dark shadow painted onto moving water.** Contacts should identify actual land and pier footprints.
- **No kilometre-wide dynamic shadow map or point light per dusk window.** Use local dynamic contact, authored creases and emissive windows.
- **No second render pipeline, GLSL fallback, duplicate tone mapping or manual gamma on colour uniforms.** Keep the render contract, stored MSAA attachments and one output conversion.

## Recommendations, ordered by gain per cost

1. **Make the crossing's sea the hero.** Share [home](../../src/render/water-render.ts) and [open-sea](../../src/proto/crossing/sky.ts) shading; start `#66D6CF → #237FA4 → #165D86` at 0/2.5/9 m, reflection `F0=0.02` capped at 0.60, and exponent-96 warm glints. **A; roughly +0.05–0.20 ms before filtering.** Smallest test: shot-2 pose, then look down and toward the sun, including the home/extension seam. **This single sea-material change is most likely to make a player think of Sunshine.**
2. **Lock the daytime palette under Neutral.** In [sky.ts](../../src/render/sky.ts) and [crossing/world.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/world.ts), start the listed sky, honey timber and pale pier swatches, exposure 1, sun 2.5, hemisphere 1.2, saturation grade off. **E/A; negligible incremental GPU work.** Smallest test: the same shot-2 still with three temporary matte colour swatches, checking lit and shaded sides.
3. **Filter glitter before increasing it.** Start 8 m/2 m normal layers, slope 0.10–0.16, exponent 96, 2–3-pixel fine-detail fade; spike Toksvig filtering on one layer first. **A/S; +0.20–0.60 ms allowance.** Smallest test: a 10-second shot-2 ride at 20 m/s with a slow yaw and bloom off; reject crawling dots or flashing bands.
4. **Anchor one shore and one pier with foam.** Extend [water-render.ts](../../src/render/water-render.ts) with the existing 0–0.6 m shore band and one 0.2–0.45 m pier-footprint band; only then test a 64 m contact tile. **E/A/S; +0.05–0.20 ms.** Smallest test: swim around one crossing pier, then pass it at speed; no foam on its shadow or across deep open water.
5. **Keep the far destination colourful through air.** Preserve [crossing/sky.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sky.ts)'s 180–3600 m fog first; trial [look-world.ts](../../src/debug/look-world.ts) haze at 0.00015–0.00030/m only if it adds distinct depth. **E/L/A; +0.05–0.15 ms.** Smallest test: shot 2 with haze toggled, ensuring the tower and warm/cool separation survive.
6. **Give near solids contact and open shade.** In [crossing/world.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/world.ts), start authored underside/crease darkening at 10–15%, with local nearby casters inside [scene.ts](../../src/render/scene.ts)'s existing 72 m shadow box; keep roughness 0.85–0.95. **A; part of the +0.20–0.60 ms scenery allowance.** Smallest test: walk under and beside one pier/deck section at noon, then sunset; do not turn on global AO.
7. **Replace one greybox cluster with original chunky seaside forms.** At the far quay in [crossing/world.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/world.ts), start three cream/ochre buildings, 0.3–0.5 m eaves/recesses and one clear landmark; use broad painted/vertex colour variation within about ±5%. **A; same scenery allowance.** Smallest test: shot-2 silhouette and shot-5 approach, with no new level layout or copied Delfino building.
8. **Add two palms with broad, slow fronds.** Reuse [look-world.ts](../../src/debug/look-world.ts)'s sway approach: 6–8 opaque curved fronds per palm, roughly 0.5–1° root sway at 0.15–0.25 Hz, stronger movement at tips. **L/A; same scenery allowance.** Smallest test: one near and one far palm during a crossing ride; reject alpha shimmer and silhouettes that look like dense fern noise.
9. **Derive golden hour and dusk from the same materials.** In [sky.ts](../../src/render/sky.ts), start the shot-3/5 palette rows, blue water under warm glints, and a few `#FFD08A` windows at emissive 1.5–2 with at most two nearby light pools. **E/A; near-zero cost for uniforms/emissive rectangles within the scenery budget.** Smallest test: shot-3 and shot-5 positions at `tod=0.55/0.62`, with the walkway readable and no invented window reflections.
