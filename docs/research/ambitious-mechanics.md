# Build a storm tide the ball can break

Boogle's most promising ambitious direction is a single, telegraphed **"Storm Tide" cycle**: a tide that rises on a schedule anyone can see coming, sand that gets wet and slumps, a ball that can smash a breach in a sea wall, rain squalls that roll in under visible clouds, and a heavy crate that washes up at king tide and only moves when two friends push it. These five mechanics fit together. The tide gives sand walls a purpose. Wet sand makes walls fail in slapstick ways. The ball becomes the tool that releases trapped water. Rain feeds the same water sim from above. The crate turns each king tide into a moment where cooperation just happens. Almost all the plumbing exists already: the water sim has an unused `setSeaLevel`, edits are host-stamped integer ops, and host-owned props follow the Boogler pattern (a codebase brief at the time, since deleted as stale; see git history). The bundle needs **no new gamepad buttons**, which matters because only View, R3 and D-pad right are free. It also stays inside every DESIGN.md principle: the world is the only antagonist, every event is announced 30-60 s ahead, and nothing is gated or scored. One strong coding agent can get it playable overnight on a `prototype/storm-tide` branch, provided each mechanic is its own toggleable plug-in and the room-wide toggles are synced. The last section is a ready-to-paste goal prompt for that run.

## The tide is the missing third of Boogle's core loop

DESIGN.md frames the loop as water gluing the ball and the farm together, with the tide as the shared threat. Today only the water-and-sand toy is fully built. Farming is "on hold" with only the mushroom stall shipped, and Phase 6 (the tide) is entirely unchecked ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md)). The team's own earlier research ranked **"Sandcastle vs Tide" first of twelve ideas, at five stars** ([docs/research/water-farming-multiplayer.md](water-farming-multiplayer.md)). The code is closer than the roadmap suggests. `setSeaLevel(cm)` exists, and `water-world.js` already pins deep sea cells to `water.seaLevel` every step. Nothing calls it over time, and the brief found no source file that implements tide or weather beyond comments ([src/sim/water.js](../../src/sim/water.js), [src/world/water-world.js](../../src/world/water-world.js)).

The design precedents explain why a cycle beats a one-off disaster. From Dust built its tension on tides that cover and expose land, plus scheduled tsunamis and eruptions that players learn to anticipate. Eric Chahi warned that "it would be very frustrating if the player can't anticipate what the plan is" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-core-of-i-from-dust-i-)). Timberborn found that taking water away on a schedule was "the best worst thing" it could do, because it turned reservoirs into durable builds people care about ([Mechanistry press](https://mechanistry.com/press/how-to-starve-a-post-apocalyptic-beaver-colony-steam-game-festival)). Breath of the Wild's director rejected player-controlled weather because not controlling it is more fun ([GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/stories/302341-nintendo-considered-letting-players-control-the-weather-in-zelda)). Fan criticism of BotW's rain shows the opposite risk: hard verb penalties feel punishing ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=187778)). So Boogle's weather should change outcomes (holes fill, walls sag) and never disable verbs.

Determinism makes this almost free on the network. Teardown syncs destruction by sending commands that every machine applies "in exactly the same way and order", not voxel data ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/teardown-developer-breaks-down-multiplayer-and-voxel-destruction-tech)). That is Boogle's "sync edits, not state" model. A tide and a squall that are pure functions of `(tick, seed)` cost **zero bytes on the wire**, and clients can draw the warning before the host says anything. Two risks need guarding. Factorio's experience is that per-tick CRCs catch divergence late, and small integer-rounding errors pile up unseen ([FFF #188](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-188)). Spreading processes need hard per-tick caps so one action can't flood the active-cell list (Noita's dirty-rect lesson, [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation/)).

The top five made the cut because each one feeds the next. **The tide** is the clock and the shared stake. Its schedule is integer table lookups (no native trig: commit `0b0b834` banned it in the sim), so late joiners see the same water. The schedule matches From Dust's "plan you can anticipate". **Wet sand slumping** answers the falling-sand insight: a few uniform local rules ("if the drop to a neighbour exceeds a threshold, move one unit; wet sand holds a steeper slope") produce collapses players predict by intuition and still laugh at ([Making Sandspiel](https://maxbittker.com/making-sandspiel); [80.lv on Noita](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation/)). The rule conserves sand and is testable. It also gives the tide teeth without touching anyone's garden. **The wall breach** is PLAN.md's own Phase 6 line, "throw the ball at a weak wall to release water". The ball currently has only one effect on shared state (slosh), so this gives the star a new job through the existing owner-emits-edits path. **Squalls** reuse the same scheduler and the same `addWater` API. They make the island change between visits (filled ponds, darker sand), and they follow BotW's rule that weather should rewrite existing systems rather than add new ones ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-makes-chemistry-just-as-important-as-physics/)). **The crate** is the human glue. R.E.P.O. and Chained Together show that a shared, slightly fragile stake plus clumsy bodies produces stories and harmless blame ([Co-Optimus](https://www.co-optimus.com/blog/article-poster/3380/the-game-where-a-piano-is-scarier-than-the-monsters.html); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/action/the-latest-friendship-ruining-co-op-game-on-steam-is-a-punishing-platformer-where-youre-chained-to-your-pals-and-its-about-to-crack-100000-concurrent-players)). Helldivers 2 shows that a single focused shared target works and several split targets fail ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/arrowheads-latest-major-order-is-exploiting-the-helldiver-2-communitys-greatest-weakness-communication)). One crate per king tide is that single target, and the hat inside is personal to each pusher ([DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) principle 6).

The deferrals are deliberate. Rafts are the best next bundle, since they ride exactly the tide this bundle creates. But a host-owned vehicle with seated riders is a second physics-network project on top of a sim project. Glenn Fiedler's ownership model supports it, but it needs its own night ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/networked_physics_in_virtual_reality/)). Salt and mycelium need the garden design the team is redoing by hand. Fire adds a destruction vocabulary that could read as an antagonist other than the world. Tethers need a spring-only fake, because joints across two client authorities rubber-band. Discovery layers (tide-clock secrets, manual pages, split secrets in the style of Animal Well) build naturally on a working tide clock ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/features/interview-how-animal-well-is-using-secrets-and-mysteries-to-be-a-different-kind-of-metroidvania/)). They should come after the clock has been playtested.

## Each layer has one owner: sim, host or cosmetic

Splitting authority correctly is what keeps the bundle from desyncing. The tide, squalls, wetness, slumping and breaches are **shared deterministic sim**: integer modules in `src/sim/` (no three.js, DOM or native trig), driven from `onTick` in a fixed order, seeded with `mulberry32`, and unit-tested for "same inputs, same hash" and sand conservation. The new `wet` layer must go into a registered state's serialize/load/hash, or late joiners and desync recovery break. Keep it sparse in the snapshot because the server caps snapshots at 256 KB ([server/README.md](../../server/README.md)). The breach is an ordinary op: the ball's owner submits `sand.smash` on a hard impact, the host validates power and range, and everyone applies it. The crate is **host-simulated**: cannon-es floats are not reproducible across machines, so it follows the Boogler model. The host runs it with floats and publishes it through presence, and other peers interpolate. The host sums the pushes it sees from players in contact and only moves the crate meaningfully with two or more. This avoids split authority, the approach the social research recommends for carry-together objects. Swells, foam lines, rain streaks, cloud darkening, wet-sand tint, collapse puffs and confetti are **cosmetic**, free to use floats and `Math.random()`, and never read by the sim.

One trap is specific to toggling. If one tab disables the tide and another doesn't, their sea levels diverge, hashes mismatch, and the room loops on resyncs. So prototype flags must be **room-wide**. The host's URL decides, the enabled set travels in a tiny registered state, and clients adopt it on snapshot. Ops stay registered in every build so validation is symmetric, and only their tick effects are gated.

On controls, the bundle needs no new buttons. The tide and squalls are things that happen to you. Breaching uses the existing throw, and pushing the crate is walking. The only optional control, the forecast ghost, takes D-pad right. That leaves View and R3, and Bump (V) still lacks a pad slot ([src/features/bump.ts](../../src/features/bump.ts)). For later bundles that need verbs such as scoop, pour or plant, the scalable answer is a **tool ring**. LB stops meaning "shovel on/off" and instead cycles tools (shovel, bucket, seed pouch). The triggers keep their slots (RT/LT/RB) but change meaning per tool, and View could open the ring on touch. This mirrors how the watering can and seed tool already reuse "use", and it keeps every control pad-playable.

## Overnight risks are performance and tide tuning, not netcode

The largest unknown is CPU. A rising tide wakes many water cells at once, and no frame-time budget is documented. Recent commits are all draw-call trims aimed at iPhone/iPad, so the team is watching the budget closely (a codebase brief at the time, since deleted as stale; see git history). The prompt therefore asks for modest amplitude (about +60 cm normal, +100 cm king tide), slump and erosion work limited to cells near water or recent edits with a hard per-tick cap, and an fps check in the scenario. The second unknown is feel. PLAN.md's open question is whether the tide is "fun or stressful", and there is only one logged playtest ([docs/playtests.md](../playtests.md)). A `?tidefast=1` flag that compresses the cycle lets the user judge the rhythm in minutes, and the toggles let them strip the bundle down to whatever feels good. Finally, the user's main checkout has uncommitted edits (`AGENTS.md`, `package.json`, an untracked `dev/look/perf.ts`). The agent should work in a fresh worktree off `origin/main` and leave those alone. It should push only to `prototype/storm-tide`. The repo's automerge only watches `claude/*` and the pre-push hook only guards `main`, so a `prototype/` branch is never merged automatically.

## Conclusion

The tide works as a forcing function more than as a feature. It is the first thing in Boogle that gives the existing toys (shovel, water, ball) a reason to interact, and it does so without a score, a timer or a gate. Each further system then becomes an input or output of the same water grid. That points to a working rule for future bundles: add only systems that read or write the water and sand layers through ops, and keep everything else cosmetic or host-presence. The morning playtest should answer one question before anything else ships: does a rising tide make friends build together, or make them anxious? The rafts, salt garden and tide-phase secrets are all worth building only if the answer is "build".
