# Construction you can watch

*Research report, 1 November 2026. How shipped games stage construction that players watch and take part in (Tiny Glade, Townscaper, Dorfromantik, Islanders, Timberborn, Anno, Manor Lords, Cities: Skylines, Kingdom Two Crowns, Factorio, Satisfactory, Fortnite and LEGO Fortnite, Valheim, Minecraft, Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Death Stranding 1 and 2, The Sims 4), turned into recommendations for the `?proto=build` prototype ([haul.md](../game-design/haul.md), "Construction you can watch") and the machine's laying run. Builds on [level-design.md](level-design.md) (section 11: Townscaper, Tiny Glade, watching spots), [communal-progression.md](communal-progression.md) (the honk chord) and [rail-as-play.md](rail-as-play.md) without repeating them. Recommendations only; no design decisions.*

**Markers.** **[V]**: the page was fetched and the words match. **[S]**: seen only in a search summary, a wiki, a guide or a review; check before quoting. Boogle numbers come from the code: the hut is 43 pieces ([src/proto/build/blueprint.ts](../../src/proto/build/blueprint.ts): 8 scaffold, 4 sills, 4 posts, 4 plates, 15 planks, a ridge, 4 roof boards, 2 sign posts, a board) dropping 0.15 s apart from 1.8 m ([src/proto/build/index.ts](../../src/proto/build/index.ts)); the helper hut costs 36 logs and a ring takes 2 logs every 0.3 s ([src/player/haul-feel.ts](../../src/player/haul-feel.ts)), so one player fills it in about 5.4 s.

## Summary

1. The games that make building a pleasure tie every small input to an immediate, physical answer: a sound with weight, a piece that lands, a shape that settles. Dorfromantik's rule: the action players do most must feel the most satisfying.
2. Watching works when the build has **phases you can name** (ground, frame, walls, roof, topping out), not one continuous fill. Timberborn, Anno and Manor Lords all stage it; Timberborn even made builds longer so players could watch.
3. Show the finished thing first, as a ghost or hologram (Factorio, Fortnite, Timberborn, LEGO Fortnite). It tells a newcomer what is being built, which Boogle's "telegraph everything" asks for.
4. The player chooses; the system builds the detail (Townscaper, Tiny Glade). Hand-authored kits of parts plus rules beat both pure procedure and per-building baked animation.
5. Sound carries much of the satisfaction: real recorded materials, played loosely and with charm (Tiny Glade), fired on the frame a piece lands (Factorio's sound accents), with many copies culled so it never becomes noise.
6. Building should react to how it is fed: Tiny Glade's roof tiles bounce when a tower rises quickly. Pace can show that more people are helping, without a UI.
7. Construction animation stops being fun when it repeats at scale: Satisfactory and Cities: Skylines players install mods to skip it. Big builds should be watchable; small repeated ones should be quick.
8. Others' contributions are visible as traces in the world (Death Stranding's roads, likes with no gameplay value), not as a ledger.
9. The completion beat is the one place for a camera move or cutscene (Death Stranding's paver, Animal Crossing's overnight reveal); during the build, players keep control.
10. For Boogle, the cheapest wins are a piece rhythm decoupled from the pour, a ghost of the finished hut, landing-synced material sounds that step up a scale, scaffolding that falls as kickable physics, and pourers' arcs that everyone sees.

## Findings by source

### 1. Tiny Glade (Pounce Light, 2024)

- **[V]** Tomasz Stachowiak: it is "tangibly pleasant to watch something being built up in front of your eyes", and much of the work is making interactions satisfying. Anastasia Opara likens the pleasure to the game seeming to pay attention to what you do. Each element (walls, roofs, windows) has its own procedural pipeline for creating, assembling and deforming, all hand-written rules; she calls procedural art "intimate" because she authored every rule. Several features went through five or six iterations from playtests ([80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)).
- **[V]** Sound designer Martin Kvale: most sounds are recorded from real bricks, doors and planks, but not literally: a flipped notebook for roof tiles changing height, fingertips on a table and chess pieces for fences. They tried four sound themes (one made of mouth sounds) and kept the real-world one because hearing materials respond helps players "intuitively understand the texture and weight" of the blocks ([Steam dev diary, "The sounds of Tiny Glade"](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2198150/view/5395937983657175292)).
- **[V]** A small reactive touch from a dev update: raise a tower quickly and a few roof tiles bounce up ([Steam dev update, "Time of Day & Lighting"](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2198150/view/5000716037482996631)). The build responds to the speed of the input, not just its result.

### 2. Townscaper (Oskar Stålberg, 2020)

- **[V]** The player only adds or removes blocks and picks colours; the algorithm picks which hand-made pieces fit and adds decoration (chimneys, benches, rarer "recipes" like gardens). Placing a block can show the block first, with its detail filling in a moment later. About 500 hand-authored tiles make up the kit, which is why a re-skin is costly. Stålberg wants big shapes predictable and small details varied, so players learn the rules ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)).
- **[S]** Reviewers single out the sound: no music, only small plips and plops of pieces falling into the water ([Design Milk](https://design-milk.com/townscaper-architecture-cityscape-pc-game/)).

### 3. Dorfromantik (Toukana, 2021)

- **[V]** Timo Falcke: the interaction the player does the most has to feel as satisfying as possible; tiny animations and sounds add impact, but none may disrupt the calm ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/sparking-joy-through-tile-placement-in-idyllic-village-builder-i-dorfromantik-i-)).
- **[S]** A perfect placement makes the tile jiggle, with a pleasant sound and a "Perfect" label ([Film Stories review](https://filmstories.co.uk/reviews/dorfromantik-review-tile-based-relaxation-game-really-pops/)).

### 4. Islanders (Grizzly Games, 2019)

- **[S]** The score a building would earn shows live while you move it, before placing; once placed, the points fly into your total with a coin clink ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/06/14/islanders); [Into Indie Games](https://intoindiegames.com/reviews/islanders-review/)). The preview and the payoff are the same object moving.

### 5. Timberborn (Mechanistry)

- **[V]** Update 6 gave every building its own construction model, which appears once materials reach an empty site; ordinary buildings get one extra stage before completion; the largest wonders go through five visible stages. The same update lengthened most build times a little so players can "enjoy the view of beavers hammering away". Earlier: grey previews of unfinished structures, and builders who no longer abandon a site halfway ([official wiki, update history](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Functional_Update_History)).

### 6. Anno 1800 and Anno 117 (Ubisoft Mainz)

- **[V]** Monuments are built in stages, each with its own deliveries and workforce: the Amphitheatre has four stages and "changes visually" at each; the devblog advises zooming in to see the detail ([Anno Union, Amphitheatre](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-the-amphitheatre-a-monument-for-the-ages/)). The World's Fair runs from first foundation through several phases to an opening framed as a celebration ([Anno Union, World's Fair](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-welcome-to-the-world-fair/)).
- **[S]** The Airship Hangar has four stages; a Pyramid's phases each grant a buff ([Anno Union archive](https://www.anno-union.com/category/devblog-en/page/9/)). Ordinary Anno buildings appear instantly; staging is kept for landmarks.

### 7. Manor Lords (Slavic Magic)

- **[S]** Villagers carry logs to the plot and put up the timber frame, then walls, then roof, in real time; a 2025 update added visible scaffolding, cranes and workers mixing mortar for advanced buildings ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/manor-lords/advanced-structures); [Digitec](https://www.digitec.ch/en/page/manor-lords-already-incredibly-good-32837)). Reviews cite watching houses go up as a highlight.

### 8. Cities: Skylines 1 and 2 (Colossal Order)

- **[S]** In the first game scaffolding and boards show while a growable builds; at high sim speed or with construction time set to zero (the Plop the Growables mod) buildings just appear ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/1648792158819993246/); [Plop the Growables](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=924884948)). At launch, Skylines 2 players missed construction animation ([Gamewatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/news/cities-skylines-2-footage-finished-spawn-in-mechanics-buildings-)).
- **[V]** A Skylines 2 mod makes buildings rise with their own models, adds procedural scaffolding that follows the footprint, tower cranes on suitable sites, a dirt and concrete stage, and animated dismantling ([ConstructionAnimation README](https://github.com/Ti4goc/ConstructionAnimation)). Scaffolding can be generated from a footprint; it doesn't need authoring per building.

### 9. Kingdom Two Crowns (Noio, Coatsink)

- **[S]** You drop coins on a spot; a scaffold goes up; builders with hammers walk over and hammer it; two builders finish twice as fast ([Kingdom wiki, Wall](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Wall); [Builder](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Builder)). Payment, scaffold and labour are three separate, visible beats.

### 10. Factorio (Wube)

- **[S]** Ghosts are semi-transparent markers of what will be built; construction robots fly supplies from the network and turn ghosts into the real thing, one entity at a time each ([Factorio wiki, Ghost](https://wiki.factorio.com/Ghost); [Construction robot](https://wiki.factorio.com/construction_robot); [FFF #10](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-10)).
- **[V]** Factorio 2.0 added "sound accents": short sounds fired on a particular frame of a machine's animation (the foundry has twelve), which keep audio in sync better than stretching a loop. Aggregation culls or quietens extra copies of the same sound; the player's own building got higher priority ([FFF #396](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-396)).

### 11. Satisfactory (Coffee Stain)

- **[S]** The build gun's hologram is blue when valid, red or yellow when blocked ([official wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Build_Gun)). Placing something plays a build effect with materials flying in; at factory scale players ask to skip or shorten it because it hides the area, and a "No Build Animation" mod removes it ([feedback post](https://questions.satisfactorygame.com/post/61554e7c831c85205234fa5a); [mod](https://ficsit.app/mod/DisableBuildingAnimations)).
- **[V]** For 1.0 the effect changed slightly, some buildings got custom ones, and a continuous line of pieces now plays as one build instead of many ([livestream transcript](https://archive.satisfactory.video/transcriptions/yt-mmdcRl-rIF8/)).

### 12. Fortnite and LEGO Fortnite (Epic)

- **[V]** Choosing a structure and material shows a hologram; pressing build shows the outline at once, then an animation fills the piece in up to full strength ([Game Informer, 2014](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2014/04/28/building-and-crafting-in-fortnite.aspx)).
- **[S]** Wood finishes in about 4 s, stone about 11.5 s, metal about 25 s; structures build faster after the first piece ([Dignitas](https://dignitas.gg/articles/a-beginner-s-guide-to-building-in-fortnite); [Fortnite wiki](https://fortnite.fandom.com/wiki/Building)). Build time is a material's personality.
- **[V]** The LEGO Assembly device (Fortnite Creative): an assembly time (default 1 s), an option to make it a held interaction that stops when you let go, and to reset progress on interruption, a hologram of the assembled prop while disassembled, and an "On Assembled" event for audio, effects or cinematics on completion ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-lego-assembly-devices-in-fortnite-creative)).
- **[S]** In the TT Games LEGO games, holding a button over a pile of bricks makes them hop into a build; a Fast Build upgrade speeds it ([GameFAQs, Skywalker Saga](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps4/265996-lego-star-wars-the-skywalker-saga/faqs/79882/upgrades)).

### 13. Valheim and Minecraft

- **[S]** Valheim colours a piece by structural support, blue through green and yellow to red, and thin support breaks pieces as you build ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/understanding-structural-integrity-valheim)). Stability is shown, not computed out of sight.
- **[S]** Minecraft gives each block a sound set, so placing reads its material ([Mojang bug report](https://bugs-legacy.mojang.com/browse/MCPE-156629)).

### 14. Animal Crossing: New Horizons (Nintendo)

- **[S]** Bridges, shops and Resident Services are built offscreen overnight: a construction fence or tent stands for a day and the finished building is the next morning's reveal; the first shop opens with a small celebration ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Nook's_Cranny); [Animal Crossing wiki, Bridge](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Bridge)). The fence is the telegraph and the reveal is the moment.

### 15. Death Stranding 1 and 2 (Kojima Productions)

- **[S]** Players drop ceramics, metals and chiral crystals into auto-pavers; other players' deposits count towards the same paver; completing one plays a short cutscene of the road being printed ([Death Stranding wiki, Highway](https://deathstranding.fandom.com/wiki/Highway)). Kojima: you can't see other players' faces, but you see their tracks and traces ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/interview/2019/09/16/hideo-kojima-answers-our-questions-about-death-stranding)).
- **[V]** Kojima on the sequel: likes have no gameplay value, players still liked them, some still build highways five years on, so DS2 added monorails for them; the team studied likes data and heatmaps for the Director's Cut ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/05/08/death-stranding-2-an-interview-with-hideo-kojima/)).

### 16. The Sims 4 (Maxis) and juice in general

- **[S]** Build mode music has eight intensity levels per track ([EA](https://www.ea.com/news/behind-the-scenes-with-the-sims-4-audio)): music that tracks how busy the building is.
- **[S]** "Juice It or Lose It" (Jonasson and Purho) layers squash, wobble, particles and sound on every event ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose)); Folmer Kelly's "Don't Juice It or Lose It" warns that polish can cost immersion ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020861/Don-t-Juice-It-or)). Dorfromantik's calm rule is the same warning in practice.

## Patterns across them

- **Three beats per build:** commitment (coins, materials, a placed ghost), work you watch (hammering, pieces, scaffold), a reveal (topping out, the morning after, the cutscene). Kingdom, Timberborn, Anno and Death Stranding all separate them.
- **Ghost first.** The finished shape is shown before work starts (Factorio, Fortnite, Timberborn, LEGO Fortnite, Satisfactory).
- **Phases, not a smooth fill.** Even five-stage wonders are a handful of visible steps; ordinary buildings get one or two.
- **Rules plus a kit.** Hand-made pieces, assembled by code (Townscaper's 500 tiles, Tiny Glade's per-element pipelines, the Skylines mod's footprint scaffolding). Nobody hand-animates each building's construction.
- **Sound on contact, with weight.** Recorded materials, loosely chosen, fired on the landing frame, culled when many.
- **Speed is a dial with a ceiling.** Build longer to be watched (Timberborn) but never so long it hides the world or blocks play (Satisfactory, Skylines complaints).
- **Others show up as traces.** Roads, likes, a second builder making it twice as fast; no ledger in the way.

## What this means for Boogle

Each item is a recommendation for the owner to accept or reject; none is decided. "Smallest prototype" is the least code that answers whether it helps, behind `?proto=build` unless stated.

1. **Give pieces their own rhythm, decoupled from the pour.** Today one player pours the hut's 36 logs in about 5.4 s and 43 pieces queue at 0.15 s, so the whole build blurs past while the player stares at a ring. Play pieces from a queue at a steady beat (try 0.25–0.4 s), let the queue speed up as it grows (Fortnite's faster-after-first) with a cap, and never skip a piece unless the tab is far away. Phase boundaries get a short pause. *Smallest prototype:* three local knobs in the build proto (gap, catch-up factor, max gap) and a motion sheet at each of 0.15, 0.3 and 0.4 s.
2. **Make every handful answer.** Dorfromantik's rule applied to pouring: each `site.stock` should produce a visible and audible answer at the site, even if it is a sill sliding into place, a nail-tap or dust. The hut meets this today (18 pours of 2 logs for 43 pieces, about 2–3 pieces per pour), but a bigger project with more pours than pieces would have silent pours. A rule for the "projects as data" shape: at least one piece, or a small reaction (nail-tap, dust, a plank shifting), per pour; and phases that end on a pour, so the pour that finishes a phase gets the phase beat. *Smallest prototype:* snap each phase's last `at` in [blueprint.ts](../../src/proto/build/blueprint.ts) to a pour boundary and add a tap-and-dust reaction for any pour that releases no piece; compare by feel.
3. **Show the ghost from the start.** Before the first log, draw the finished hut as a faint outline (or the scaffold only, translucent) so a newcomer sees what is coming; each piece fills its ghost when it lands. Principle 7, and every construction game above does it. *Smallest prototype:* a second instanced mesh with all 43 boxes in a translucent node material, hiding each one as its real piece lands.
4. **Name the phases and make the boundary a beat.** Group the hut into four or five phases (scaffold, frame, walls, roof, sign) as Timberborn and Anno do. A phase boundary gets a bigger thump, a dust ring and a short chord; the last piece (the sign board) is topping out. Keep the scaffold tipping over: it is the reveal and it fits slapstick. *Smallest prototype:* a `phase` field per piece in the blueprint and one extra sound and dust burst when a phase completes.
5. **Let the scaffolding fall as physics toys.** When the hut is done, turn the scaffold poles into short-lived Local Rapier bodies that topple, bounce and can be kicked for a few seconds before they sink. That ties construction to principle 2 (physics toys) and gives the reveal a funny, different result each time. *Smallest prototype:* swap the scripted tip for eight dynamic boxes with a 6 s lifetime, Local only.
6. **Sound: material samples, on landing, stepping up a scale.** Replace the synthesised thump with a few recorded or found wood sounds per kind (post, plank, roof board), chosen at random with small pitch variation (Tiny Glade's real-but-playful sources). Fire them on the landing frame, not the spawn (Factorio's accents). Within a phase, step the pitch up a pentatonic scale per piece so progress is audible with eyes closed; the phase boundary resolves the phrase. Cap at a few sounds per site per beat (aggregation). *Smallest prototype:* the existing oscillator thump with a pentatonic step per piece in the phase, then one recorded sample set if that works.
7. **React to the pour's speed, not just its total.** Tiny Glade bounces roof tiles when a tower rises fast. If pieces arrive faster than the beat (several pourers, or a full stack emptied), drop them from higher, with more squash and a brighter sound, so a busy site looks busy. This shows group effort with no UI and serves principle 4. *Smallest prototype:* scale drop height and squash with queue length.
8. **Make each pourer visible to everyone.** Every remote pourer standing in a ring sends arcs of logs from their back to the site (presence already says who carries what; the arcs are Local). The piece their handful pays for flashes their colour on landing. A second different pourer adds a sound layer, a small version of the honk chord ([communal-progression.md](communal-progression.md)). The plaque stays the record; this is the trace (Death Stranding). *Smallest prototype:* Local arcs from any avatar inside a ring to the hut, with no new presence key if the ring position and stack count already imply pouring.
9. **Keep the camera with the player during the build; frame only the reveal.** No cutscene per piece (nobody above does it). While a player stands in a ring, bias the follow camera a little to frame both them and the hut (multi-target framing). On topping out, offer a 1–2 s ease of the framing towards the hut for players within view distance, never taking input away; this is where Death Stranding and Animal Crossing spend their camera. *Smallest prototype:* a framing bias toward the hut's centre while inside a ring, behind a local knob, and a motion sheet with and without it.
10. **Place rings and huts for watching.** The hut should be in the camera's view while you pour: with the camera behind the player facing the ring, the hut wants to sit beyond the ring along the usual approach, not to the side. Each site also wants a spot to watch from (a slope, the court), as level-design.md recommends. *Smallest prototype:* check the three sites' approach directions in a `/look` still from a player standing in each Helper ring; move the hut offset if the hut is out of frame.
11. **Catch-up for late joiners and the time away.** A late joiner or reload now sees the stage snap into place. Instead, replay the last few pieces (or the whole build at speed if short) on first sight, like a time-lapse. For the helper huts' offline freight, play what was banked as an Animal Crossing-style reveal the first time someone walks up: "this happened while you were away" (principle 9). *Smallest prototype:* on a site's first look, queue the last N pieces as drops instead of placing them.
12. **Authored kit, procedural assembly.** Keep the build as data, as it is now: a list of pieces with a kind, a transform, a phase and an `at`. Author the pieces in Blender as a per-era kit (post, beam, plank, roof board, sign) and export named nodes in build order via the asset pipeline. Keep the motion (drop, squash, dust, tip) in code, shared by every building. Generate scaffolding from the footprint (as the Skylines mod does) instead of authoring it per building. Hand-animate only hero moments (opening day). This follows Townscaper and Tiny Glade and avoids baking an animation per building. *Smallest prototype:* one hut exported from Blender with nodes named `03-post-a` and so on, and a loader that turns them into the blueprint table; plus a function that makes scaffold poles and ledgers from a bounding box.
13. **Treat build time as a dial per thing.** Big, rare builds (huts, stations, era landmarks) earn the watchable pace; small, repeated ones (fence runs, the track laid by many sections) should be quick, or play as one build for a line, as Satisfactory moved to, before players ask to skip them. Far-off sites build without drops (a level of detail for construction). *Smallest prototype:* a distance check that skips drops beyond the current hearing range (40 m) and places pieces silently.
14. **The laying run is construction too.** The machine's 45 s crawl can use the same tools: a rhythm (one sleeper per beat, a clack synced to it), phase beats every few metres, a ghost of the rails ahead, a place to stand and watch, and something to do alongside it (ride the machine, kick sleepers). If it still feels like dead time, consider Kingdom's two-builders rule: the run goes faster with more players standing by it, capped at 2x; that changes a lockstep tick and needs its own rules change. *Smallest prototype (Local only):* a sleeper-clack synced to sleeper placement and a ghost of the next 10 m of rails, then a playtest of the run with and without them.
15. **Calm while building, goofy at the end.** Keep piece feedback small and pleasant (Dorfromantik), and spend the slapstick on topping out: the scaffold collapse, a puff, the sign swinging. Too much juice on every piece over a 43-piece build becomes noise.

## Gaps

- No first-hand source was found on Townscaper's block pop animation, Frostpunk's construction visuals, Planet Coaster's placement feel, Valheim's build sounds or the Sims 4 build-mode placement sound; those games appear here only through secondary sources or not at all.
- Tiny Glade's procedural audio showcase is a video ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tSr74_p23c)) and was not watched; it may say how sounds scale with wall length.
- Stålberg's talks ("Beyond Townscapers", SGC 2021; "Organic Towns from Square Tiles", IndieCade Europe 2019) were not watched.
- Nothing above is multiplayer construction watched by several people at the same instant except Fortnite (combat) and Kingdom's local co-op; the group-visibility recommendations (7, 8) are extrapolation and need a playtest.
