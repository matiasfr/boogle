# Together interactions: low-skill synchronous multiplayer for 3-8 friends

Scope: what makes it great when a whole friend group is online at once, in games that need little fine control. Target: Boogle (three.js + cannon-es island, one beach ball, creatures, water, weather, tide, PeerJS host-peer, deterministic shared sim). Boogle already ships some of this: Honk (T) with a 3+ player chord within ~1 s (`src/features/honk.js`), context Ping (G) (`src/features/ping.js`), volleyball Bump (V) (`src/features/bump.js`), and island gifts. A sibling note, `research_notes/Ambitious mechanics for Boogle/social_multiplayer.md`, covers physics networking and game modes in more depth; this note focuses on synchronous group moments, input simplicity and "needs N players" hooks.

Legend used in inferences: **Input** = 1-button / direction-only / precision-needed. **Cost** = Low (cosmetic room message, no shared state), Med (host-stamped op via `src/world/sync.js` or ball-ownership path), High (cross-peer physics constraints between players, new authority rules).

## Physical / emergent co-op and play (Gang Beasts, Human: Fall Flat, TRDS, Wobbly Life, Peak, Super Bunny Man, Moving Out, Overcooked, Fall Guys, Wii Sports)

### Takeaway
The physics-comedy genre runs on a tiny verb set (move, jump, grab/hold, release) applied to the same rules for objects and other players; laughter comes from the gap between intent and floppy result, and from players being able to both help and hinder each other with the same verb. Precision is deliberately low: the challenge shifts from dexterity to communication and timing.

### Cited Findings
- Human: Fall Flat co-op is "an entirely shared experience, for better and for worse": partners can help move items or drive vehicles, but nothing stops one bundling the other "into a wheelie bin and driving it off a cliff"; heavy objects are easier to control "with two pairs of hands"; co-op lets players "reach new places and find new solutions" — [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come)
- Human: Fall Flat's controls map each arm to a trigger/mouse button ("L2 and R2 or mouse buttons"); some achievements reward pure play such as "throwing a speaker through a window" — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come)
- Creator Tomas Sakalauskas saw his son ignore puzzles to parkour and wander, which made him laugh and pushed the game toward being less about watertight puzzles; 8-player co-op and skins came from community feedback, and he said the game becomes more fun when players "add themselves to the experience" — [Fanatical](https://www.fanatical.com/en/blog/human-fall-flat-stumbling-upon-a-physics-based-gem)
- Gang Beasts: each shoulder button controls one arm; tap punches, hold grabs the nearest object, person or surface; grabbing with both hands gives leverage; climbing alternates the grab buttons while holding jump — [Giant Bomb wiki](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Gang_Beasts); PC Gamer calls controls "not one-button simple but intuitive", and notes most matches devolve into frantic button-mashing — [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/gang-beasts-review)
- Gang Beasts' developer (2014) described a climbing mechanic designed to let you recover after being attacked, a taunt tied to comebacks, and planned non-violent options like hugging and hand-holding — [bit-tech interview](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/interview-gang-beasts/1/) (early-access era; may not reflect release)
- Super Bunny Man: a shoulder button grabs objects or your partner; grabbed players can roll together, cross gaps and use boost to launch each other; one player can hoist the other up by the ears. In co-op, if either player falls in a hole or hits spikes, the attempt fails — [FingerGuns review](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2024/11/25/super-bunny-man-review-switch-a-hop-skip-and-a-prayer/); [GameGrin](https://gamegrin.com/articles/so-i-tried-super-bunny-man)
- Peak: unconscious players can be carried, which pauses their death timer; passed-out players can't move, use items or be heard; bandages/first-aid revive; campfire altars revive dead players (the wiki says one-time use; other guides claim no cooldown — conflicting) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/peak-how-to-revive-heal); [peak.wiki.gg](https://peak.wiki.gg/wiki/Climbing)
- Peak's Scout Cannon: an infinite-use cannon locked to an angle; after a fuse delay, the object or Scout inside is launched — [peak.wiki.gg](https://peak.wiki.gg/wiki/Mystical) (exact wiki page uncertain; cited via search snippet)
- Peak was built in a month-long jam in Seoul by Landfall + Aggro Crab, conceived as a "friendslop" co-op hangout game from the start; it reportedly sold 11 million copies on Steam — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-was-meant-to-be-friendslop-game-from-the-start); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/co-op/after-a-few-months-of-work-led-to-11-million-copies-sold-on-steam-peak-devs-embrace-what-many-companies-refuse-to-learn-were-not-going-to-continually-have-a-graph-go-up); studio head: "Why not make a game where the point is to hang out with your friends?" — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/adventure/peak-boss-doesnt-mind-us-calling-it-friendslop-why-not-make-a-game-where-the-point-is-to-hang-out-with-your-friends)
- Moving Out vs Overcooked: Overcooked's progression requires coordination, whereas Moving Out "encourages mayhem" — [Red Bull interview](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/smg-studios-moving-out-interview) (search snippet; page did not render); previews describe the shared arc of calm planning that "devolves into fits of laughter and lots of frantic yelling" — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/113741/moving-out-pax-west-2019-preview-couch-surfers); one preview dubs the subgenre "Confrontational Co-op" — [WellPlayed](https://www.well-played.com.au/moving-out-preview-total-chaos-has-never-been-so-fun/)
- Overcooked: a fan-maintained page attributes to lead designer Phil Duncan the idea of "cooperative chaos" where the challenge is communication, not controls, with a deliberately small button count — [shapes.inc fandom page](https://shapes.inc/fandom/overcooked/author) (weak source; not verified against a primary interview)
- Totally Reliable Delivery Service: ragdoll physics delivery sandbox, up to 4 players, drop-in/drop-out; reviewers liked the comedy but some found the co-op maddening/weak — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/totally-reliable-delivery-service-is-a-ragdoll-physics-game-about-terrible-drivers); [co-op.gg](https://www.co-op.gg/pc/game/totally-reliable-delivery-service); [COGconnected review](https://cogconnected.com/review/working-totally-reliable-delivery-service-review/)
- Fall Guys: one stick and three buttons (jump, dive, grab); reviewers note "immediate simplicity" though the physics take a few rounds — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=361328); [Richer Sounds review](https://www.richersounds.com/blog/Entertainment/game-review-fall-guys-ultimate-knockout/) (search snippets; which review said which line is unconfirmed); concept was "a 100 person Takeshi's Castle"; designers switched from a fixed to a free camera — [WayTooManyGames interview](https://waytoomany.games/2020/08/18/interview-with-anthony-pepper-senior-designer-behind-fall-guys/); grab duration and post-grab stun were reportedly nerfed after beta (via a mirrored article, weak source) — [mirror](https://jira.phpfox.com/p/how-to-grab-in-fall-guys)
- Wii Sports: the goal was something "everyone in the family can relate to", leading to Miis as a stand-in for each family member — [Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/wii_sports/0/1); its purpose was to show how easy the controller was, with depth added via a hidden skill rating and 60+ graded tennis opponents — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2006-10-25-wii-sports-has-depth.html)

### Inferences
Catalogue of transferable mechanics for Boogle (proposals, not cited claims):
1. **Grab-anything (one button, same verb for ball, props and players).** Hold to hold, release to drop; moving while holding drags. Grabbing a friend lets you tow them through water or pull them up a dune. Input: 1-button. Cost: High for player-on-player (two peers own two bodies; needs a "carried" state where the carrier becomes authoritative for the carried player's position, like the ball's claim/epoch path), Low-Med for props.
2. **Carry a downed/soaked friend.** Peak's carry pauses a timer; Boogle has no death, but a "waterlogged / knocked over by a wave" state that a friend can lift you out of (or that ends faster when someone carries you) gives a helping verb with no fail state. Input: 1-button. Cost: High (same as above).
3. **Throw / launch a friend.** Super Bunny Man launch, Peak cannon. A palm-tree catapult or a sand-built ramp the ball and players can ride. A shared fixed "cannon" prop is far cheaper than player-throws-player: the launched player applies the impulse to their own body locally. Input: 1-button to enter, 1 to fire. Cost: Low-Med (local impulse + cosmetic broadcast).
4. **Two-hands heavy object.** Driftwood log, beached whale toy, treasure chest: moves at 0 speed with one grabber, slowly with two, briskly with three. Human: Fall Flat proves "two pairs of hands = control". Input: direction only once grabbed. Cost: Med (host owns the object; each client submits "I'm pushing with dir d" and host sums forces — avoids cross-peer joints).
5. **Ball games with a tiny verb set.** Boogle already has grab/throw and Bump; a "keepy-uppy count" (how many consecutive bumps by different people before the ball touches sand) is a perfect Wii-Sports-grade game: everyone understands it, it scales to 8, and Principle 4 ("different people helping") is literally the scoring rule. Input: 1-button + positioning. Cost: Low (count derived locally from observed touches; host only if it feeds progression).
6. **Comedic failure without loss.** The genre's laugh comes from floppy failure and from helping/hindering with the same verb; Boogle should keep player-vs-player effects to shoves, splashes and ragdoll tumbles (cosmetic and recoverable) and never touch other people's crops (Principle 5). Journey's evidence below shows unconstrained shoving into pits poisoned co-op; Boogle's equivalent risk is shoving someone into deep water repeatedly — make tumbles short and self-righting.
7. **Precision flags.** Gang Beasts-style alternating-arm climbing and Human: Fall Flat two-arm control are precision/coordination-heavy; avoid per-arm controls. Fall Guys' one stick + 3 buttons is about the ceiling for Boogle's pad budget.

### Gaps

- Rocket League-style ball play: not researched in this pass (no sources gathered on its social design).
- No primary Overcooked (Ghost Town Games) interview retrieved; the "cooperative chaos / few buttons" claim rests on a fan page.

## Social expression and communication (Journey, Sky, Webfishing, Animal Crossing, Rec Room/Roblox, Fortnite emotes, Apex pings)

### Takeaway
The best low-skill social tools are wordless, positive-only, context-aware and cheap to spam: Journey's single chirp, Sky's candle/hand-hold/gift rituals and Apex's context ping. Removing competitive power (and names, and HUD) made strangers attend to each other; mutual-consent rituals made bonds feel earned.

### Cited Findings
- Journey has two interactions besides moving: jump and a chirp button that interacts with players and some AI elements; Chen: "I want the interaction to be about exchange of emotions or feelings, rather than exchange of blows." Names are hidden until the credits; no lobby, no HUD — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/); [Destructoid GDC notes](https://www.destructoid.com/journey-inspired-by-world-of-warcraft-loneliness/)
- Chen's motivation: in World of Warcraft, the more people he met, the less connected he felt; he wanted "a world where everyone is the same" — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/journey-inspired-by-world-of-warcraft-loneliness/)
- Journey originally gave items to one player only; because that created competition, they were changed to respawn for the second player — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)
- Journey playtesters spent time shoving each other into a pit; producer Robin Hunicke said the team removed the physics so players couldn't push each other — [blog repost of a 2012 Siliconera interview](https://copyrightisshit.blogspot.com/2012/03/journey-playtesters-kept-offing-each.html) (secondary); an early resource system also made playtesters distrust each other — [Shacknews interview](https://shacknews.com/article/75483/interview-jenova-chen-looks-back-at-journey)
- Journey partners recharge each other's scarf (flight meter) by staying close — [Journey wiki](https://journey.gamepedia.com/Scarf); [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Journey_(2012))
- Sky: hand-holding lets one player give control to another and be guided around the world; new players appear as silhouettes until candles are touched together, unlocking outfits and more actions; gifting acts as a "thank you"; abilities unlock as a relationship deepens. Social play defined as activity "performed near other players, which encourages simulating the thoughts and feelings of others" ("we're talking about empathy"); forced interactions reduce trust; clear goals made players ignore each other, so the team balanced open-ended and goal-oriented play — [Game Developer, John Hughes GDC Summer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/using-emotion-to-drive-social-play-in-i-sky-children-of-the-light-i-)
- Sky: friendship requires both players to light a candle (mutual consent + cost); players on the same bench can chat; hearts thank helpers for flying together or accompanying; hearts buy emotes, fireworks and instruments (expression loop); veteran costumes signal helping rather than spending; social bonds don't affect main progress — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-sky-children-of-the-light-use-altruism-to-motivate-players-to-socialize-in-games-)
- Sky designer Atlas Chen: aim to keep the chaos while making people behave nicely, building on Journey's positive stranger relationship — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/sky-children-of-the-light-interview-inspirations-future-plans-and-more); Sky's social features are incidental and ad hoc; some doors need two players' weight — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/sky-children-of-the-light-is-a-sweet-slightly-awkward-journey/); Sky's message system was inspired by Dark Souls (Chen talk on reducing toxicity) — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/mmo/somehow-dark-souls-was-the-inspiration-for-player-messages-in-the-kindest-mmo-around)
- Webfishing: the developer describes it as designed around being social and hanging out in the camp; fishing is the reason to share space; loop is cast, wait, reel, maybe catch something cool; extras include a playable guitar and chalk drawing — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/heres-an-adorable-cozy-little-multiplayer-chatroom-fishing-game/); [Start Menu](https://www.startmenu.co.uk/home/winter-spectacular-2024-alexandra-day-webfishing); one post says a solo dev expecting ~100 players sold 1.5M in 3 months (unverified) — [Substack](https://betweenmusicandgaming.substack.com/p/webfishings-got-talent)
- Apex ping: one button marks a point, shows a marker for teammates and triggers a character voice line; context-sensitive (item, enemy, location, acknowledge, cancel); Respawn playtested for a month with voice off and fake names to tune it — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/respawn-played-with-muted-mics-to-get-i-apex-legends-i-smart-comms-system-just-right); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/respawn-used-fake-names-and-disabled-voice-chat-to-test-apex-legends-amazing-ping-system); framed by EA as accessibility and a way to avoid unwanted voice chat; EA's patent is pledged free — [ESA / EA](https://www.theesa.com/trust-and-safety/leading-technologies/electronic-arts/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/apex-legends-ping-system-is-a-tiny-miracle-for-fps-teamwork-and-communication)

### Inferences
Catalogue (proposals):
8. **Honk chord → harmony moments.** Boogle's honk already detects 3+ honks in ~1 s. Extend: a sustained "hum" (hold T) whose pitch blends with nearby hummers; when N people hold together, a cosmetic effect grows (shells glow, fireflies gather). Input: 1-button hold. Cost: Low (each client detects locally from received messages, as honk does). Journey's chirp shows one expressive button carries a whole relationship.
9. **Hand-hold / follow-me.** Sky's hand-hold (one leads, one is guided) is a superb low-skill helper for kids or a phone player: hold the button near a friend to link; leader steers, follower's input is optional. Input: 1-button. Cost: Med (follower's client simply steers toward leader's broadcast position; no physics joint needed — avoid cross-peer constraints).
10. **Proximity buffs, Journey-scarf style.** Standing near friends recharges something cosmetic-but-felt (jump height, swim stamina, glow). Rewards gathering without negotiation (Principle 4). Input: none. Cost: Low (local, from remote positions; never feeds shared sim).
11. **Mutual-consent ritual.** Sky's candle touch: two players press the same button near each other within a window to "bond" (unlocks a shared emote, a high-five, a matching scarf colour). Input: 1-button, loose timing. Cost: Low-Med (personal reward stored per player, Principle 6).
12. **Gifts as thanks.** Boogle already has island gifts and pockets; let you toss a fruit/shell to a friend (it arrives as a pickup) — Sky shows thanks-giving is a self-sustaining social loop. Cost: Med (pickup ownership).
13. **Webfishing-style "reason to share space" activities.** Fishing off the pier, guitar/drum on the beach, chalk-in-sand drawing. Sand drawing fits Boogle's editable terrain, but large-scale drawing must go through terrain edits (Med) or be a cosmetic decal layer (Low).
14. **Ping stays the comms backbone.** Ping already exists; adding "acknowledge" (ping a ping) and a "come here" ping that draws a gathering ring would make group events coordinate without chat (Apex testing shows voice-off design is viable).
15. **Removal over moderation.** Journey removed shoving and per-player items; Boogle should make every shared pickup respawn per player or be non-rivalrous, and keep shoves gentle.

### Gaps
- Fortnite emotes: searches returned only SEO-spam pages; no credible design source on why group dancing works. Animal Crossing visit etiquette, Rec Room and Roblox hangout design were not researched with sources in this pass.
- No primary source found on Sky's "shared flight"/wing-light sharing or chat-bench mechanics beyond the Game Developer article's bench mention.

## "Needs N players" mechanics (heavy lifts, multi-person switches, group triggers, synchronized actions, gathering)

### Takeaway
Precedents: Sky's two-player doors, Human: Fall Flat's two-hands-for-heavy-objects, Pokemon GO raids that require multiple people at one place and time, and Boogle's own 3-honk chord. The key lesson from Pokemon GO research is transparency: show how many are needed, where and when, or players fall back to external coordination.

### Cited Findings
- Sky: some puzzles need two players, e.g., a door that needs two players' weight to push open — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/sky-children-of-the-light-is-a-sweet-slightly-awkward-journey/)
- Human: Fall Flat: heavy objects are hard alone and controllable with two — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come)
- Pokemon GO raids require players to physically meet at the same time at a Gym; the game had no in-game features to identify the required number of people, timing or location, so communities organised on Reddit, Facebook, WhatsApp and Discord; the paper (510 survey respondents, 25 interviews) recommends designing for transparency and social affordances — [Bhattacharya et al., CHI 2019](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/pubs/PoGoRaids_CHI2019.pdf)
- Critics argue raid lobbies hide whether others want to raid until they join, making coordination hard — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2022/06/29/massively-on-the-go-pokemon-gos-community-day-is-still-more-about-catching-than-community/); Niantic resisted remote raid passes because its intent is to bring players together in person — [Inven Global](https://invenglobal.com/articles/18314/inducing-trainers-to-go-outside-again-niantic-remote-raid-passes-are-working-in-a-way-we-never-intended)
- Super Bunny Man's co-op relies on one player hoisting or launching the other past obstacles one couldn't clear alone — [FingerGuns](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2024/11/25/super-bunny-man-review-switch-a-hop-skip-and-a-prayer/)

### Inferences
Catalogue (proposals; all should be solvable solo but slowly, or skippable, per Principle 3):
16. **Pressure plates / weigh stones.** A tide gate, sluice or treasure hatch opens with N players standing on it (like Sky's door). Show N as pips that light per person — the CHI raid lesson. Input: walk there. Cost: Med (host counts players in the zone from presence, stamps an op).
17. **Bell / gong ring-together.** A lighthouse bell or shell chimes: any one ring is a nice sound; rings by K different players within a window trigger a big effect (calm the storm early, summon the fish school, launch fireworks). Same pattern as the existing honk chord, but host-detected if it changes shared state. Input: 1-button. Cost: Low if cosmetic; Med if it changes weather/tide.
18. **Group lift / tug.** Heavy log, boulder or beached whale toy: speed scales with distinct pushers (see #4). Tug-of-war rope on the beach as a light competitive variant: two teams, direction-only input + mash. Cost: Med.
19. **Human pyramid / boost.** Standing on a friend's head gives a boost (Super Bunny Man hoist): reach a high palm fruit or the lighthouse ledge. Input: jump. Cost: Med (stacking on a remote body is a contact problem; simplest is a "boost pad" granted when your feet are within a radius of a friend's head).
20. **Ring of friends.** N players standing in a circle (or around a campfire / sandcastle) "complete" it: a ring of light, a dance emote syncs everyone. Input: walk there. Cost: Low-Med.
21. **Synchronized jump / splash.** Everyone jumping into the lagoon within a second makes a giant wave (a cosmetic splash scaled by count; optionally a tiny host-stamped water impulse). Input: 1-button. Cost: Low cosmetic; Med if it pushes shared water (must be integer, host-stamped, deterministic).
22. **Relay / chain.** Ball passed through K distinct players without touching sand (see #5), or a bucket brigade carrying water to a dry plot. Rewards "different people" over grinding (Principle 4).
23. **Design rule from raids:** always show the requirement in-world ("2/3"), where it is (ping it), and when (telegraph), so nobody needs chat.

### Gaps
- No design postmortem found quantifying how many-player gates affect drop-in groups (risk of a gate nobody can open when only 1-2 are on). Sky and Pokemon GO handle this with optional rewards and remote workarounds respectively; Boogle needs a solo fallback (slower, or a creature helper).

## Spontaneous and scheduled events that pull everyone together

### Takeaway
Shared clocks create gatherings: Sky's 10-minute social-light events on a fixed cycle, Animal Crossing's Sunday-night fireworks, Guild Wars 2's dynamic events that scale with whoever is nearby. Events should be telegraphed in-world, short, and joinable mid-way with credit for anyone who helped.

### Cited Findings
- Sky's Grandma's dinner and Polluted Geyser are 10-minute events on a continuous daily cycle (geyser roughly every two hours); players arrive early, Grandma hands out meals every minute, and players light them for wax; community tools (Sky Clock) give countdowns — [steamah guide](https://steamah.com/sky-children-of-the-light-social-light-timed-events-schedule-for-granny-geyser-and-turtle/); [gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/sky-children-of-the-light-daily-time-specific-wax-events-guide.html) (2024 schedule; may have changed; players reported outages in 2024 Steam threads — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2325290/discussions/0/4515506995731586075/?l=czech))
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons' fireworks show runs at 7 p.m. local time every Sunday in August; players can make custom firework patterns from their own designs; Redd's raffle hands out sparklers and party items; the same update added Dream visits to other islands — [Nintendo via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200728005579/en/Nintendo-News-Fireworks-Shows-Dreaming-and-More-Make-Their-Way-to-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons); [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/654282-animal-crossing-new-horizons-firework-displays-how-to-watch-fireworks-shows); [Hypebae](https://hypebae.com/2020/8/animal-crossing-how-to-customize-fireworks-tutorial-new-horizons-acnh-august-festival-update)
- Guild Wars 2 dynamic events scale with the number of players around (most to ~10; big ones like The Shatterer to ~100), even counting non-participants; the goal was to end kill-stealing and camping: more players means more enemies, and the event scales back down if people leave; players are rewarded even if they left before completion — [GW2 wiki](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Event); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/63774/guild-wars-2-dynamic-events)
- Sky's team found clear, compelling goals made players ignore each other — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/using-emotion-to-drive-social-play-in-i-sky-children-of-the-light-i-)

### Inferences
Catalogue (proposals; use seeded RNG + shared time, telegraph 30-60 s ahead per Principle 7):
24. **Sunset ritual.** At in-game sunset, a campfire on the beach lights itself; players sitting around it (crouch near) light lanterns that float up; count of lanterns = distinct players present. A daily communal moment that's a natural screenshot. Input: walk + 1 button. Cost: Low (cosmetic + presence count); can feed communal progression via a host-stamped "lanterns tonight" tally.
25. **Big wave / king tide warning.** Boogle's tide is the antagonist: a telegraphed rogue wave that everyone sandbags together; GW2-style scaling (wave strength vs players present, or rather, needed sandbags scale down when fewer are on). Cost: Med (deterministic shared sim already exists).
26. **Creature visit.** A whale/turtle surfaces offshore for 3 minutes; if 3+ players swim out and touch it, it gives everyone a ride or sprays a fountain. Pop-up "wow" moment. Cost: Med (creature state host-owned, seeded schedule).
27. **Pop-up ball game when enough are on.** When 4+ players are present, driftwood goalposts or a volleyball net washes up; disappears when people leave. Opt-in by walking into it (Principle 3). Cost: Low-Med.
28. **Race on the incoming tide.** A buoy appears; first to swim around it and back rings a bell; everyone gets confetti, the winner gets a cosmetic. Light competition, very low precision. Cost: Low-Med.
29. **Fireworks / meteor shower night.** Shared sky spectacle (scheduled weekly in real time, à la ACNH Sundays), with player-coloured fireworks launched by anyone; Low cost, pure cosmetic.
30. **Photo moment.** A periodic "group photo" prompt (when 3+ players stand in frame near a landmark) snaps a polaroid everyone keeps; emotes sync. Cost: Low (client-side render-to-image).
31. **Credit for anyone who helped.** GW2 rewards even players who left early; Boogle events should credit every distinct participant (Principle 4/6) and never require staying to the end.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on how often scheduled events should occur for small friend groups (Sky/GW2 serve large populations). Boogle with 3-8 friends likely needs events triggered by presence ("enough people here") rather than only wall-clock, but this is inference.
- Destiny public events, Fortnite live events, Sea of Thieves world events were not researched.

## Scaling gracefully from 1 player to many

### Takeaway
Two proven patterns: (a) GW2-style scaling, where difficulty/reward tracks how many people are present and anyone can complete it; (b) Journey/Sky-style optionality, where social play adds richness but never gates progress. Sony pushed Journey to work single-player even though it was designed for co-op.

### Cited Findings
- GW2 events scale up and down with nearby players so "anyone can participate in and complete them, regardless of how many people are in the zone" — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/63774/guild-wars-2-dynamic-events); [GW2 wiki](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Event)

### Inferences
- **Solo-possible, group-faster.** Every N-player gate (#16-#22) needs a solo path: heavy log moves slowly alone; bell ring needs 3 rings by anyone over a longer window when fewer are on (window = f(players online)); a creature (Boogler) can count as a helper. This mirrors GW2 scaling without punishing small sessions.
- **Scale the requirement to presence, not to a fixed number.** E.g., "majority of players online" or min(3, online). Cost: Low (host knows peer count).
- **Diminishing, distinct-player rewards.** Reward the number of *different* contributors (Principle 4), capped, so 8 players feel spectacular but 2 still complete it.
- **Spectacle scales with count, not the difficulty.** Honk chord, synchronized splash, lantern count: more people = bigger cosmetic payoff. Cheapest way to make "everyone on" feel special with no balance risk.
- **Loose coupling.** Never fail a group because one member fumbles (Super Bunny Man's co-op fail rule is the counterexample).

## Design writing (Journey/Sky GDC, physics party design, "toys not games", emergent multiplayer)

### Takeaway
Thatgamecompany's line: strip competitive power, identity markers and UI so players notice each other; give a minimal expressive verb; make bonds mutual and costly enough to mean something; let helping be its own reward. Physics-party designers lean on toys first: Human: Fall Flat pivoted after watching a child ignore puzzles to play.

### Cited Findings
- GDC 2013 "Designing a New Emotional Experience in Journey": prototyping began around the music; goal was a new emotional experience — [Game Developer / GDC Vault](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-)
- Chen: "If I'm in a room online with another player, and I have a gun, what am I going to do?" — removing power makes the other player the focus — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)

### Inferences
- These sources converge with Boogle's DESIGN.md Principles 1 (toys before chores), 4 (automatic cooperation) and 5 (only the world is the antagonist). The strongest addition is Journey's "remove the means" approach to griefing and Sky's "mutual consent" for anything bonding.
- Implementation summary for a peer-hosted browser game: the cheapest high-joy items are presence-scaled cosmetics detected locally from room messages (honk chord pattern: #8, #10, #20, #21 cosmetic, #24, #29, #30). Medium: host-counted gathering triggers and host-owned heavy objects driven by summed client intents (#4, #16-18, #25-28). Expensive: true cross-peer player-to-player physics (carry, throw, grab-a-friend: #1-#3, #19) because two clients own the two bodies; prefer "one side applies to itself" impulses and kinematic follow over joints.
