# A roundhouse and light train management

_Note added 6 November 2026: the owner moved the main-game target to roughly six hours, a rough target rather than a rule, with solo and groups of two or three close together ([decision log](../decisions.md#the-shape-of-the-game)). Read eight-hour figures below as the earlier target._

_Research report, 2 November 2026. A walkable roundhouse, rideable turntable, shared engines and wagons, and the smallest useful management layer for Boogle. Live web research across Sid Meier's Railroads!, A-Train, Mini Metro, Transport Fever 2, OpenTTD, Railway Empire, Factorio, Satisfactory, Dorfromantik, Death Stranding, Animal Crossing, Pikmin and Ratchet & Clank; checked against this checkout's rail prototypes and plans. Recommendations and prototype hypotheses, not an approved feature or a description of shipped gameplay._

**Verification.** **[V]** means fetched primary-source text or inspected repository code. Manuals establish documented mechanics; developer accounts establish their stated reasoning, not measured player satisfaction. **[S]** means community documentation or secondary/search-only evidence. **[I]** means inference or a Boogle proposal. Every proposed dimension, duration, tuning value and scope limit below is a **hypothesis**, not a measured result. No reference game was played, no video was watched and no Boogle prototype was run for this report. The GDC abstract and the thesis abstract were read; neither is treated as a watched talk or a replicated experiment.

## Summary and decision baseline

**[I] Build a place where friends improve and enjoy their railway, with one small configuration choice per line.** The strongest first test is a roundhouse with two distinctive engine displays, a turntable you can ride, and a single choice between a quicker trip and a bigger delivery. Route, stop order, loading, departures and safe train spacing stay automatic. An extra wagon with no meaningful downside is an upgrade, which can be delightful without pretending it is a strategy decision.

Read first: [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), [the decision log](../decisions.md), [research index](README.md), [compounding infrastructure](compounding-infrastructure.md), [maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md), [eight-hour pacing](eight-hour-pacing.md), [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md) and [the game as built](../game-design.md). No principle change is proposed.

**[V] The 2 November owner direction fixes the boundary:** the rail builds the world; camps put goods on the network without routing or quotas; players still haul each segment; train and town numbers benefit the whole room. The first island's connection unlocking a roundhouse is recorded as an **idea, not committed**. This task asks how to test it. Six eras and approximately eight hours are the working campaign direction; the network banks one project while nobody plays. Trains remain ride-only and timetabled, with hub-only fast travel. The accepted crossing reference is roughly 1.2 km at 20 m/s, with much higher speeds for later eras. [Decision log, audit picks and trains](../decisions.md#trains-and-the-world).

Do not inherit older research's removal of segment hauling or eight-era schedule. Maximal rewards' personal engine loans, speed rolls and large collections are **proposals**, not prerequisites. Eight-hour pacing's link counts and minute marks are budgets, not unlock conditions. The owner also found the two-camp choice in `?proto=place` unconvincing: offering two buttons does not establish a decision. ## What the reference games let you decide, and what they automate

### 1. Sid Meier's Railroads!: engine character and a repeating service

**[V]** Its original manual describes choosing destinations and cars at each stop, after which the train repeats that route and loads/unloads automatically. The engine selector is explicitly a **Roundhouse screen**. It recommends the newest engine early, then distinguishes fast light passenger work from hauling heavy cargo economically later. Train Table mode removes money, maintenance costs and competition while retaining railway composition. [Feral edition of the original manual, printed pp. 24–29](https://cdn1.macgamestore.com/d2/manuals/railroads_manual.pdf).

**[I] Transfer:** make engines appealing objects with distinct jobs; let a configured train run itself. Its early “newest is best” advice is an honest upgrade ladder, not deep choice. Boogle can turn the roundhouse screen into bays players walk among. Reject per-stop car manifests, priorities and economic competition. A pretty locomotive selector is only the beginning of a diegetic building, not proof of embodied interaction.

### 2. A-Train: inspect the whole train, not just its locomotive

**[V]** In **A-Train Exp.+**, buying a train exposes capacity, speed, operating costs and allowed car count. More cars increase capacity; speed can be reduced for schedule matching. Those two purchase settings cannot subsequently be changed in that edition. Its city guide explains automatic material production and development dependent on transport, materials and electricity, and advises shortening underused trains to reduce costs. [Artdink, buying trains](https://www.artdink.com/manual/aexpplus/english/train14/train14.html), [city development](https://www.artdink.com/manual/aexpplus/english/about02/about02.html).

**[I] Transfer:** show the completed train's useful outcome, with its length visible in the yard. Do not copy irreversible purchase configuration or operating losses. Once Boogle removes expenses, attaching an available wagon may become unconditionally better; that is a reward, not an optimization puzzle. These mechanics are edition-specific, not claims about every A-Train game.

### 3. Mini Metro: very few editable objects, immediately visible consequences

**[V]** Dinosaur Polo Club's beta notes introduced moving placed locomotives and carriages to other lines/trains. Its later Creative mode supplies unlimited locomotives, carriages and interchanges. The current press kit describes changing demand, network upgrades and separate challenge/creative modes. [Developer's railcar-reallocation notes](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=287980&enddate=1414967942&feed=steam_community_announcements), [Creative mode](https://dinopoloclub.com/2018/11/20/1254/), [press kit](https://dinopoloclub.com/press/mini-metro/).

**[I] Transfer:** players decide where capacity belongs while trains perform the service. A line can be recognized by its stations and symbols rather than a spreadsheet. However, scarce fleet allocation gets much of its urgency from crowding and failure; removing those conditions changes the decision. Boogle should borrow the readable intervention, not manufacture a shortage. The [developer postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-dinosaur-polo-club-s-i-mini-metro-i-) **[V]** also documents balance and interface work exceeding expectations despite deliberately small scope. Minimal presentation is not evidence of cheap balancing.

### 4. Transport Fever 2: the useful comparison is an entire service

**[V]** Players define a line's stop list and assign vehicles; vehicles repeat it. Terminal assignment is initially automatic, while loading rules and alternative terminals can be edited. Train composition shows combined performance: the slowest vehicle limits top speed, power matters on slopes, and excessive length can hurt platform loading. The manual recommends extending trains as traffic grows. [Urban Games' official manual, lines and vehicles](https://wiki.transportfever2.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:linesvehicles).

**[I] Transfer:** preview journey time and deliverable cargo together before fitting anything. Boogle can automate the complete preset service, including platform fit. Its authored network does not need player-authored lines, terminal exceptions, vehicle maintenance or freight filters. A model of realistic power, weight and gradient is unnecessary until two deliberately authored profiles fail to express the intended choice.

### 5. OpenTTD: automate repetition, but recognize how much remains

**[S, project-hosted community manual]** Orders cover destinations and load conditions; shared orders update several vehicles together. Autoreplace changes fleet models without individually rebuilding every vehicle. Timetables can be autofilled and vehicles spaced, but the documentation also explains slack and retuning after speed changes: a faster vehicle can spend its gain waiting for the old timetable. [Orders](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Orders), [replacement](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Replace%20vehicles), [timetables](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Timetable).

**[I] Transfer:** preserve the service when changing the train, and recompute departures so an upgrade has an actual benefit. The player should never repair a timetable after fitting a boiler. This is documentation evidence, not a source audit of the current release. In particular, do not infer that every train is automatically and permanently spaced without setup.

### 6. Railway Empire: optional complexity does not have to be the default

**[V]** The original Railway Empire manual distinguishes fast passenger engines from engines capable of hauling freight uphill, and includes player-edited signals. Railway Empire 2 launched with automatic signalling; update 1.1 added a separate manual mode after player requests. Kalypso explicitly recommends automatic mode and warns that misconfigured manual signals cause blockages. This is **the sequel's** change, not a claim that the first game has the same default. [Original Railway Empire manual, About the game and Controls](https://download.kalypsomedia.com/manuals/Railway-Empire_Manual_PC_UK_ONLINE.pdf), [publisher's update 1.1 explanation](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/railway-empire-2-update-1-1-manual-signals).

**[I] Transfer:** choosing a useful train need not imply choosing signals. Boogle should keep even more operational detail internal. Do not offer an “advanced” signal editor merely because railway enthusiasts might enjoy it: supporting it creates another game and an additional way to obstruct friends. Automation still needs correct physical passing arrangements.

### 7. Factorio: repeated configuration is a cost even in a deep logistics game

**[V]** Wube identifies repetitive refuelling arrangements as a motivation for schedule interrupts. Shared interrupts and train groups avoid editing every schedule. Generic trains can serve a pool of routes, with depot handling when destinations are full. The follow-up introduces wildcard interrupts and station priorities, explaining that a generic pool can otherwise neglect remote mining outposts. [Friday Facts 389](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-389), [Friday Facts 395](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-395).

**[I] Transfer:** the player states an intention once; the system handles repetitions and fairness. Do not expose a little programming language to make Boogle's automatic freight work. A general dispatch pool is also not free simplicity: starvation, reservations and priority policy still need solving. Fixed, protected services are a smaller first version than “all trains go wherever needed.”

### 8. Satisfactory: being on foot does not prevent a management-heavy system

**[V]** Coffee Stain's Update 5 notes describe train stop conditions, load/unload filters, block/path signals and collisions. A single train on its own track works without the shared-track signalling requirement; intersections introduce reservations and potential deadlocks. The player can recover a derailed train by interacting with it, but must fix the cause. [Developer's Update 5 announcement, Trains section](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Satisfactory&appids=526870&enddate=1638304636&feed=steam_community_announcements).

**[I] Transfer:** walkable machines and automated transport can coexist. Borrow seeing the train you assembled run through the place you built. Boogle should automate its route and cargo policy, and exclude collisions, repair and signal configuration. A first-person or third-person viewpoint alone does not make logistics light; the number of maintained rules does.

### 9. Dorfromantik: composition is already a reward

**[V]** The player places and rotates tiles; local quests return more tiles and unlock further possibilities. The developer describes placing demands on the board because intuitive counting was easier to communicate than other criteria, and emphasizes the feel of the most repeated interaction. The train is a landscape/quest element, not a fleet-management precedent. [Official game description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1455840/Dorfromantik/), [Timo Falcke interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/sparking-joy-through-tile-placement-in-idyllic-village-builder-i-dorfromantik-i-).

**[I] Transfer:** an engine rolling out, a carriage coupling, and a formerly empty bay filling can repay a connection before any statistics. Preserve these physical traces. Do not attach a score target to every coupling, or claim a railway economics simulation is necessary to make a town look alive.

### 10. Death Stranding facilities: a memorable place to prepare for the next journey

**[V]** Kojima Productions' Director's Cut guide ties equipment fabrication and new transport tools to deliveries and facility terminals. It describes a support skeleton unlocked through an order, a buddy bot that can carry cargo and take Sam to a facility automatically, and terminal access to other facilities. The player still decides what to bring and how to travel. [Developer's beginner guide](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/en/death-stranding-directors-cut-beginners-guide).

**[I] Transfer:** arrival → visible service → better next expedition. The roundhouse can be a recognizable threshold between building and enjoying the network. Death Stranding's terminals also demonstrate the limit: a physical building can still open many menus. Keep Boogle's benefit near the actual engine and provide a short outcome card; do not import cargo-list administration, repairs or material storage bills.

### 11. Animal Crossing's Resident Services and Pikmin's Onion: one place explains what is possible

**[V]** Nintendo's Animal Crossing support directs the Resident Representative to Tom Nook at Resident Services for next steps and to Isabelle for island evaluation. It also says only the Representative can complete some progression steps on a shared island. [Nintendo support, Portuguese page](https://pt-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/45845). **[I]** Borrow the easily remembered civic address and visible community progress. Reject the privileged player: a Boogle room cannot depend on its first visitor returning to authorize an engine.

**[V]** Nintendo's Pikmin 4 guide explains that matching Onions enable growing a type's numbers, and the take-out/return screen offers an area hint plus a button that automatically chooses a useful squad. [Nintendo's recruit guide, sections 9 and 11](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/pikmin-4-guide-for-fresh-recruits/). **[I]** Borrow a physical home for a collection, a small composition decision and a sensible default. Do not borrow Pikmin loss or a required perfect squad. Neither game eliminates menus; both give them a clear location and purpose.

### 12. Ratchet & Clank upgrade vendors: acquisition, development and transformation

**[V]** PlayStation documents raritanium as a resource allocated to weapon upgrades. Its Rift Apart PC feature explains that using a weapon levels it, unlocks vendor options and eventually changes its form. [Official game guide](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart/), [developer/publisher launch feature](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/07/26/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-is-out-now-on-pc-what-to-expect/).

**[I] Transfer:** give the engine a recognizable before/after and let players try it immediately. Keep the identity of a favourite while it improves. The vendor fantasy is useful; a many-cell upgrade grid is not necessary. In Boogle, deliveries or key connections can unlock a room-wide fitting directly, without personal XP, a shared wallet, or hundreds of tiny stat purchases. The exact vendor animation and timings were not measured here.

## How the building can replace menus

**[V]** Marcus Andrews' developer-authored UI analysis argues for choosing interface techniques around player function rather than insisting on one category. The [Beyond the HUD thesis abstract](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/d5fe6889-4cc6-49c2-ba56-0d759e2f37eb) describes a design space and player-agency guidelines, not a universal result that world-space UI is superior. Dino Ignacio's [GDC 2013 abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017723/Crafting-Destruction-The-Evolution-of) establishes a talk about Dead Space's evolving diegetic interface; no specific talk claim is used. [Andrews, Game UI Discoveries](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-ui-discoveries-what-players-want).

**[I] Hypothesis: the building works when its geometry does the selection and the machine performs the confirmation.** A board bolted to a wall containing seven nested screens merely relocates the menu.

| Player intention            | Physical replacement                                                                                           | Information still needed                                                                                |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| See what the room owns      | Two or three open bays with actual engines; running engines have a named empty berth and live destination sign | Name, available/in service, origin; never a duplicate pretending to be the active engine                |
| Compare engines             | Stand between broadside silhouettes and inspect one                                                            | Two outcomes: next trip duration and crates per departure; a legible local overlay can repeat the plate |
| Choose a line               | Two large destination plaques showing recognizable town/camp icons and route shape                             | Current train and next departure; colour plus shape/name                                                |
| Add a wagon                 | Interact at a large coupling marker; a staged wagon rolls into place                                           | Exact capacity change and any travel-time change, before confirming                                     |
| Fit an upgrade              | Inspect a visibly different boiler/roof/chassis on a rack; one press previews, another commits                 | The actual benefit and whether it applies now or at the next stop                                       |
| Enjoy or show the train     | Walk onto the turntable or around the engine; rotate toward a bay                                              | Snap destinations, motion cue, clear exit path                                                          |
| Understand what friends did | Permanent new engine/module plus a small recent-change plate                                                   | Actual connection of origin, no contributor ranking or claim that an absent visitor performed it        |

Start with a semicircular shed, **three bays** and an approximately **10–12 m turning bridge**, sized to the test locomotive rather than a full passenger/freight consist. Put the passenger platform on a through track **outside the moving bridge**, with the wagon attachment berth beside it. Turn only the locomotive; the fixed passenger deck never has to be removed to change power. These are greybox hypotheses. A huge realistic roundhouse would force long walks, camera obstruction, many storage tracks and a difficult coupling simulation.

The bridge is a solid, rideable platform over a shallow, escapable pit, with a stationary walkable apron around it. Use a generous approach zone and snap-to-bay action; no precise wheel alignment or holding a crank for a minute. Compare a **3–5 s** turn with a shorter turn, at full player scale, standing at the outer edge as well as the centre. Avoid a low roof hiding the train and large fixed foreground beams hiding the prompt. Keep camera control; do not force every visitor into a showcase shot. Occupancy never blocks motion indefinitely, and motion never crushes or traps people. A loose prop is a toy, not a brake on the railway.

**Controls verified against current code:** [hands.ts](../../src/player/hands.ts) uses Interact on **E / X–Square / touch**, consistent with the newer owner decision; AGENTS' older B-button text is stale. Reuse `registerInteractable`, the existing [control registry](../../src/player/controls.ts) and glyphs. Use spatial choice plus a short focus card, not a simulated mouse pointer. Keep normal look/move available; any optional comparison overlay uses the existing cancel/back convention and does not pause the room. Essential text must remain readable at the actual iPad view even when the world sign is oblique. Inspect labels for the engine in focus only; do not place a floating dashboard over every bay.

**Do not make home visits the new chore.** First fitting and collection display belong here; later station plaques can expose the same two configurations, taking effect at the next safe maintenance stop. A return to the roundhouse should be attractive because something new is there, not compulsory before every project. That extension is later than the first prototype.

## The smallest honest decision set

**[I] A real decision needs competing benefits that matter now, a visible consequence and tolerable reversal.** It does not require permanent punishment. Separate strategic allocation, choosing a look and celebrating an unlock; all can be enjoyable, but they are different activities.

| Candidate                    | When it is real                                                                            | When it is false or harmful                                                                                                   | Boogle recommendation                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Which line gets which train? | Two services benefit differently from speed and batch size                                 | One line is unserved without the only engine; one model dominates every route; identical lines make assignment arbitrary      | Choose a service profile on each already functioning line; defer a scarce movable fleet |
| Capacity versus speed        | Visible waiting cargo and repeated rides create competing uses                             | Production cannot fill either train; the town has already banked its allowed project; slow option is below comfortable travel | Two complete profiles, a service floor, truthful whole-trip preview                     |
| One more wagon               | Added mass/length changes a relevant outcome, or it competes for an explicitly useful slot | No downside and no other use for the wagon; “more” does nothing because supply is capped                                      | Celebrate guaranteed additions; test a tradeoff only as one complete Hauler profile     |
| Which upgrade first?         | Two near-term projects offer different useful benefits                                     | A shared coin pile becomes first-come spending; the optimal prerequisite is mandatory                                         | Connection milestones grant upgrades; no upgrade currency in version one                |
| Which engine do I like?      | Silhouette, motion, name and origin make it personal                                       | Rarity colour disguises universally worse performance                                                                         | Admit this is an expressive choice; preserve favourites with era-appropriate capability |
| Dispatch now or wait?        | Timing itself is the intended game                                                         | A friend holds everybody's train; full-load waiting wastes a rider's evening                                                  | Automatic departures, no dispatch job                                                   |

### Capacity needs a bottleneck, speed needs a whole journey

**[I, illustrative arithmetic]** For one two-stop shuttle, use `L` metres each way, cruise `v` m/s, ramp duration `a` seconds at each end, `d` seconds at each stop, and `C` delivered crates per round trip. For a long leg that reaches cruise, the existing [wagon profile](../../src/sim/wagon.ts) has approximately:

```text
oneWayRun = L / v + a
cycleSeconds = 2 * oneWayRun + 2 * d
transportCeilingPerMinute = 60 * C / cycleSeconds
actualRate <= min(productionRate, transportCeilingPerMinute, receivingRate)
```

This is a saturated single-source shuttle calculation, not a general network simulator. It excludes player access, initial waiting and loading beyond the assumed dwell. Using the owner's crossing reference `L = 1200`, and proposed `a = 2`, `d = 6`:

| Configuration       | Cruise | Crates | Run each way | Round trip | Saturated cargo/min |
| ------------------- | -----: | -----: | -----------: | ---------: | ------------------: |
| Guaranteed baseline | 20 m/s |      8 |         62 s |      136 s |                3.53 |
| Swift               | 25 m/s |      8 |         50 s |      112 s |                4.29 |
| Hauler              | 20 m/s |     12 |         62 s |      136 s |                5.29 |

Both candidates improve the baseline. Swift saves **12 seconds of running per crossing**; Hauler delivers **50% more per full departure**, approximately **24% more sustained cargo than Swift** here. A 25% speed increase is not 25% less journey time. At just three crates produced per minute, both carry roughly the same long-term output; capacity is then a false throughput choice. A twelve-crate project with stock ready can instead finish on Hauler's first arrival versus Swift's second. That is a benefit a player can see.

With one train, mean platform wait under uniformly distributed arrivals is approximately half its cycle: **68 versus 56 seconds** here. This is not a measured player average. The calculation exposes a larger problem than top speed: long round trips can make the platform frustrating. The first side-by-side ride test can start at departure to isolate ride feel, but a second test must include a deliberately missed train. Do not add trains or shorten dwell without a safe-spacing design. No player-facing timetable editor follows from that engineering need.

The short local line and the long crossing should have different purposes: supplied construction versus recurring travel. Do not rig demand solely to force the player to select the designer's “correct” engine. A new connection may make an old choice worth reconsidering. Nothing requires constant retuning, daily adjustment or optimal throughput. If everybody picks Swift and never wants Hauler after seeing a full delivery, remove the performance choice and keep the satisfying wagon unlock.

## Make an upgrade large enough to experience

All of this section is **[I]**. An engine upgrade should change **a visible part, an observed journey or delivery, and the next possibility**. This applies [maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md) to the owner's emphasis on shared trains and towns, without adding that report's entire loot system.

- **Show the mechanism.** A bigger boiler is lowered into place, a chassis receives a new set of wheels, or a complete wagon rolls up and couples with a short settle. Use physical assembly, matching the [owner's construction decision](../decisions.md#trains-and-the-world); avoid uniform scale-up. A silhouette should read from the station and from beside another player.
- **Use a familiar comparison.** The next departure crosses the same landmarks sooner. Start by comparing 20 to 25 m/s, then adjust from play; do not jump straight to the 50 m/s late-era reference. Keep the route and camera comparable. More shake or a higher-pitched sound cannot substitute for time actually saved.
- **Give capacity a physical receipt.** Show the extra occupied crate positions, the bigger unloading sequence and a building completing on that arrival. No cargo multiplier applied only to the HUD. Keep room for every player on the passenger deck regardless of freight selection.
- **Separate a milestone upgrade from a sidegrade.** A new era can improve both profiles. “Install the new engine” can be the gratifying irreversible progress; “which configuration suits this line?” can remain reversible. Do not present ten 2% increments as ten meaningful decisions or raise town costs to cancel the improvement.
- **Keep a small number of engine identities.** First compare two silhouettes, not dozens of stat rolls: a compact quick engine and a squat hauling engine. Name and origin can make an individual memorable. The heritage engine remains visible and rideable where it still makes sense; choosing its look should not quietly penalize everyone's necessary transport.
- **Let rarity add identity before exclusivity.** Later, a distinctive finish, unusual exhaust shape or source-themed cab can arrive with a generous reveal and durable provenance. Functional rare traits are a separate test: one bounded shared effect with a guaranteed normal equivalent, no stacking across six players. Do not require a rare roll for a route, a platform slot or acceptable speed.

A useful power fitting discovered by a person must not become power that vanishes when they log out. **Proposed later rule:** discovery adds the functional pattern permanently to the room; the person's name, finish and collectible ownership remain theirs. There is no consumed loan and no transfer of their earned item. This is an alternative to maximal rewards' personal engine loan proposal, not an owner decision. Version one can avoid the question entirely by granting functional engines deterministically to the room and deferring personal train loot.

## Several players, one railway, nobody appointed manager

Everything in this section is **[I]**, shaped by DESIGN rather than proven by a reference game. Animal Crossing supplies a useful warning about privileged progression roles; its permissions are not Boogle's model.

1. **Unlock service, then offer improvement.** Every connected line starts with a functioning default train/service. The roundhouse never becomes an additional approval gate after the connection. Players can all ride, inspect and fit unlocked configurations; nobody needs a driver, mayor, crew leader or minimum group.
2. **Unlock patterns, not a contested inventory of essential engines.** Version one's two engine designs can serve both lines. A line has a stable train identity; choosing a design does not steal the only engine from another line. The yard stores and displays engines and records those in service, but does not simulate a finite commercial rolling-stock market. This deliberately gives up Mini Metro-style scarcity to meet Boogle's automatic-cooperation rule.
3. **Keep the baseline and all destinations.** A configuration cannot remove service, erase cargo, reduce passenger access or undo an earned capability. Optional tuning can change the balance above the baseline. Even this can annoy riders, so changes are announced and take effect only at the home stop; a train already carrying people completes its trip under the advertised configuration.
4. **Commit atomically per line.** Inspection and previews are Local. A request includes the line and expected configuration revision; the first valid server-ordered request commits the pending change. A stale request gets the new state and a readable “changed to Hauler” result, never a silent override or lost item. Different lines can be configured independently.
5. **Make busy mean a short machine action, not player ownership.** One accepted configuration runs its fitting and next complete round trip before another replacement. Do not let a visitor hold a global editor lock while browsing or accumulate an endless command queue. The turntable has its own brief, visible action; it never blocks editing another line. An accepted fitting takes precedence over further toy rotations at its stop, so repeated spinning cannot postpone service indefinitely. No persistent lock survives a disconnect.
6. **Never remove the floor under a rider.** Preserve every car's ID, deck and pose through engine changes. Version one's wagon addition is permanent; the two profiles change speed and automatic load capacity on that fixed consist, not the number of detachable decks. Use a separate locomotive fitting berth and keep timetable transitions continuous. Changing an engine's appearance must also retain its occupied collision surface. Jumping, standing on the boiler and late boarding are implementer checks, not responsibilities for the owner.
7. **Show one public result.** Friends see the same fitted train and subsequent cargo. Credit the connection and contributors without ranking; late joiners inherit the capability immediately and see the lasting object. Snapshots reconstruct state without replaying every past workshop reveal. Offline banking stays at one project; the roundhouse does not choose or complete unseen expeditions.

Equal access cannot promise identical preferences. A per-trip limit and protected baseline prevent rapid disruption, but do not solve every disagreement over the best profile. If casual co-op still becomes a tug-of-war, **remove public performance switching before adding votes, permission tiers or reservations**: retain automatic good configurations plus personal cosmetic selection. Deliberate adversarial moderation is outside this small-room prototype; do not describe revision checks as complete grief prevention.

### Authority, with standard-engine comparisons

**[V]** Unity's Netcode [NetworkVariables documentation](https://mp-docs.dl.it.unity3d.com/netcode/current/basics/networkvariable/) distinguishes current state synchronized to late joiners from one-off RPCs and shows a server-owned door that any client may request to operate. Unreal's [property replication documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/property-replication?application_version=4.27) describes server-to-client properties and notes that intermediate changes need not all be observed. Godot's [high-level multiplayer documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.3/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html) distinguishes authority-only RPCs from any-peer requests and exposes the sender's identity. These are comparable authority concepts, not identical persistence or transport implementations.

**[I] Boogle differs:** persistent fleet configuration is replayed through its existing server-stamped Lockstep edits and saved snapshots, not merely replicated properties or a Host field that resets. Train position remains a function of the shared schedule. The proposed minimum saved facts are unlocked designs/fittings, stable service/train IDs, current configuration and revision, and a pending configuration with an authoritative activation tick/stop. Reuse the service's arrival identity so changing configuration cannot credit the same freight twice. No new socket, transaction database, general job scheduler or economic simulation is justified.

Keep the turntable's shared action descriptor small: start tick, start/end bay and duration; reconstruct motion from it. If it must survive a reconnect/server restart, keep the descriptor in saved rules alongside the yard state. Local rendering and kinematic collision realize that schedule; **physics itself is not Lockstep**. Broadcasting a pose every frame is unnecessary for a fixed scripted turn. [Godot's AnimatableBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animatablebody3d.html) **[V]** similarly documents linear/angular velocity for moving bodies affecting other bodies, but Boogle must verify its own Rapier carry and camera behavior rather than infer it from another engine.

Follow [world rules](../../src/world/rules/README.md) and [DESIGN's state kinds](../../DESIGN.md#pick-how-each-feature-shares-state): validate IDs, eligibility, reach where needed, bounds and revisions; integer quantities; no transcendental or physics math in shared rules. `apply` changes state; `onApplied` begins the show; `onSnapshot` restores quietly. A queued edit does not fit the engine. New implementations are `.ts` despite older `.js` examples in the rule recipe. The eventual rail-service work owns deterministic tests for duplicate delivery, configuration transitions and snapshot round-trips; a co-op scenario is justified for this new sync contract. No feature/UI unit tests.

## Evidence limits

- The sources support different mechanisms, not one proven Boogle recipe. A transport tycoon's whole purpose is optimization; a walkable hub's whole purpose need not be. No study here establishes that two profiles will sustain an eight-hour co-op adventure.
- The clearest primary evidence is Railroads!' engine roles, Artdink's whole-train parameters, Wube's configuration burden, Coffee Stain's signal costs, Nintendo's helpful defaults and access restrictions, and Ratchet's visible transformation. OpenTTD details remain community-manual evidence. A-Train Exp.+ and Railway Empire 2 are explicitly distinguished from their series.
- The GDC and academic abstracts establish relevant work, not detailed findings beyond their text. Mini Metro's local Switch co-op, described by [port developer Radial Games](https://minimetro.radialgames.com/), does not validate persistent online concurrency or a third-person yard.
- The current Boogle platform and timetable seams were read, not exercised. There is no verified turntable, train collection, shared management implementation or device performance result in this task.
- No minute mark for unlocking the roundhouse is settled. “First island connected” needs the implementation brief to name the exact connection ID; it must not silently become “first track segment” or a new two-hour gate. Use the connection completion event, not elapsed time.

## Recommended first version

**[I] A compact workshop with optional two-profile service tuning; no transport-company simulation.** Build this only after the owner accepts the experiment. Do not change the existing plan or tick a playtest box for this report.

**Solo feel spike, approximately 10–15 minutes:** an isolated roundhouse beside a seeded completed connection, one actual train, two visible engine alternatives, one freight-wagon addition, two snap positions and one complete out-and-back ride. A small source/town comparison exposes the difference between 8 and 12 crates. Compare automatic configuration against Swift/Hauler. Label local freight and seeded progress as prototype state. No generic graph, fleet scheduler, personal collection or production save migration is needed to answer whether the machine and decision are enjoyable.

**Smallest shared version if it earns promotion:** two preset, physically nonconflicting lines, each with one stable service and guaranteed baseline. One short supply line and one longer journey give configurations a reason to differ. Two engine designs, reusable on either line; one standard cargo-wagon module; fixed passenger access for the room's full cap of eight. Engine choice and load capacity are **two complete presets**, not independent menus: two settings across two lines give four joint configurations to understand. Swift carries a lighter batch quickly; Hauler fills more of the same available cargo space. The first wagon addition is a permanent upgrade for both profiles, not a repeated attach/detach operation. One guaranteed town/connection milestone adds the fitting; no new currency or harvest bill. The initial three bays are a working display capacity, not a capacity tax or promise to render a large collection.

Keep the turntable **off the operational mainline**. It can turn a parked engine while the other service continues. Applying a change to an active line waits for its home stop, keeps the passenger deck stable and completes a bounded fitting sequence within a tested dwell. Do not route every ordinary departure through the table. The first version has no simultaneous train merging, competing routes across a single-track choke point or extra trains per line. A later extra locomotive would require recomputing conflict-free calls and checking platform wait times; it is not a free “+1” feature.

A shared implementation must prove the once-only delivery/configuration contract above; the Local demo cannot be promoted merely because it looks right in two tabs. Use existing entities, the renderer and the proposed service seam if approved, without building the full rail-slice as a dependency of a research experiment. Open a framed still of the yard and watch a real-time turntable/fit/ride clip; separately exercise simultaneous requests, late joins and reconnects. Preserve the same physical train identity in storage, fitting and service. If this demands a large yard-scheduling system, simplify the presentation to stationary fitting before broadening the runtime.

At the campaign scale, introduce the building once, then at most one new functional fitting/design at a key connection or substantive town change; many cosmetic rewards need not become management tasks. Do not add six roundhouses for six eras or hold back an already-earned era until someone visits home. The old bays and engine become visible history. This fits the [eight-hour budget](eight-hour-pacing.md) and the [maximal reward cadence](maximal-rewards.md) without conflating plentiful presents with plentiful mandatory choices.

**One line on what's new:** “Walk among your engines, turn one on the bridge, and fit the train for a quicker ride or a bigger delivery.”

**One gut question:** “Did improving this train make you want to use the railway again?”

## Explicitly do not build

- **Player signal placement, block/path editors, manual switches, dispatch priorities or timetable editing.** Safe operations are the implementation's responsibility, not another player's job.
- **Per-cargo destinations, quotas, filters, manifests to configure or full-load waits.** Connected camps already supply the network automatically; bounded dwell protects riders.
- **Manual driving, firing, stoking, brake management, realistic coupling alignment or shunting puzzles.** They contradict ride-only transport or turn a quick fitting into specialist work.
- **Derailments, fuel shortages, repairs, wear, theft, sabotage or train-loss states.** Nothing threatens earned infrastructure; funny motion must not damage the room.
- **A shared wallet, bidding, engine resale, debt, salaries, fares, land-value management or operating profit.** Unlock shared capability through the rail loop, without letting one person spend another's earnings.
- **Rare-only functional access, duplicate fusion bills, multi-affix engine loot, per-player power stacking or attendance-dependent loans.** Later rarity needs its own bounded test, not an implicit dependency of the roundhouse.
- **An appointed dispatcher, Resident Representative, whole-room editor lock, mandatory votes or crew-size requirements.** Any friend must be able to contribute, and solo must work.
- **Selling/reassigning the last service, recalling occupied trains mid-trip, detaching occupied cars or charging passenger slots against cargo.** Configuration cannot strand friends or erase their floor.
- **A general routing/traffic AI, freeform yard builder, realistic turntable clearance solver, roundhouse expansion tree or fleet-sized parking simulation.** Two presets and authored geometry do not justify these systems.
- **Hundreds of stored engines rendered at once, bespoke garages for every era, a broad upgrade grid or mandatory return visits per delivery.** Content and inspection costs grow faster than the number of meaningful decisions.
- **Offline stockpiles beyond the owner's one-project cap, auto-selected future connections or an extra progression track to fill eight hours.** More workshop work is not automatically more adventure.

If the capacity/speed tradeoff does not earn its place, retain the roundhouse as a physical upgrade-and-collection toy and automate configuration. That is a valid first version of the owner's idea, not a reason to compensate with signalling, scarcity or bigger bills.
