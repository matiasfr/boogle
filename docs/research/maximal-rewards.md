# Maximal rewards on the railway

> **Verdict:** Gift Freight: delivered cargo builds the town and reveals frequent gifts.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Start with Gift Freight: let delivered cargo build the town and reveal frequent personal gifts. 2. Budget 30–40 finds an hour and hundreds of tactile arrivals, with automatic keeping and grouped reveals. 3. Compose one rising phrase from harvest through pour, construction and unloading, ending in a world response.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [The shape of the game](../decisions.md#the-shape-of-the-game).

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Constant, exuberant payouts; personal uniqueness and useful finds; cargo as a capsule; finite incremental growth across six eras and about eight hours, followed by a toy box. Live web research plus inspection of Boogle's current rail prototype. Recommendations for the owner to react to, not decisions or an implementation plan approved for construction._

**Brief:** make the world extremely generous. The owner wants “handing out a lot of rewards that feel good,” integrated with the mechanics and world. Start from what landed: magnet pickups, construction you watch, and trains substantively growing the town with cargo from rail expansion. The proposed answer is **a railway that repeatedly turns your work into a shower, a changed place, and something recognizably yours**. Useful rare finds are welcome.

**Verification, following [sea-crossings.md](sea-crossings.md).** **[V]** means fetched primary-source text or inspected repository code; it does not mean played or frame-measured. **[S]** means a wiki, guide, secondhand report, or search-only evidence, even when that page was fetched. **[I]** means inference, calculation, or a proposed Boogle value. No reference game was played or timed for this report; GDC slides were read, not the accompanying talks watched. All proposed timing, odds, reward rates, asset quantities and campaign schedules below are **[I]**, unless explicitly marked as current code. “Dopamine” is the owner's feel target, not a claim that this research measured neurochemistry.

## What this adds, and what stays fixed

Read alongside [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), the audit picks and 2 November entries of [decisions.md](../decisions.md), and [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md), especially steps 6–7. The separate [rewards-rarity-gacha report](rewards-rarity-gacha.md) came first. Its recommendation of roughly three or four substantial claims in a 60–90-minute session, and its exclusion of useful random rewards, are too gentle for this brief. Here the starting target is **30–40 personal finds per active hour**, hundreds of satisfying material arrivals, and several memorable shared reveals.

The earlier reports already cover the bolt synthesis recipe, harvest contact, construction, infrastructure, and basic rarity. This report uses [collect-sounds](collect-sounds.md), [harvest-feel](harvest-feel.md), [Ratchet & Clank feel](ratchet-and-clank-feel.md), [compounding infrastructure](compounding-infrastructure.md), and the [game-feel skill](../../.claude/skills/game-feel/SKILL.md) as foundations. Its new work is the orchestration of those pleasures, the payout economy, useful unique objects, and two complete rail-native systems. [Brainrot hooks](0.1/9-brainrot-hooks.md) contributes combinatorial identity and provenance; its old rare odds, contribution luck and prestige reset are not adopted.

- Hands-on work still makes construction materials. Every section still gets hauled; cargo and depots shorten the haul. A gift neither consumes that material nor substitutes for doing the section.
- No money, hazards, decay, shared wallet, required gambling, compulsory mini-game, or minimum party. Ordinary guaranteed tools can finish everything.
- The six eras remain wood, steam, iron/harbour, diesel/concrete, electric, future. Connections advance them; the clock does not. Preserve the old railway and buildings as history.
- Keep the owner's compact bundle, immediate magnet response, approved construction animation, rideable timetabled trains, and later guaranteed traversal unlocks. Do not revive stoking, balance chores, the cut synth groove, or rejected wagon sounds as reward features.
- The one unfinished town project may finish while everyone is away; returning does not claim an unlimited offline factory. No missed-day penalty.

This serves **“The rail builds the world,” “Communal world, personal rewards,” “Telegraph everything,”** and **“Leave something for the next visitor.”** Principle 1, **“Toys before chores,”** still binds: test the shower against the same playable haul with ordinary feedback. A better reward is an amplifier worth testing; it is not evidence that an unpleasant haul has become a good verb.

## Make a payout maximal, beat by beat

**[I] The unit of spectacle should be a phrase, not a popup.** Small objects establish density; a coherent sequence makes that density feel like a windfall. A reward can occupy the world for seconds without occupying the player's input or camera for even one frame.

### A proposed exceptional manifest: 1,800 ms, with a continuing world aftermath

Times begin at the **locally observed accepted arrival**, not at the button press. Transport, authoritative grant and animation are separate. A rare parcel is already earned; its staging cannot revoke it.

| Relative time                      | Picture, sound and meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before arrival, 2–5 s when in view | A sealed crate has an unusual moving silhouette and two small lights. The approaching train identifies a real delivery; no fake roulette strip. The seal signals at least a special result, not an undisclosed chance of nothing.               |
| 0–90 ms                            | The wagon settles; the seal compresses and cracks. A short low body sound anchors the object. Other reward audio ducks 3 dB over 40 ms. Normal movement, camera and harvesting continue.                                                        |
| 90–240 ms                          | Lid lifts; a narrow beam grows from the crate, with a silhouette readable through it. Brightness builds locally rather than flashing the whole screen.                                                                                          |
| 240–780 ms                         | Six to twelve represented pieces follow curved magnet paths, with onset gaps shrinking from 80 toward 40 ms. The first ladder is quantity; no extra inventory item is implied by every sparkle. A modest count sits above the receiving object. |
| 780–1,050 ms                       | The first item turns outward; the train's source stamp appears on its tag. A second trait reveals with a different instrument. For three gifts, disclose all three within this window, not three consecutive full ceremonies.                   |
| 1,050–1,400 ms                     | Final chord, short bloom on the parcel, surrounding residents hop or wave, town module seats into place. A pet jumps onto its perch or a hat appears on the parcel stand. The world confirms what the player received.                          |
| 1,400–1,800 ms                     | Text settles, then leaves the action area. Material receipts and personal ownership were already recorded; the new item stays available. Audio releases over 180 ms.                                                                            |
| Next 8–12 s                        | Pennants, residents and the newly fitted object make the event visible from a passing train. This is a background aftermath, not twelve seconds of confetti or a blocked action.                                                                |

Routine parcels compress to **550–700 ms**. A town step gets **3–4 s** of coordinated construction and celebration; an era ending gets **6–8 s** plus an enduring changed skyline. No repeated 8-second box opening. A screenshot cannot validate this timing: use real-time motion and audio in the eventual prototype.

### How many payouts can coexist without becoming noise?

**[I] Separate four lanes:** contact, accumulation, discovery, transformation. A hit gets tactile contact; a bundle gets a cadence; a new pet gets a reveal; an era gets a world event. Escalate their **shape and consequence**, not the loudness of every sound.

- **One conductor per local view.** Coalesce same-source arrivals within 120 ms into a phrase. Several peers still receive every entitlement, but the shared platform plays one unloading performance. Prioritize transformation, then discovery, then quantity. Keep at most one foreground label; other items remain visibly mounted or enter the collection quietly.
- **A bounded sound ladder.** Reuse the detailed clink recipe in [collect-sounds](collect-sounds.md); add phrase transitions for half-full, full and released. Keep its proposed eight-voice ceiling as a starting limit. At the top, change chord, rhythm or timbre instead of climbing into a squeal. Peggle's phrase boundaries support this distinction; they do not prescribe these Boogle numbers.
- **Overlapping rewards get grouped, never lost.** Three simultaneous rare finds share one 1.8-second burst, three silhouettes and one final chord. At most one major foreground flourish in 8 seconds; subsequent results join the aftermath. This limits presentation, not earning.
- **The ending matters.** Every burst lands, leaves an object or changed structure, and releases the mix. Travel, looking around and arranging a toy naturally give lower-intensity stretches. Do not create artificial dry spells by withholding drops.
- **Number placement expresses causality.** “6 stone” rises from the unloading heap; a house visibly completes; “+4 residents” appears by the new doorway. A personal rarity beam is above its crate. Keep a giant central number for a deliberate era or exceptional manifest, not each sleeper.
- **Comfort is a presentation variant.** Reduced motion removes shake, camera punch and fast beam expansion, replacing them with a steady outline and short chord. Avoid whole-screen flashes. The same quantities, recognizability and generosity remain. The shared [particle pool](../../src/render/particles.ts) currently has 720 slots **[V]**; start with at most 120 live reward particles per local view, then measure, rather than multiplying that by players and crates.

## Uniqueness that people can recognize and use

**[I] A unique identifier is not a unique-looking object.** Use four independent things: a strong base silhouette, two visible trait layers, a modest functional quirk when appropriate, and a true story of acquisition. The default generated name may be funny; the player can rename it without losing its origin.

Example: **Pebble, the Copper-Crested Porter**. Stubby stoneback pet; copper crest; unusually small proportions; carries the last four already-harvested pieces over the final eight metres to a ring. Tag: “Found on the first quarry delivery, Steam era, with Ana and Bo.” No invented calendar date or contribution ranking. If the server does not record a trustworthy date, use the milestone and era, not `Date.now()` inside the simulation.

For an initial grammar, 12 base forms × 6 palettes × 4 trims × 3 approved proportions × 8 quirks gives **6,912 combinations** before names and provenance. That is a theoretical product, not a claim of 6,912 equally distinct assets: compatibility exclusions reduce it, some combinations read alike, and two players can still roll the same look. Do not promise global exclusivity. The promise is a recognizable individual with a personal history. A shiny finish and an excellent passive need not correlate; players can love either.

## Plenty of rewards across a finite game

**[I] Density has three different units:** material contacts, completed payout phrases, and personal finds. Thirty personal finds an hour does not mean thirty new 3D models an hour, nor thirty menu choices. Auto-keep almost everything; aim for only **4–8 voluntary equip or customization decisions an hour**.

Campaign pacing below is a storyboard for a solo player, not a timer or a new map specification. Faster or slower play moves the milestones. Reuse approved route lengths; the newer owner crossing target of roughly a minute at 20 m/s supersedes the older sea-crossing report's shorter recommendation.

The population is a world count, not a demand to simulate 128 elaborate agents. Keep a representative local crowd and show the rest through occupied houses, services and the town sign. These counts need level-art review against the owner's “busy town at most.”

Show **actual cargo delivered**, **actual metres of connected rail**, and **actual residents housed**. Celebrate cargo totals such as 10/50/100/250/500/1,000 and distance thresholds that fit the authored map. Do not multiply the number of real crates because a scoring effect says ×4. Per-era plaques can show the era's achievements beside the cumulative totals. These are records, not spendable resources or quotas.

The prestige-like beat is **a new growth gear with your old work still standing**: town makeover, inherited locomotive, new guaranteed construction/travel capability, fresh visual family, and an immediately reachable next camp. No reset of railway, residents or inventory. Do not put a cosmetic bar between an otherwise completed key connection and its next era.

At hour ten, the campaign is over and the player can deliberately compose a ridiculous train, arrange the town, show an old companion to a new friend, discover fresh combinations, and revisit vistas. Open free recombination of unlocked parts, direct access to baseline reward families, and a voluntary “surprise me” generator at the finale. Procedural surprises can continue; no promise that another hundred hours will unlock the real ending. Keep acquisition provenance distinct from post-finale generated variants.

## Free rolls: exciting mechanics, brief boundaries

**[I] Keep:** guaranteed useful contents, an occasional multiple reveal, source-themed pools, truthful advance rarity cues, category wishes, protection against repeated bases, saved streak protection and an alternative way to choose the appearance you want. The tension can be “which wonderful thing?” and “will there be another?” without a losing result.

**Drop even with no money:** fake near misses, lengthy forced openings, streak loss, timed exclusives, rare requirements, repeat-content energy, tiny stat upgrades that demand constant inspection, and friend-versus-friend luck rankings. They cost time, clarity or trust without making a generous train more interesting. No clinical claim is needed to reject those costs.

Starting draw table: **60% ordinary variant, 30% special variant, 10% wonder**, with a wonder by draw 3 initially and then no more than 5 consecutive non-wonders. The sixth draw is guaranteed if the first five missed. In a continuing sequence, that cap changes the effective wonder rate from 10% to about **21.3%**: expected draws per wonder are `(1 - 0.9^6) / 0.1 = 4.68559`. These are intentionally generous odds. Thirty-two finds therefore support roughly seven big finds, not thirty-two big ceremonies. “Wonder” is an exceptional trait or combination, not a promise of a new base model. Exact combination rarity provides the uniqueness; scarcity does not have to do all the work.

Publish the base odds and guarantee in the collection, including that guarantees alter realized odds. Roll outcome first, then choose an honest reveal. Let a wish category bias the next guaranteed wonder without reducing normal rewards. A new base is preferred until that category's available bases are owned; then give new combinations. Keep probability tables versioned and deterministic.

## One shower together, different things to show

**[I] A train has one manifest and several personal envelopes.** The town gets its material once. Every player present for the credited world deed gets their own finds, whether harvesting, carrying, riding or messing with a toy nearby; no last-hit award or contribution leaderboard. The receipt records eligible server-known roster identities when the deed happens. Leaving before the train arrives preserves the envelope. A spectator entering later sees the same arrival performance but does not create extra cargo or retroactively duplicate every deed.

New arrivals do get guaranteed current-era tools and one welcome selection. Missed era rewards remain claimable once per person, with an honest “welcome to Steam” provenance rather than pretending they built the quarry. Returning players get their already-earned envelopes plus the reveal of the one completed offline project, not a reward per minute absent. Room-local ownership is the first implementable scope; cross-room accounts and identity recovery are separate work, not something browser-local IDs already guarantee.

Shared rare reveals use side-by-side silhouettes or the carriage's display sockets. Friends see the actual equipped hat, pet and train details afterwards; an optional inspect interaction shows its name, useful trait and origin. Uniqueness is comparable as a story, look and function, not a ranked total. Loaning a useful engine helps the room while its identity and ownership remain personal. Public decoration placement must not erase another person's item; start with assigned display sockets, not unrestricted communal rearrangement.

## Deterministic rewards, local exuberance

**[V] Current limits matter.** [`src/proto/net/index.ts`](../../src/proto/net/index.ts) shares laid sites and tuning, but saves cargo piles, delivered counts and town stage in localStorage. Its `land(kind)` updates delivery totals from a crate-animation callback. [`timetable.ts`](../../src/proto/net/timetable.ts) is also local. The demo currently uses 24 logs per section, 6 wood/6 stone for its first two town steps, 1.8 seconds per produced crate and a cruise change from 6.5 to 10 m/s. These are prototype values, not the eight-hour economy. Attaching durable prizes to `land()` would let presentation and reloads affect ownership.

**[I] Production architecture:** persistent receipts, rolls and useful traits are Lockstep; the crate show is Local. A proposed `src/sim/rewards.ts` owns integer weighted draws, duplicate protection and the receipt model; proposed `src/world/rules/rewards.ts` owns validation, saved state and ticks; proposed `src/features/rewards.ts` adapts accepted results to views and effects. Register the rule by appending, and follow [rules/README](../../src/world/rules/README.md). This is a new subsystem, not a change to the meaning of the existing [`gift.take`/`gift.toss`](../../src/world/rules/gifts.ts) toy operations.

1. Accepted section, first delivery or milestone creates a uniquely identified entitlement. Derive recipients from the server-stamped actor/roster and server-known room participation; [`roster.ts`](../../src/world/rules/roster.ts) already uses stamped `e.u`, not a player ID asserted in an edit. New participation facts need an explicit authoritative contract; do not trust a client list.
2. Use [`mulberry32`](../../src/sim/rng.ts) with a stable integer hash of world seed, domain, deed ID, recipient ID, draw ordinal and table version. Pin the hash and draw ordering. Independent per-receipt streams prevent a cosmetic particle or unrelated rule from consuming the next reward. The seed is reproducible, not cryptographically secret.
3. Persist each resulting item, entitlement/reveal state, pity counter, acquired component set and processed source ID. Reject replay of the same source. The stored result survives reload, reconnect and table changes; saving only a seed would let a changed catalogue change an old prize.
4. Cargo manifests and stop/arrival ticks must become authoritative before they grant co-op rewards. In Gift Freight, save the result at the deed and schedule its reveal for a verified delivery. In Wonder Works, reserve the entitlement at the deed, then finalize its item once from the next accepted manifest's source set; a frontier fallback uses the last verified source set. Persist that result before animating. A dormant or offline client receives its completed rewards on snapshot; it need not run an animation to collect them. A delayed train may delay the show, never lose the entitlement.
5. `apply` changes state and returns facts. `onApplied` schedules the local show; `onSnapshot` reconstructs equipment without replaying a room's lifetime of fireworks. A queued `submitEdit` is not a grant. Useful speed changes occur at the next authoritative stop, preserving riders and arrival timing.
6. Test pure roll determinism, duplicate source rejection, pity across save/load, catalogue validation, material conservation, eligibility and reconnect round-trips. A two-client scenario is warranted when this sync contract is implemented. No UI unit tests. Follow the namespace/version rules for mixed builds and include an Atlas story for the new ownership flow.

**Why this architecture, compared with standard engines?** **[V]** Unity exposes restorable [`Random.state`](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Random-state.html); Unreal provides seeded, repeatable [Random Streams](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/random-streams-in-unreal-engine); Godot provides separate seed/state and warns its RNG algorithm is an [implementation detail](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_randomnumbergenerator.html). Those are repeatability tools, not automatic multiplayer ownership. **[I]** Boogle differs by replaying a pinned TypeScript algorithm and receipt rules on server and clients; it should not adopt an engine-global RNG shared with effects. This also avoids serializing the current `mulberry32` closure. Local sound, particles and easing may use floats and local randomness; never use them to decide a prize.

## Two complete systems to react to

Everything below is **[I]**, including timelines. Choose one for a full prototype; do not stack both economies. Both use automatic storage, the odds and ownership rules above, and a guaranteed useful choice at the first town step.

### A. Gift Freight — the town thanks you along the line you built

**Preferred first.** Every completed section awards one personal find; the first cargo from each newly connected camp awards three; a town step awards three; a junction, crossing or era completion awards two each; a first discovery or first win at a mini-game awards one. Different milestones on the same delivery stack their contents into one manifest. Repeating a mini-game remains play; it does not outperform building as an infinite prize farm. No per-hit lottery, additional quest list or spendable ticket wallet.

The entitlement is earned by the deed. Its envelope is attached to the next cargo arrival at town, where the crate opens and the source material visibly constructs something. The return train carries a display of the gifts down the player's line; friends can see or equip them at any stop, with no trip home required. After 90 seconds of active play with an earned but unrevealed envelope, the frontier depot presents it automatically so a slow timetable does not become a reward gate. The train still displays it later without awarding it twice. Before the first working train, the construction machine presents the first section gifts.

The arrival hypothesis is specific: **seeing the cargo that your connection enabled become both a building and your unusual pet makes the next connection desirable.** An unrelated box spawned beside a HUD meter is a useful comparison condition, not equivalent integration.

### B. Wonder Works — your network creates spectacular combinations

This alternative puts more of the reward's identity into **what travelled together**. Use the same deed schedule and 32/36 personal finds in the example hours, but each find is generated from its manifest's source motifs, an owned or starter component and a new rolled trait. Snapshot the selected component when earning the entitlement; resolve the source traits from the authoritative manifest, never the rendered crates. One source is sufficient; two-source combinations emerge as the railway expands, without a manual routing puzzle. Every result is a usable full object plus reusable components, not a fragment waiting for seven more pieces. Retain A's construction-machine presentation before trains run and its 90-second frontier fallback.

At town, a three-step scoring performance counts actual cargo, recognizes up to two source motifs, and activates one optional fitted carriage effect. For example, **6 crates × 10 cheer = 60; two origins add 20; a matching festival carriage doubles 80 to 160 cheer**. Cheer is an automatic, cumulative decoration counter, never a wallet or a construction requirement. Every 100 cheer fills an available decorative socket; after the sockets are full, it animates the existing festival instead of creating unlimited meshes. The six actual crates still count as six and drive ordinary town construction independently. Cap the optional celebration multiplier at ×4; cargo amounts and progress thresholds stay legible.

The personal finds inherit visible motifs: forest + quarry can yield a timber-bodied stoneback pet; harbour + electric can produce a sail-crested lantern carriage. The player may freely copy an owned trait into a favourite at a station. Combination outcomes are shown before deliberate recombination, like an experiment preview; the arrival's new trait remains a surprise. No pet destruction, breeding timer, randomized failure or repeat cost.
