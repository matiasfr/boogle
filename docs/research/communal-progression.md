# Let friends restore the island together

The right shape for Boogle is a **short, finite restoration of the island**. A handful of landmarks start broken: the lighthouse is dark and the windmill sails stand still. Each one wants a few *different* things that friends do anyway while playing: a fruit knocked down by the ball, a shell from the shore, a grown mushroom, three friends ringing a bell together. Once a landmark has what it wants, it changes for good and becomes a new toy. Progress is shown only as world state, never as a score. It belongs to the island, not to any player, so nobody falls behind, and the rewards that stay personal are cosmetic. For the moments when everyone is online, the template is already in the code: the honk chord, which notices when **3+ different players act within about a second**. Generalised into bells, sunset lanterns, multi-pusher crates and passing streaks, it rewards being together with no precise input. Two plumbing gaps come before any of this. First, cross-session persistence lives in the last host's localStorage unless the snapshot server is deployed and `VITE_BOOGLE_SERVER` is set, and neither could be verified. Second, `world.epochMs` is saved but never read, so **nothing on the island changes while nobody hosts**. Taking stock, the ball, the honk and ping, the mushrooms and the sync plumbing are the core worth keeping. The shovel should shrink from four pad slots to one dig verb. The 4,100-line Boogler-and-gifts cluster should stop being a personal visual novel and become the island's shared friend. The storm-tide branch's wet-sand slumping, forecast ghost and island generator should stay parked. The player evidence behind all of this is thin: one group session and one solo impression. So the cuts should go out first behind the existing room-wide prototype flags.

## Progress belongs to the island and shows up as world state

The games that make shared progress feel meaningful without grind share a pattern. Ordinary things go into a visible target in the world, and **the world physically changes** in return. Stardew Valley's Community Center has **6 rooms and 30 bundles**. Each room repairs something specific: the bridge to the quarry, the greenhouse, the minecarts placed across the map, the bus to the desert ([Stardew Valley wiki: Bundles](https://stardewvalley.fandom.com/wiki/Bundles); [ExitLag](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/stardew-valley-bundles/)). In Animal Crossing: New Leaf, a project gets a plot on the map, villagers chip in small daily donations, and the building appears **the next morning** with a ceremony. Only one project can be under construction at a time, and one of them, Club LOL, costs six villager signatures instead of money ([Nookipedia: Public works project](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Public_works_project)). New Horizons' museum starts as a tent after 5 donations and becomes a building after 15 more ([Nookipedia: Museum](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Museum)). Terra Nil makes restoration the whole game and gives it an ending: you recycle your machines and fly away ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/terra-nil-interview-release-date-trailer/)). In every case the reward is a *named change at a named place*, and the player's input is coarse: you hand something over and the game does the building.

The anti-grind levers in these games can be listed and are cheap to copy. The goal sets are small and finite. Goals come from play already happening: New Leaf's station is rebuilt after 100 visits, and its topiaries appear if the town has no weeds on Weeding Day ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Public_works_project)). New Horizons' island rating quietly penalises **more than six of the same item in a 64-square block** and withholds a perfect rating once tree points pass 220, which pushes variety over volume. Its 3-star K.K. Slider concert **keeps recurring even if the rating later drops**, so unlocks never take themselves back ([Nookipedia: Island evaluation](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Island_evaluation)). A Short Hike says the summit needs as few as seven golden feathers; finding more makes the climb easier but is never required ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)). Viva Piñata does progression with no chores at all. A species shows up as a black-and-white visitor once the garden meets its conditions, and turns full colour as a resident when it meets more. The conditions describe the garden, such as plant counts and terrain proportions, not tasks to repeat ([Wikipedia: Viva Piñata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viva_Pi%C3%B1ata_(video_game))). The early ones are trivial; two Whirlms are enough to attract a Sparrowmint ([HEXUS](https://hexus.net/gaming/reviews/xbox-360/7384-viva-pinata-xbox-360/?page=2)).

DESIGN.md's first principle ("Toys before chores") seems to clash with any communal tally. The resolution is to keep counters out of sight. Oskar Stålberg left score out of Townscaper because "when you keep score, you play to the score", and score makes players stop looking at the world they are making ([PC Gamer via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/townscapers-developer-radically-casual-design-191518493.html)). So the host keeps integers, and players see a half-lit lamp, a windmill missing two sails, or a grey gull silhouette on the shore. The second clash is with principle 6, "Communal world, personal rewards". The split is clean. **Progress is the island's**: there are no per-player ladders and no attendance requirements. That avoids Valheim's problem, where a friend who misses a boss night falls behind ([Guild Order wiki](https://guildorder.com/es/games/valheim/wiki/boss-and-biome-progression)), and Lost Garden's warning that splitting friends by progress breaks relationships ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2017/01/27/game-design-patterns-for-building-friendships/)). **Rewards stay personal and cosmetic**: a hat, a scarf colour, your name on a plaque.

Mapped onto Boogle, the patterns rank like this by fit and cost:

| Pattern (precedent) | Boogle form | Shared state | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restoration bundles (Stardew, Terra Nil) | Lighthouse lamp lit in stages, windmills restarted, each wanting 3-5 *different* things, each counted once | A want-bitmask, done tick and contributor names per landmark | None: deliver, throw, or be there |
| Delayed reveal (New Leaf "next morning") | A finished landmark completes at the next session start or after a short host-timed delay, so absent friends see the reveal | One tick per landmark | None |
| Attract by conditions (Viva Piñata) | Gulls, the fish school and fireflies turn from silhouettes into residents when island states hold (deep connected water, N grown mushrooms, a lit lighthouse) | Stage 0/1/2 per species | None: the conditions are world states |
| Communal log with personal credit (ACNH museum, Alba) | An island log of first sightings tied to tide, night and weather, each with the finder's name | A bitset plus first-found tick and name | Low: look and press |
| Positive-only traces (Death Stranding) | A friend's sandcastle or channel can receive a "nice" honk, which only extends how long it glows | Small counts on edits | None |
| Sticky threshold events (ACNH 3-star concert) | Once the island is lively enough, a festival or migration recurs for good | One bit per event | None |

The costs are tiny. Each row is a few hundred bytes of integers in one `registerState`. It is hashed, sent to joiners and saved by the existing plumbing, far under the server's 256 KB snapshot cap ([src/world/sync.ts](../../src/world/sync.ts); [server/README.md](../../server/README.md)). The landmarks already exist. The lighthouse sits on the central hill and its dusk beams already run on the shared clock. The windmill sails are solid and already bat the ball ([src/pois/lighthouse/site.js](../../src/pois/lighthouse/site.js); [src/pois/windmills/index.js](../../src/pois/windmills/index.js)). Making them start dark and still, and come alive through communal play, gives the mainland the reason to exist that the solo playtest found missing ("still sort of empty") ([docs/playtests.md](../playtests.md)). Each finished landmark should leave a **new toy, not a bigger number**: a beam that can light the ball at night, sails turning again to bat the ball, or a bouncier mushroom variety. That keeps principle 2, "The ball stays the star", at the centre of the arc.

## Nothing grows while nobody hosts, and that has to change first

Today the host saves the snapshot (terrain deltas, water, mushroom plots, gift items, prototype mask) every **30 s** to its own localStorage. It writes to the snapshot server only if `VITE_BOOGLE_SERVER` was set at build time. A new host loads the newest of the two copies ([src/world/sync.ts](../../src/world/sync.ts); [src/netplay/net.js](../../src/netplay/net.js)). The PLAN item "Deploy `server/` (Fly.io)" is unchecked, `.env.example` leaves the variable commented out, and the deploy workflow reads it from a repository variable that the research could not confirm is set ([.github/workflows/deploy.yml](../../.github/workflows/deploy.yml)) ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md); [.env.example](../../.env.example)). If it is unset, a different friend hosting next week starts from an empty island and all communal progress is lost. That is the **first thing to check**, before any progression code. The second gap is time. `world.epochMs` is written, serialised and validated, but no system reads it. Growth, fruit regrowth (2,400 ticks, 4 min) and shore wash-ups all count ticks, and ticks only advance while someone hosts ([src/sim/gifts.ts](../../src/sim/gifts.ts); [src/sim/mushrooms.ts](../../src/sim/mushrooms.ts)). Principle 9, "Leave something for the next visitor", is therefore unimplemented: the island is a frozen diorama between sessions.

The fix fits the determinism rules. When the host loads a snapshot, it computes the elapsed wall-clock time once, converts it to an integer tick count, caps it at one to three in-game days, and submits a stamped `world.catchup(n)` op. Every peer then applies a closed-form advance. Growth counters clamp at their target, a capped number of shore wash-ups and fruit ripenings are rolled from the seeded RNG, and attracted species get one check. `Date.now()` enters only as the host's chosen integer, never inside sim code.

The rule for what may change while players are away comes from Animal Crossing's history: **grow while away, never decay while away**. Older Animal Crossing games wilted flowers and grew weeds and rafflesia on absent players' towns ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/what-happens-when-you-abandon-your-animal-crossing-new-1844574997)). New Horizons stopped flowers wilting ([Nookipedia: Flower](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Flower)). Its "your island misses you" emails were still called a guilt trip ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/nintendo-animal-crossing-new-horizons-hiatus-guilt-trip-email/)). So in Boogle, things bloom, ripen and wash up while friends are away. Nothing players made or planted expires. Creatures greet a returning player with delight, never with reproach. The existing expiry of loose fruit and shore items is fine, because it is ambient turnover of free gifts, not loss of anyone's effort.

Asynchronous play also needs visible traces of who did what. Lost Garden argues that because proximity is weak in asynchronous games, they depend more on **strong reciprocation loops** ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2017/01/27/game-design-patterns-for-building-friendships/)). One concept piece on long-distance co-op proposes a sharp test: a player returning after 24 hours should feel grateful to their partner within 60 seconds. It also names the failure mode, an async layer that reads like patch notes ([Idealist](https://buttondown.com/idealist/archive/sandbox-saturday-long-distance-co-op-farmcraft/)). Boogle can pass that test cheaply. Store the room-local name and colour of whoever completed each want and each log entry. Keep a per-room "last seen tick" in the returning player's localStorage (there are no accounts). On join, highlight what changed *in the world*: the newly lit lamp pulses, a new resident gull waves, and a small plaque reads "Day 12 · the windmills turn again · Ana, Leo". Kojima kept Death Stranding's likes worthless and positive-only on purpose ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/kojima-details-the-subtly-social-gameplay-of-i-death-stranding-i-)). Boogle's credit should work the same way: recognition, never currency.

## The honk chord is the template for every together moment

The together-games evidence points at a few cheap rules. Physics comedy runs on a tiny verb set used on objects and friends alike. Human: Fall Flat's designers note that heavy objects are hard to handle alone and become controllable once a second player grabs on ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come)). Sky has doors that need two players' weight ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/sky-children-of-the-light-is-a-sweet-slightly-awkward-journey/)). Guild Wars 2 events scale up and down with whoever is nearby, and reward people who left early, so anyone can finish them ([GW2 wiki](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Event)). Thatgamecompany's lessons are about removal. Journey respawned items for the second player because one-player items bred competition ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)). Sky's team found that **clear, compelling goals made players ignore each other**, and that forced interactions reduced trust ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/using-emotion-to-drive-social-play-in-i-sky-children-of-the-light-i-)). The one study of "needs N people" design the research found, a CHI 2019 paper on Pokémon GO raids (510 survey respondents, 25 interviews), found that when the game hid how many players were needed, and where and when, players moved the coordination to Discord and WhatsApp. The authors call for in-game transparency ([Bhattacharya et al.](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/pubs/PoGoRaids_CHI2019.pdf)).

Boogle's honk already embodies most of this. When honks from **3+ different players** land within about a second, they play as a chord with sparkles over everyone involved ([src/features/honk.js](../../src/features/honk.js); [src/sim/social.ts](../../src/sim/social.ts)). It is the **only "different players together" detector in the codebase**, and it fits principle 4, "Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated". Using it for progression needs one architectural change. Today each client detects the chord locally from room messages, which is fine for sparkles. Once a chord lights a lamp or calms a squall, the host has to detect it and emit a stamped op, or peers will disagree about whether it happened. The same applies to every trigger below that touches shared state. Three design rules also apply to all of them. **Show the requirement in the world** ("2/3" as pips lighting on a stone). Give every gate a **slower solo path**, for example a requirement of `min(3, players online)` or a longer window when fewer friends are on, so a lone visitor at an odd hour is never blocked. And **let the spectacle scale with the head count while the difficulty does not**: eight friends get a huge chord, two still complete it.

| Together moment | Precedent | Input | Authority | Cost | Feeds the island? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lighthouse bell or windmill gong: rings by K different players in a window | Honk chord, Sky doors | One button (existing "use") | Host-detected op when it changes state; local when cosmetic | Low-Med | Yes: lamp stages, sticky festival |
| Sunset lanterns: at in-game dusk a beach fire lights; each friend nearby releases one lantern | Sky's timed social events | Walk + one button | Presence count; host stamps "lanterns tonight" into the log | Low | Yes: a nightly log entry |
| Multi-pusher flotsam crate (0.3 m/s alone, 1.8 with two, 2.2 with three+) | Human: Fall Flat, Palia group nodes | Walk into it | Host-simulated prop (Boogler pattern) | Med, already built on the storm-tide branch | Optional; gives a personal hat |
| Passing streak: the ball touched by N *different* players before it lands makes it glow brighter | Wii Sports' tiny verb set | Positioning + throw or bump | Local count; host only if it feeds a want | Low | One want ("a five-friend rally") |
| Synchronised splash: everyone jumping into the lagoon within a second makes a giant wave | Honk chord | Jump | Cosmetic | Low | No |
| Weigh-stone gate with pips, opening a sluice or hatch | Sky two-player doors, CHI transparency | Stand there | Host counts presence, stamps an op | Med | Yes: a want |
| Hand-hold follow (leader steers, follower's input optional) | Sky hand-holding | One button | Follower steers toward the leader's broadcast position, no joint | Med | No; access for kids and phone players |

Two families stay out for now. Grab-a-friend, carry and throw-a-friend are the funniest physics-party verbs, but in Boogle two clients own the two bodies, so they need a new authority rule, and joints between two client-owned bodies rubber-band. Repeated shoving into deep water is the other risk. Journey's team reportedly removed physical pushing after playtesters kept shoving each other into a pit ([secondary repost of a Siliconera interview](https://copyrightisshit.blogspot.com/2012/03/journey-playtesters-kept-offing-each.html)). Boogle's tumbles should stay short and self-righting. Ping remains the communication backbone. Respawn tuned Apex's ping by playtesting for a month with voice chat off ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/respawn-played-with-muted-mics-to-get-i-apex-legends-i-smart-comms-system-just-right)), which suggests a "come here" ping that draws a gathering ring would let friends converge on a bell or crate without chat.

The passing streak deserves a second look because the codebase already tried a version of it. The keepy-uppy co-op streak was cut "in a design pass" (`6d5c880`) with no recorded playtest reason. Its value is unknown, not disproven. Recast as "how many *different* friends touched it", shown as a glow rather than a number, it turns principle 4 into the scoring rule without becoming a score.

## Keep the ball, shrink the shovel, and make the Boogler everyone's friend

`src/` is about **14,200 lines**. The together engines are the ball with its slapstick layer, the honk chord and ping, and shared sand and water editing. The largest single cluster, **the Boogler plus island gifts at about 4,100 lines (29%)**, runs on personal localStorage progression rather than communal progression (a codebase inventory at the time, since deleted as stale; see git history). The verdicts below follow from the two questions in the brief: does this make the moment better when everyone is online, and can it carry the island's progress without grind or precision?

Nothing on `main` warrants deleting a whole system. The cuts are verbs and sub-features: flatten, the bucket HUD, the shovel toggle, the pocket as a currency, the modal dialogue box, and the friendship ladder's windowed caps and nosiness penalties. On the storm-tide branch, three pieces stay parked (below).

The two hardest calls need saying plainly. **Sand is the only system whose output already persists communally, and it is also the most precision-heavy.** The answer is to keep sand as a toy and stop asking it to carry progression. No landmark should want a particular shape of sand. Simplifying the shovel to one context-sensitive dig frees **lb, lt and rb**, which together with the already-free **view and r3** gives five pad slots. That covers Bump and leaves room for later. The storm-tide branch's forecast collides with Boogler talk on D-pad right, so it gets nothing.

**The Boogler is the second hard call.** It is the biggest investment and a contributor's work (friendship and mushrooms came from grantgliner, `ea29f9f` and `c8a2729`). Its physical half already serves play together. Its talk half is a solo visual novel bolted onto a co-op toy: the dialogue box swallows game input while open ([src/boogler/dialogue.js](../../src/boogler/dialogue.js)), and its points ladder caps pets at 8 per 10 minutes and gifts at 4 per 20 minutes ([src/boogler/friendship.js](../../src/boogler/friendship.js)). Converting the bond to a shared state keeps the character and the writing and changes who they belong to. That conversation should happen with the person who built it.

The storm-tide branch built six mechanics plus extras, and only the picks matter here. It is **+5,236 lines across 62 files** ([PROTOTYPE.md](https://github.com/matiasfr/boogle/blob/prototype/storm-tide/PROTOTYPE.md)). The **telegraphed tide clock** fits as a shared rhythm that costs zero bytes on the wire: a king tide is a natural time for flotsam, which is how wants get supplied. The **multi-pusher crate** is the best built example of automatic co-op with a personal reward. The branch's own caveats argue against the rest. **Wet-sand slumping** makes walls taller than about 65 cm sag "right after" building, which the branch itself says "may feel punishing", and it adds a new hashed per-cell layer. The **forecast ghost** is a solo planning aid that runs a copy of the water sim and takes a button that is already used. The **procedural island generator** runs against the move to a smaller, fixed space, though the solo note did call "the procedural island" cool. **Squalls** are worth merging only if rain waters everyone's mushrooms at once, a follow-up the branch itself suggests. Otherwise they are drama with a sim cost.

## A first slice fits in three small registered states

The order matters because each step depends on the one before it. First, confirm the snapshot server runs and the deploy sets `VITE_BOOGLE_SERVER`. Without that, nothing below survives a change of host. Second, add the catch-up op. It is grow-only, capped, and unit-tested so that the same snapshot plus the same elapsed time gives the same hash. That alone makes the island change between visits. Third, register a `landmarks` state with ops for giving a want and for a host-detected chord. Start with the lighthouse: three or four wants (a ripe fruit knocked down by the ball, a shell, a grown mushroom, a three-friend bell), the dusk beam dark until they are met, and a plaque with contributors' names. Fourth, register the island log (`log.discover`), idempotent and credited by name. The shovel simplification and the Boogler-and-gifts merge can follow in parallel. They go behind prototype flags first, so one evening's play can compare them with the current game. All three new states are tiny integers, testable in `src/sim/` with node's test runner, and add almost nothing to the snapshot.

The playtest questions follow from the design. Did you notice what changed since your last visit, and do you know who did it? Did a chord or bell happen without anyone organising it? Did anyone ignore their friends to chase a want? Sky's team saw clear goals make players ignore each other, so the last answer is the early warning that the wants are too legible or too many. The log should also record who played and how many. The current evidence is two entries: one group session ("looks like it's working") and one solo session by Matias ([docs/playtests.md](../playtests.md)). Lost Garden's key metric for friendship games, getting players from a first co-play session to a second ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2017/01/27/game-design-patterns-for-building-friendships/)), is the right one here. It is also the one the log cannot yet answer.

## Conclusion

This direction works by **making progression read world states rather than player inputs**. That one move removes the fine-control problem without deleting the toys: sand, water and the ball stay as expressive as they are now, because no goal ever asks for a precise shape, only for things to have happened (a fruit fell, three friends rang the bell, fish found deep water). Townscaper's split between a coarse player gesture and a rich system response is the same idea. In Boogle, the island's host-side integers do the system's job. Self-determination research found that autonomy, competence and relatedness each independently predicted enjoyment and future play in online multiplayer ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)). Friends choose what to do (autonomy), the landmark visibly changes (competence), and the plaque names whoever did it (relatedness). Grind is what happens when only the competence leg is kept and fed by repetition.

The arc should also **end**. Terra Nil finishes with the land restored. Stardew's Community Center is about thirty bundles, not a treadmill. A friends-only island with roughly 8-15 wants across three or four landmarks can be "finished" in a few weeks of casual sessions. After that the honk chord, the bell and the ball carry it as a toy box. New arcs can come as rotating seasons that come back later rather than expiring, the way Sky converts seasonal currency and Deep Rock Galactic reopened old seasons ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/mmo/sky-children-of-the-light-seasonal-candles-locations); [GGRecon](https://ggrecon.com/articles/deep-rock-galactic-interview)). For a group of three to five friends, the scarce resource is not content but **being online at the same time**. The progression is mostly a pretext for gathering, and it succeeds if friends come back to see what the others did, and stay because the others are there.
