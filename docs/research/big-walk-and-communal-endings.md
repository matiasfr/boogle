# Research: Big Walk, Untitled Goose Game and communal endings

*Research report, 30 October 2026. What House House (Big Walk, Untitled Goose Game) and Jenova Chen (Journey) have said about communication as play, comedy built from systems, and endings that feel shared. It feeds a goofy story and a communal finale for the Boogle 2.0 concept. 21 sources were fetched and 82 claims extracted; the 25 strongest went through the three-vote adversarial check. 24 survived and 1 was refuted. Tags as in [progression-feel-exploration.md](progression-feel-exploration.md). Proposal: [docs/proposals/story-and-finale.md](../proposals/story-and-finale.md).*

**Sourcing caveat.** Most Big Walk detail comes from secondary press (Kotaku, GamesRadar/Edge, PC Gamer, Game Informer), Wikipedia and fan guides. The launch press release is the only primary House House source for Big Walk. Big Walk launched three months ago, so patches may still change things.

---

## 1. Big Walk

### 1.1 What it is (verified)

- **The game:** a co-op walking-and-talking adventure by House House, made "in cooperation with Panic" ([press kit](https://www.bigwalk.game/presskit/)).
- **Release:** 4 August 2026, on Steam (PC/Mac), PS5 and Switch 2.
- **Players:** 2 to 12, with no single-player mode.
- **Press release:** "It takes around 10-15 hours and at least one friend to see the (first) ending ... Big Walk supports a minimum of 2 and up to 12 players." ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/it-s-time-for-a-big-walk-out-now-on-pc-mac-ps5-and-nintendo-switch-2))
- **[Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/big-walk/a-walk-to-remember)**, 9/10: "You can have up to 12 players in a lobby – puzzles will still be sized for four players – but you cannot play alone."

### 1.2 A short voice range is the core (verified)

- **Press release (primary):** "voices fade across distances, echo down corridors, muffle through walls, and crackle through walkie-talkies."
- **House House kept the range on purpose.** Patch 1.005 raised only the overall volume. In the studio's words: "we consider it an important part of the game's design that you can only hear players who are close to you" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/puzzle/big-walk-patch-addresses-voice-volume-complaints-with-restraint-we-consider-it-an-important-part-of-the-games-design-that-you-can-only-hear-players-who-are-close-to-you/)).
- **[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Walk):** the range is "shorter than many other co-operative games of the same type".

### 1.3 Tools and gestures turn communication into play (verified)

- **Press release:** "Point, wave, and gesture (or just flail around wildly) ... Walkie-talkies, laser pointers, binoculars, megaphones, flares, whiteboards (?), cowbells (??), a big golden head (???)".
- **[PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/puzzle/untitled-goose-game-studio-unpacks-its-new-open-world-co-op-game-big-walk-and-it-looks-like-nothing-else-ive-played-before):** "I can't just bark over my mic for directions: I'll need to use a megaphone, or one of my friends will need to use a flare".
- **Game Informer:** "Soundproof rooms forced our team to figure out how to communicate nonverbally."

### 1.4 Folk games, not aha puzzles (verified, developer quotes in secondary press)

Nico Disseldorp, to [GamesRadar/Edge](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/6-years-after-untitled-goose-games-viral-success-its-devs-seek-solace-in-a-chill-co-op-puzzler-we-were-building-the-game-around-ourselves-as-we-went):
- Less like "contraptions that you're all butting your heads up against until someone has the 'aha' moment... And more like the kinds of social game you might play on a real bush walk – you know, 20 Questions, or Charades ... using the video game level to make a bunch of different little folkgames".
- On influences: "We are drawing from Keep Talking And Nobody Explodes and We Were Here".

Disseldorp, to [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-creators-of-big-walk-break-down-the-goty-contenders-development-puzzles-and-stunning-ending-2000725133):
- "the scale of the interaction was actually the revelatory thing".
- "whenever you can hear something, it's really obvious that other people will be able to hear it too".

A challenge involves some cooperation but "never takes the focus away from the people you're playing with".

### 1.5 Traces left by friends who wander off (verified)

From [GamesRadar/Edge](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/6-years-after-untitled-goose-games-viral-success-its-devs-seek-solace-in-a-chill-co-op-puzzler-we-were-building-the-game-around-ourselves-as-we-went):
- Disseldorp: "They get to play the game they want, and your game is richer for knowing that they're out there somewhere while you're playing."
- Gillespie-Cook: "You're almost tracking them, detective-style."
- The article calls it "user-generated environmental storytelling": lights left on, items dropped.

*Applying this to friends who are offline is our inference.* In Big Walk the wanderers are online in the same session.

### 1.6 The ending is a ritualized goodbye (verified 2-1 for the origin story, 3-0 for the structure)

Disseldorp, to [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-creators-of-big-walk-break-down-the-goty-contenders-development-puzzles-and-stunning-ending-2000725133):
- A playtest group finished a build that had no ending. "It felt like we needed to make the catharsis ourselves." The developers joined, congratulated the players, and watched them swim away and log off one by one.
- "Having this ritualized goodbye as part of the game's ending felt really important ... Something had to happen at the end that felt like it met your accomplishment."
- The alternative, he says, is players logging off when they get bored.

**Structure** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Walk)): there are two endings.
- "The Big Goodbye" is behind a large wall, opened with a black key from a black tower, and leads to a giant black sphere with more puzzles.
- A second ending needs 100% completion: a white key opens a hidden theatre.

**Refuted (0-3), do not use:** that the finale's ritual sound breaks proximity audio so separated players hear it.

## 2. Untitled Goose Game: comedy from systems

### 2.1 Small, mundane stakes (verified)

From the [developer Q&A](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-honk-an-i-untitled-goose-game-i-q-a) (primary):
- "Every now and then during development we'd come up with an idea for something bigger, like mucking up a wedding for example, but we'd always let those ideas go".
- "Anything out of the ordinary is the result of the goose."

### 2.2 Moments first, then systems (verified)

From the Q&A and [Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-) (primary):
- The team "had to work backwards and add systems that could support the moments we wanted": idle loops, reactions, vision cones, remembered positions.
- Disseldorp: "The list was mostly a bunch of slapstick cliches ... we'd go back to the list and see what seemed like it would fit." One example from the list: "Steal someone's glasses, they grope around on the ground for them".
- "it was funnier when the goose tried to steal things when people weren't looking." The humans tidying up after the goose makes the mischief cyclical.

### 2.3 Music as a narrator (verified, secondary)

Dan Golding's adaptive Debussy score switches stems by coarse goose state: silent when idle, low-energy when sneaking, active when chased. It gives players "their very own musical narrator ... bringing personality and humor to each encounter" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/getting-goosey-with-debussy-creating-an-adaptive-score-for-i-untitled-goose-game-i-)).

## 3. Journey: shared, not competitive (verified, secondary)

Jenova Chen at DICE 2013 ([MCV/DEVELOP](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)): "I want the interaction to be about exchange of emotions or feelings, rather than exchange of blows ... Then we have to get rid of the power."

## 4. What the evidence supports

1. **A finale is a ritual that meets the group's achievement** (Big Walk). Otherwise friends just log off.
2. **Keep the world's stakes mundane** and let the players (and The Boogler) be the source of absurdity (Goose).
3. **Imagine the funny moment first, then build the system that produces it.** Keep a list of slapstick clichés to draw from (Goose).
4. **Let music narrate play by coarse states** (Goose).
5. **Communication can be play:** limited reach, tools to reconnect, and folk games like charades instead of aha puzzles (Big Walk).
6. **Friends' traces enrich your game while they're elsewhere** (Big Walk). Extending that to offline friends is our inference.
7. **Remove the power to harm** so that a shared moment is about presence (Journey).
