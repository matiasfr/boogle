# 9. Brainrot hooks: what the 2025–26 tycoon hits do, and what Boogle can take

*Research brief, 30 October 2026. Follows SETTLED.md and briefs 3 and 7 (resource loop: haul stack, pour rings, Track-or-Helper sockets, ~60 items per section). **[V]**: I fetched and read the linked page this session. **[S]**: from a search summary, a fan wiki I couldn't fetch, or a guide that other guides contradict. Fan guides for these games disagree a lot, so every number is a snapshot or a range, never a spec.*

**"Fortnite Droid" is *Star Wars Droid Tycoon***, an island Epic made for the May 2026 Star Wars push (code 7865-8305-9184). You buy blueprints from Jawas, build droids in your own workshop and expand it. It is non-combat, with no stealing. Coverage calls it a reinterpretation of the Steal the Brainrot format, and in late May it sat in Fortnite's top 10 next to *Steal the Brainrot* and *Go Up for Brainrots* [S, [fortnite.gg](https://fortnite.gg/island/7865-8305-9184), [games.gg](https://games.gg/it/news/fortnite-creative-il-genere-che-ha-finalmente-conquistato-i-giocatori/)] [V, [IDC Games](https://idcgames.com/en/fortnite/news/fortnite-transforms-into-a-galactic-workshop-with-droid-tycoon-and-star-wars-style-droid-building-2026-05-04-13-15-12984)]. Of all the hits, it is the closest model for Boogle: build and collect, with no theft.

## 1. Game by game

### Steal a Brainrot (Roblox, 2025)
- **Loop.** Meme characters ("brainrots") walk down a central conveyor. You buy one, it walks to a pedestal in your base and earns cash per second. You buy better ones and repeat. Players start with 100 cash, and each server has 8 bases [V, [allthings.how: stealing & rebirths](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-roblox-efficient-stealing-events-and-rebirths)].
- **Rarity ladder** (one guide's table) [V, [allthings.how: rarities](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-brainrots-rarities-and-prices-full-list/)]:

  | Tier | Cheapest (cost / income per second) | Dearest (cost / income per second) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Common | 25 / 1 | 1.7k / 14 |
  | Rare | 2k / 15–20 | 9.5k / 70 |
  | Epic | 10k / 75 | 45k / 300 |
  | Legendary | 35k / 200 | 340k / 1.8k |
  | Mythic | 300k / 1.4k | 4.8M / 16k |
  | Brainrot God | 6M / 18.5k | 10M / 50k |
  | Secret | 75M / 300k | 200B / 100–200M |
  | OG | 350B / 300M | 500B / up to 350M |

  - Each step costs and pays about **5–10×** the last, and the ladder spans about 10 orders of magnitude.
  - The payback time stays near **100–1,500 s** at every tier (cost ÷ income). Bigger numbers keep the same rhythm.
  - Tiers overlap: the best Epic out-earns the worst Legendary.
  - Guides disagree on how many tiers there are (7, 8 or 9) [S].
- **Mutations** (Gold, Diamond, Rainbow, Galaxy, Candy…) multiply income, from about ×1.25 for Gold to around ×10 at the top. **Traits** stack on top of that [S, [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-legendary-brainrots-earnings-steal-brainrot)].
- **Pity.** On public servers a Legendary spawns about every 5 minutes and a Mythic about every 15. A Brainrot God appears at milestones [V].
- **Stealing is the core social hook.** You walk into a base, grab a brainrot and run. While carrying it you are slowed and can't use items, the owner gets an alert, and one hit sends it home. Bases lock for 30 s on join, then for 60 s on a re-lock, and each rebirth adds 10 s [V].
- **Rebirth** resets your cash and brainrots. It gives a permanent multiplier, a longer lock and more slots; the second rebirth adds a second floor. **Index** (collection-book) bonuses are permanent [V]. Guides list 15–17 rebirth levels, with conflicting costs [S, [Beebom](https://beebom.com/steal-a-brainrot-rebirth-guide-levels-and-rewards/)].
- **Events** are rituals players perform (three players in a triangle with one brainrot start a Galactic event) and concerts [V].
- Peak load: Roblox hit 47M concurrent users, with Steal a Brainrot above 15M [S, [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2563322/roblox-breaks-concurrent-user-record-after-grow-a-garden-and-steal-a-brainrot-player-surge?amp=1)].

### Steal the Brainrot (Fortnite UEFN)
- A licensed port of the same loop by FeRinS (3225-0366-8885). It won Best Overall Experience at the 2026 UEFN Community Awards [S, [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/fortnite/uefn-community-awards-2026-results/)].
- **The day after** Epic enabled in-island purchases (9 January), it added "Present Rot" bundles of random rewards at up to 4,900 V-Bucks (about $35–40), plus a **100 V-Buck roulette wheel** with single-digit odds on "Secret" rots [V, [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Fortnite-players-slam-Steal-the-Brainrot-microtransactions-as-gambling.1205005.0.html)].
- Epic's rules allow random items priced between 50 and 5,000 V-Bucks, provided the odds are shown. Players called it a casino aimed at children [V].

### Grow a Garden (Roblox, 2025)
- **Loop.** Buy seeds, plant them and sell the harvest. Crops keep growing while you're offline [S, [News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/07/30/millions-are-flocking-to-grow-virtual-gardens-in-viral-roblox-game-created-by-teenager/)]. It peaked at about 21.3–21.6M concurrent players, a record for any game [S, [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/robloxs-grow-a-garden-hits-industry-record-213m-concurrent-users-surpassing-pubg-fortnite-and-more)].
- **The seed shop restocks about every 5 minutes**, and Robux skips the wait. Common seeds are almost always in stock; Legendary to Divine seeds are rare [S, [Gamezebo](https://gamezebo.com/walkthroughs/grow-a-garden-seeds)].
- **Mutations are the rarity engine** [V, [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/grow-a-garden-mutations)]:
  - Gold ×20 at a **1%** natural chance; Rainbow ×50 at **0.1%**.
  - Wet ×2 from rain or sprinklers; Moonlit ×2 at night; Shocked ×100 in thunderstorms.
  - Some combine: Celestial + Aurora → Cosmic ×240.
  - Multipliers multiply each other, and the wiki's maximum is ×201,050. Gold and Rainbow exclude each other.
  - **Weather is the main mutation source**, so the sky itself is the slot machine.

### 99 Nights in the Forest (Roblox, 2025)
- Co-op survival. Each run you gather, upgrade the campfire (a bigger safe zone, less fog) and survive the nights.
- **Meta-progression:** Diamonds, from chests, strongholds and late nights, buy **classes** (about 10–600 Diamonds) and class talents. Diamonds are farmable without paying [S, [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/99-nights-in-the-forest-classes-all), [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-classes-99-nights-forest-costs-perks-upgrades)].
- **Lesson:** co-op runs plus permanent personal unlocks between runs. Your class is your visible identity.

### Pet Simulator 99 (Roblox)
- You break objects for coins, hatch eggs and take a rebirth multiplier.
- **Ladder:** Normal → Golden → Rainbow → Huge → Titanic → Gargantuan, plus Shiny variants [V, [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/pet-simulator-99-inside-roblox-s-most-aggressive-monetization-machine)]. Fan guides put a base Huge at about 1 in 2M per egg [S].
- **Monetization** [V, RoWatcher]:
  - Luck passes: 275 Robux for 200% hatch luck, up to 3,250 Robux for "Huge Hunter".
  - Exclusive eggs at about 400 Robux.
  - Weekly packs that get dearer as you progress.
  - Event pets that are gone for good after events about every two weeks, and deliberate power creep.
- A parent's guide calls it "gambling-lite" [S, [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/parent-s-guide-to-roblox-pet-simulator-99)].
- **This is the anti-model.**

### Adopt Me (Roblox)
- **Neon = 4 fully grown copies of the same pet** combined at the Neon Cave. **Mega Neon = 4 Neons**, so 16 originals [S, [adoptmevalues](https://www.adoptmevalues.app/blog/neon-and-mega-neon-pets-explained), [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/roblox/how-to-make-neon-pet-adopt-me/)].
- Rarity here is **made by effort, not luck**: each step multiplies the work. Trading drives value.
- **Lesson:** this is the healthiest rarity ladder in the genre, because it is deterministic, visible (pets glow) and can't be gambled.

### Bee Swarm Simulator (Roblox)
- Your hive is a visible grid of bees, so it is your display case.
- **Royal Jelly** rerolls a bee at about 70% Rare / 27% Epic / 3% Legendary / 0.004% (1 in 25,000) Mythic, with a 0.4% chance (1 in 250) of **Gifted**, a variant with a permanent bonus [S, [BSS wiki](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Royal_Jelly), fetch blocked].
- Gifted bees also raise hive-wide bonuses, so variants matter collectively, not just one by one.

### Cookie Clicker (incremental classic)
- A **golden cookie** appears every 300–900 s (about 7.5 min on average) and stays for 13 s [V, [wiki.gg](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Golden_Cookie)]:
  - Frenzy (about 40%): ×7 production for 77 s.
  - Lucky (about 40%): a lump of cash.
  - Click Frenzy (about 3%): ×777 per click.
- This is the ambient "lucky moment" pattern: rare, short, joyful, and it costs nothing to miss.

### Melvor Idle (incremental classic)
- Every skill has a pet. The chance per action is (action time in s × skill level) ÷ 25,000,000 [S, [Melvor test wiki](https://testwiki.melvoridle.com/w/Pets)].
- **Lesson:** **luck grows with rank and with time spent**, so long play is rewarded without paying. A pet is a badge of dedication.

### Fortnite tycoons generally
- Tycoons, simulators and survival are UEFN's proven genres for long, repeatable sessions [S, [UEFN Central](https://uefncentral.com/blog/how-to-make-money-fortnite-creative-2026)].
- I found nothing specific on **LEGO Fortnite tycoons**, so this brief doesn't cover them.

## 2. The common hook anatomy

```
   ┌──────────── EARN (passive income per second, offline growth) ────────────┐
   ▼                                                                           │
 BUY / ROLL ──► rarity tier + variant roll ──► DISPLAY (pedestal, hive,       │
 (conveyor,      (Common…Secret; Gold 1%,       garden, pet at heel) ──► ─────┘
  shop, egg)      Rainbow 0.1%)                       │
   │                                                  ├─► SOCIAL: others see it (steal / trade / visit)
   │                                                  └─► INDEX: collection book, permanent bonus
   ▼
 PRESTIGE (rebirth): reset cash and units → permanent ×, more slots, new floor
```

The numbers that make it feel good:

1. **A constant payback rhythm.** Each tier costs and pays 5–10× more, but cost ÷ income stays at minutes. A new number always arrives in about 2–10 minutes.
2. **Overlapping tiers.** The best of one tier beats the worst of the next, so every purchase is a small decision rather than a fixed march.
3. **Long-odds variants on top of the tiers.** 1% Gold and 0.1% Rainbow, multiplying each other, give a second, separate jackpot axis.
4. **Pity.** A Legendary every about 5 minutes and a Mythic every about 15 means a dry spell has a ceiling.
5. **Visible display.** Rare things sit where others walk past them. Most of a rarity's value is being seen.
6. **A permanent collection bonus.** The Index, Melvor's pets and Bee Swarm's gifted bonuses all mean collecting the set is its own progress, separate from income.
7. **Prestige.** Rebirth turns "I'm rich" into "I'm faster from now on" and a new floor to fill.
8. **Weather and ritual events.** Rarity tied to world conditions, which players read and chase together.

## 3. Healthy vs exploitative

Boogle has no monetization, friends-only rooms and DESIGN.md principles 1–10. With no money in the game, the gambling vector itself is gone: Zendle & Cairns found loot-box *spending* linked to problem-gambling severity (n = 7,422, η² = 0.054, replicated at η² = 0.051) [S, search summary of the abstracts; [PLOS ONE 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/139116/), [2019](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0213194)]. Several of the hooks are still exploitative **without money**, though.

**Take:**
- Tier ladders with visible display.
- Low-odds cosmetic variants that come from ordinary play.
- **Count-based** pity.
- Deterministic combine ladders (Adopt Me).
- A collection book with a permanent, small bonus.
- Luck that scales with rank (Melvor).
- Visiting and admiring.
- Lucky moments that cost nothing to miss (golden cookie).
- Weather and ritual events, as long as they are tick-based and never limited-time.
- Prestige as a fresh, faster run.

**Never take:**
- **Stealing, raids and base alerts.** They are Steal a Brainrot's core, and DESIGN.md principle 5 forbids them outright ("Players can't destroy what other players made… *Avoid:* stealing").
- **Restock timers, wall-clock pity, limited-time and event-exclusive items.** SETTLED forbids timers, and "gone forever" is FOMO even without money.
- **Paid or paid-for luck**, and any "roll" you trade a resource for. Spending your haul on a spin is a slot machine with no money in it, so chance applies only to things you'd do anyway.
- **Power creep that makes old rares worthless** (Pet Simulator 99).
- **Income leaderboards.** They break SETTLED's "never feel judged".
- **Rarity that gates a place, toy or route** (principle 3).
- **Trading economies.** They pull toward value-hoarding and talk about scamming; a friendly gift is enough.

## 4. Eight ways Boogle could use rarity

Shared conventions:
- Every roll that others see happens in a Lockstep `apply`, using `mulberry32` seeded from (room seed, tick, player id, pile or site id). Its effects go in `onApplied`.
- Per-player pity counters live **in Lockstep state**, not in a tab's memory (AGENTS.md: no per-peer counters).

### 4.1 Rare finds in the haul (shiny logs, golden ore)
- **What's rare:** a variant of the current age's material. Each age has its own set:
  - wood: birch-striped, amber-sap, golden log;
  - iron: rust-red, meteorite, golden ore;
  - and so on through maglev (superconductor crystal).
- **How you get it:** a chance on each `pile.take`, with count-based pity.
- **Where it shows:**
  - On your stack: a glowing piece in the wobble, with a sparkle and a chime on pickup.
  - Others see it through a short presence field (`sr`: the tier codes on your stack).
  - When poured, it shows as a special sleeper or rail in the laid section, there for good, with your name on the plaque line.
- **What it does:** cosmetic only, and it counts as 1 like any other piece. The *track* remembers it.
- **Sync:**
  - The roll is Lockstep, in `pile.take`.
  - The stack stays Local plus Owner presence `sr`.
  - `site.stock` gains an additive `rare` field that records where the special piece sits. It is client-asserted, since the stack is Local; trust the client among friends, as brief 7 already does for the stack.
- **Risk:** low. Keep it no slower than brief 7's "density beats rarity" pickup: no pause and no popup.

### 4.2 Named helpers with quirks
- **What's rare:** a helper with a name, a hat and a quirk: whistles, carries two, naps on a wagon, rides the ball.
- **How you get it:** chance on each helper built, and guaranteed by the 5th helper of an age.
- **Where it shows:** it works in the world, gets a name tag on approach, and is listed at the station.
- **What it does:** cosmetic, plus at most a tiny perk (+5% speed for that one helper). That keeps helpers from becoming the optimal choice.
- **Sync:** Lockstep. The roll happens in the helper-build op, and the helper's id, name seed and quirk are stored in site state. Movement is drawn Local from the tick.
- **Risk:** low to medium. A perk that's too strong makes Helper always beat Track, so keep perks cosmetic.

### 4.3 Train liveries and engine variants
- **What's rare:** a paint scheme or engine skin for each age (an Ember Express, a Seaside Blue).
- **How you get it:**
  - Deterministic: earned by pouring the section where a rare piece landed.
  - Or from completing an age's Find set (see 4.4).
- **Where it shows:** on the train everyone rides. One player picks which unlocked livery runs; it's a toggle in the cab, so nobody fights over it.
- **What it does:** cosmetic.
- **Sync:** the unlock is Lockstep (a bitset). The current choice is Host or Lockstep.
- **Risk:** low.

### 4.4 Collection book on the miniature model
- **What's rare:** "Finds", 6–10 per age: every rare piece variant, every named helper, every station character's moods, curiosities in the world.
- **How you get it:** by any play at all. The first time anyone finds one, it is logged with the finder's name.
- **Where it shows:** on the Big Walk-style model at stations. Each find appears as a tiny figurine at the spot where it was found, and the book is a ring of pedestals around the model.
- **What it does:** completing an age's set gives a livery (4.3) and a small **personal luck bonus** for everyone who contributed an entry.
- **Sync:** Lockstep. It's a bitset plus (tick, finder), the same shape as brief 3's `log.discover`.
- **Risk:** low. It also serves principle 9 ("leave something"): late arrivals see a full model.

### 4.5 Station characters' moods and visitors
- **What's rare:** a station character dressed for an occasion (the stationmaster in a party hat) or a guest who drops by.
- **How you get it:** world conditions you can read: Lockstep night or weather (both stateless functions), or a pour tick.
- **Where it shows:** on the platform, logged in the book.
- **What it does:** cosmetic, plus a funny line. The Boogler-style copy rule applies.
- **Sync:** Lockstep without state, a function of tick.
- **Risk:** low. It must stay tick-based, not "this week only".

### 4.6 Rare discoveries per age (heirlooms)
- **What's rare:** one heirloom per age, a single physical object such as the first iron spike, a brass whistle or a maglev coil.
- **How you get it:** deterministic. It's found at a spot that the ancient stones reveal, or dug out of a section when the age's discovery fires. Exactly one exists per room.
- **Where it shows:** carried on top of the stack, then set on the model.
- **What it does:** cosmetic. Placing it can be one of the "leads" that drives the age's evolution, but never the only one.
- **Sync:** Lockstep, as a pile with a single item.
- **Risk:** medium. Never make it required, and an age must also advance without it (principle 3).

### 4.7 Combine ladder (Adopt Me style)
- **What's rare:** higher tiers made from lower ones, e.g. 8 Fine → 1 Rare, 8 Rare → 1 Gleaming. The ratio must make this route *slower* than expected luck (4 Fine ≈ 48 pickups would beat a natural Rare at ≈ 80), so it stays a safety net, not the main road.
- **How you get it:** deterministic. Walk into a "polisher" ring at a station with matching pieces on your stack.
- **Where it shows:** on your stack and on the track.
- **What it does:** it gives patient players a guaranteed route to the top tier, so bad luck can never stall anyone.
- **Sync:** Lockstep op `polish`.
- **Risk:** medium. It's a third ring, so a little more management, and it competes with pouring. It could be optional and late.

### 4.8 Heritage (prestige without loss)
- **What's rare:** after the maglev ending, a "Heritage" run: the island keeps its line, and a new, faster replay starts with old liveries, gold-trimmed track and higher rare odds.
- **How you get it:** finishing the line, then everyone agreeing.
- **Where it shows:** the model gets a gold band.
- **What it does:** rare odds rise and your personal tier carries over. Nothing is taken away.
- **Sync:** Lockstep, with a state `version` and `migrate`.
- **Risk:** high design cost. It's v3 scope, and the "reset" framing needs care.

## 5. Recommended rarity system

Rarity in Boogle should be **the stories on your stack and on the track, not more income**:
- Every piece you pick up has a small chance to be special, and a special piece shows on your back, chimes, and is laid into the railway forever under your name.
- Odds are per pickup and never per minute.
- Pity is count-based, so nobody's dry spell is long.
- A deterministic combine path (4.7) or the collection book guarantees the top tier to anyone who keeps playing.
- Your **contribution tier** (the guild-style rank) raises your luck a little, Melvor style. That makes the tiers feel like "I've become lucky here" rather than a grind meter.
- Casual players are never touched. A rider or ball-bopper still sees rare sleepers in the track, liveries on the train and figurines on the model, and the station characters' moods happen to everyone.
- There is no readout of who found how many. The book credits firsts only.

Assumptions: about 60 pieces per section and about 50 sections, so a typical player picks up 600–1,000 pieces over the game.

| Tier | Per-pickup odds | Pity | Per player, whole game | Wood-age example | Shown as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | the rest | — | — | log | plain stack piece |
| **Fine** | 1 in 12 | — | about 60 | knotty log | warm tint, soft chime |
| **Rare** | 1 in 80 | guaranteed by 150 | about 10 | birch-striped log | glows, sparkles; named sleeper in the track |
| **Gleaming** | 1 in 600 (top rank: 1 in 400) | guaranteed by 900 | about 1–2 | amber-sap log | light beam on pickup; room-wide toast "Ana found an amber-sap log!"; gold sleeper; figurine on the model |
| **Heirloom** | not random: 1 per age per room | — | shared | first iron spike | carried on top; sits on the model forever |
| **Named helper** | 1 in 4 helpers | guaranteed by the 5th | a few per room | "Clive, who whistles" | name tag; listed at the station |

- Contribution tiers raise Fine, Rare and Gleaming odds by up to about +50% at the top rank.
- Station characters thank high-tier players by name, giving credit and never shame.
- The rarity numbers are first guesses, to tune in playtests like the soft cap of 20.

## 6. Open questions

1. **When an age passes, can its rare finds still be got?**
   - (a) Gone with the age.
   - (b) Freight from that age's helpers can carry them, at the same odds.
   - (c) Only in Heritage.
   - **Lean: (b).** Nothing is ever permanently missed. That is FOMO's opposite, and it fits brief 7's "the line automates the past".
2. **Should rares do anything besides look nice?**
   - (a) Purely cosmetic.
   - (b) Count double when poured.
   - (c) A tiny personal luck boost.
   - **Lean: (a), plus collection-set bonuses only.** Doubling turns rarity into a speed race and makes casuals feel slow.
3. **Is a rare find personal or shared?**
   - (a) Yours alone.
   - (b) Gift it by throwing it to a friend.
   - **Lean: (b).** It's the friendly version of trading. A thrown piece is a Local flight; the thrower submits where it landed (DESIGN.md "Crossing kinds").
4. **Toast volume for Gleaming finds.**
   - (a) Room-wide toast and sound.
   - (b) Only nearby players see it.
   - **Lean: (a), once per find.** Social proof is most of what rarity is worth. Make the toast skippable and rare by design.
5. **Should the combine ladder (4.7) ship in the first slice?**
   - **Lean: no.** Ship 4.1, 4.4 and 4.2 first. Add the polisher only if playtests show bad-luck frustration that pity doesn't fix.
6. **Weather-linked variants** (Grow a Garden's best idea)?
   - **Lean: yes, later.** Night or rain ticks could double Gleaming odds for wood, as a stateless function of tick. Players read the sky together, and it is never a countdown.
7. **Heritage/prestige (4.8): v2 or v3?**
   - **Lean: v3.** v2's ending should feel complete.
