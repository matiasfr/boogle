## Answer first

**[P] Make the Roundhouse the place where a useful railway becomes your group's railway.** Trains automatically bring materials closer, carry everyone, and make newly connected places productive. The shed offers a physical turntable, recognizable engines, and one reversible choice per functioning line. Start with one train, one wagon, one fitting and one useful repeat journey. Leave freight routing, departures and safety automatic.

Research should mean **finding a material, connecting its source and seeing a useful design become possible**. It should not mean producing science points, repeating deliveries for XP or paying twice to discover and fabricate the same fitting. Materials pay for visible shared construction; personal cosmetic rewards remain personal. An extra train must open or improve an actual service, not simply occupy a collection slot.

Disposition: **bounded experiment after the existing first-path/station verdict; research can proceed now**. Serves [DESIGN](../../DESIGN.md): **Toys before chores**, **Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated**, **Communal world, personal rewards**, **Telegraph everything**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, and **Show, don't tell**. The HTML is a design explainer, not an in-game menu specification or an economy simulator.

## What trains do

1. **Move known goods automatically.** A connected source supplies a bounded station/rack; an automatic service carries reserved cargo toward an already chosen construction job. Friends still contribute the last short carry and see it become track. Supply is useful because it retires an old commute, not because a throughput score goes up. [DESIGN core loop](../../DESIGN.md#the-core-loop), [rail economy](rail-economy-model.md#answer-first).
2. **Carry people and their things.** Every operational line has usable passenger space independent of cargo capacity. Sitting and stable boarding are baseline usability, never a paid comfort tier. The rail journey lets friends gather, spot a new place and revisit an old one. Trains remain ride-only; people can walk the built connection too. [Owner train decisions](../decisions.md#trains-and-the-world).
3. **Unlock lines through delivered capability.** Build a connection to enable its source; fit an available-material kit to service the next kind of route. A purchased engine never substitutes for unbuilt track. A concrete/steel/cargo gate must have its bootstrap material obtainable on the near side or on foot. Scouting can precede freight export: movement ability reaches the place; railway connects its useful output.

**[P] Flow:** source → local works/station → reserved load → automatic train → frontier rack → voluntary carry → installed track/town/fitting → new reachable source. A first-source discovery also reveals a design, but reveals create **zero stock**. Cosmetic entitlement branches from the achievement into each player's collection, never back into material output.

### No shared wallets does not mean no communal materials

**[P]** Personal earned looks cannot be spent by another player. A player's carried goods stay on their own accounted back until deliberately contributed to a named job. Depositing four steel into a particular chassis dedicates those four pieces to it; it does not put them in a freely spendable room purse. Connected-source supply is communal infrastructure output, with each shipment assigned once to a visible project. Do not sweep absent players' packs into a purchase.

Keep the critical connection's allocation protected before optional fittings request surplus. Only one optional fitting is funded at a time; changing its display selection cannot move already contributed material. No half-funded-project cancellation in the first build, and no repeat payment to switch an owned configuration. Either complete it or leave the baseline service running. Every bill is fixed for 1–6 players; last-deliverer credit grants neither train ownership nor exclusive power. Late joiners immediately use the world's fitted trains and can claim their own eligible cosmetics. The donor's appearance is not a power loan that disappears when they leave. These are proposed player rules to implement under the existing [production material/reward contracts](../plans/production-core.md#transaction-contract-every-unit-has-one-home), not a new ledger architecture.

## Research without a second job

**[P] Three node kinds, one short tree:**

```text
First connected service → early shed R1 → free profile/appearance fitting
Source discovery → design visible → source connected → material fitting
  cement → B1 bulk cradle ────────────────┐
  steel → S1 chassis   + S4 junction ────┼→ more useful lines
  fibre → comfort (three-area only)      │
  crystal → X1 seed cradle (five only)    │
  copper → U1 motor / U5 bays + U3 spur ─┘
Ability discovery → explore source on foot → permanent shared access
```

The material discovery reveals the fitting silhouette and its use. The first connected shipment unlocks the fabrication opportunity; it is ordinary accounted stock, not consumed again as a research sample. The named material bill then builds the fitting once. A necessary new cargo carrier instead grants its pattern at discovery so the first local sample can bootstrap it. Keep the next one or two useful nodes visible, with further shapes hinted at physically; the HTML's full tree is an owner planning view.

Use the historical book as an optional index of what the world has learned, with little physical pages/parts beside the engines. Do not revive friendship, repeated gifting, distinct-player counts, a day counter, global research queue or speculative new Boogler dialogue. Discoveries should be reachable with current abilities; no mini-game, rare box or absent friend's item is a required research key. [Historical book rules](../proposals/boogler-tech-tree.md#rules-every-page-follows), [current principles](../../DESIGN.md), [ability gate audit](ability-upgrades.md#authority-current-behavior-and-proposal-boundaries).

### Capacity and speed must alter an outcome

**[P] Controlled comparison, not a new campaign forecast:** use the existing [paper travel function](../../dev/economy/campaign.ts), 1,200 m, acceleration 2 m/s², and eight seconds dwell at each end. Long-leg run time is `L/v + v/a`; cycle is `2*run + 2*dwell`. Excludes access walking, transfer handling, congestion and timetabled missed departures beyond the stated wait model.

| Profile  | Capacity units |  Speed | One-way run | Cycle | Saturated units/min |
| -------- | -------------: | -----: | ----------: | ----: | ------------------: |
| Baseline |             24 | 20 m/s |        70 s | 156 s |                9.23 |
| Swift    |             24 | 25 m/s |      60.5 s | 137 s |               10.51 |
| Hauler   |             32 | 20 m/s |        70 s | 156 s |               12.31 |

Swift saves **9.5 s** per run; Hauler moves a prepared **32-unit kit in one departure instead of two**, provided both loading and receiving racks hold 32. A rack capped at 16 or 24 negates that advertised single-load result. At a source rate of **6 units/min**, all profiles are source-limited: fitting a larger wagon creates no extra production. With uniform random platform arrivals, mean waiting is half-cycle: 78 s baseline/Hauler versus 68.5 s Swift. These are assumptions, not measured waits; they expose why a missed-train trial matters. Do not solve them by making players dispatch trains manually.

The HTML lets the owner switch saturated versus source-limited supply and inspect this exact comparison. Profile selection is free once unlocked. These mutually exclusive experimental profiles are **not** the model's bundled three-area S1 benefit; if the choice adds no wanted decision, retain the unambiguous upgrade and automate the profile.

## The light management loop

**[P] Notice a useful new job → inspect its engine/part → turn the display bridge → fit once → assign to an already built line → board and see the difference.** A short loop at a place, not a standing occupation.

- **Smallest toy:** one engine, one wagon, two snap angles and an off-mainline turntable. Turning it cannot stop ordinary departures. No precise coupling or parking puzzle.
- **Smallest real choice, second comparison only:** two preset lines, a short supply job and a longer ride, each retaining its baseline service. Pick Swift or Hauler for each. No line can be left unserved by a friend taking its only locomotive. At this stage “assign” changes a service configuration; it is not a scarce-fleet optimization game.
- **Cost visibility:** one pending fitting shows its dedicated materials physically; the train shows the installed part. Source-limited freight is labelled as such in the planning page. A future in-world view should show the actual pile and destination rather than present an unexplained rate.
- **Co-op conflict:** anyone can inspect or request. One accepted request per line settles at the next safe home stop; competing requests receive a clear result. No room-wide vote, persistent dispatcher role, global editor lock or mid-trip removal of a rider's floor. This adopts the existing [Roundhouse recommendation](roundhouse-management.md#numbered-recommendations-and-the-smallest-prototype-for-each), leaving its implementation to the scheduled-service promotion.
- **Future returns:** new engines and appearances attract voluntary visits. Previously owned profiles should eventually be selectable at stations, with safe-stop application; do not require a Home commute for every cargo batch.

Exclude fuel, wear, damage, wages, fares, upkeep, auctions, resale, automatic purchasing, timetable editors, signal placement, cargo filters, per-stop manifests, scarce seats and research XP. Add none as a substitute for a weak upgrade payoff. Keep the first version to one useful fitting; the catalogue above is a campaign envelope.

The old [rail-economy model](rail-economy-model.md) likewise warns that its eight hours depends on unbuilt content, but its six-era table is historical and its rates are not this model's rates.

**[M] The train purchase currently lacks economic payback.** Omitting S1 finishes at 447.5 / 464.7 minutes, versus 447.9 / 467.9 with it. Omitting U1 and U5 together finishes five-area at 459.1 versus 467.9. Purchasing can change scheduling and introduce its own acquisition work; these are net whole-route comparisons, not isolated efficiency estimates. Passengers, missed boardings and management choices are not modeled. Therefore neither “train upgrades save time” nor “players will enjoy buying them” follows from the campaign totals. The model currently caps shipments by rack as well as wagon capacity, explaining one potential mismatch between a nominal capacity upgrade and delivered batches.

**[P] Campaign envelope:** introduce the shed at its earned first-connection event; spend the middle game making cement/steel exports useful; allow at most one substantial train fitting per material frontier; reserve late copper for the five-area alternative. Cap required hands-on yard interaction at roughly **six 60–90 s visits (6–9 minutes total)** inside existing fitting/activity budgets, not added to them. Keep optional comfort/cosmetics outside that cap. Aim for a functional fitting's first clear use within **2–5 minutes**, with at least **15 minutes of useful route remaining**; these are test thresholds, never timers. The final hour should mainly enjoy the built network. Do not require a post-ending grind to justify the last engine.

Before pricing the new candidates, the next **offline** comparison should replace/rename S4 as the later expansion, insert the early-shell event without extra authored time, reserve core supply before optional bills, and compare source/rack/batch bottlenecks. Do not insert every candidate simultaneously. Recheck route-first, optional-first, late join and absence. The current model has no production renewal and no passengers; the production rules have counted renewal but do not prove this campaign. Neither is a substitute for the other.
