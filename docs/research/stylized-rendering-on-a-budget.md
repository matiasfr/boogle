# Paint the island inside the materials

> **Update, 30 October 2026.** Boogle has since moved to `WebGPURenderer` with TSL node materials and a
> `RenderPipeline` (one output pass for tone mapping), on purpose: the owner wants the high end (compute, TRAA,
> bloom, GTAO, SSGI) on modern devices, iOS 26 Safari included. Read "add a pass" below as "add a pass on a quality
> tier, measured on device". The in-material looks here are still the floor every device gets, and the GLSL snippets
> translate to TSL node slots ([src/render/README.md](../../src/render/README.md)).

Boogle can reach the Sable, A Short Hike and Elysium tier of stylized look on iPhone and iPad without adding a single post-processing pass, because nearly every look worth having lives inside the fragment shaders it already runs. Apple's tile-based GPUs reject hidden opaque pixels before shading them, so overdrawn opaque geometry is close to free, while alpha blending, `discard` and full-screen render targets are where the cost lands. That inverts the usual desktop priority list: inverted-hull outlines, vertex-shader wind and baked ambient occlusion are cheap on an iPad, while screen-space outlines, SSAO, LUT grading and radial-blur god rays are expensive. Boogle's water already uses "vertical depth" (water height minus a heightmap ground), which is exactly the fix the three.js community recommends for depth-buffer foam that breaks at grazing angles, so the shoreline needs art direction rather than new plumbing. The strongest prototypes, in order, are: toon-banded water with a moving shore foam line and shallow-water caustics; palm-frond sway and terrain grass tufts driven by one wind uniform; a global in-shader time-of-day grade plus sun-tinted fog; inverted-hull outlines on the merged props and the ball; and vertex-baked AO with blob shadows for the night. Each adds zero to a handful of draw calls. Real-time GI in the style of Tiny Glade, and anything that needs a depth texture or a second scene render, should stay off the table for mobile.

## On an iPad, blending and extra passes cost more than draw calls

Apple GPUs bin a render pass's geometry into tiles, **discard occluded primitives per tile before fragment shading**, and keep the working pixels in on-chip tile memory whose bandwidth and latency are "many times" better than device memory ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/metal/tailor-your-apps-for-apple-gpus-and-tile-based-deferred-rendering.md)). Apple's own guidance sorts fragments into three classes: opaque ones that let hidden-surface removal skip everything beneath them, "feedback" ones that use `discard` (it names **alpha-tested foliage** explicitly), and blended translucent ones; the latter two forfeit some of that saving, and **load and store actions "consume the majority of your app's system bandwidth"** ([Apple WWDC20 session 10632](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632)). The same session says a depth pre-pass is unnecessary on Apple GPUs and that 16-bit math raises occupancy, so `mediump` in hand-written GLSL is worth keeping.

Every post-processing effect in three.js is at least one extra full-screen store and load. Hard numbers for iPhone are scarce. A vendor blog claims **5–10 ms per full-screen effect on mid-tier mobile**, an unverified figure ([svilenkovic](https://svilenkovic.com/3d/three-js-postprocessing)). A three.js forum thread documents LUT and SMAA passes producing **black streaks on iPhones and MacBooks** but not on a Snapdragon Android ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/streaks-appear-after-postprocessing-is-enabled-on-apple-devices-iphone-macbook/55245.md)). Boogle currently has no composer and no depth texture, and its recent commits cut draw calls by merging and instancing. The right rule follows directly: **new looks go into existing materials, or into a few opaque instanced draws; nothing gets its own full-screen pass.** One corollary sits in plain sight. The 1300 m sea plane is a blended surface that covers much of the screen, so it is already Boogle's biggest translucent fill. Any new translucent layer, such as god-ray cones, fog cards or alpha grass, stacks on top of it.

Material choice matters too. three.js documents `MeshStandardMaterial` as "somewhat more computationally expensive" than Lambert or Phong ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html)). Boogle uses it for flat-shaded, vertex-coloured terrain and props with no environment map, so it pays for PBR without showing it. A `MeshToonMaterial` with a three-step `gradientMap` (which must use `NearestFilter` to keep hard bands ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshToonMaterial.html))) is both the more stylized and the cheaper option. A swap like this needs an on-device A/B rather than an assumption, because the terrain's baked vertex shading already does much of the work.

## Boogle's water already uses the trick the web community converged on

The standard stylized-water recipe is a shallow-to-deep colour ramp plus a foam mask where the water is thin. Web implementations usually get the thickness by comparing the surface with the scene depth buffer ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/unlit-water-shader-with-foam/11641); [MasterAllArts](https://masterallarts.pages.dev/learn/threejs-from-zero/s12-06-stylized-water-foam/)), and that approach has known failures. In Elysium, the best-known stylized world built on vanilla three.js, **foam vanished at grazing angles and depth water banded on some Android devices**. The suggested fix was to use vertical depth for foam, and vertical depth plus Fresnel for colour ([Elysium thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/elysium-the-most-beautiful-stylized-world-on-the-web/55541)). Boogle's `waterMat` already decodes ground height from a 16-bit heightmap texture and computes `depth = surface − ground`, with a Fresnel blend to the sky. The robust version is already in place, at the cost of one texture sample.

What is missing is stylization. Today the depth ramp is two smooth gradients and the shore foam is a soft band modulated by a sine. Three changes, all ALU-only inside the existing draw, would move it toward Wind Waker and A Short Hike. First, **posterize the ramp into three or four flat bands** (turquoise shelf, teal, deep blue) with a thin lighter rim at each band edge; this is the single most recognisable "toon water" cue. Second, replace the sine foam with **one or two crisp foam lines that advance and retreat** with a slow phase, so the beach looks like it is breathing. The same phase can later carry the tide telegraph that DESIGN.md principle 7 asks for. Third, add **caustics on shallow sand**: the cheap form samples one black-and-white caustics texture twice at different scales and scroll directions and multiplies the two ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/chaep-caustics-unshade/)), and Unreal's version is about **49 instructions and one texture sample** faded by depth ([HRI Tech](https://medium.com/hri-tech/cheap-water-caustics-in-ue4-ee1d3ac0cae1)). Because Boogle's shallow sea is drawn at about 50 % alpha over the sand, drawing caustics in the water shader where depth is under about 1.5 m reads as light on the seabed without touching the terrain material.

Literal Wind Waker water is a trap on mobile. A popular recreation builds its foam from **about 100 procedural circles evaluated twice per pixel, on top of six-octave fBm** ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/wind-waker-water-no-textures-needed/)). That is affordable on a small desktop plane, but not across a full-screen sea at device pixel ratio 2. Baking that pattern once into a 128 px tiling texture gives the same look for one sample. The sim-water surface (`water-surface.js`) already shares uniforms with the sea, so banding and foam logic should go into a shared GLSL snippet and be applied to both, which keeps streams and ponds visually consistent with the ocean.

## Wind, grass and outlines can ride the meshes Boogle already merged

Vegetation wind on the web is a vertex-shader sine plus noise everywhere it appears ([keaukraine](https://dev.to/keaukraine/efficient-webgl-vegetation-rendering-4g2g); [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/04/how-to-make-the-fluffiest-grass-with-three-js/)). The costs that bite on mobile are vertex throughput and overdraw. On low-end Android the WebGL vegetation demo was **vertex-bound, so it lowers density and turns wind off there**. Per-instance CPU culling cost about 10 ms plus about 20 ms of texture uploads, while **4×4 tiled culling cost about 1 ms** ([keaukraine](https://dev.to/keaukraine/efficient-webgl-vegetation-rendering-4g2g)). For the toon look, BotW's grass is plainly coloured geometric blades rather than alpha cards ([hexaquo](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-1-theory/)), which is also what Apple's opaque-first guidance favours.

The cheapest win needs no new draw at all. Boogle's palms, fronds and rocks are a single merged mesh, so adding a per-vertex sway weight (zero at trunks and rocks, rising toward frond tips) and a few lines in `onBeforeCompile` makes **every palm sway for zero added draw calls**. Shadows can stay static unless the depth material is patched too. Grass tufts are the second step: an opaque instanced tuft of three to five triangles, coloured from the terrain's own grass palette, with one `InstancedMesh` per coarse terrain chunk so frustum culling stays at the chunk level. Density should be halved on `isTouch`, and tufts should be removed where `editDelta` shows dug or piled sand so that digging visibly clears them. Placement is cosmetic, so it may use floats, but seeding from the island RNG gives every visitor the same meadow. Tufts that bend away from the ball and from players, passed in as a small uniform array the way splash rings are, serve principle 2 directly: they give the ball something to do.

Outlines follow the same logic. Screen-space edge detection, the method community analysis attributes to Sable ([Reddit](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/howdidtheycodeit/comments/17k126c/how_did_they_make_the_outline_shader_in_sable)), needs depth and normal buffers plus a full-screen pass. The **inverted hull** draws each outlined mesh a second time with back faces pushed out along their normals. Its cost scales with mesh count, which is how three.js's `OutlineEffect` works on WebGL; on WebGPU the counterpart is `ToonOutlinePassNode`, which only outlines toon materials ([three.js OutlineEffect](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OutlineEffect.html); [three.js ToonOutlinePassNode](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/ToonOutlinePassNode.html)). Because Boogle merges its props, **outlining every palm and rock is one extra draw**, and the ball, avatars, Boogler and instanced mushrooms add a few more. The hull is opaque and back-face only, so tile hidden-surface removal handles it well. Two lessons from Sable apply. Shedworks found outlines a "really useful tool" for depth, and **fades them with distance so objects don't pop in** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)), so the hull width should be scaled in screen space and fed the same fog factor. A Short Hike's "soft" outline, softer than pure black, is a better fit for a beach toy than ink lines ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)). Tint the hull a darker shade of the island's own palette.

## Fog, sky palettes and baked AO deliver most of the mood for free

Sable's creative director called distant fog **"really, really key"** to readability, customised it per biome, and built the day-night cycle on top of it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Boogle already drives fog colour from its 20-minute cycle in `sky.js`, which has full day, sunset and night palettes for the sky, water tint, hemisphere light and cloud glow. The cheap upgrades sit inside shaders. One is **sun-aware fog**, warmer toward the sun at golden hour and bluer away from it, made by replacing the shared `fog_fragment` chunk so every built-in material inherits it; the water shaders already reimplement fog with shared uniforms. The other is a **global grade uniform** (tint, lift and saturation) applied at the end of every material. Grading pipelines elsewhere blend many LUTs by time of day, one Unreal project with 15 of them ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/advanced-daylight-system-with-colour-grading/28489)), but Boogle's palette table already plays that role without a render target. Stars as one `Points` draw faded in at night, and cloud undersides tinted by the sunset glow, finish the sky. Unreal developers have flagged clouds that stay white under a dark sky as a tell ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ue4-skysphere-blueprint-color-curve-assets/316885)).

God rays have a cheap form and an expensive form. three.js ships a screen-space `GodRaysShader` radial blur ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/module-GodRaysShader.html)), which means another full-screen pass. The budget version, used in Godot, Unity and VR, is a **cone or card mesh with additive blending, a gradient falloff, scrolling dust noise and a Fresnel edge fade** ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/fake-godrays-godot-4-2/); [FakeLight](https://realanthonycolmenero.itch.io/fakelight)). For Boogle that means a few shafts under the palm canopy or toward a low sun, shown only around sunrise and sunset. They are blended fill, so they should stay small on screen and switch off at noon and at night.

Global illumination is where references and budget part ways. Tiny Glade built its own Vulkan renderer to control "pretty much the whole pipeline" with compute shaders ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)). Its rendering engineer describes half-resolution ReSTIR serving up to 32k shadowed lights ([Bluesky post](https://bsky.atproto.gaycatgirl.sex/@h3r2tic.bsky.social/3lclorzdibv2a)), and a Townscaper-like builder experiments with screen-space GT-VBAO ([80.lv](https://cdn.80.lv/articles/screen-space-ambient-occlusion-test-for-upcoming-cozy-city-builder)). None of this ports to a phone browser. The cheap substitute already exists in Boogle: `terrain.js` bakes shade into vertex colours, darkening hollows by their Laplacian and brightening crests. Extending that with a **heightfield occlusion term**, computed on the CPU in the same per-edit region refresh, would darken trench floors and the bases of sand walls the way AO would, for zero GPU cost. Darkening vertex colours at the feet of palms and rocks during the merge does the same for props. Since the shadow pass is skipped at night, **blob shadows** (one instanced set of soft quads under the ball, players and Boogler) keep objects grounded. Sable's team deliberately gave moonlight shadows so players could judge their position ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). A blob costs far less than restoring the shadow map and keeps the ball readable in flight at night, which serves principle 2.

## Five prototypes, ranked by look per millisecond

The ranking weighs visible impact against added draws and fill on an iPad, and favours work that serves a DESIGN.md principle. Every item is cosmetic and stays out of the deterministic sim.

| Rank | Look | Technique | Added cost | Principle served |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Toon shoreline | Banded depth ramp, 1–2 moving foam lines, shallow caustics (two scrolled samples) in `waterMat` and the sim-water shader | 0 draws; ~1 texture sample + ALU on water fragments | Toys before chores (the water is the main toy); Telegraph everything (foam phase can carry the tide warning) |
| 2 | Island wind | Sway weight on the merged props mesh; opaque instanced grass tufts per chunk that part around the ball and players, cleared by digging | 0 draws for palms; ~1 per visible grass chunk, density halved on touch | The ball stays the star; Slapstick over precision |
| 3 | Time-of-day mood | Shared `fog_fragment` with sun-tinted fog; global grade uniform from the `sky.js` palettes; star `Points`; tinted cloud undersides | 0–1 draws, no render target | Telegraph everything (weather and night read at a glance) |
| 4 | Soft outlines | Inverted hull on props mesh, ball, avatars and mushrooms; screen-scaled width; palette-tinted; fogged out with distance | ~5–8 draws, opaque back faces | The ball stays the star (silhouette readability on small screens) |
| 5 | Grounding at night | Heightfield AO baked into terrain vertex colours on edit; AO at prop bases; instanced blob shadows | 0 GPU for AO; 1 draw for blobs | Leave something for the next visitor (dug trenches read clearly) |

Two experiments sit just below the line. Swapping `MeshStandardMaterial` for toon or Lambert materials is likely cheaper and more on-style, but it interacts with the baked vertex shading and needs an on-device check. Golden-hour god-ray cones are lovely but add blended fill on top of the sea, so they belong after the items above and only at sunrise and sunset. Three things to avoid: an `EffectComposer` chain, SSAO, and any technique that needs a depth texture or a second scene render. Moving to WebGPU and TSL is not needed for any of these looks, and Boogle's custom `ShaderMaterial`s for the water and sky would have to be ported. Each prototype should be checked with `npm run perf` for draw and program counts, `npm run look` for stills and motion, and a real iPad for frame time.

## Conclusion

The research mostly confirms that Boogle's existing choices hold up. The heightmap-driven water depth, palette-driven sky and vertex-baked terrain shading are the same moves the better web projects reach by trial and error, made earlier and more cheaply. What changes is where the next art budget goes: not into new passes, but into the shader code of surfaces already being drawn, plus a few opaque instanced draws. A useful working test for any proposed look is whether it can be written as lines in an existing fragment shader or as one more opaque merged or instanced mesh. If it can't, it probably doesn't belong on an iPad.

Two uncertainties should shape the order of work. Public per-pass timings for three.js on specific iPhone and iPad models don't exist, so every prototype needs its own on-device measurement. The primary sources for Wind Waker's water, Townscaper's lighting and Sable's outline method are talks and community analyses, not code. The techniques above are reconstructions that are proven in other engines, and they should be judged by how the island plays rather than by how faithfully they copy a reference game.
