# Looks that are mostly code

*Research report, 1 November 2026. Which shipped games look good without much bespoke art (Tiny Glade, Townscaper, Bad North, Sable, Tunic, A Short Hike, Firewatch, Alto's, Journey, Breath of the Wild, Wind Waker, Hi-Fi Rush, Valheim, Lethal Company, Return of the Obra Dinn, Teardown, Sea of Thieves, Astro Bot, Kirby, Animal Crossing, Lego Builder's Journey, Untitled Goose Game, Monument Valley, Islanders, Dorfromantik, Peak), what their look is made of, and three look directions for Boogle on WebGPU + TSL with the M4 iPad Pro as the floor ([boogle-2.md](../proposals/boogle-2.md)). Builds on [stylized-rendering-on-a-budget.md](stylized-rendering-on-a-budget.md) (tile GPUs, toon water, wind, outlines, baked AO), which this does not repeat. Recommendations only; no design decisions. `docs/proposals/visual-target.md` was not on `main` when this was written: its `?proto=look` measurements beat every cost guess here.*

**Markers.** **[V]**: the page was fetched and the claim matches it. **[S]**: seen only in a search summary, a recap or a forum; check before quoting. Boogle facts come from the code at `c8ad8ed`: vertex-coloured, flat-shaded `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` terrain ([src/adapters/terrain.ts](../../src/adapters/terrain.ts)), 44 plain `MeshStandardMaterial`s elsewhere, linear fog 110 → 520 m ([src/constants.ts](../../src/constants.ts)), day, sunset and night palettes in [src/render/sky.ts](../../src/render/sky.ts), PCF sun shadows, 4x MSAA, Neutral tone mapping ([graphics.md](../game-design/graphics.md)).

## Summary

1. The cheap looks all spend on **light, air and colour, not on surfaces**: Sable, Firewatch, Valheim, Tunic and Alto's lean on fog, sky and lighting palettes over plain or untextured meshes.
2. **Fog is the art director.** Sable called it key to readability; Firewatch picks fog colour from a gradient by distance; Alto's blends whole palettes (light, ambient, fog) by time and weather. Boogle already has the palette table; it lacks fog that *changes colour with distance and height*.
3. **One palette row per mood** (time of day, weather, era) is the cheapest content multiplier in the list: a few hex values re-light the whole island. It is also how eight eras could look different with no new textures.
4. **Flat colour is enough when shapes are good**: Untitled Goose Game ships untextured, Townscaper and Bad North use tiny palettes, Tunic started flat and added noise, AO and texture only later.
5. **Outlines buy readability, not style**; every user fades or softens them (Sable by distance, A Short Hike "soft"). Hi-Fi Rush's full comic look (halftone, hatching, coloured shadows) took a custom deferred renderer and artist-placed volumes: not cheap.
6. **Low-resolution rendering** (A Short Hike, Lethal Company, Obra Dinn) is the only style that *saves* GPU time, but it fights small screens, UI legibility and distant silhouettes.
7. **Toy materials** (Astro Bot, Kirby, Animal Crossing, Lego) come from rounded shapes, soft roughness, rim light and good AO; the cost is in AO and depth of field passes, which an M4 can likely afford at reduced resolution.
8. **Expensive-looking water** in stylized games is a stylized layer on a physical base (Sea of Thieves: FFT plus authored foam); for Boogle the existing heightmap water plus banding is still the right base.
9. three r187 ships nearly every building block in TSL already: `MeshToonNodeMaterial`, `MeshMatcapNodeMaterial`, `triplanarTexture`, MaterialX noise, `exponentialHeightFogFactor`, `posterize`, `bayer16`, `OutlineNode`, `SobelOperatorNode`, `PixelationPassNode`, `Lut3DNode`, `GTAONode`, `DepthOfFieldNode`.
10. **Recommendation:** a "painted diorama" look (soft two-band light with coloured shadows, sky-matched gradient fog, palettes per time and era, faded soft outlines), with toy-material touches on characters and vehicles. Smallest test: one flag that swaps fog, light ramp and palette on today's island, before any asset.

## The games

### Procedural geometry, lit well: Tiny Glade, Townscaper, Bad North, Islanders, Dorfromantik

- **Tiny Glade.** Look: hand-painted-feeling castles generated around the player's strokes, under very soft bounced light. How: proceduralism carries the geometry (Anastasia Opara's windows store a signed distance to the nearest window in a single float vertex attribute, which drives plaster damage around them [S] ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade))); lighting is a custom ray-marched global illumination renderer (Tomasz Stachowiak's GPC 2024 talk is titled "Rendering Tiny Glade with entirely too much ray marching" [S] ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42191767))). Code vs art: geometry and light are code; the art is in tuning rules. **The GI is the expensive half and doesn't port**; the vertex-attribute trick (one float per vertex that a shader turns into wear) does.
- **Townscaper.** Look: pastel houses, crisp dark lines, soft occlusion in corners. How: Oskar Stålberg wrote his own lighting and AO, adapting his voxel AO method to non-voxel tiles and simplifying ambient light for low-end platforms [S] ([X](https://x.com/OskSta/status/1168168400155267072); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/townscaper-adds-lighting-options-flying-towns)). Its .obj export ships three small "magic" textures, and a WebGL recreation found lines drawn by a texture-mapping trick and window openings cut with a stencil boolean [V] ([Reinder Nijhoff](https://reindernijhoff.net/2021/11/townscapers-rendering-style-in-webgl/)). Code vs art: a hand-modelled tile kit, but colour comes from a palette and light from code.
- **Bad North.** The same author's earlier game: tiny island dioramas, small palette, soft minimal style; Stålberg credits the design constraints for the look [S] ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indie-spotlight-plausible-concept-on-bad-north/)). He calls his newer shoreline a refined version of what Bad North and Townscaper did [S] ([X](https://x.com/OskSta/status/1666429884208381953)).
- **Islanders.** Blocky low-poly islands; the co-founder says the blockiness came from prototyping and the rule was to ask whether each decision could be simpler [S] ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/06/14/islanders)). Lesson: the greybox *became* the art.
- **Dorfromantik.** Naïve-art houses and trees, impressionist landscape colours, palettes that shift with the season; "minimalism and simplicity" is one of four pillars [S] ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/dorfromantik-interview/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/sparking-joy-through-tile-placement-in-idyllic-village-builder-i-dorfromantik-i-)).

### Light and air over flat shapes: Sable, Firewatch, Alto's, Tunic, Valheim, Monument Valley

- **Sable.** Look: Moebius-like flat colour with ink outlines. How: lighting first for readability, distant fog "really, really key" and tuned per biome, the day-night cycle built on the fog system, outlines that fade with distance, a moonlight shadow so players always cast one; the two-person team picked a desert partly because a denser world was out of reach [V] ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Community reconstructions draw the lines as a full-screen edge pass on depth, normals and colour [S] ([coleslow](https://coleslow.dev/blog/moebius-shaders-1/)). Code vs art: low-poly models with flat colour; fog, light and lines are code.
- **Firewatch.** Look: a poster painting you walk through. How: Jane Ng's GDC 2015 talk on translating a 2D poster style into 3D; fog carries colour into the scene with its colour controlled per layer of depth [S] ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022295/The-Art-of); [Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch/)); the sky is a three-colour gradient (top, middle, bottom) with sun and horizon terms added [S] ([Campo Santo blog](https://blog.camposanto.com/post/112703721804/this-blog-post-is-an-in-detail-explanation-of-part), 403 to fetch). The widely copied "stylistic fog" reimplementation fetches the fog colour from a gradient texture per pixel, by depth [V] ([grrava](https://grrava.blogspot.com/2018/08/stylistic-fog-from-firewatch-with.html)). Code vs art: hand-made models and textures exist, but most of the mood is fog and sky gradients that an artist picks.
- **Alto's Adventure and Odyssey.** Look: silhouettes against gradient skies. How: a rotating sky plane and predefined colour schemes for directional light, ambient light and fog, blended live across dawn, day, dusk, night and weather [S] ([Harry Nesbitt, Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/shots/1578755-Dynamic-Lighting-System)); one person did the art and code of the first game [S] ([harrynesbitt.com](http://www.harrynesbitt.com/blog/the-making-of-altos-adventure/)). **The closest match to what `sky.ts` already is.**
- **Tunic.** Look: bright, boxy, rounded corners, matte surfaces. How: early prototypes were flat colour on low-poly geometry, relying on light for depth; detail came in layers (noise, then AO, then hand-painted texture); geometry blocked out with ProBuilder [S] ([Wireframe via Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/features/wireframe-cover-star-tunic-and-the-art-of-keeping-a-secret/)). The team named rounded corners and matte textures as the style [V] ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-content-for-no-one-an-interview-with-the-team-behind-tunic)). Lesson: **the layers are in cost order**, and each ships on its own.
- **Valheim.** Look: PS1-ish low-poly, low-res textures, under modern light, fog, weather and post. Iron Gate says the style suits their taste, runs on older hardware and makes assets quicker, and that ambient effects and post-processing lift it [S] ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/ps1-graphics); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/valheim-good-graphics-lighting-low-resolution-textures/)). The proof that **lighting can carry crude assets**, including at mid-morning fog and dusk.
- **Monument Valley.** Orthographic camera, flat-shaded pastel architecture, built in Blender [S] ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/art-spotlight-monument-valley-2/)). Little on its shading is public; it shows how far composition and a palette go with no lighting model at all.

### Pixels, dither and grime: A Short Hike, Lethal Company, Return of the Obra Dinn, Peak

- **A Short Hike.** The world renders to a low-resolution render texture (from the GB Camera package), the terrain uses triplanar mapping, the splat map shows only its strongest channel for crisp edges, and shore foam compares water depth with the terrain behind [V] ([Adam Robinson-Yu thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1113100182655262721.html)); objects get a soft outline so they read with so few pixels [S] ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)). One person; nearly all look is code.
- **Lethal Company.** Renders at a fixed 860×520, stock Unity HDRP volumetric fog and lights, plus one custom posterize filter with edge detection, bloom and colour grading [S] ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/how-to-achieve-lethal-company-s-graphics-with-unity-hdrp); [daily.dev recap of Acerola](https://daily.dev/posts/the-strange-graphics-of-lethal-company-5knvykwlu)). Lesson: **crude assets plus low resolution plus heavy atmosphere** reads as a style and runs fast.
- **Return of the Obra Dinn.** Renders 8-bit grey at 800×450, thresholds to 1-bit against an 8×8 Bayer matrix or a 128×128 blue-noise field; to stop the dither swimming, the pattern is mapped onto a sphere around the camera so it holds still when the camera turns (not when it moves), thresholded at 2x and box-downsampled [V] ([Lucas Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-32/)). Lesson: **any screen-space pattern (dither, halftone, hatching) needs anchoring** or it shimmers.
- **Peak.** A jam-born climbing co-op: saturated characters on softer, gradient-shaded mountains, low-fidelity enough for a handful of artists [S] ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-was-meant-to-be-friendslop-game-from-the-start); [savedpixel](https://savedpixel.com/blog/friendslop-art-style-blender-guide/)). No technical source found. Relevant mostly as proof that **goofy friend co-op does not need polish**, only readable bodies.

### Cel, painterly and comic: Breath of the Wild, Wind Waker, Hi-Fi Rush, Journey

- **Breath of the Wild / Tears of the Kingdom.** Started from Wind Waker HD and moved toward more realistic form and material to make "things anyone could do" believable [S] ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/14/takizawa-explains-how-the-wind-waker-hd-inspired-the-visual-style-for-breath-of-the-wild/)); the press called the result painterly and gouache-like. Cel shading is a deferred pass over visible pixels (two light bands, rim only where the sun lights) [S] ([setsideb](https://setsideb.com/breath-of-the-wild-cel-shading-break-glitch/); [Roystan](https://roystan.net/articles/toon-shader/)), and without its cel pass and fog the world looks "drab" [S] ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/65156/breath-wild-looks-dead-without-cel-shading/index.html)). Lesson: a two-band ramp plus **atmospheric perspective** does most of the work; the rest is a huge art team.
- **Wind Waker water.** No official breakdown found; community ports reproduce it as flat cel colours plus a scrolled world-space lattice of circles or Voronoi cells [S] ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/wind-waker-water-no-textures-needed/)). Covered already in the earlier report.
- **Hi-Fi Rush.** A deferred toon renderer on a modified UE4 at 60 fps native: halftone dots in light, hatching in shadow (sized by luminance, kept on the models), shadow colour from a world-space 3D-texture volume, a custom fast GI bake, SDF face shadows, toon post-process volumes, shadow-only lights, capsule shadows for enemies [V] ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034251/3D-Toon-Rendering-in-Hi); [FOTH notes](https://www.foth.top/article/gdc-2024-hifirush-toonrendering-notes/), third-party). Lesson: **coloured shadows are the single most stealable idea**; the rest is a studio-scale renderer and per-area artist control.
- **Journey sand.** John Edwards' GDC 2013 talk: three years to get there; a Gaussian random normal per grain for glitter, rim lighting, an ocean-like specular sheen [S] ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017742/Sand-Rendering-in); [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2019/10/08/journey-sand-shader-3/)). All shader, no art: a beach is mostly sand, so **one material done well pays everywhere**.

### Toys, plastic and clay: Astro Bot, Kirby, Animal Crossing, Lego, Untitled Goose Game

- **Astro Bot.** The goal was a game that "feels like a toy"; characters were first prototyped from primitive shapes, and art follows gameplay [S] ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/08/26/the-evolution-of-astro-bots-adorable-character-design/); [icon-era recap](https://icon-era.com/threads/gdc-2025-the-making-of-astro-bot-astro-bot-took-3-years-of-development-and-was-pitched-to-sony-back-in-may-of-2021.16650/)). Its rendering talk ("Flex and Fun") covered fluids and mesh deformation; no material breakdown found.
- **Kirby and the Forgotten Land.** Baked GI, modest dynamic shadows, 30 fps on Switch [S] ([Famiboards, DF notes](https://famiboards.com/threads/digital-foundry-kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-on-nintendo-switch-the-digital-foundry-tech-review.2407/)). The toy look comes from soft shapes and baked light, not exotic shading.
- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons.** Atmospheric scattering for the sky, PBR tuned for art over accuracy, restrained depth of field; characters read as soft plastic dolls [S] ([Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2019/06/24/animal-crossing-new-horizons-tech-analysis-explains-why-it-looks-so-pretty/)).
- **Lego Builder's Journey.** Realistic plastic from HDRP plus ray tracing; staff note that a brick's edge reads a little brighter [S] ([Unity](https://resources.unity.com/games/lego-builders-journey-rendering-realistic-lego-bricks-in-unity); [Rambling Brick](https://ramblingbrick.com/2021/07/13/going-on-a-builders-journey-with-light-brick-studios-fan-media-round-table/)). The bright bevel is the cheap part; the ray tracing is not.
- **Untitled Goose Game.** Low-poly, flat colour, no textures by choice, carried over from their 2D game; lighting method unpublished (forum guess: flat ambient and faint shadows) [S] ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/an-untitled-i-untitled-goose-game-i-analysis); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/220550/untitled-goose-game-rendering)). **The goofiest game here has the least rendering**, which matters for Boogle.

### Voxels and water: Teardown, Sea of Thieves

- **Teardown.** Each object is a box ray-marched into a 3D palette texture (one byte per voxel into 256 colours); a 1-bit world volume drives soft shadows, AO, reflections and volumetrics; everything is noisy and denoised over time, then bloom, DoF, exposure [V] ([Acko.net](https://acko.net/blog/teardown-frame-teardown/)). The look is lighting on a deliberately crude palette; the cost is several temporal passes. **Too heavy for the iPad floor**, but the 256-colour palette index is a portable idea.
- **Sea of Thieves.** FFT ocean; colour blends deep and subsurface tones by view angle, sun direction and a wave-peak mask; foam from wave peaks and depth intersections, blurred with feedback and mixed with artist textures; clouds are opaque meshes with per-vertex fake scattering, composited at quarter resolution [V] ([SIGGRAPH 2018 talk](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)). Two ideas port: **subsurface tint on wave crests** (one mask, no new pass) and **cloud meshes instead of ray marching**.

## The techniques, by bang for buck

What each costs in Boogle's renderer. "In-material" means a few ALU ops in a node slot of a material already drawn; "pass" means a full-screen pass in the `RenderPipeline`. On a 13" M4 iPad Pro at device pixel ratio 2 (2752×2064, about 5.7 Mpx; 11": 2420×1668 [S] ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/apple-silion-m4-processor-family-all-we-know-specs-benchmarks-pricing-release-date))), one RGBA16F pass reads and writes roughly 90 MB a frame, about 5 GB/s at 60 fps of the M4's 120 GB/s: a back-of-envelope estimate that the `?proto=look` numbers should replace.

| Distance + height gradient fog, colour taken from the sky in the view direction | Firewatch, Sable, BotW | `scene.fogNode = fog(colorNode, factorNode)`; `exponentialHeightFogFactor`; sky colour reused from `sky.ts` | in-material | none |

## What this means for Boogle

Three directions that need little bespoke work. Each is a different answer to "what carries the look"; they share the cheap base (palettes, fog, baked AO, vertex colour) Boogle mostly has.

### A. Painted diorama (Firewatch, Sable, Alto's, BotW)

- **Look:** flat vertex-coloured shapes under a soft two-band light, shadows tinted (warm-lit sand, cool violet shade, never grey), fog that melts into the sky with distance and pools low over water, soft palette-tinted outlines that fade out at range. Sky and sea carry the mood.
- **Techniques:** `MeshToonNodeMaterial` (or a small shared custom lighting model) with a 3-texel ramp for terrain and props; shadow colour from the hemisphere ground colour so it follows `sky.ts`; `scene.fogNode` whose colour is the sky colour along the view ray mixed with a distance gradient, and whose factor adds `exponentialHeightFogFactor`; rim term on characters and the ball; inverted-hull outlines on the merged props, avatars and ball, width scaled to screen and multiplied by the fog factor.
- **Procedural vs asset:** all procedural. Assets: palette rows (hex values) and one ramp texture.
- **Effort:** small. Material swaps through `patchNode` and one fog node; about a day to a prototype, a week to tune.
- **iPad cost risk:** low. No new pass; the toon model is cheaper than Standard; outlines add a handful of opaque draws.
- **Across eight eras:** each era is a palette row (sky, fog, light, shadow tint, saturation). Wood age: warm, clear, saturated; steam to diesel: fog closes in, browner, lower saturation, smoke tinted by the fog colour so it belongs; electric: the air clears and the sky blues again; monorail and maglev: cooler, crisper, longer view distance, more neon at night. The mainland reads as a fogged silhouette that sharpens era by era, which is also the telegraph the layout research asks for ([level-design.md](level-design.md), rules 6 and 7).
- **Goofy co-op read:** good. Flat colour and outlines keep bodies, ball and props readable on a small screen (Goose shows goofy needs no more); coloured shadows keep slapstick sunny. Risk: it can look like a nice postcard rather than a toybox; bodies need a rim or a brighter palette to pop.

### B. Toy box (Astro Bot, Kirby, Animal Crossing, Lego)

- **Look:** everything is a toy on a table: rounded bevelled shapes, soft plastic, painted wood and tin, a warm key light, a bright rim, deep soft contact shadows, a touch of depth of field so the island feels small.
- **Techniques:** keep `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` (roughness 0.4–0.7, little metal); rounded geometry in code (`RoundedBoxGeometry`-style bevels; a bright edge term from a per-vertex bevel attribute, as Lego's bricks show); rim plus wrap lighting in `emissiveNode`; `GTAONode` at half resolution for contact shadows; optional `DepthOfFieldNode` tilt-shift on a high tier; an environment light (a small generated room or gradient cube) for soft specular; matcaps for characters only if a code look falls short.
- **Procedural vs asset:** procedural materials and bevels; shapes need more care (rounding everything), and a matcap or env map would be an approved image.
- **Effort:** medium. Every builder of props has to round and bevel; GTAO and DoF need tuning and a quality setting.
- **iPad cost risk:** medium. GTAO and DoF are full-screen passes needing depth and normals (an MRT output); likely fine on an M4 at half resolution, but that is the `?proto=look` question.
- **Across eight eras:** each era is a *toy material*: wood age as painted wooden toys, steam as tin toys, iron and steel as die-cast, diesel and concrete as chunky moulded plastic, electric and monorail as glossy plastic with light-up parts, maglev as translucent candy plastic. Material is a palette row too (roughness, metalness, rim, clearcoat), so it is mostly numbers.
- **Goofy co-op read:** the best fit with "Toys before chores", "Physics toys first" and "Slapstick over precision": a ball you throw at friends on a toy island. Risk: the grimy industrial eras fight clean plastic (grime has to become paint and soot decals), and realistic-ish materials show weak modelling more than flat colour does.

### C. Chunky pixels (A Short Hike, Lethal Company, Obra Dinn)

- **Look:** the world rendered small and scaled up crisp, posterized, ordered dither in gradients and fog, soft outlines, UI and text at full resolution.
- **Techniques:** `PixelationPassNode` or a scene render target at 1/3 to 1/4 resolution with nearest upscale; `posterize` and `bayer16` in the output pass, anchored to world or view-sphere coordinates (Obra Dinn) so it doesn't crawl; triplanar noise on terrain; existing fog and palettes.
- **Procedural vs asset:** all procedural.
- **Effort:** small to medium. The pass is quick; stable dither, UI split, and the interaction with MSAA and [adaptive-res](../../src/features/adaptive-res.ts) (both become moot) are the work.
- **iPad cost risk:** lowest; it saves fill. But on a 13" screen held at arm's length, chunky pixels at 1/4 res make the distant mainland and small players mush, and sub-pixel shimmer when the camera moves is a known problem.
- **Across eight eras:** weakest. Pixel size or dither could change by era (finer pixels as tech advances is a cute gag once), but the eras' main difference, more stuff further away, is what low resolution hides.
- **Goofy co-op read:** charming and nostalgic, and pixel art forgives crude models. Risk: legibility at a glance on a tablet, and it caps the high end the owner wants (TRAA, bloom, GTAO make little sense on a 640-pixel image).

### Recommendation

**Direction A, painted diorama, as the base, with B's character treatment** (rim, wrap light, rounded shapes and a slightly glossier material on players, The Boogler, the ball and vehicles). A is the cheapest, adds no full-screen pass, runs on the existing vertex colours and palette table, gives each era a look for the price of a palette row, and leaves B's GTAO and depth of field available as high-tier extras rather than the base. B's era-as-toy-material idea is worth keeping as the way vehicles and buildings change between eras even if the world stays painted. C is best kept as a fun option (a "retro" setting), not the target. Three things this report would avoid: halftone or hatching in screen space (it swims without Obra Dinn's anchoring and costs a pass), ray-marched GI or voxel lighting (Tiny Glade, Teardown), and textures painted per object before a look is chosen (DESIGN.md, "Code first, images by approval").

### Smallest prototype on today's island

One cosmetic, Local feature behind a URL flag (`?look=diorama`, or a variant inside the `?proto=look` harness if it already switches looks), touching no sync state:

1. **Gradient fog.** Set `scene.fogNode`: colour = the sky dome's colour along the view direction (reuse `sky.ts`'s `skyColor` function), mixed toward a deeper tint near the ground; factor = today's range fog plus an `exponentialHeightFogFactor` layer under about 4 m. Compare with the current flat fog at noon, sunset and night.
2. **Two-band light with coloured shadow.** Swap the terrain and merged-props materials to a toon lighting model with a 3-texel ramp, the shadow band tinted from the hemisphere ground colour; leave water, avatars and particles alone.
3. **One era row.** Add a second palette row (say "steam": hazier fog, browner horizon, lower saturation) and a debug key that blends to it, to test whether a palette alone reads as a different era.
4. **Check.** `npm run look` stills at noon, golden hour and night, flag on and off, plus a motion sheet; `npm run perf -- --render` before and after; frame time on the M4 iPad Pro. The playtest question: *at a glance, does the island look painted and does the steam row read as a different time?*

Outlines, rim light on characters and the toy materials come after, one at a time, each with its own before/after still.
