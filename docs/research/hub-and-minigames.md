# Research: a social hub plus mini-games, with personal progression

*Research report, 30 October 2026. What the designers of Club Penguin, Toontown, Habbo, Animal Crossing, Splatoon, Jackbox and Fall Guys said about hub spaces, mini-games, personal economies and drop-in play. The question behind it: should Boogle move toward a Club Penguin shape, with the island as a hub, many small mini-games, and individual character development instead of communal meta-progression? 20 sources were fetched and 82 claims extracted. The 25 strongest went through a three-vote adversarial check: 20 survived and 5 were refuted (listed at the end). Tags as in [progression-feel-exploration.md](progression-feel-exploration.md): **verified** (3-vote check passed), **fetched** (page loaded, not vote-checked), **secondary**. The proposal built on this is [docs/proposals/hub-and-minigames.md](../proposals/hub-and-minigames.md).*

---

## 1. Club Penguin

### 1.1 Hanging out came first (verified)

Lance Priebe on his own [RocketSnail page](https://rocketsnail.com/clubpenguin/), describing Experimental Penguins (July 2000):

- "Players named a penguin. They chatted. They explored four rooms. That was it. ... strangers were showing up every day to hang out together."
- At Club Penguin's launch (24 October 2005): "Penguins waddled around. They threw snowballs. They played mini-games. They dressed up."
- (fetched) "Designing without an audience is dangerous. I needed to watch how people actually played."
- (fetched) "I had five years to experiment, explore, and listen to the audience before we ever called it Club Penguin."

### 1.2 One room, one activity (verified)

Priebe, interviewed in Amber Atherton's *The Rise of Virtual Communities* ([excerpt](https://www.studio.dirt.fyi/p/club-penguin)):

- "Each public room in-world was carefully designed for the activity that we wanted to encourage in that room."
- Rooms are "essentially screens". Town is the central hub; the Coffee Shop and Dance Hall are for socialising, and the shops sell items. There are also secret rooms such as the Boiler Room.

### 1.3 Earn in mini-games, spend on yourself (verified)

- Priebe ([excerpt](https://www.studio.dirt.fyi/p/club-penguin)): "When you step out of the game with pockets full of earned coins, there would be a shop selling items and clothing."
- (fetched) On the igloo: "their own room that they could furnish. ... Now we look back, it's clear that these items were the social fabric of our users' identities."
- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Penguin) (secondary): "Each player was provided with an igloo for a home. ... Members had the option of opening their igloo so other penguins could access it via the map."
- **Caveat:** for most of the game's life, buying clothes, furniture and pets needed a paid membership. Free players could earn coins they couldn't spend on most of those things. About 90% of users never paid (Wikipedia, citing The Washington Post).

### 1.4 Parties and open-ended props (verified 2-1)

- Priebe: "The parties were key to Club Penguin's success. ... everyone who attended a party received a free exclusive item that evidenced their attendance." There was at least one party a month (Wikipedia, secondary).
- Lane Merrifield in a [2007 interview with 360kid](https://www.360kid.com/blog/2007/11/interview-with-club-penguin-founder-lane-merrifield/): for the Halloween party the team built a movie theater with a concession stand that was "totally unmanned, with no agenda ... That's the kind of play we try and build into Club Penguin".
- (fetched) A neglected puffle "never dies. It just runs away." Progression is gentle, never built on loss.
- (fetched) The team's rule was the eight-year-old test: "If it doesn't matter to an eight year old, it doesn't matter."

### 1.5 Personal coins, shared outcome (fetched)

Merrifield at AGDC 2008 ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/agdc-i-club-penguin-i-s-merrifield-on-why-genuine-counts), secondary): Coins for Change had "two and a half million kids gave away two billion coins to help decide where $1 million would go to". It shows a personal currency feeding a shared, world-level outcome by choice.

### 1.6 Safety took people, not only software (fetched)

- Merrifield: the filter list changed by "several hundred words a day", and a 150-person support team answered 5,000–7,000 emails a day ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/agdc-i-club-penguin-i-s-merrifield-on-why-genuine-counts)).
- About two-thirds of staff worked in moderation and customer service ([Engadget 2008](https://www.engadget.com/2008/05/09/has-the-mmo-industry-been-irresponsible-with-children/)).
- Boogle's rooms are private friend groups, so this matters only if the audience widens.

### 1.7 Club Penguin Island and Rewritten (verified, secondary sources)

- **Club Penguin Island** was Disney's 3D mobile successor. "Membership unlocked most of the game's full content, while free players had a meaningful but limited experience." It failed amid "the continued negative perception among fans, and intense competition" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Penguin_Island); that cause is Wikipedia's synthesis, not a designer postmortem).
- Island moved coins and XP to quests and daily challenges, away from mini-games (fetched). Disney's staff letter cited only "increasing global competition" and cutting costs ([Engadget 2018](https://www.engadget.com/2018/09/28/disney-shuts-down-club-penguin-island/)).
- **Club Penguin Rewritten** was a fan-run copy of the original 2D room-based game. It launched on 12 February 2017 and reached one million *registered* players on 4 October 2017 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Penguin_Rewritten)).
- Players used Rewritten to recreate proms and graduations (fetched), and organised on Discord ([Rebel Federation](https://clubpenguin.rebelfederation.com/the-club-penguin-rewritten-legacy/), blog). Its HTML5 port lost some mini-games.

## 2. Other hubs

### 2.1 Toontown: separate activities that keep friends together (verified)

Mike Goslin's [2004 postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/postmortem-disney-online-s-toontown):

- "To join a group playing mini-games, just join them on the mini-game trolley. ... a Toon may always teleport to another Toon on his or her friends list."
- Toons move freely between districts (copies of the world), so there is "no concept of being isolated on a particular game server".
- "damage bonuses are awarded when multiple Toons hit the same Cog ... weaker Toons are usually happy to have a more powerful Toon join their battle because everyone benefits". Each player earns experience and quest items on their own. (That this narrows the gap between new and veteran players is our inference.)
- (fetched) Each Toon's progress was more gags, more *options*: "easy to learn but difficult to master ... The ease of the initial experience is critical for attracting and keeping both younger children and non-gamer adults."
- (fetched) SpeedChat was menu chat with "a finite set of possible sentences".

### 2.2 Habbo: rooms, not a map (verified, plus fetched)

Sulka Haro, in a [2009 Game Developer interview](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-i-habbo-i-s-sulka-haro-talks-design-social-dynamics):

- (verified) "Habbo doesn't really have any topology in itself... It's just rooms that you access through this navigator thing that we have."
- (verified 2-1) "we added the secondary currency in Habbo, where you can actually earn stuff now... It's not an obvious solution that most of the players would have asked for."
- (fetched) "practically all the content on the servers is created by the players themselves".
- (fetched) A warning on looks: "the established players pretty much immediately recognize new players just by how they dress."

### 2.3 Splatoon: the plaza as the jumping-off point (verified 2-1, secondary)

- Hisashi Nogami, via [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/?p=197619): "The plaza is a hub area of sorts – a jumping off point for all the features and modes in the game."
- (fetched) You can inspect other players' gear in the plaza, so cosmetics work as social information.
- (fetched) The first prototype used plain blocks, and the look came after the play worked.

### 2.4 Animal Crossing: goals you pick, no end state (verified)

Aya Kyogoku and Katsuya Eguchi on New Leaf ([Game Developer, 2014](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/diversity-communication-and-i-animal-crossing-new-leaf-i-)):

- "we did make sure to add features that really allow users to come up with their own goals."
- "there's not an end goal where once you reach this goal, you finish it." This holds for New Leaf only; New Horizons rolls credits.
- (fetched) Eguchi: "there's time spent alone and time spent together", and "something in there that anybody could find interesting".

### 2.5 Jackbox: a room code and one task at a time (verified)

From [Built In Chicago (2020)](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/jackbox-games-design-party-pack), secondary:

- "They punch in a shared room code on a web page and are immediately connected." No app download is needed.
- "limit the user's choices, give them one task at a time ... Once the user plays a few, they have essentially learned how to play all of them."
- (fetched) Jackbox keeps a backlog of about 50 concepts, picks five per pack, and playtests weekly with groups of 3–8.
- **Caveat:** Jackbox locks rooms mid-round and offers an audience mode instead, so drop-in happens at the lobby.

### 2.6 Fall Guys: one sentence, players versus the course (fetched)

Joe Walsh in [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/feature/69833/how-mediatonic-made-the-takeshis-castle-of-video-games-in-fall-guys/), secondary:

- "We want to be able to explain what the users are doing within a sentence."
- "We want players to be able to jump in and have a good time without tutorials on every single level."
- Levels need "moments for epic one in a million chance impacts".
- The concept was "players versus the obstacle course", growing through seasons of new levels and costumes.

## 3. What the evidence supports

1. **Hanging out comes first.** Four rooms and chat brought strangers back daily. Mini-games and dressing up go on top of that (Club Penguin).
2. **Give each space one activity.** One room, one thing to do (Club Penguin); the hub is a jumping-off point (Splatoon, Habbo).
3. **Make separate spaces easy to join and leave together.** Board the trolley together, and teleport to a friend at any time (Toontown).
4. **Earn in mini-games, spend on yourself.** Spend on looks and a personal space you can open to others (Club Penguin igloos). The currency should be earned, not bought (Habbo).
5. **Hold parties with a free item for being there.** Leave unstaffed props for made-up play (Club Penguin).
6. **Let players set their own goals, with no end state** (Animal Crossing).
7. **Progress should add options, not power.** Toontown added more gags. Shared bonuses make veterans welcome rather than resented.
8. **Never gate personal content behind something friends can't all reach** (Club Penguin Island's paywall).
9. **One sentence, one task, no tutorial.** Make it players versus the course, with room for one-in-a-million moments (Fall Guys, Jackbox).

## 4. Gaps

- **Empty hubs with 1–6 players.** No verified evidence exists on how a room-based hub feels when almost empty. The one claim about this (an empty Ski Village on a dead fan server) was refuted. For a game with 1–6 friends, this is the key unknown: splitting three friends across rooms might leave every room empty.
- **Mini-game counts.** No verified claims on how many mini-games stick, or which ones, in small drop-in groups: WarioWare, Wii Sports, Card-Jitsu, Sled Racing, Cart Surfer and Mario Party all came back unverified.
- **Vehicles and exploration as hub connectors.** Not covered in this round. The earlier [progression-feel-exploration.md](progression-feel-exploration.md) covers vehicle feel (Lonely Mountains, Mario Kart Wii, TotK, cannon-es) and exploration (BotW, A Short Hike, Sable, Journey).
- **Personal progression without accounts.** No source covers per-player progression that lives only in the browser.
- **Incremental machines inside a social hub.** Not verified here; [progression-feel-exploration.md](progression-feel-exploration.md) covers incremental design in general.
