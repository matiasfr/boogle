# Ending and after: finish the railway, keep the world

> **Verdict:** End at the Home-port ring closure and keep the world afterward.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. End the campaign at the accepted Home–port ring closure, with optional branches outside completion. 2. Pay off a landmark visible since chapter one; keep the port guaranteed and the shard's role conditional. 3. Separate permanent completion from an optional, replayable celebration.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [Audit picks](../decisions.md#the-audit-picks-2-november).

_Research report, 2 November 2026. The ending of Boogle's roughly eight-hour rail campaign, celebration across uneven attendance, and life in a finished friend room. Live web research and repository inspection; structural alternatives for the owner and future story collaborators, not an approved finale or implementation plan._

**Working recommendation [H]:** finish when the last Home–port connection closes the ring, make that achievement permanent, and let friends revisit its celebration. Test **arrival at the long-visible port** against **a homecoming through what the group built** first. Keep the archipelago, trains and personal finds. A satisfying goodbye and a pleasant reason to return can coexist; neither requires an infinite progression economy.

**Verification.** **[V]** means fetched primary-source text or inspected repository code/documents; **[S]** means secondary reporting, community documentation or search-only evidence; **[H]** means a Boogle hypothesis or recommendation. Developer interviews are primary evidence of the developer's intent, not measured evidence of every player's response. GDC abstracts were read; their videos were not watched. No reference game was played or timed for this report. Prototype durations, production-cost comparisons and predictions about return behaviour are hypotheses, not estimates validated by production or retention data. Reference-game ending spoilers follow; no Boogle plot is proposed.

## What is already decided, and what this report adds

**[V]** [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) makes the railway the communal progression, with personal rewards, no hazards or decay, solo-capable drop-in play, and persistent server rooms. This report serves **Toys before chores**, **Communal world, personal rewards**, **Telegraph everything**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, and **Leave something for the next visitor**. It proposes no principle changes.

The [decision log's audit picks](../decisions.md#the-audit-picks-2-november) explicitly override older entries: six eras; preset links with some choice of order; a final Home–port link closing the ring; every segment hauled, with the network bringing goods closer; one project banked while nobody plays; thanks without order or ranking. The metropolis is visible at the start, walkable at the end, and built in its final state. Home grows only into a busy town. The archipelago remains a toy box after the finale. Older entries saying the ring is undecided do not reopen that audit pick.

The mayor, opening days, som and knacks remain parked. Existing Boogler lines stay intact. Collaborators may determine the story, the meaning of a landmark and who speaks; nothing here requires a mayor speech, revelation, rescue, catastrophe, departure or new antagonist. The recent cosmetic-first gift direction leaves useful personal rewards open. The roundhouse is a liked proposal waiting on the station test, not an existing post-game system. Crystal pillars have a liked construction direction in open question 19, but the distant shard's significance and finale role remain undesigned.

This adds the **completion contract**: what ends once, what can be experienced again, what absentees receive, and what remains worth doing. The options below stay downstream of [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md): an ending cannot rescue an uninteresting railway.

## What the reference games establish

### Big Walk: a shared finish need not close the hangout

**[V]** House House's [FAQ](https://bigwalk.game/faq/) explicitly describes a handcrafted beginning, middle and end, while permitting later visits simply to hang out. It also describes host-owned saves and a required cooperative group: its attendance and hosting model differs from Boogle's persistent solo-capable rooms. **[V, interview]** House House describes replacing an inconclusive playtest departure with an intentional group goodbye ([developer interview](https://kotaku.com/the-creators-of-big-walk-break-down-the-goty-contenders-development-puzzles-and-stunning-ending-2000725133)).

**[H] Boogle transfer:** give players a moment they can recognise as finishing together, then leave them somewhere playable. Do not require the original party to reconvene before the world can finish. A ritual can end an evening without ending access to the place.

### Death Stranding: completing infrastructure and resolving a story are different beats

**[V]** Kojima Productions' [Director's Cut guide](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/en/death-stranding-directors-cut-beginners-guide) describes connecting America and benefiting from other players' structures. **[S]** The ending returns to a final personal delivery; after credits, Episode 15 permits continued deliveries in a period set two weeks before the final events ([Episode 14 walkthrough](https://www.gamespot.com/gallery/death-stranding-full-walkthrough-episode-14-guide/2900-3213/), search text; [Gamepressure post-game guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/death-stranding/can-i-play-after-completing-the-game/zdccdc), fetched). This is a fictional time placement, not a requirement to erase the player's constructed network.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** let a large engineering achievement lead to a small human-scale arrival, ride or walk. The last emotional action need not be the largest material delivery. Avoid the temporal rewind: Boogle's completed railway should remain visibly completed, with later visitors living after that achievement. Story collaborators can add a personal beat without putting progress behind a long cinematic.

### Outer Wilds: familiar pieces make a new whole

**[V, composer interview]** Andrew Prahlow describes individual travellers' instruments designed to combine; the article describes their final campfire ensemble ([music interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-hauntingly-beautiful-music-of-i-outer-wilds-i-/)). **[V, abstract only]** Alex Beachum's [GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027368/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity) describes curiosity-driven exploration and knowledge-led progression. The ending's emotional material is encountered before its final assembly, rather than introduced as a new checklist at the end.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** places, material shapes or musical phrases established during construction can become recognisable together when the network closes. This is a compositional hypothesis, not permission to introduce an instrument hunt, timed group chord, cosmic destruction or a mystery whose answer is required to build. A continued friend world needs its own post-ending contract; the campfire alone does not supply one.

### A Short Hike: the promised destination can be enough

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu describes the summit as a respite and a fresh perspective, with optional fishing, boating and volleyball around the climb ([developer account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)). The main destination and optional activities have different jobs.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** reaching a place seen for hours can be a sufficient payoff if being there changes the view and understanding of the journey. A walkable port quay looking back toward Home is more valuable than an enormous skyline that cannot be entered. Leave toys and overlooked corners available without declaring them unfinished obligations. The source supports a design approach, not a measured claim that a short summit treatment will satisfy an eight-hour group campaign.

### Journey: a visible destination and recognition of companions

**[V]** [thatgamecompany's description](https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/) frames anonymous companionship around a looming mountaintop. Jenova Chen's [GDC abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018751/Designing) describes a deliberately designed emotional arc. **[S]** The end credits identify companions encountered along the journey, rather than only presenting a final pair ([community documentation](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2725750756), search text).

**[H] Boogle transfer:** remember the people who were part of the campaign across sessions. A final-night attendance photo is lovely but insufficient credit. Keep the visible goal and the recognition; Boogle does not need Journey's anonymity, peril or restart structure. A friends' railway should show names without turning hours, deliveries or the last bolt into a ranking.

### Valheim: completion can coexist with a home worth inhabiting

**[V]** Iron Gate's [1.0 FAQ](https://www.valheimgame.com/support/valheim-1-0-faq/) preserves old worlds while explaining the limitations of generating new content in already explored terrain; beginning again is a choice. **[S]** The [1.0 review](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/valheim-review/) reports an ending followed by continued play, and describes building and decorating as pleasures alongside progression.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** a place the group recognises and has shaped can remain a destination after the frontier ends. Preserve old engines and first structures. Do not import bosses, raids, upkeep or the assumption that enormous procedural terrain is needed for a lasting home. An optional separate fresh room is compatible with keeping the original; resetting the shared room is not the default.

### Satisfactory: make the campaign's cumulative work visible at its endpoint

**[V]** Coffee Stain's [1.0 release account](https://coffeestain.com/news/satisfactory-1-0-launch/) describes Project Assembly's structure expanding above the Space Elevator across phases and the final Phase 5/Tier 9 additions. **[S]** Community documentation records completion and permission to continue production ([story documentation](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Story), indexed text; the wiki was not fully accessible).

**[H] Boogle transfer:** the endpoint should visibly depend on the network players assembled. The final bridge can contain recognisable structural parts supplied by earlier places. Do not adopt a final order whose appeal is principally very large quantities. Boogle's payoff is connection and access; factory optimisation, routing and ever-larger throughput targets would change the game. A formal success state can release pressure while leaving machines working.

### Factorio: a victory condition does not have to consume the sandbox

**[S]** The [official-hosted community wiki](https://wiki.factorio.com/Rocket_silo) distinguishes the base game's rocket victory from continued play. Space Age moves the endpoint beyond the rocket to the [solar system edge](https://wiki.factorio.com/Solar_system_edge), so “the rocket ends Factorio” needs that version qualification. **[V]** Wube's [Galaxy of Fame](https://www.factorio.com/galaxy) makes completed Space Age runs something to inspect and share.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** declare a finite success while allowing further use of the same network. A completed room can become a place to show friends, not a machine that must constantly improve. Borrow the clear endpoint and inspectable result; do not make throughput comparison, leaderboards or repeated launches the post-game requirement.

### Dorfromantik: change the relationship to the same landscape

**[V, developer notes via mirror]** The [Creative Mode update](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/7246821/) describes unlimited tiles, skipping and deleting tiles, and continuing an exhausted Classic run in Creative Mode. The original Steam announcement did not expose its full body here; the fetched text is a mirror of the developer's release notes.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** finishing can turn a goal-led landscape into an expressive one without throwing it away. Boogle should make the transition explicit: the main railway is done; now use it, dress it and explore it. Do not copy unrestricted deletion into a shared room where other people's work is protected. Unlimited material alone also supplies neither good places nor meaningful choices.

### Animal Crossing: an open end is supported by personal projects and other people's traces

**[V, developer interview]** Katsuya Eguchi discusses asynchronous family connection through a shared world, letters and message boards, and players setting their own goals ([interview](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/interview/11229/animal-crossing-interview)). **[S]** In New Horizons, K.K. Slider's first concert leads to the Island Designer tools ([guide](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons-terraforming-n-how-to-unlock-k-k-slider-and-the-island-designer-app)). A milestone can open expression instead of closing play.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** let friends leave a train appearance, keepsake display or completed little place for the next visitor. Keep gifts and visits available on the group's schedule. Calendar-exclusive items, chores and a resident with superior authority are unnecessary to this benefit. Meaningful customisation still needs a bounded authoring and ownership design; “be like Animal Crossing” is not a cheap feature specification.

### Stardew Valley: perfection is a different promise from finishing the main arc

**[V, release documentation]** The [1.5 changelog](https://www.stardewvalley.net/stardew-valley-1-5-update-full-changelog/) documents a perfection tracker, a summit event and further rewards at full completion. This is an extensive completionist goal with a distinct acknowledgement, not simply an instruction to continue farming indefinitely.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** optional collecting can have its own recognition after the railway ends. Do not make the eight-hour ending require every rare cosmetic, mini-game, optional branch or absent friend's personal progress. Start without a global 100% meter: it would recast the generous toy box as a list of deficits. If a completionist layer is later wanted, make its finite scope and independence from the main ending explicit.

### Astroneer: repeated local discoveries can converge at one readable object

**[S]** The [Gateway Portal documentation](https://astroneer.wiki.gg/wiki/Gateway_Portal), available here through indexed text, places the original core progression's ending at a satellite activated with the planets' geometric triptychs; it triggers a cutscene and credits. Community reports describe resuming the save afterwards, but the exact return location was not verified and is not a design dependency. **[V]** System Era's later [Awakening update](https://blog.astroneer.space/p/awakening/) adds a separate mission chain; it should not be conflated with the original core endpoint.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** a final assembly can make separate places visibly belong together. Use the already-built network as the proof; do not ask players to revisit every camp to fetch a duplicate key after its work is complete. Crystals can provide a consistent visual language without deciding that the shard is an alien machine or opening another mandatory campaign.

### Sky: a repeatable climax has costs as well as benefits

**[V]** thatgamecompany's [Eye of Eden help page](https://thatgamecompany.helpshift.com/hc/en/17-sky-children-of-the-light/faq/590-what-is-eye-of-eden/) describes a dangerous final zone, exchanging Winged Light for Ascended Candles, a weekly reset, and a choice to wait for others or move on.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** allow people to accompany a friend through a meaningful ending again. Reject the attrition, weekly currency opportunity and repeated recovery work: all would fight Boogle's no-hazards, no-decay direction. Replaying a celebration should not require earning back access or becoming the best source of rewards. Keep the distinction between a replayable experience and a resetting economy.

### Deep Rock Galactic: a reason for another outing, with a comfortable return

**[V]** The [developer's game description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/548430/Deep_Rock_Galactic/) offers procedural cooperative missions. The [Season 5 announcements](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=548430&enddate=1718276526&feed=steam_community_announcements) describe another bounded expedition and returning to the hub; season selection lets friends pursue different historical passes together. That restoration is not total: the announcement excludes original season-specific assignments.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** “let's take this train to that place and come home” is a usable evening shape. It needs an enjoyable excursion, not a login streak. DRG's procedural missions, combat and reward catalog are substantial production systems; Boogle cannot promise equivalent endless freshness from a few optional rail branches. Borrow finite outings and non-expiring access, not a service-game content obligation.

### Mini Metro: the functioning network can become the toy

**[V]** The [developer's Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/287980/Mini_Metro/) distinguishes Normal play from stress-free Endless and Creative modes, with map sharing and procedural audio.

**[H] Boogle transfer:** a railway can remain enjoyable to watch and alter after the main pressure is gone. Preserve train rides, departures and cosmetic choices while avoiding passenger failure, timetable optimisation or a score that demands more capacity. Mini Metro supports the possibility of another mode of attention; it does not prove that watching Boogle's preset network will retain a group for months.

## What final construction would actually feel complete?

**[H] Complete the promise the player has been reading.** The port has the strongest established claim: it is visible from the beginning and reachable at the end. The final Home–port link makes the long outward journey into one connected circuit. Under [six-eras](../proposals/six-eras.md), the frontier reaches the port before the direct link closes the ring; those can be the approach and completion beats of one final chapter. Do not label the first arrival “campaign complete” and then reveal another compulsory railway bill.

The last build should use the established action: carry a final manageable segment's materials, see them become solid structure, and ride across. Show that earlier camps now supply the workfront and that different eras remain in the finished landscape. A particularly legible final joint, receiving platform or joined pair of approaches can distinguish this construction without requiring a new endgame resource, a boss substitute or every optional spur. Every required place should belong to the connected network; “whole world” need not mean covering every shoreline in rail.

**Should the future line reach the hour-one shard? [H] Yes to paying off a visible promise; conditionally to making this shard the final promise.** The prototype has two different things: the moving objective beacon in [signs.ts](../../src/proto/place/signs.ts), and `buildFar()`'s distant island, broken ring and bright shard in [land.ts](../../src/proto/place/land.ts). Neither is an approved finale site. An owner reading the beacon as a floating crystal does not establish a plot or a map connection.

There are three coherent placements:

- **Earlier payoff:** reach the shard during the outward chain, learn something or encounter crystal construction there, then let the port remain the final horizon. This pays curiosity sooner and avoids holding every mystery until hour eight.
- **One final arrival:** position the shard at or alongside the port approach so the future line reaches the already-promised destination and the strange landmark together. This needs a deliberate geography decision; do not silently move an existing island into the metropolis.
- **Optional after-journey:** the completed railway makes a short excursion to the shard convenient. Label it optional before the campaign closes, and do not put the only explanation of the main ending behind it.

A separate mandatory shard line after Home–port would change the settled endpoint. Do not add it under the name of post-game. Nor should the future train simply bypass the first eight hours: its final route should reveal the value of the earlier railway, with old trains still useful and visible.

## Completion, celebration and attendance are three separate things

**[H] Save completion once; let celebration be revisited.** The authoritative acceptance of the final required connection records completion. Queuing its edit, playing an animation, boarding a train or rolling credits does not. A nearby prompt can announce that the next segment completes the ring, with a clear consequence before delivery. Anyone can finish it alone. No full-lobby button, attendance timer or mayor approval.

Afterwards, a permanent platform marker offers the chosen celebratory ride or view. Present players may go together, leave, or continue exploring. A returning friend can experience it with veterans later; that is a commemorative journey through the completed world, not pretending the construction just happened again. This cannot restore the original shared surprise: the tradeoff is that nobody's absence blocks everyone else's progress. Replays must not move every player, interrupt ordinary service or issue another completion reward. Let players keep camera control, sit through the existing Interact convention, disembark normally and opt out of large presentation effects. A quiet acknowledgement still communicates success.

An example attendance case makes the policy concrete:

| Person                                                              | What they should see and receive [H]                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Built the first bridge, missed the last three sessions              | The ring is visibly finished; a brief “since your visit” notice; their name in campaign recognition; any completion keepsake waiting without expiry; the invitation to take the commemorative route. |
| Helped explore and rode with friends, never delivered the last load | Recognition as a companion, without requiring resource contribution or inventing a construction claim.                                                                                               |
| Finished alone after others logged out                              | Immediate completion and acknowledgement, with no implication that they alone built the world.                                                                                                       |
| Disconnected during the final build or presentation                 | Correct completed world on reconnect; no duplicate grant; replay available.                                                                                                                          |
| First-ever visit to an already completed room                       | Honest welcome to the group's finished railway, full access to shared transport, and their own beginning. No fictitious founder credit.                                                              |

**Credit the campaign's people, not its final lobby.** Store an inclusive set of companions who actually entered the campaign before completion, and keep contribution provenance separately where it exists. A person needs no minimum playtime to count as a companion. Show equal-weight names with no numeric order, totals or podium. Do not populate empty seats with avatars implying absent friends attended; a remembrance display can name them honestly. Freeze the historical completion record while allowing later visitors their own memories.

[roster.ts](../../src/world/rules/roster.ts) already persists server-stamped player identities and names. It is a useful identity source, not a complete campaign attendance or event ledger. [welcome-back.ts](../../src/features/welcome-back.ts) already explains changes without blocking entry; add completion as another remembered fact when implemented. [identity.ts](../../src/player/identity.ts) currently identifies a browser, so “waiting for you” cannot promise automatic cross-device recovery. Account portability is separate work, not a prerequisite for a first-room test.

## What friends do afterwards

**[V]** The world staying open is already decided. **[H]** Keep that literal: same room, same railway, same towns, same personal collection. No prestige wipe, decay, compulsory new era or fictional rollback. A separate fresh room could later offer another campaign to willing friends without changing this one; today's **Start fresh** identity flow is not a shared-world reset tool.

Offer a small menu of purposes in the world, not a mandatory post-game checklist:

| Return invitation [H]          | What must remain enjoyable                                                                                            | Scope and limit                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “Come see what we made.”       | A ride from the hub, a recognisable first bridge, a view of the completed ring, room to stop and talk.                | Reuses places and transport; a tour is not new campaign content.                              |
| “Try my ridiculous train.”     | Personal cosmetic finds mounted or previewed without consuming them; a quick departure to see the result move.        | Needs explicit ownership. Trying a shared display never transfers or spends its owner's item. |
| “Let's go out to that corner.” | Optional walks, toys and a few authored side projects reachable by rail.                                              | A finite reserve, not an endless generator. No requirement that a group complete all of it.   |
| “Show our new friend around.”  | Immediate shared access and a small thing the newcomer can make their own.                                            | Social meaning differs from doing an eight-hour campaign from scratch.                        |
| “Let's use the roundhouse.”    | One bounded train choice, a visible turntable and a satisfying dispatch, if the station test establishes this is fun. | No mandatory schedules, breakdowns, supply balancing or unattended upkeep.                    |

**Rewards live on as identity and expression [H].** Deliver a generous finale package of individually owned cosmetics, with source and campaign context, to eligible companions regardless of final-night attendance. Keep the personal original when it is displayed on a shared train; another person selecting a train cannot spend it. The exact guest-preview policy needs its own choice before implementation. Later visitors can earn or receive the same cosmetic family through an explicitly labelled welcome or later activity; historical provenance distinguishes the original occasion without making the appearance forever unobtainable.

After the finite campaign's generous payouts, make dressing, combining and trying owned finds freely repeatable. An optional source of new variants needs a finite, disclosed catalog or an authored generator, duplicate handling and its own budget; it is not assumed here. Do not fund returns with tiny drop chances, weekly exclusives, streaks, loss while absent or endless stat inflation. Shared train and town upgrades can remain at their attained levels; a heritage engine need not win a speed contest to be worth taking out. The latest [cosmetics-first decision](../decisions.md) is the baseline, not this report's permission for useful rare items.

**Hypothesis to test:** familiar places plus expressive trains will support occasional reunions. None of the sources establishes that this alone creates frequent long-term retention. Friends being satisfied enough to stop is also success for a finite eight-hour game. If sustained return is an owner goal, it requires demonstrated enjoyable repeatable play or more authored content; do not hide that cost behind “toy box.”

### A newcomer in a finished room

**[H]** Use the existing hub arrival, not an automatic teleport to a veteran or a compulsory finale movie. Explain the present in one small line: this group has connected the railway; ride it or make something your own. Give access to world-level transport and any required traversal capability without replaying personal progression that would separate friends. The shared world already earned that access.

Offer one authentic small action: fit a welcome cosmetic to a personal display position, help with an unfinished optional project, or choose a departure together. A personal display position can be reused; do not consume a finite “newcomer job” on every account. Do not detach an old track segment so the newcomer can pretend to rebuild it. A new person may share the celebratory route immediately, but their first keepsake should say they joined this world, not that they founded it.

This cannot recreate discovery of an unbuilt archipelago. Acknowledge that tradeoff: joining friends is the primary promise in a finished room; a fresh campaign belongs in a separate room if the group wants one. The entry seam is [arrival.ts](../../src/player/arrival.ts), not a second onboarding application.

### State architecture: ordinary engine practice, adapted to Boogle

**[V]** Unity's [RPC versus NetworkVariable guidance](https://mp-docs.dl.it.unity3d.com/netcode/2.3.2/learn/rpcvnetvar/) explicitly asks whether late joiners need a fact; persistent state supplies it, transient RPCs do not. Unreal [replicated properties](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/replicate-actor-properties-in-unreal-engine) propagate authoritative values, and Godot's [MultiplayerSynchronizer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_multiplayersynchronizer.html) synchronises configured properties from an authority. None of those replication mechanisms alone is a disk-save policy.

**[H]** Apply that separation through Boogle's [world contract](../../src/world/README.md) and [rules recipe](../../src/world/rules/README.md), rather than adding an engine-style cinematic framework:

- **Durable shared facts:** completion ID/version and tick, the required connection set, historical companion references and any shared claim receipts belong in registered, serialised, hashed Lockstep state. Completion follows accepted construction state; reject duplicate finalisation. Derive presentation from snapshots for late joiners. Do not put durable completion in Host presence, which can restart.
- **Personal state:** ownership and acknowledgement follow the eventual personal reward system. If one-time claim receipts are saved in the room, keep recipient identity and item ID explicit; displaying an object is not transferring ownership. This report does not choose a new account backend.
- **Transient experience:** Local camera, particles and sound read those facts. An optional shared replay invitation can be a validated room message; missing it cannot lose completion or a reward. If a replay needs mid-ride synchronisation, expose its current start tick as appropriate session state, not a new permanent completion event.
- **Physical train:** keep the existing transport/physics authority. An actor standing on a moving train never becomes Lockstep physics because a finale uses the train.

The old [opening rules](../../src/world/rules/opening.ts) demonstrate saved timing but embody an older opening-day design; do not revive their gathering condition. Likewise, the [network prototype's](../../src/proto/net/index.ts) `land()` callback is not authoritative campaign completion. When implementation reaches these contracts, use the existing replay/save-load tests for deterministic facts and a narrowly justified multiplayer scenario for reconnect/late-join claims, following the repository's test diet.

**Chapter one needs seams, not a finale platform [H].** Reserve the port sightline, keep one first construction recognisable, and use stable connection and reward identifiers as those systems graduate. Preserve only the companion/provenance facts an approved recognition design needs; do not record hours of motion or speculative analytics. Do not implement all four finales, an event editor or the roundhouse while the station loop is still being tested. No box in [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md) is completed by this research.

## Four distinct ending shapes for the owner

Every option keeps the settled ring closure, persistent room and optional replay. The choice is **what finishing feels like**, not which plot is true. Test alternatives separately before combining anything; four climaxes in sequence would dilute the ending and inflate the last chapter. All test setups and durations below are **[H]**, not work already performed.

### A. Arrival at the horizon — “We can finally stand there”

**Shape.** Complete Home–port, cross the new direct line and step onto the long-visible waterfront. The memorable view reverses the opening view: Home and the routes between it and the port are now behind you. The city remains somewhere to walk, with the train ready for another trip. The shard can share this arrival only if the owner picks that geography; no explanation is required here.

**What is new in the test:** the distant silhouette becomes a small walkable arrival, with a view back over your railway. **Gut question:** “Did getting here feel like finishing?”

**Smallest test.** Reuse the place prototype's construction and ride, mock a completed network, leave one final span, and provide two platforms and a small solid quay. Show the distant view first, then let the owner finish and arrive in roughly five minutes. Use no speech or reward shower in the first comparison. Agents check solo completion, an off-train friend, a later arrival and the return path. A fresh miniature test evaluates legibility; it cannot establish eight hours of anticipation.

**Chapter-one consequence.** Reserve the skyline and final receiving platform now; make its silhouette recognisable at the actual camera. Seed at least one later reverse view of Home. Do not promise a giant explorable city in the skyline while budgeting only a platform.

**Cost and failure.** Cheapest structural test, but the final walkable port is real environment work. Fails if it feels like one more station or an attractive wall. Preferred first baseline because it directly pays an existing promise.

### B. Homecoming — “Look what our little line became”

**Shape.** The last link completes the ring; the optional celebratory trip brings friends back toward Home through familiar work. The emotional destination is the transformed starting place. Keep the first wooden structure and heritage train recognisable. This is an ordinary ride with deliberately good views, not the cut wagon show, forced side-on camera or onboard chores.

**What is new in the test:** a short route passes the first span, a grown town and a later crossing before returning home. **Gut question:** “Did coming home make the journey feel complete?”

**Smallest test.** Use three blockout landmarks and a short test loop, with the same final construction as A. Give the owner a brief first encounter with the starting place, then compare its changed view on the ride. Reuse the normal train and controls. Agents check disembarking, a missed departure and joining a later service; never require a full real-size ring lap to see the test's point.

**Chapter-one consequence.** Keep the first bridge, station approach and town silhouette as later viewing anchors. Protect return sightlines when laying out future districts. This requires selective continuity of places, not storing every historical world state.

**Cost and failure.** Medium composition work on an existing route; expensive if turned into a cinematic spanning every island. Fails if the ride feels like a compulsory commute or a history tour of places nobody recognises. A full-campaign test is particularly important here.

### C. The connected world answers — “All these places belong together”

**Shape.** Ring closure causes a brief, legible response across connected stations: local light, a material motif or a musical phrase resolves into a whole. Then the world settles into its normal living state. A shard might be one response point, but whether it is natural, engineered or unexplained stays for collaborators. No threat is defeated, no compulsory chord is played and no old place is destroyed.

**What is new in the test:** three already-connected landmarks visibly respond to the final joint. **Gut question:** “Did that make the whole railway feel connected?”

**Smallest test.** Three simple visible markers, a final span and a brief authored sequence, followed by quiet. Use existing materials and audio infrastructure; no new post-processing system. Compare with the same completion without the sequence. Agents inspect from each location, with audio off, while looking away, and after reconnecting. A permanent changed marker must still explain completion if the transient response was missed.

**Chapter-one consequence.** Introduce the motif at the first genuine connection and repeat it sparingly at later places. If a musical culmination is selected, composition starts early; random new finale music cannot establish recognition retrospectively. Preserve the distant shard's recognisability without assigning it a plot.

**Cost and failure.** Medium for three nearby cues; high for bespoke effects across a huge archipelago and an elaborate score. Fails if it reads as unrelated fireworks, another collectible gate, or an unexplained world change. Do not reinstate the cut opening-day sounds as placeholders for an approved sound direction.

### D. The railway is yours to play with — a roundhouse handover

**Shape.** After closing the ring, friends bring the future train into a roundhouse that holds the history of their fleet and personal finds. The ending is the release from expansion into choosing a train, dressing it and taking it out together. Normal services continue: selecting an exhibit cannot strand another visitor. This handover is a change of purpose, not a new owner, shared wallet or management obligation.

**What is new in the test:** one turntable choice lets you take a recognisably personal train onto the finished line. **Gut question:** “Now it's finished, do you want to take another train out?”

**Smallest test.** Only after the station test: two existing engine representations, two cosmetic fittings, a simple turntable and one safe departure onto an existing line. No upgrade tree, full fleet simulation or new economy. Agents check competing selections, personal item ownership, leaving the room and a newcomer using the shared transport. A static display alone cannot establish whether dispatch is fun.

**Chapter-one consequence.** Keep early trains identifiable, introduce an empty cosmetic mount before its first gift, and reserve a plausible depot footprint. If selected later, the roundhouse can open at the first island connection as already proposed; do not withhold the entire feature until the ending.

**Cost and failure.** Cheap only if the roundhouse earns its place independently; otherwise the most feature-heavy option. Fails if the “reward” is a management menu or assembling outfits is the only reason to use an uninteresting railway. It must pass the no-reward toy test as well as a generous-reward version.

### What these small tests cannot establish

**[H]** Compare A and B first using the same ending state; choose C if a world response proves more meaningful, or D if train expression becomes a strong toy. A may still have a quiet shared keepsake, but do not automatically stack all four. Record one gut answer and what happened, not a questionnaire for the owner.

Before committing to final production, run a real multi-evening campaign with uneven attendance: someone leaves early, someone misses the finish, someone joins after it, and someone plays alone. Agents handle save/load, duplicate rewards and late-join edge cases. Observe whether the group recognises completion, whether the absentee can reconnect with the achievement, and what anyone voluntarily does next. Offer a later unscheduled return opportunity without a reward deadline. A short vertical slice cannot validate an eight-hour emotional payoff or long-term return rate.

## Numbered recommendations

1. **End the campaign at the accepted Home–port ring closure, with optional branches outside completion.** Serve **Telegraph everything**; preserve the [audit pick](../decisions.md#the-audit-picks-2-november) and budget the approach and finish together in [eight-hour pacing](eight-hour-pacing.md). No extra final ore tier or 100% collection gate. **Smallest check [H]:** the last ordinary segment clearly changes the room from building the main line to having finished it.
2. **Pay off a landmark visible since chapter one; keep the port guaranteed and the shard's role conditional.** Serve **Telegraph everything** through [six-eras](../proposals/six-eras.md), [crystals and wonder](crystals-and-wonder.md) and the separate beacon/shard in [place](../../src/proto/place/land.ts). **Smallest check [H]:** compare the opening sightline with a walkable reverse view; ask whether arriving fulfils that promise.
3. **Separate permanent completion from an optional, replayable celebration.** Serve **Designed for drop-in, drop-out** using the [world rules contract](../../src/world/rules/README.md), not the old [opening-day gate](../../src/world/rules/opening.ts). **Smallest check [H]:** finish alone, reconnect, then revisit with a friend without rebuilding, duplicate rewards or taking control away from others.
4. **Recognise campaign companions equally and preserve absent friends' rewards without expiry.** Serve **Communal world, personal rewards**; build on [roster identity](../../src/world/rules/roster.ts), honest provenance in [maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md), and the nonblocking [welcome-back seam](../../src/features/welcome-back.ts). **Smallest check [H]:** an early companion missing the finale is remembered; a newcomer is welcomed without invented founder credit.
5. **Keep the completed room intact, with further play and any fresh campaign explicitly separate.** Serve **Leave something for the next visitor** and the [open-toy-box decision](../decisions.md). Preserve trains, town growth and personal collections; no prestige reset or fictional rollback. **Smallest check [H]:** reload after the ending and take an ordinary trip through the same changed world.
6. **Let generous personal rewards become free expression after the campaign, with no scarcity deadline or stat treadmill.** Serve **Toys before chores** and the [cosmetics-first decision](../decisions.md), refining [maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md). **Smallest check [H]:** friends enjoy trying and showing owned fittings when no new drop is promised; replaying the finale is never the optimal farm.
7. **Use the roundhouse only if its own bounded choice and dispatch are fun.** Serve **Toys before chores**; keep the [roundhouse proposal](roundhouse-management.md) behind the station work in [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md). **Smallest check [H]:** two train choices and one departure invite another ride without schedules, upkeep or a management tree.
8. **Give new players immediate shared access and an honest personal beginning in a finished world.** Serve **Designed for drop-in, drop-out** through [arrival.ts](../../src/player/arrival.ts); offer a reusable personal expression action, not catch-up grinding or dismantled progress. **Smallest check [H]:** a fresh browser joins at the hub and can reach friends and make one thing its own.
9. **Offer reunions, tours, expressive trains and finite excursions as invitations, while allowing the game to be finished.** Serve **Toys before chores**; update the assumptions behind [friend-group return](friend-group-return.md), without importing attendance rewards, daily pressure or an unfunded endless-content promise. **Smallest check [H]:** observe what friends choose on a later return with no deadline or bonus.
10. **Choose one of the four ending shapes through small comparative tests, then validate it across a real campaign.** Serve **Small and readable** and **Telegraph everything**; reserve only the selected chapter-one sightlines, heritage, motifs or depot seams in [six-eras](../proposals/six-eras.md) and the next [place test](../plans/find-the-fun.md). Start with A versus B; keep story authorship open. **Smallest check [H]:** one gut answer per short prototype, followed by an uneven-attendance multi-evening test before calling the ending proven.
