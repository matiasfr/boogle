# Audio architecture for Boogle

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Native Web Audio for a co-op railway world, with M4 iPad Pro Safari and a controller as the principal listening and performance target. Project-specific architecture and migration advice; no runtime changes._

**Recommendation:** keep Boogle's one AudioContext and existing sound identities. Add a small audio service that owns admission, routing, lifetimes, spatial perspective, assets and interruptions. Use procedural sound for responsive toys and the chosen babble voice, recordings for material detail and sustained environments, and the owner's music for selected crossing reveals. No audio library earns a dependency yet.

## 1. The owner's decisions constrain the architecture

The [Sound decision](../decisions.md#sound-listening-pack-audit-2026-11-01) keeps prompt/pop, deny, the haul count direction, honks, mini-game sounds, hut construction, board whoosh and the track machine. It rejects the synthesized groove takes, wagon whistle/clacks, freight thumps, stoke, grind, soapbox sounds and opening-day call/bell/swap. **The wave remains unchanged pending play.** A new mixer is not permission to restore rejected sounds, trim The Boogler's copy, or redesign every kept instrument.

Animal Crossing-style **formant-synth babble is selected and implemented**. The earlier synth-versus-sample-bank proposal is answered. Ratchet & Clank bolts remain the named pickup reference; the actual material and progression should be Boogle's. The owner wants calmer, less white-noise-heavy ambience, independent honks that form chords, and eventually their own music. House/UK garage production expertise is a reason to expose clean stems, trims and cue markers, not to invent a generic backing groove.

The 2 November direction is a roughly eight-hour game with **six** eras: wood, steam, iron/harbour, diesel/concrete, electric, future. Cargo visibly grows towns, many little residents make them inhabited, each rail segment still involves the player, and music rewards beautiful crossings. Maximal rewards do not require every subsystem to remain loud. Electric does not erase the industrial world. Current `place` and crossing prototypes are evidence of mechanics, not a finished global audio design; the proposed first crossing and express timings are not universal train speeds. Keep useful prototypes and their dependencies in place.

## 2. What actually exists

**[C] Boogle already has a mixer.** [audio.ts](../../src/render/audio.ts) creates one context and five independent gain buses. Music is trimmed **−13 dB**, voice **−12 dB**, ambience/SFX/UI **0 dB**. Music and ambience share a **−9 dB** voice duck. Everything reaches a master gain of `0.8 × volume²`, then a compressor with threshold **−20 dB** and ratio **6:1**, then the destination. Master volume and mute persist. These are parameter values, not measured output loudness.

### Real problems, in order

1. **No common admission or lifetime contract.** Local gates exist, but overlapping pickup bursts, workers, train arrivals and celebrations can all pass their independent gates. Stop/fade/dispose and future-schedule cancellation are inconsistent. Most one-shots stop their sources; lack of explicit `disconnect()` alone does **not** prove a memory leak.
2. **Mix ownership is split.** Bus gain, hidden helper gain, recipe gain and distance gain all contribute without a shared meter or catalogue. The master precedes compression, so moving the volume slider also changes the compressor's operating level. A 6:1 compressor is not a guaranteed true-peak brickwall limiter.
3. **Distance and meaning disagree.** Camera versus player position, different falloffs, mostly centred world sound and UI-routed physical pickups are all present. UI feedback can intentionally be nonspatial; physical contact should not change category accidentally because it reuses a clink.
4. **Boolean ducking and stale clocks cannot scale to residents.** One speaker can release another's duck. NPC duck release occurs when the final syllable is scheduled, before its audible tail ends. A retained lane needs an interruption policy rather than relying only on `getAudio()` refusing new work.
5. **Assets and interruptions have no shared policy.** There is no decoded-memory ledger, active-place loading contract, coherent music readiness check or central hidden/return reset. These are missing capabilities, not measured failures on today's device.
6. **The listening harness conceals some comparisons.** It peak-normalizes takes to −1 dBFS, up to 40 dB boost, and trims one-shots. Its manifest preserves raw peak/RMS, but equal sample peaks are not equal perceived loudness and trimmed files cannot prove response latency. Existing cut-moment checks are not coverage of every cut sound.

Do not consolidate different artistic recipes just because both contain a sine and an envelope. Share identical math, noise and lifetime machinery; keep wood, voice and honk definitions distinct.

## 4. Mixer, definitions and voice ownership

### Bus tree and mix states

Keep the public root buses while moving their implementation behind the existing facade. The eventual graph is:

```text
voice (foreground babble; ambient barks separately controlled) ─┐
ui (menus and personal confirmations) ─────────────────────────┤
sfx ─ contact / machinery / social subgroups as needed ────────┤
ambience ─ natural / settlement subgroups as needed ───────────┤→ mix processing → user master/mute → output
music ─ cue/stem gains ────────────────────────────────────────┤
optional shared short world reflection return ────────────────┘
```

Each root needs separate **authored trim, user setting and temporary mix/duck gain**. Do not implement all three by overwriting one AudioParam. A music slider must stay muted through conversation release or a zone crossfade. Initially retain today's five buses, exact effective levels, master order and existing volume keys; moving user master after processing is a subsequent calibrated change. Subgroups only appear when they solve an actual mix conflict. Future player voice chat, if built, gets its own independently controlled route, not NPC duck rules by accident.

The eventual routing rule is semantic: the log's physical landing/body belongs to spatial SFX; the personal count/payout accent belongs to UI. A single pickup may deliberately contain both, with one admission bundle. Compare this split against today's kept balance before changing it; do not move the entire magnet recipe to another bus and accidentally make it louder.

Use named, small mix states such as `conversation`, `crossing-highlight` and `menu`, combined with explicit ownership. `duck(reason, targets)` returns a releasable token. Multiple claims resolve to the strongest required attenuation per target, rather than adding every claim's dB. A conversation token lives through the last audible syllable/tail and is released on skip, leave, interruption and disposal. Ambient resident babble does not duck the whole world.

Initial audition: foreground speech lowers music by 6–9 dB, continuous machines by 3–6 dB and natural ambience by 3–6 dB, while contacts and honks remain legible. Ramp over roughly 30–60 ms and release over 200–400 ms, then listen for pumping. These are **ramp durations**, not `setTargetAtTime` time constants; today's 30/250 ms constants approach their targets gradually. During an approved highlight, reduce sustained train/air masking by 3–6 dB instead of raising music into the ceiling. [DynamicsCompressorNode](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/DynamicsCompressorNode) has one input, not an external sidechain input **[V]**; explicit duck envelopes need no custom DSP compressor.

### Definitions as data; recipes as functions

Define semantic IDs close to their domain: `wood.chop`, `log.seat`, `rail.fastener`, `machine.chuff`, `train.roll`, `resident.effort`, `pickup.count`, `social.honk`. Each typed definition supplies route, reference trim in dB, sample variants or a recipe, priority tier, per-emitter/per-family cap, retrigger interval, spatial range, maximum tail and source-cost estimate. Continuous emitters additionally expose meaningful parameters such as speed/load/effort. Do not introduce a generic JSON DSP graph, a giant central switch, or an editor before these definitions prove useful.

The play request supplies emitter ID, position, optional event ID and relevant parameters. The renderer receives a `BaseAudioContext`, destination, start time and injectable random source; it returns a handle with `stop(at, fade)`, `dispose()` and completion information. A **handle owns every source, future start, envelope and auxiliary node** in its gesture. Sources report completion; pool slots are not reclaimed only when another sound happens. Exceptions and failed decoding release reservations. Context generation changes invalidate pending asynchronous work.

`AudioBufferSourceNode` is deliberately single-use; its decoded buffer can be shared and fresh sources are cheap ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioBufferSourceNode), **[V]**). Pool logical slots and reusable routing resources where useful, not already-played buffer source nodes. Reset pooled gain/pan automation before reuse. Long loops need real source handles; fading to zero is not virtualization.

### Admission and stealing

Use **two ceilings**: logical gestures/loops and scheduled-or-active native sources, including LFOs, delayed bounces, music overlap and steal-release tails. Count filters, panners, convolution and decoded bytes alongside them for diagnosis. A 15-source clink and a one-buffer hit cannot have the same cost assumption.

Provisional starting envelope, aligned with the parallel construction report: **24 steady / 32 peak logical voices, 96 source nodes maximum**, with no more than eight world spatial paths initially. These are experimental application ceilings, not Web Audio/iPad limits. Count releasing voices against the hard limits. Keep four logical slots and sixteen source slots of the ceiling as headroom for protected new gestures and releases; if there is no safe room, reject a lower-ranked request rather than exceeding the cap during a fade. Reservations are atomic for the selected recipe's maximum overlapping cost. Several full clinks can exhaust the source budget long before 24 logical voices: simplify decorative modes/bounces or pre-render a variant, not silently oversubscribe.

| Priority              | First candidates                                                                 | When crowded                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Protected foreground  | Addressed dialogue, UI confirmation, the player's direct contact                 | Reserve capacity. Repeated held input still has a rate gate. Keep the essential attack; simplify its sweetener first.                                         |
| Social / nearby focal | Friends' honks, nearest visible machine, foreground train, an admitted music cue | Preserve identity/continuity. Reduce honk timbral layers before deleting the chord's defining notes; do not steal a music stem mid-phrase for a random knock. |
| Other local activity  | Nearby residents, remote construction contacts                                   | Per-emitter caps, audibility ranking, modest hysteresis. Select a few readable gestures.                                                                      |
| Background detail     | Distant work, ambience spots and decorative tails                                | Drop or aggregate first; never queue dropped one-shots for later.                                                                                             |

Within a tier prefer effective audibility and relevance, then age; do not repeatedly steal and restart the same moving train at a threshold. Start with one foreground conversation plus one ambient bark, one detailed nearby machine and reduced neighbors. A loop can be **virtual** as a small parameter/phase record without native sources; when relevant again, start at a suitable phase with a fade. An obsolete chop or cargo landing stays dropped. One foreground train gets rolling, traction, body and a transient budget together, not four voices per carriage.

## 5. Space, places and era timbre

### Panning cost: measure the model, not the class name

`PannerNode` supports `equalpower` and `HRTF`; the latter uses a head-related response, while equal-power panning is a simpler model ([API documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PannerNode/panningModel), **[V]**). Browser implementer Paul Adenot's [Web Audio performance notes](https://padenot.github.io/web-audio-perf/) **[V, older implementation guidance]** explain the extra work around HRTF/convolution and moving sources. That is a qualitative reason to compare them, **not a current M4 benchmark or evidence that all PannerNodes are expensive**.

Default to **mono world sources → gain → StereoPannerNode**, with distance calculated once by a shared helper. Keep authored stereo music and broad ambience stereo. Use player-head position for distance and camera orientation for screen-relative pan; choose an explicit camera-listener mode for a cinematic such as the first fall. This avoids third-person zoom changing a held log's level. Test rotation around a source and mono downmix; stereo pan alone cannot distinguish front/back or height.

Compare two to four HRTF foreground emitters against simple pan on headphones only if front/back localization matters to play. Equal-power `PannerNode` is also a valid implementation choice. Measure fixed scene/source counts with plain pan, equal-power and HRTF on the actual iPad, then listen on its speakers and headphones. Record duration, OS/build, sample rate, frame times and audible dropouts; run long enough to expose sustained load, with no competing renderer. No published source here justifies a millisecond cost or safe HRTF count for this game.

Use explicit entry/exit ranges and cull **before** allocating a graph. A `maxDistance` on inverse/exponential [PannerNode distance models](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PannerNode/distanceModel) is not a hard zero-gain cutoff **[V]**. Update moving-emitter distance/pan at roughly 20–30 Hz with AudioParam ramps, initially 20–50 ms, and compare against quicker camera rotation. Static one-shots can capture position once; long tails/train loops cannot.

Start by preserving the retained machine's 45 m range. Then audition a near 0–12 m contact perspective, a 12–45 m yard, a 45–120 m district texture and at most one more distant train silhouette; these are authored ranges, not realistic propagation. Avoid sub-only weight on iPad speakers: include a readable midrange body. No raycast per voice per frame. An authored sheltered/open zone and an occasional low-pass transition can establish a first useful distinction. One quiet shared short reflection may earn its cost later; do not copy the prototype's convolver into every place.

### Ambience belongs to a place, not each resident

An acoustic place definition references a stable place ID and authored bounds/blend margin, exposure/water character, optional work activity and an era palette. The place/entity presentation supplies it to the audio service; the mixer never imports `src/proto/place/index.ts` or queries physics itself. Activate the current place and one neighbor, with hysteresis and a bounded crossfade; two beds are a ceiling, not a requirement. Choose a gain law deliberately: equal-power blends suit uncorrelated beds, whereas correlated variants can swell and may need complementary linear gains.

Natural sound continues through town upgrades. Distant residents become occasional grouped activity, not one footstep and vowel loop per person. Nearby visible handoffs can have object/effort accents, but ambient chatter leaves space between phrases. Replace or omit the current tonal ambience pad when actual music enters; a hidden F/D/B-flat harmony should not fight the owner's track. A silent musical bus must mean no compulsory ambient backing song.

| Diesel and concrete   | Compact load/traction texture, solid receivers  | Add mass, not permanent sub and hiss.                                |
| Electric              | Smooth traction, relays, wheel/rail detail      | Industrial life remains; no compulsory sterile quiet.                |
| Future                | Precise locks, sparse mineral/crystal resonance | Replace roles, rather than adding a sixth full soundscape.           |

These timbres are hypotheses implementing the six-era [decision log](../decisions.md), not a request for six complete kits now. First prove one station and one crossing, as the construction report recommends.

## 6. Entities, accepted events and sync kinds

Audio is a **service**. A train/resident/building entity owns emitter handles in its presentation lifecycle and updates them through its existing entity methods. Destroying the entity, losing a remote or leaving the room releases them. Do not turn every short sound into a world entity or add a frame loop per voice. A central scheduler/service may need one documented allow-listed loop; do not grow `eslint-suppressions.json`. Render recipes take positions/parameters, not imports of player state, entities, world rules or three.js.

| Trigger                                | What should produce audio                                                                                                                                   | What must not happen                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Local anticipation**                 | Immediate tool swish, held-object motion or quiet button response                                                                                           | Claim that a shared transfer/build succeeded before acceptance. Do not replay the same confirmation on acknowledgement. |
| **Lockstep accepted edit**             | Adapter `onApplied(e, tick, result)` creates an accepted presentation event. If the visible piece travels, its landing callback plays the material contact. | Sound in `apply`; reward on `submitEdit() === true`; replaying every saved placement after `onSnapshot`.                |
| **Owner / transferable Owner physics** | The rendered, smoothed train/object state drives speed/load loops and local contact presentation.                                                           | Extra presence fields carrying oscillator phase, or audio feedback read by simulation.                                  |
| **Host shared facts**                  | A host-authored public ceremony, if later approved, supplies a semantic event/time. Private dialogue remains local.                                         | Each tab inventing a shared reward; restoring the cut opening-day soundtrack.                                           |
| **One-off social event**               | Existing validated/rate-limited honk/ping messages trigger local admission and spatial presentation.                                                        | One packet per partial, syllable or audio buffer; bypassing the room API.                                               |

Deduplicate accepted presentation by the existing stable operation/result identity where available; otherwise keep a bounded local key derived from its tick and edit position. Associate delayed contact with the accepted piece ID and cancel it when that presentation disappears. Late join/snapshot reconstructs ongoing loops quietly and skips historical transients. Exact duplicate bursts need checking at the promotion boundary, not a new wire protocol to fix Local sound.

The [place prototype](../../src/proto/place/index.ts) currently performs many contacts in local animation callbacks and directly mutates prototype state. Preserve that scope while testing its sound. When the mechanic graduates, wire confirmation to the selected sync kind; do not describe today's `place` arrival callbacks as already server-accepted operations. [World rules](../../src/world/rules/README.md) and [owner sync](../../src/netplay/owner-sync.ts) remain authoritative about state.

## 7. Procedural sound, recordings and iPad asset budgets

| Keep procedural                                                                                                           | Prefer recorded/edited material                                                                                       | Hybrid when useful                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Selected formant babble; immediate honk harmony; retained UI/minigame instruments; existing pickup clink pending audition | Wood/metal/stone contact variation, scrape/handling, train rolling/traction/body, natural ambience, the owner's music | A dry contact recording plus a restrained pitchable resonance; speed-layered rolling plus procedural air; pre-rendered clink variants plus live pitch/level variation |

Procedural does not automatically mean cheaper: one decoded mono hit costs one source, while the modal clink may cost fifteen. Samples do not automatically mean better: a long recorded train loop can repeat obviously and resist load changes. Preserve the approved sound first, then compare the relevant gesture in context. Procedural babble remains the selected solution; no recorded syllable-bank commission.

### Recording and delivery

Start with **four variations each** of wood seat/chop, metal fastener, loaded crate placement and a short handle/latch gesture. Record dry mono **48 kHz / 24-bit WAV** masters, consistent mic distance and recording gain, clean lead-in and full natural decay; avoid clipping, aggressive denoise, mastering compression and baked reverb. Keep stereo for environments and music where the width is intentional. A phone recording can establish a direction before a studio session. Engineer edits onset/loop points and creates runtime derivatives; preserve original masters outside the deployable asset set.

Use descriptive stable filenames (`wood-seat-soft-01.wav`), material/action/intensity/variant metadata, intended route and trim, plus a rough reference mix. Gain variation in code should be small enough not to undo performed accents; no-repeat selection is Local. Ask for six to eight takes only after four prove worth repeating. More files are not a substitute for choosing the right body and tail.

Short critical effects can ship as trimmed PCM16 WAV initially: simple decoding, exact samples and small absolute sizes. For longer beds/music, audition AAC/M4A against Ogg Opus at matched quality/level. WebKit added Ogg Opus/Vorbis support in Safari/iPadOS **18.4** ([release notes](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/), **[V]**); do not assume every supported iPad OS has it, or that container playback proves a precise decoded loop. Test `decodeAudioData` using the actual candidate files, including first-frame padding, total decoded frames and a loop seam. Keep a known-good derivative. MP3 is not the first choice for tightly aligned loops; do not infer sample alignment from an extension.

### Decoded memory is the budget that matters

[decodeAudioData](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BaseAudioContext/decodeAudioData) decodes a complete file and resamples to the context's sample rate **[V]**. Encoded file size does not predict resident PCM. Budget `frames × channels × 4` bytes, using actual decoded lengths; compression reduces transfer/cache bytes, not that formula.

| Example at 48 kHz, float32 decoded [calculated]                             | PCM memory |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------: |
| One second, mono                                                            |  0.183 MiB |
| Sixty 0.4-second mono hits                                                  |   4.39 MiB |
| 30-second stereo bed                                                        |  10.99 MiB |
| Four stereo stems, 16 bars at **132 BPM as an example**, 29.09 seconds each |  42.61 MiB |
| Four stereo stems, 8 bars at the same example tempo                         |  21.31 MiB |

**[H] Start with 20 MiB resident SFX/ambience and 40 MiB music; 60 MiB steady total and 80 MiB temporary total during transitions.** These align with the parallel construction proposal and narrow the older journey report's music-only allowance. Four 16-bar stereo stems at the example tempo already exceed 40 MiB before tails; use shorter sections, appropriate mono roles, fewer stems or a stereo bounce. IRs and generated noise belong in the PCM ledger too. Browser/decoder temporary copies and the GPU's memory are additional: 80 MiB is neither total application memory nor a safe OS eviction threshold.

Load a tiny core first (provisional **under 1 MiB encoded**), current-place effects next, then the next likely place/crossing while walking or dwelling. Keep music off the first-play critical path; a first music pack under roughly **2 MiB encoded** is a useful download experiment, not a fixed quality requirement. Limit simultaneous decodes to one, or two only after iPad measurement. Fetch into a deduplicated promise cache; catch failures and remove failed entries so retries work. Use content-versioned URLs and ordinary HTTP caching first. Decoded buffers have reference counts and an inactive LRU: never evict a playing buffer or retain every era forever. Release encoded ArrayBuffers once no longer needed. Cancel/discard stale decode results by context/room generation.

A missing cosmetic SFX can skip. A music arrangement needs its declared required roles ready together; a missing bass must not produce an accidentally broken first impression. Use an explicitly approved stereo fallback or silence. No service worker/cache database, general streaming decoder or huge sound sprite is necessary for the first kit. Use existing Vite asset URL conventions and lazy loading, not eager imports of all eras.

## 8. Music stems and the train timetable

First use **one owner-approved stereo bounce**, with a known drop offset, on one crossing. [journey-music.md](journey-music.md) already supplies the landscape-first direction. The archived groove clock has a useful output-timestamp mapping; its loader/grid require review before promotion, and its old placeholder groove stays archived.

Then accept a typed cue manifest: track/version, native BPM and meter, exact section length, required roles, stem gains, loop start/end **in frames with the asset's sample rate**, entry/exit/drop markers, tail length, optional chord map and permitted transitions. Convert frame markers to the decoded rate once and verify the resulting boundaries. All stems start from the same export origin, with identical structural lengths. Reverb tails may extend beyond a boundary and must be counted during overlap. Four roles is the first target; six only if the arrangement and measured budgets justify it. Baked UK garage swing stays in the audio; do not swing the playback grid a second time. Do not normalize each stem independently: preserve the producer's balance and check their sum against the reference bounce, allowing for deliberate nonlinear master processing differences.

**Scheduling rule [H]:**

1. Derive the intended reveal time from the train's actual timetable and direction. [crossing/line.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/line.ts) provides acceleration/braking-aware `trainAt` and an **outbound-only** `timeAtS`. A return needs a direction-aware inverse; `distance / cruiseSpeed` is insufficient.
2. Choose an authored lead-in: `cueStart = revealTime − dropOffsetSeconds` (or `N × 240 / BPM` for N bars in 4/4). If readiness/boarding makes that impossible, choose a shorter cut or skip this highlight. Never slow the train or delay gameplay for the score.
3. Map that game/presentation time to AudioContext time. [getOutputTimestamp](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/getOutputTimestamp) relates output context time to `performanceTime` **[V]**. Account explicitly for the clock used to render the train and any remote interpolation; aligning to the latest network time can put sound ahead of the visible train. Do not subtract latency twice after using an output timestamp.
4. Schedule decoded sources on the audio clock, starting related stems at the same instant/offset. A replenishment wake around 25 ms and horizon around 100 ms is a starting point from [Chris Wilson's two-clock explanation](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling) **[V]**. The timer wakes the scheduler; it does not determine each beat. Direct honks, chops and landings bypass musical quantization.
5. Once the build/drop is committed, keep its tempo stable. A future phrase can extend an authored holding section; major timetable changes abandon the exact scenic hit rather than scrub, time-stretch or repeatedly restart a drop. Stop or resolve cleanly on dismount/arrival. Familiar rides may remain music-free.

Each tab maps a shared cue epoch/route fact to its **own** audio clock; no audio buffers or per-beat messages cross the room. Shared time helps friends hear corresponding sections, but network jitter, device latency and acoustic placement prevent a promise of sample-perfect playback between devices. For a quiet join/resume, skip to a suitable future phrase only if the cue remains relevant; otherwise wait for another ride. No music scheduler runs the simulation, and `onBeat` must not start delaying physical feedback when music is introduced. Harmony selection and optional decorative answering notes are separate from contact timing.

## 9. Safari/iPad lifecycle, worklets and thermal cost

### Unlock and interruption are a state machine

Keep one context. Expose `locked`, `running`, `suspended/interrupted`, `closed` and a diagnostic failure state through the service rather than forcing every recipe to recover it. Today's gesture listeners are a useful base. [HTML's activation model](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/interaction.html#tracking-user-activation) lists trusted activation-triggering inputs **[V]**; gamepad polling is not a substitute for a trusted tap/key event. A returning controller-only player can otherwise remain silent. If resume requires interaction, show a small **Enable sound** action on existing UI, with touch and keyboard support, without blocking play or inventing another gameplay pad binding. Preserve mute/volume intent.

Observe context state, visibility and room/menu lifecycle. On hiding/Leave, stop admission, cancel pending transients, release ducks and continuous sources, and suspend audio when appropriate. The world/network follows its existing hidden-tab policy; do not suspend gameplay because the audio device stopped. On return, attempt a bounded resume, invalidate old timing mappings, rebuild only currently relevant loops and re-establish music at a suitable future boundary. No backlog of pickup ticks or babble. Handle races between an outstanding `resume`, a late decode and a room change. Closing/recreating a context is recovery for a genuinely closed/broken context, not something done every focus event.

The optional [Audio Session API](https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-session/) remains a draft **[V]**. Retain the guarded `playback` choice initially and check actual silent-switch/background/external-music behavior; it is not a universal Safari fix. Record `state`, sample rate, timestamp progress and pending recoveries for diagnostics. WebKit bug [273511](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=273511) documents older interruption/resume trouble; [323104](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=323104) is a newer report of silence despite advancing time and visible signal. **These are reports, not reproduced Boogle defects or proof that today's M4 has either bug.** They explain why a running context and nonzero analyser cannot establish that a person heard output. Do not blindly toggle session categories or repeatedly create contexts based on a bug-thread workaround.

### Native DSP now; worklets only for demonstrated needs

WebKit shipped AudioWorklet support in Safari 14.1 / iPadOS 14.5 ([release announcement](https://webkit.org/blog/11648/new-webkit-features-in-safari-14-1/), **[V]**). The existing recorder already uses one. **No custom runtime worklet is needed** for the current mixer, synths, panners, voice admission or source scheduling. A worklet becomes reasonable for a measured expensive custom DSP instrument, a proven streaming requirement or a needed limiter that native nodes cannot supply. It adds real-time code/GC constraints and debugging work, not free CPU.

Let the device choose context sample rate; log the actual value and derive all sample counts from it. Requesting 48 kHz is not an optimization guarantee. Export masters at 48 kHz for consistency, then measure decode/resampling behavior at the device's actual rate. The older WebKit [44.1 kHz worklet distortion report 274507](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?format=multiple&id=274507) is **resolved fixed**, not grounds for asserting a current 44.1 kHz defect. In future recorder work, process the block length actually received rather than assuming the current 128-frame implementation forever.

Measure audio while the real WebGPU scene and Rapier run: a dense town, construction bursts and train transit for 15–30 minutes on the M4, at a recorded volume/output route and repeatable graphics settings. Compare audio disabled, baseline and candidate under otherwise identical conditions; sample decode spikes as well as steady state. A JS frame profiler alone does not isolate audio-thread cost. Track counts, decoded bytes, scheduling misses, frame hitches and audible glitches; do not claim an exact audio CPU percentage from rAF timings. Prefer fewer active emitters, short tails, cached buffers and one shared effect over lowering all sound quality. Desktop remains supported, with the same behavior and a possibly higher measured ceiling later.

## 10. How an agent who cannot hear can make trustworthy progress

Use two complementary paths, preserving the [existing harness](../../dev/audio/render.ts):

1. **Recipe diagnostics:** render the same injected-context recipe in [OfflineAudioContext](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OfflineAudioContext) **[V]**, with controlled random input, duration and start time. Check finite samples, silence, DC, peak, envelope/tail, band energy and mono behavior. This is faster-than-real-time signal inspection, not proof of real-time scheduling, interruption handling, iPad speaker quality or enjoyment. A small `dev/audio/offline.ts` can own this without changing the runtime AudioContext door.
2. **Actual mix capture:** extend `dev/audio/render.ts` to keep untrimmed, unnormalized float PCM alongside optional audition files. Capture the real graph and a timestamped trigger log so onset can be compared with action/visible contact. Preserve raw files before integer encoding, since normalization or clipping during export can hide faults. Add recipe and gameplay takes for NPC babble and the current `place` loop, which are missing from the present pack.

Measure sample peak, standards-based **true peak**, RMS, momentary/short-term/integrated loudness as appropriate, crest factor, tail duration, and gross spectral/mono changes. A short isolated tick's integrated LUFS is not a useful universal target. Spectra can expose excess hiss or missing low-mid body; they cannot choose a pleasing instrument. Fixed seeds reduce comparison noise, but do not expect bit-identical oscillator/filter output across browsers or assert a golden waveform is the design.

For mix reference, Sony's **2013** [ASWG-R001](https://gameaudiopodcast.com/ASWG-R001.pdf) recommends −18 ±2 LKFS for its portable platforms, −24 ±2 for home platforms, no more than −1 dBTP, and representative **whole-game** measurement for at least 30 minutes **[V]**. This is not an Apple/web requirement. **[H]** Use roughly **−18 LUFS ±2 for a representative active portable mix** as an initial comparison, with quiet periods allowed to remain quiet; leave roughly 6 dB premaster headroom during authoring and check final true peaks below −1 dBTP. Do not push every scene, SFX or stem to that loudness, and do not claim today's compressor enforces the peak ceiling. Start by measuring the retained baseline. Adjust densities/trims before adding more compression; if stressed output still requires reliable limiting, evaluate that explicit need separately.

The owner gets a small listening pack: baseline/candidate at **original relative mix levels**, an optional separately labelled loudness-matched timbre comparison, and a 30–60-second context sequence containing quiet space, repeated work, a resident, cargo arrival and a friend honk. Include source commit, route, settings, raw/preview distinction and a simple HTML index/manifest in the existing output workflow. A picture or video without verified sound is not an audio audition. Request one gut verdict: **“Does the work still feel good when the town gets busy?”** One line on what changed; the agent handles edge cases.

Worth retaining as **development harness checks**, consistent with AGENTS.md's test diet:

- Known kept events produce finite, nonempty output; explicitly cut moments remain silent, without banning a shared recipe used legitimately elsewhere.
- A repeated workload stays within admission/source/decoded-byte ceilings; after stop/leave/interruption, one-shots and owned loops return to the documented idle floor.
- Mixer controls affect the intended routes, mute survives scene transitions, overlapping duck tokens release correctly, and protected contact is still admitted under the selected stress mix.
- Candidate music assets decode, align in frames, meet required-role readiness and loop without a discontinuity detectable in the raw signal; a device audition still judges the seam.
- On the actual iPad: cold unlock, controller-only return, hide/show, screen lock, another app interrupting audio, output-route change and Leave/rejoin. Confirm heard output and absence of an event backlog.

Do not add UI/audio-feature unit tests or a new scenario merely to test the implementation. Use the existing audio harness for fixtures/diagnostics; a new browser scenario is justified only by a new sync contract. No waveform score, loudness score or automated spectrum threshold can approve the artistic result. An agent unable to hear should report signal and lifecycle evidence accurately, prepare the audition and keep uncertain timbre changes behind the relevant prototype rather than label them approved.

## What not to build

- A middleware clone, node editor, new audio context, general ECS sound entity, per-feature frame loop, or a Tone/Howler dependency without an approved requirement it uniquely simplifies.
- HRTF, convolution, occlusion raycasts or full Doppler on every resident, machine or carriage. First prove selective foreground localization and material identity.
- A universal rhythm quantizer for contacts, audio-driven lockstep, networked oscillator phase, per-syllable room messages or promises of sample-perfect cross-device sound.
- Six complete era libraries, a recorded NPC syllable bank, automatic placeholder music, restored rejected train/opening sounds, or a broad timbre rewrite disguised as refactoring.
- A streaming/time-stretching worklet, custom compressor/limiter, persistent asset database or sound-authoring UI before a measured limitation demands it.
- A large audio regression suite, brittle golden waveforms or numerical approval of taste. Keep diagnostics small and purposeful; inspect and listen to the actual result.
