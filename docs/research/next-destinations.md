_Research report, 5 November 2026. Live primary-source research on destination-led progression, seven candidate destinations after the starter island, three roughly eight-hour arrangements, physical upgrades, uneven co-op attendance and the smallest next proof. **Recommendation [H]:** follow the starter island with a reachable chalk coast, a working steel harbour, the suspended geology already promised on the horizon, an inhabited high port, and finally the original island and The Boogler. Every main connection should export a useful capability, visibly improve somewhere familiar, and open a different kind of exploration. The world keeps its history; the machines acquire parts.

## What the reference games establish

### 1. A Short Hike: a destination promises an experience, not just an item

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu describes designing for the summit's respite and changed view, while allowing the player to take the opposite route and still find worthwhile activities ([developer account, 2021](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)). His [IGF interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-adam-robinson-yu-s-i-a-short-hike-i-/) describes landmarks, scenery-implied discoveries and balancing expected paths while allowing getting lost. The [GDC postmortem abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A) establishes its deliberately small scope and initial four-month deadline; it supplies no eight-hour pacing formula.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** arriving under the ring, on a high quay or at the original lighthouse should itself be worth the journey. Supply the upgrade near that experience, not instead of it. Give an area a small legible destination and several ways to approach; don't multiply footprint merely because trains get faster.

### 2. Wind Waker: islands carry identity; repeated transit can exhaust it

**[V]** Nintendo describes the sea setting as a foundation for inventing distinct islands and eccentric inhabitants. The same interview acknowledges the original game's later pacing drag and revisiting it for the HD version ([Iwata Asks, part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But-/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But--807230.html)). This supports variety and pacing review; it does not establish a universally satisfying crossing time.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** separate a chalk bowl, vertical trees, riveted harbour and suspended stone with sea and distinct arrival profiles. Repeated journeys should become easier because the engine and route improved. Do not make every resource recipe send the player back across a familiar sea. A destination also needs a person, activity or useful interior at walking scale.

### 3. Spirit Tracks: constrained expansion can make the goal readable

**[V]** Aonuma and Iwamoto describe spending about a year on free track laying, struggling with where players should build and story access. They changed to restoring a prescribed network; their testing found it easier to understand, while expansion retained appeal ([Nintendo, train-design interview](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Spirit-Tracks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Spirit-Tracks/3-Play-That-s-Only-Possible-on-a-Train/3-Play-That-s-Only-Possible-on-a-Train-226909.html)). They also discuss driving and avoidance challenges, which are different from Boogle's ride-only trains.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** offer a visible destination and two plausible corridors before a general editor. Later build-your-own-track can start from the owner-recorded designated places. This preserves direction without a marker and avoids making open-ended rail tooling a prerequisite for the first new island. Keep train operation out of the player's compulsory job.

### 4. Death Stranding: improvement is felt against a remembered route

**[V]** Kojima Productions' [Eastern Region guide](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/en/DSTips) sequences route aids, connection and vehicle restoration through the opening journey. In a [2025 interview](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=404517), Kojima says continued player interest in building highways informed the sequel's monorails; he describes Likes as appreciation without money or strength effects. These are infrastructure and recognition precedents, not evidence for live co-op or Boogle's delivery rates.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** let friends first understand the walk, then see a supported crossing or engine retire that inconvenience. Keep a remnant of the old path beside the new line. Carrying balance, damage and maintenance would add a different game; a short satisfying contribution and meaningful frontier choice must do the work here.

### 5. Dredge: one vehicle carries capability between distinct regions

**[V]** Black Salt's [game description](https://www.blacksaltgames.com/) explicitly connects equipment upgrades to deeper fishing and farther destinations, and regional quests to new abilities. In its [developer postmortem interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/trawling-in-the-deep-how-black-salt-games-made-spooky-fishing-rpg-i-dredge-i-), the team describes mapping four main regions, placing routes and inhabitants, then fitting distinct regional threats through iteration. Its resource, quest and travel systems support the same vessel across that geography.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** give a region an identifiable spatial problem and reward that travels onward. Replace threat differences with safe construction/traversal differences: low footing, steep approach, long unsupported span, occupied street. Night can offer one contrasting visit; Dredge's fear, damage and ticking pressure should not become Boogle's travel tax.

### 6. Outer Wilds: places can also teach understanding

**[V]** Alex Beachum describes knowledge as what survives its loop and explains why an early village meteor failed: players did not yet know the ordinary village well enough to find its disruption interesting ([developer interview, 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)). Kelsey Beachum's [GDC narrative abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027368/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity) describes diegetic, player-directed exploration. The abstract is not a transcript, and its speaker is Kelsey, not Alex.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** establish an ordinary clamp and supported beam before a strange stone changes what they can carry. A matching collar at home can become newly understandable after visiting the source. Boogle still needs persistent material and ability progress: a veteran explaining a discovery should help friends without exhausting the whole activity for them.

### 7. Satisfactory: exploration can remove construction friction back home

**[V]** Coffee Stain's [1.0 release account](https://coffeestain.com/news/satisfactory-1-0-launch/) describes an artefact-researched Dimensional Depot that reduces repeated storage trips, and Project Assembly visibly expanding with each phase. The description does not establish a Boogle-like completion time or say all exploration rewards are linear milestones.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** the next island should improve the player's next build and the home workshop. Prefer physically delivered kits at a frontier rack to an invisible universal stockpile. Exploration earns convenience; routine withdrawals should not consume the time that convenience was meant to save.

### 8. Factorio: Space Age — different destinations, exportable rewards

**[V]** [Friday Facts 373](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-373) describes a small set of predefined planets with different resources/mechanics and meaningful order choices. [FFF 417](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-417) records the failures: excessive length, repetition and awkward scaled logistics. Wube reduced costs and recipe steps, differentiated planet mechanics, and allowed planet-specific producers to be exported, improving motivation and progression. Its current [content overview](https://factorio.com/game/content-space-age) distinguishes Vulcanus, Fulgora, Gleba and Aquilo; the development diaries are historical accounts, not current balance tables.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** this is the strongest structural reference. A cement coast and steel harbour should solve different problems, then make the existing network better. An optional order choice is meaningful only if the first reward changes the second expedition. Avoid rebuilding the starter economy on every island or attaching an equally larger bill to every efficiency gain. Do not import spoilage, military requirements or interplanetary scheduling.

### 9. Timberborn: show stages, then let completion remain playable

**[V]** Mechanistry's [Update 6 experimental notes, June 2024](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=1062090&enddate=1720701633&feed=steam_community_announcements) describe wonders with five visible construction stages, an explicit completion acknowledgement and continued play afterwards. They also describe overhang construction and automated sluices. This is a versioned design precedent, not a survey of today's full game.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** let a bridge or roundhouse acquire recognizable working pieces before completion, and leave the resulting place usable. The ordinary segment needs fewer beats than a campaign landmark. Drought, water management and its very large wonder bills are not prerequisites for making construction legible.

### 10. Islanders: remove progression layers that do the same job

**[V]** Friedemann Allmenröder describes cutting a day cycle that gated progress, instead directly awarding more buildings through points. He also describes block mockups for communicating layouts and repeatedly simplifying features ([developer interview, 2019](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/06/14/islanders)). This is evidence for concise feedback and scope control, not freight-driven town growth or a persistent archipelago.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** a connected source can directly enable a new construction kit. It need not also advance a town level, global technology age and research meter. Build the town addition where its use is apparent; preserve the old town rather than copying a fresh-island reset structure.

### 11. Ratchet & Clank: carry the toolset forward and vary its use

**[V]** Insomniac's [Rift Apart gameplay article](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=348557) describes dash, wall-running and Rift Tether used for traversal as well as combat. Marcus Smith explains that Ratchet and Rivet originally differed, but the team unified their upgrades after players were frustrated by losing access between characters; he also distinguishes optional pocket dimensions ([direct interview passages in this preview](https://press-start.com.au/previews/2021/05/12/ratchet-clank-rift-apart-preview-a-big-step-forward/)). This is a single-player character-switching lesson, not proof of co-op entitlement design.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** later destinations can teach combinations of existing abilities instead of adding a button every hour. Shared access should follow the group; a late friend should not lack the move needed to travel with them. Optional mastery routes can remain personal discoveries. Reuse the accepted hook pull, not the rejected swing.

### 12. Animal Crossing: the world connects people on different clocks

**[V]** Katsuya Eguchi explains wanting shared family activity even when he returned after his children were asleep, using the persistent world, letters and message boards to connect them ([GDC 2006 interview](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/interview/11229/animal-crossing-interview)). This supports asynchronous social traces, not the proposition that calendar waits are necessary.

**Boogle transfer [H]:** the returning friend should see the completed hoist, a new roof and a ready route, then find a fresh useful action. Make absence helpful and comprehensible. Keep funded completion separate from selecting the next adventure, and leave personal discoveries and cosmetics available without expiry.

### Synthesis

**[H]** Use three interacting kinds of progress: **reach** (a permanent route), **capability** (a tool or machine can now do something different), and **recognition** (a familiar place visibly carries the expedition's result). A destination earns its production cost when it contributes all three. Its silhouette supplies anticipation; its local verb supplies play; its export gives the rest of the world a reason to remember it. Not every destination needs a unique ore or avatar move.

### Keep differences mechanical and small

Chalksound asks **through or around**. Kiln Harbour asks **one long span or the old edge**. Canopy Reach asks **above or below**. Suspended Coast asks **where can a support descend**. High Port asks **how can a line fit among existing places**. The original island asks **which familiar solution completes our line**. Colour alone cannot distinguish these jobs.

The sledge is the only proposed new compulsory avatar tool in the recommended spine; each following area closes with a construction capability or useful connection. Bombs remain an alternative to test if a breach action is more enjoyable, not a second mandatory demolition system. Do not re-award the starter's jump, hook or glider, or create an ability every chapter simply to satisfy a schedule. Keep a construction task between movement discoveries, consistent with the 5 November pacing call.

The port remains an existing place; deliveries do not rebuild it from nothing. Its production boundary is one usable district plus a skyline, with honest visible access. The original island ending can reuse familiar landmarks but needs its own new-player test. No prophecy, dialogue rewrite or paid gate is authored here: the recorded entrepreneur joke remains for the narrative pass, and money never becomes a new completion requirement in this proposal.

## Three ways to fit an eight-hour arc

**[H] All times are content allowances, not timers, measured completion times or quotas.** They include first approaches, normal rides, local discovery, building and meaningful returns. They exclude nights/days spent offline and optional post-ending play. Reference player: a curious solo player or a pair sharing discoveries; friends may finish faster. Never raise costs to restore a target time. Each large area should contain roughly 60–120 minutes worth of authored opportunities and a clear stopping point, while allowing a focused player to leave earlier.

The table sums to **480 minutes**. Canopy Reach is omitted or a later optional branch; the observatory replaces 10–15 minutes of Suspended Coast exploration if chosen, rather than extending its budget. Without night it is a daytime lens room or cut entirely. There is no compulsory intermission or full-network lap.

**Why this first [H]:** the material dependencies are easy to read and there is only one new first-destination grammar to test. **Risk:** it becomes a conveyor of unlocks. Each area therefore needs a local route choice and one retained optional route, and each exported benefit must be used before another is introduced.

### B. Choose cement or steel first — two orders, then converge

**60 starter + 90 Chalksound OR Kiln Harbour + 90 the other destination + 90 Suspended Coast + 75 High Port + 75 original island = 480 minutes.** Both lower landings must be reachable using starter timber and ordinary fittings. Cement-first makes the harbour's receiving apron easier to build; steel-first brings a hoist/span kit back to simplify the chalk cliff route. Neither new material can be required to land at its own source or the other first destination. Canopy Reach stays optional; the observatory stays inside the shard's allowance.

The first selection changes which shared service appears at home and which route is easier next. Chalksound's sledge opens its local shortcuts; the harbour's ordinary approach cannot depend on already owning it. A destination visited second needs a different route solution using the earlier export, rather than the same tutorial with a larger stockpile. Keep both visits worthwhile without requiring every optional structure in either.

**Why choose it [H]:** it turns “where next?” into a concrete group decision without adding another full environment. **Risk:** two entry states and alternative construction solutions cost more to author, and one order may dominate. Test both orders against the same later goals; each first reward must help the second expedition in a visibly different way.

### C. Wonder first — pay off the horizon before heavy industry

**60 starter + 90 Suspended Coast + 90 Canopy Reach + 90 Kiln Harbour + 75 High Port + 75 original island = 480 minutes.** Reach the shard's lower shore with starter timber; its seed is used on the outbound gap, never required for its own discovery. The seed makes a small home work deck possible before the grove supplies lightweight spans and covered gathering space. Steel then extends load and span capacity, followed by the port's equipped electric corridor and the final approach. The optional observatory remains inside the shard's 90 minutes.

Chalksound is omitted or optional in this arrangement. Laminated timber/towers and later steel kits must provide the required conventional supports; the port's ordinary foundations already exist. Do not quietly reintroduce a compulsory cement trip or a sledge gate. Each export changes one familiar home corner, so even the early extraordinary discovery is grounded in an ordinary use.

**Why choose it [H]:** it quickly rewards curiosity about the starter's existing horizon and gives the campaign a different texture: wonder → living landscape → industry → inhabited city → homecoming. **Risk:** the most impossible place arrives before anticipation builds, and crystal supports may trivialize later construction. Bound the first collar to its demonstrated support geometry, leave the steel span/load problem intact, and test whether the grove still feels worth reaching after the shard. Do not solve that risk with an unexplained resource tax.

### Content accounting before a map grows

The old [economy study](rail-economy-model.md#a-rough-six-era-table-near-eight-hours) demonstrated how easy it is to hide most of a campaign inside an unnamed “content” allowance. These tables name beats but do not prove their duration either. Before committing to 480 minutes, time one complete later area with a fresh player and record exploration, construction, known travel and exposed supply waiting separately.

For a first 90-minute paper budget, try **20 arrival/orientation + 20 traversal/discovery + 25 route building and first use + 15 export/home consequence + 10 breathing room = 90**. These are ceilings to populate, never instructions to lengthen actions. If the real activities take 45 enjoyable minutes, record a 45-minute area and revise the campaign scope. Optional toys cannot be the hidden missing 45 minutes. A ten-minute local proof cannot validate this whole chapter or its emotional pacing.

For repeated crossings, retain the owner's first-sea starting point of about a minute. An illustrative 1.2 km cruise at 20/30/40 m/s is **60/40/30 seconds**, before stops and acceleration; only the first value is the recorded starting preference, the later two are experiments. Improve an engine and the relevant line before the faster service appears. Do not lengthen the sea to cancel the improvement or promise these speeds before ride and loading checks.

### Example storyboard: steel span, six readable beats

1. The arriving cradle contains the committed beam kit; the receiving cradle has an obvious matching empty space.
2. A clamp closes around one piece. The source loses that piece as the transfer begins; the piece remains visible in transit.
3. The jib pivots toward the surveyed gap; the hook rises only enough to clear the deck.
4. The piece seats on visible bearings. The clamp opens and pins/latches close.
5. The remaining kit follows; the magazine is visibly smaller, the supported rail visibly longer.
6. Scaffolding withdraws and the machine moves onto the completed section. A friend can walk or ride the result.

**Proposed moving-parts sheet [H]:** jib at its base yaw pivot, hoist at the drum with a vertical hook carriage, clamp halves at hinge pins, feed tray on a slide, latch at a hinge, wheels on axle pivots. Initial study ranges: jib ±60°, hook 0–3 m, clamp 0–45°, tray 0–1 m, latch 0–90°. Those are blockout ranges, not measured final dimensions; check load clearance against the real Buddy and machine before modelling. A beauty reference must show separate parts and clear joints, never a fused arm in one pose.

For large quantities, use honest grouping: a slatted crate with a visible fixed capacity and exposed top layer, plus separate partial stock. Every decrement must change that physical stock or move into a visible transfer. Decorative stock must be plainly scenery, not indistinguishable usable material. Avoid rendering thousands of loose pieces merely to communicate one account; grouping must remain legible rather than conceal stock changes behind an arbitrary progress bar.

Future evidence needs the whole cycle in real-time capture, consecutive frames and stage curves, including empty/full magazine, interrupted work and a late visitor seeing the settled result. This report supplies no such verification and proposes no visual-only callback as the source of progress.
