## Why the two references read differently [P/V/D]

These are visual recipes, not recovered Nintendo shaders. Publisher screenshots are primary visual evidence; interviews establish intent. No ROM, extracted texture or decompilation is used here. No verified Nintendo GDC talk or art-book passage specifying these games' cloud algorithm was found; do not turn an art interpretation into an implementation claim.

### The Wind Waker: graphic shapes and open air

Nintendo's [original GameCube gallery](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-GameCube/The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-269039.html), especially [the sailing image](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/images/migration/games_7/screenshots/gamecube_4/the_legend_of_zelda_the_wind_waker/the_legend_of_zelda_the_wind_waker_3.jpg), shows flattened white cloud masses against broad blue/lavender colour zones, with strong separation from the patterned sea. Rounded, curling cloud contours read as drawn marks; sparse internal value changes keep them legible at a distance. The owner's **swirly cel-cloud / banded-gradient / stylized-sun** target is a useful exaggeration of that graphic language. Deliberate colour bands need not mean quantization artefacts, nor does every scene use visibly stepped gradients. **[V/D]**

In Nintendo's [Iwata Asks, “Over-designed”](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/5-Over-designed/5-Over-designed-807330.html), Takizawa relates the HD look to visiting southern islands; the team describes conveying comfortable sun, shade and breeze through selected light and colour rather than photographic realism. This supports simplifying toward a feeling. It concerns **HD's revised lighting**, not proof that the GameCube original used HDR bloom or a particular cloud shader. **[P]**

**Boogle translation:** three or four broad palette regions with softened boundaries, irregular flat cloud silhouettes, a few restrained curl motifs, one cream sun disc with a small halo. Leave broad clear areas around landmarks. Do not apply cel shading to Buddy or replace the existing detailed sea to make the sky match. **[D]**

### Super Mario Sunshine: saturated depth and humid brightness

Nintendo's [GameCube gallery](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-GameCube/Super-Mario-Sunshine-268918.html) provides contrasting views: [upward tropical view](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/images/migration/games_7/screenshots/gamecube_4/super_mario_sunshine/super_mario_sunshine_1.jpg) has a saturated cyan-blue field and soft, puffy white cumulus; [open-water view](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/images/migration/games_7/screenshots/gamecube_4/super_mario_sunshine/super_mario_sunshine_14.jpg) has a much paler, hazier distance. Big rounded crowns, cool soft undersides and small distant clusters suggest depth. A luminous horizon joins sea and air; darker overhead blue preserves the white clouds' contrast. The lesson is a **smooth blue gradient plus horizon haze**, not flattening every cloud into a white sticker. These small promotional images establish appearance, not exact colour samples or the game's full range of weather. **[V]**

The [2002 developer interviews, translated by shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mariosunshine/), identify Koizumi's resort/vacation intention and the remembered sensation of playing with water. This is primary developer testimony through a third-party translation, not official English technical documentation. It supports the summer-place feeling, but says nothing definitive about cloud texture dimensions, scattering or a skybox format. **[P]**

**Boogle translation:** retain a smooth ramp, stronger low-angle haze, unevenly distributed puffy clusters at several angular sizes, subtle cool bases and a restrained hot sun. The owner preferred the current sky's more natural population over the six-cluster toy sky; our inference is to address distribution and shading, not just more saturation. **[D/L]**

Rare's developer paper [The Technical Art of Sea of Thieves, SIGGRAPH 2018](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf) is a useful independent precedent: it combines stylized FFT water with art-directed clouds without expensive raymarching. It supports keeping Boogle's capable ocean under simpler sky art; it does not establish Nintendo's methods or justify porting Rare's cloud system. **[P/D]**

## Procedural and imported options in our TSL sky [L/D/E]

The cheapest coherent solution is to keep **one baked cube per chosen look**. The existing `bakedArt` callback can create an original sky or sample an imported image; consumers need not know its origin. Do not put new cloud noise into every ocean fragment. Do not draw cloud sprites only over the background and expect the FFT shader to reflect them.

These are planning allowances, not agent-speed promises or GPU benchmarks. At equal cube size, all baked art choices retain roughly the **same steady-state texture sampling** as today. Their main costs are authoring, loading and retained textures. `sky-core.ts` still calls `atmosphere.update()` even with `bakedArt`: **the existing art override does not remove CPU LUT preparation**. An imported sky is not automatically faster. Skipping unused atmosphere work is a separate measured optimization after visual acceptance.

### Memory and preparation arithmetic

For six RGBA16F faces with a complete mip chain, payload is approximately `6 × N² × 8 × 4/3 = 64 × N²` bytes. That gives **64 MiB at 1024**, **16 MiB at 512**, **4 MiB at 256**. One similarly sized filtered environment adds approximately another cube's worth; scratch targets, LUTs, decoder buffers and driver padding are additional. This is allocation arithmetic, not measured residency. Two prepared 512 raw/filtered pairs are approximately **64 MiB** before scratch. Prefer one selected prototype per page load; do not preload the whole catalogue.

A decoded 4096×2048 RGBA8 panorama adds **32 MiB** before mips (**42.7 MiB** with ordinary mips); a 2048×1024 source is **8 / 10.7 MiB**. RGBA16F doubles those figures. The source texture can be released after successful bake/readiness if no retained node needs it. PNG/HDR compressed download size is unrelated to those allocations. For scale, a hypothetical 2 MiB transfer at 20 Mbit/s is about **0.84 s**, excluding latency/decode/upload. Do not substitute whole-pack sizes for a selected asset's shipping cost.

Reuse the already implemented `skySize` remapping when comparing 512; do not reintroduce hard-coded mip-10 assumptions for a smaller image. [Three's PMREM documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html) describes roughness-filtered radiance; **ordinary raw-cube mips and PMREM roughness levels are different contracts**. Water currently expects the former; PBR expects the latter. Keep the installed WebGPU generator path rather than copying the WebGL-typed examples on that documentation page.

Multiplying LDR into HDR range cannot recover clipped sun information. Give it an explicit radiance scale while holding production exposure fixed. Compose cloud coverage into **transmittance `1 − coverage`** independently of whether the source has alpha. Paint/replace the lower hemisphere deliberately: it affects the read-back ground fill even though visible sky sampling clamps below the horizon.

A panorama with a sun needs an explicit policy. For Kenney or HDR photography, remove the embedded sharp sun for the shared radiance cube and retain one aligned analytic disc/key, or deliberately suppress the extra disc and account for the water's separate solar lobe. Simply adding Boogle's sun over a sun-bearing panorama gives two suns or double energy. Never infer physical radiance from the brightest LDR pixel. Check longitude wrapping, poles, cube-face handedness, orientation against `sunDirection`, and seams at every sampled mip. The existing WebGPU cube bake already compensates its x-direction convention.

Prepare textures, raw cube, PBR filtering, fill readback and first-use shaders before revealing the scene. `cube.needsPMREMUpdate` alone does not prove the first consumer is warm. Keep one linear-HDR output pipeline and the existing disposal/restore path. For a later hot-swap, extend the prepared-look table with the complete sky/light/fill package; a new background assignment alone leaves reflections or lighting stale. No runtime rebakes, weather loop, shared time change or network/schema work is needed for this comparison.

## Numbered recommendations for Boogle [D]

1. **Prototype `?sky=windpaint` first: original painted-cel direction.** Extend the `bakedArt` selection in [`water/flags.ts`](../../src/render/water/flags.ts) and [`frozen-sky.ts`](../../src/render/water/frozen-sky.ts), using original direction-space masks derived from [`toy.ts`](../../src/render/water/toy.ts), broad softened palette bands and limited swirl accents. Parameterize a cream disc/halo in [`sky-core.ts`](../../src/render/water/sky-core.ts); keep its sun and cloud-alpha contract. Keep current water optics, fixed Neutral and lights, with deliberate lower-hemisphere fill. This best tests the newly requested Wind Waker vocabulary without an imported asset. **Smallest proof:** current versus windpaint at the same shore and town cameras; verify whether clear silhouettes improve character without making water look striped. Stop for owner judgement after the bounded comparison.

2. **Prototype `?sky=summer`: improve our existing cumulus recipe.** Reuse [`toySky()`](../../src/render/water/toy.ts), varying density/size and soft base shading, with a smooth saturated blue ramp and stronger horizon haze. Preserve the physical sun direction and radiance. A new sky-only selector must not inherit `toyWater` or the old toy AgX default; use [`post.ts`](../../src/render/water/post.ts)'s production output. Keep `sky=toy` as a historical control, not silently changed. **Smallest proof:** current / existing toy / summer with identical water settings. Look especially for the too-blue town fill noted in [sunshine-sky](sunshine-sky.md), and for naturally populated rather than evenly spaced clouds. No need to reopen the old sky prototype or clock.

3. **Prototype `?sky=gobi` as the hand-painted audition, with Kenney day as the CC0 backup.** Use [GOBI's CC BY 4.0 cloud strips](https://gobistudio.itch.io/free-clouds-tiles-textures) inside our summer gradient, downscaled for the chosen cube and composed in sky-direction space. Inspect the actual PNG alpha/height before choosing a mask; preserve correct coverage and sun transmittance. Bake through [`createWaterSkyFor()`](../../src/render/water/sky-core.ts), so the painted clouds also illuminate and reflect. Include GOBI credit, licence and modification notes in the asset record/credits. **Smallest proof:** summer versus gobi, keeping palette and cloud coverage comparable, to isolate whether painted contour/shading earns the image dependency. If no-attribution simplicity matters more, [Kenney day](https://kenney.nl/assets/skyboxes) is the complete 4K CC0 substitute, with baked-sun treatment first. Do not add both imports merely to fill a catalogue.

### Bounded proof and stop condition

Start each comparison at today's **1024** cube size to isolate art; separately compare **512** only on the promising look. Hold the scene, sun direction, camera, resolution, water/wave state, fixed gain and saved tuning state constant. Use the existing look harness and named shore/town views, add a sun-facing view, horizon pan, Buddy/foliage shade, lit sign and waterline/underwater sample. Inspect the same clouds in water, seams during a full turn, pale horizon blending, and whether the sun/reflection/shadows agree. Keep FFT, shore, foam and caustic detail visible.

For the later build, record source revision, selected recipe/file hash, cube size, bake count, ready-to-first-use time and raw/filtered/source allocations. Expect **one initial bake and zero play-time bakes**, no new steady-state scene passes, and no additional persistent cube for a single selected look. Compare cold/warm preparation separately on the M1 Max; do not extrapolate a safe mobile budget from draw counts. These are proposed checks, not results of this report.

The builder inspects stills and motion evidence before the owner plays; update the [owning water chapter](../game-design/water.md) only when implementation actually changes the view. One gut question: **“Which sky makes this island nicer to spend time in while keeping the water you liked?”** The current sky remains a valid winner. No rollout, automatic tuning beyond that judgement, or replacement of the accepted baseline is authorized by this report.
