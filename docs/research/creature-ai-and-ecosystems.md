# Fill the beach with cheap, honest critters

Boogle should fill its island with lots of critters that are cheap, cosmetic, and react to the ball, plus at most two creatures with real AI. It should not try to simulate an ecosystem. The research points to a four-tier authority model. Fish, gulls and fireflies are purely local cosmetic critters. Crabs follow a shared, seeded spawn schedule but move locally. A few host-simulated characters (the Boogler, a giant-crab event) are published in presence. Integer sim state is used only for what changes the world or must persist, such as pet affinity. Movement comes from Craig Reynolds' boids and steering behaviours, which still beat anything fancier at this scale ([Reynolds](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/)). Decisions come from a small utility layer with inertia, or simply from the shared 20-minute sky clock ([Game AI Pro ch.9](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf)). Rendering uses one InstancedMesh per species, animated in the vertex shader ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/3.1/tutorials/3d/vertex_animation/animating_thousands_of_fish.html)). The priorities, in order: a fish school that lives in water players dig and scatters when the ball splashes; gulls that burst off the sand; night crabs from shared burrows; a smarter Boogler who chases the ball; and a personal, tameable companion. Predator–prey population dynamics are the one idea to defer. They oscillate, they need stabilisers, and they create loss states that clash with "toys before chores" and "only the world is the antagonist".

## Three rules from 1987 still move every school and flock you need

Reynolds' boids model has three rules. Separation steers away from crowding neighbours, alignment matches their average heading, and cohesion moves toward their average position. Each boid looks only at flockmates inside a distance-and-angle neighbourhood ([Reynolds, Boids](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/)). The naive algorithm is **O(n²)**, and a spatial structure that keeps boids sorted by location brings it to **nearly O(n)** ([Reynolds](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/)). For Boogle that structure already exists: bucket critters by the terrain's cell grid. Even the official three.js GPGPU birds demo, at **1,024 birds**, does an all-pairs loop on the GPU. It flees a single "predator" point and stores a wing-flap phase in a spare texture channel ([three.js example](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/webgl_gpgpu_birds.html)). That predator point is a useful template. In Boogle the predator is the ball, a jumping player or a splash, and the scatter is the toy.

Individually watched critters need something better than blended force vectors. Reynolds' 1999 paper added wander, flee, evasion, arrival and containment, arranged as **action selection → steering → locomotion**. Wander works by nudging a persistent steering target instead of re-rolling each frame, which avoids jitter ([Reynolds GDC 1999](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/)). Andrew Fray shows that averaged behaviours cancel out, for example chase plus avoid giving roughly zero. Fray says this matters most "when the player can pick out and watch individual entities." His fix, context steering, has each behaviour write an interest map and a danger map over a handful of heading slots before choosing a heading. On F1 2011 it **removed 4,000 lines** and improved avoidance ([Game AI Pro 2 ch.18](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter18_Context_Steering_Behavior-Driven_Steering_at_the_Macro_Scale.pdf)). The split for Boogle: fish and gulls use boids, while crabs, pets and the Boogler use 8–16-slot context steering. Danger slots cover deep water, the mushroom patch, players and the ball. Interest slots cover wet sand, burrows and the owner.

## Visible needs and time-of-day schedules beat deep brains

Utility AI is the standard way to drive needs-based critters. Each consideration normalises an input to **0–1** using "bookends" such as 0–100 m and passes it through a response curve. The consideration scores are multiplied, and "runtime" and "cooldown" considerations stop repetition ([Lewis, Game AI Pro 3 ch.13](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)). Dave Mark's "infinite axis" system, shown at GDC 2013 and used in Guild Wars 2, is the canonical form ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018040/Architecture-Tricks-Managing-Behaviors); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_system)). Two practical details matter most at small scale. First, always taking the top score feels "very robotic," so pick by weighted random among near-best options. Second, re-deciding every frame causes dithering. The fix is to favour the current action or decide only when it ends, as The Sims Medieval did ([Game AI Pro ch.9](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf)). Priority buckets ("dual utility") keep "flee the ball" from ever competing with "nap" ([Dill, Game AI Pro 2 ch.3](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter03_Dual-Utility_Reasoning.pdf)).

The stronger lesson is to keep needs few and visible. Project Highrise reached **1,000 NPCs at 60 FPS** partly by dropping hunger and tiredness models. Their reason: hidden internal state confused both designers and players. Robert Zubek concludes that the value of detailed NPC modelling falls as NPC count rises ([Game AI Pro 3 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter34_1000_NPCs_at_60_FPS.pdf)). Rez Graham says looping idles and random walks look like NES-era filler. His alternative for background critters is a schedule that takes the time and returns "go here and run this animation" ([Game AI Pro ch.36](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter36_Breathing_Life_into_Your_Background_Characters.pdf)). Boogle already has the schedule input. `src/sim/sky-clock.ts` gives every client the same time of day and moon phase from `world.tick`. Slime Rancher shows the needs half done right. Its slimes are "driven by a series of wants and needs" that produce "hundreds of tiny, emergent stories" ([GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024296/A-Thousand-Tiny-Tales-Emergent)). The hidden hunger value shows on the slime's face, and feeding calms agitation ([Slime Rancher Wiki](https://slimerancher.fandom.com/wiki/Hunger); a fan source, treat details cautiously).

Rain World is the ecosystem reference, and its structure is small. Each creature has a **relationship table** to creatures, objects and places, plus optional modules: PreyTracker, ThreatTracker, FriendTracker, ItemTracker, DenFinder and DiscomfortTracker. Its notable interactions are unscripted ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)). The creatures pursue their own survival rather than patrol routes. Designers shaped behaviour mostly through level layout and "room attractiveness" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). For Boogle, the equivalent is a ten-line data table per species, listing what it fears, likes and treats as home. Players then shape behaviour the way Rain World's designers did, by digging and piling sand. World of Zoo's animals used negative-feedback control loops so they stayed believable when players rearranged their pens and "actively try to mess with them" ([Ramsey, Game AI Pro ch.44](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter44_A_Control-Based_Architecture_for_Animal_Behavior.pdf)). Boogle needs that robustness because every player is a terraformer. Its Boogler today is the stale random-walker Graham warns about: pick a reachable target, walk, wait 1.5–5 s ([`src/boogler/boogler.js`](../../src/boogler/boogler.js)).

## Spawn tables keyed to habitat outlast population sims

Animal Crossing makes its world feel alive with tables, not simulation. Fish appear by time window (9 AM–4 PM, 9 PM–4 AM), habitat (pond, river mouth, sea, pier) and weather; the coelacanth appears **only in rain**. Shadow size telegraphs what is there ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Fish/New_Horizons)). That design serves Boogle's "telegraph everything" and "leave something for the next visitor." When habitats come from shared state, such as water depth from the integer sim, wet sand and dug ponds, a pond one friend dug yesterday has fish in it today, with no creature state saved at all.

Real predator–prey dynamics are the wrong fit. Lotka–Volterra systems cycle, and games built on them, like The Reef, are explicitly about how hard it is to keep both species alive ([The Reef](https://jakemchugh1.itch.io/the-reef)). They can be stabilised with refuges, which match observed mite populations ([Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/joining-or-opting-out-of-a-lotka-volterra-game-between-predators-/)), or with a shared predator ([Cheon](https://www.mech.kochi-tech.ac.jp/cheon/ecogame/ecolo-game06a_e.html)). Each stabiliser is one more thing to tune. Rain World's own balance swung on placement luck, and streamers' first sessions varied widely ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). In a drop-in toy where crops are protected, a collapsing gull population is a chore and a loss state. The strongest case for population dynamics is long-term "living island" texture. If Boogle wants that, it should keep coarse integer counts per region with a floor above zero, feed them only from habitat, and let them change only spawn rates.

## Cost and authority: one draw call per species, four tiers of truth

Rendering is cheap if every species is a single `InstancedMesh`: one draw for all instances, updated with `setMatrixAt` plus `instanceMatrix.needsUpdate`. Remember `computeBoundingSphere()` after moving instances, or frustum culling hides them ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html)). Animation belongs in the vertex shader. The Godot fish recipe uses four cosines (side-to-side, pivot, a wave travelling down the body, twist), masked so the head stays still, with a random phase and **50–150% speed** per instance. It claims "thousands of animated objects, even on low end hardware" ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/3.1/tutorials/3d/vertex_animation/animating_thousands_of_fish.html)). The same trick covers gull wings and crab legs. This matters because Boogle's recent commits cut draw calls and mesh cost ("Draw each mushroom's spots as one mesh", "Use a coarser sea mesh"), and iPad play over Backbone is a target. Cosmetic critters should also use AI level of detail: tick only near the local camera and slower farther out, the standard practice Ben Sunshine-Hill formalises as a noticeability budget ([LOD Trader, Game AI Pro ch.14](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter14_Phenomenal_AI_Level-of-Detail_Control_with_the_LOD_Trader.pdf)).

Authority follows from one hard fact. Floats are not reliably identical across machines, and transcendental functions differed between AMD and Intel in Battlezone 2 ([Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/)). Boogle's recent "Ban native trig in the deterministic shared sim" commit already reflects this. That gives the four tiers below. The Boogler shows tier 3 works. The host publishes `g: { p, r, w }` in presence, others interpolate, and clients walk him locally after 3 s without a host. His brain uses floats because he never touches terrain, water or crops ([`src/boogler/boogler.js`](../../src/boogler/boogler.js)).

| Tier | What it is | Shared? | Use for | Plugs in via |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cosmetic local | Floats, `Math.random`, per-client positions | No | Fish, gulls, fireflies, sand hoppers | `src/features/<name>.js` with `onFrame`, reading water/terrain read-only, reacting to `onApplied` edits |
| 2. Shared schedule, local motion | Spawn place and time are a pure function of `world.tick` + `world.seed` (mulberry32); motion is cosmetic | Spawns yes, paths no | Crabs emerging from burrows, a coelacanth-style rare visitor in rain | Same as tier 1, with the schedule in a pure `src/sim/` module and unit tests |
| 3. Host-published | Host simulates, broadcasts a few bytes in presence; local fallback | Yes (approximate) | The Boogler, giant-crab event, at most ~3 entities | Boogler pattern: presence field, interpolation, `STALE_MS` fallback |
| 4. Integer sim state | `registerOp`/`registerState`, integer, hashed, persisted | Exactly | Pet affinity, anything that drops seeds or edits the world | `src/world/sync.js` contract, `src/sim/` pure logic, tests for determinism and round-trips |

Tier 2 solves a real co-op problem. If crabs are purely local, "look at that crab!" points at nothing on your friend's screen. If the burrow and emergence tick are shared, everyone sees a crab pop out of the same hole at the same moment, even though its scuttle path differs slightly between clients.

## Prioritized creature ideas for Boogle

| # | Creature | Tier | Behaviour (cheap version) | Principle served | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shallows fish school** | 1 | Boids (grid-bucketed) in any sim-water cell deeper than a threshold and in the surf. Scatter (flee, speed boost) from ball splashes and swimmers, then re-school. One InstancedMesh, Godot-style vertex swim | Ball stays the star (every splash scatters fish); Toys before chores; Leave something (dug ponds get fish) | S |
| 2 | **Beach gulls** | 1 | Loaf on dry sand facing the wind, burst into a boids flock when the ball lands or a player runs near, circle, resettle. Roost at night from the sky clock. Vertex-shader wing flap | Slapstick over precision; Ball stays the star | S |
| 3 | **Night crabs** | 2 | Burrows seeded on wet sand. Emergence at dusk and low tide comes from tick+seed. Context steering sideways scuttle, flee players, dive into the nearest dug hole. Shovel a burrow and it relocates | Telegraph everything (crabs mean evening); Only the world is the antagonist (crabs never harm crops) | M |
| 4 | **Smarter Boogler** | 3 | Utility layer over the current walker: chase-the-ball, follow-a-player, nap at night, watch the sunset, avoid water, admire mushrooms. Inertia and weighted random. Moods visible on his face and in his name tag. He can nudge the ball back toward players through his existing kinematic body | Ball stays the star; Designed for drop-in (alone is pleasant) | M |
| 5 | **Fireflies / glow plankton** | 1 | Points drifting over wet sand and the mushroom patch at night, brighter around fresh splashes. Pure function of time plus cheap noise | Leave something (night has its own look) | S |
| 6 | **Personal companion (tameable hermit crab or mini-Boogler)** | 4 + owner-simulated | Offer a mushroom cap or bounce the ball to it. Each try is a probabilistic success, as with Minecraft's **1/3 per bone** wolf ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Wolf)), with hearts or a collar as feedback. Affinity is an integer op per player. Once tamed, it follows its owner, is simulated by the owner and published in the owner's presence like the player avatar, and teleports back if stranded beyond ~12 m (Minecraft rule) | Communal world, personal rewards; Cooperation is automatic (anyone can feed it) | M–L |
| 7 | **Giant crab low-tide event** | 3 | Telegraphed 30–60 s ahead, crawls out at low tide. Players chase it off with ball hits and splashes. Drops personal cosmetics | Telegraph everything; Only the world is the antagonist | M (after tide lands) |
| 8 | **Regional critter counts (ecosystem lite)** | 4 | Integer per-region counts fed by habitat (water depth, wet sand) that only modulate tier 1–2 spawn rates, with a floor above zero | Leave something for the next visitor | Defer |

Each idea is a plug-in file in `src/features/`, with its CSS imported by the feature and DOM built in JS. Pure logic lives in `src/sim/` for unit tests, and no shared files change beyond the usual one-liners. Ideas 1, 2 and 5 never touch the world contract. Idea 3 adds a pure, tested spawn-schedule function. Idea 4 extends the existing presence field. Idea 6 is the only one that needs a new `registerOp`/`registerState` pair, plus a gamepad slot via `registerAction` for "offer". Weather reactions (fish and gulls hiding in rain, a rain-only visitor) can wait for the weather cycle on PLAN.md's stretch list. When weather lands, its state is a pure function of tick+seed, so tiers 1–2 can read it for free.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "how smart should Boogle's creatures be" to "who needs to agree they exist." Most of the life on a beach is set dressing that reacts to players. It can be fully local, float-based and nearly free, as long as it reacts to the shared things friends notice: the ball, splashes, dug water and the sky clock. Only a few creatures earn shared truth, and the cheapest shared truth is a schedule rather than a simulation. A seeded "when and where does it appear" delivers most of what an ecosystem would, without a desync surface or a way to lose.

The unresolved risks are empirical, not architectural. No source measured instanced-critter counts on iPad-class GPUs inside three.js, or the presence bandwidth of extra host-published entities over PeerJS. The fish school is the right first build partly because it answers both questions cheaply: build it, profile it with `npm run look` and `?netdebug=1`, and let those numbers set how ambitious the companion and the Boogler's new brain can be.
