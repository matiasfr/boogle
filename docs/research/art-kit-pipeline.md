# Art kit pipeline: repeatable pieces, physical assembly, one coherent world

_Research report, 2 November 2026. An agent-driven production pipeline for Boogle's track, trestles, receiving stations, machines, cliffs, scaffolded buildings, engines and cosmetic attachments across six eras. Live primary-source research and repository inspection at `a88effba`. Proposes a contract, folder layout, validation workflow, device budgets and a two-place station proof. Research only: no new kit, gameplay, approved plan or device performance is claimed._

**Evidence:** **[V]** primary text, developer interview answers, documentation or paper fetched and read; **[A]** official talk listing/abstract only; **[L]** local source or manifest inspected; **[I]** inference/proposal for Boogle; **[H]** untested hypothesis or estimate. No talk recording was watched. Morten Olsen's trim-sheet slides and Valve's illustrative-rendering paper were read as documents. Blender's public manual fetches failed; its bundled official manual/API excerpts were read through the Blender documentation tools instead. No live Blender scene was changed. No game or iPad benchmark was run; the existing asset freshness check was run successfully.

## Answer and scope

**Make the kit a small, versioned system of compatible parts and assembly recipes. Agents generate, fit, rebuild and inspect it; the owner judges the few choices that establish its character.** A directory of attractive meshes is insufficient. The deliverable includes connection frames, usable space, construction order, collision descriptions, near/far representations and evidence that the same pieces work in a second place.

The best next step is to formalize the working **station frame and feeder**, then reuse it at another existing station with a different orientation and approach. Do not begin by producing all six eras. Keep reusable structural families, era-specific shape additions and place-specific arrangements separate. This extends the working Python → GLB path rather than introducing a general building editor or replacing three.js.

The principles served are [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)'s **Telegraph everything**, **Toys before chores**, **Leave something for the next visitor**, **Small and readable**, and **Code first, images by approval**. The [owner's decision log](../proposals/boogle-2.md) supplies the sunlit Mario Sunshine target, physical piece-by-piece construction, solid-looking surfaces that are solid, six eras, Buddy, and the M4 iPad Pro floor. [Rail slice](../plans/rail-slice.md) remains a draft awaiting approval; this report does not authorize its implementation.

The main hypotheses to test are:

| Hypothesis                                                                                             | What would support it                                                                                                            | What would disprove it                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H1: constrained kits make agent art cheap in practice.**                                             | The second station uses the same built geometry and contract, with less than half the first placement's integration/rework time. | Each location needs mesh patches, manual export fixes or a new generator branch.                   |
| **H2: shared shape rules plus era additions preserve identity.**                                       | Wood, electric and future silhouettes differ, while a mixed-era street still looks like Boogle.                                  | Eras are recolours, or the same street looks like unrelated asset packs.                           |
| **H3: simple bevels, deliberate normals and restrained cavity colour are enough for ordinary pieces.** | They hold up at the real camera beside the approved look without unique texture painting.                                        | Contact and material identity still fail in motion, requiring a more expensive treatment.          |
| **H4: most routine production can run without owner modelling.**                                       | Agents complete repeat assets and verification; the owner only selects/rejects a small comparison.                               | Owner repair time becomes the recurring bottleneck.                                                |
| **H5: bounded batches and faithful proxies fit the target.**                                           | Actual M4 Safari cold arrival and sustained construction stay within the declared frame/residency envelope.                      | Only a staged Mac still meets it, or distant kits cannot unload without losing identity/collision. |

## Lessons from production

### Bethesda: prove the joins before decorating the kit

**[V]** Joel Burgess and Nathan Purkeypile describe kits as systems, with concept, proof, greybox and production work. Their Skyrim transcript stresses compatible footprints, room for wall thickness, gameplay dimensions and testing a kit by looping it back onto itself. A misaligned loop should lead to fixing the underlying module rather than accumulating special filler pieces. Artist and level designer develop the kit together. [Developer-authored GDC transcript](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design). **[A]** Their Fallout 4 session extends the modular-kit/iterative-level-production subject; its recording was not watched. [Official session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023202/-Fallout-4-s-Modular).

**[I]** Boogle's equivalent is a builder testing the station while authoring its parts: rotated feeder, back approach, repeated bay, ground-height change, and a returning loop of beams. Make joints precise but silhouettes deliberately irregular away from them. Avoid importing Bethesda's world scale, staffing model or numerical player metrics. Our proof is two useful arrangements, not an enormous complete kit.

### Tiny Glade and Townscaper: procedural charm is a set of authored decisions

**[V]** Tiny Glade's developers describe hand-authored procedural rules and specialised generation work. Supporting elevated buildings created further support/overlap questions; seemingly small user requests led to repeated interaction redesign. [Opara and Stachowiak interview](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games). This establishes the cost of making combinations behave gracefully, not a promise that a generator makes art for free.

**[V]** Townscaper's own description establishes an irregular grid whose block configurations become houses, arches, stairs, bridges and yards. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291340/Townscaper/). **[A]** Stålberg's [Konsoll talk](https://konsoll.org/talks/the-story-of-townscaper/) is a primary technical reference, but only its listing was inspected. The exact dual-grid/WFC implementation is not independently verified here; no recommendation requires reproducing it.

**[I]** Borrow context-sensitive **approved combinations**: a beam end has a cap, a corner has a matching wall, a raised floor has supports. Generate candidates offline and retain accepted recipes. Do not rerun a whole-town solver after each delivery or deform a station arbitrarily around terrain. Use small shape families and explicit adapters for awkward sites. The owner's current construction requirement needs inspectable intermediate pieces, whereas an instantaneous Townscaper transformation can conceal its assembly.

### Astroneer: simplify the art process while retaining physical information

**[V]** In the 2016 developer interview, Adam Bromell explicitly describes eliminating textures as a production advantage for the small team. Jacob Liechty describes conveying information through visible character/equipment responses. This is an account of that development stage, not proof that every later Astroneer asset is texture-free or uses a particular vertex shader. [System Era interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/inspired-space-inside-the-development-of-astroneer).

**[I]** Let broad coloured surfaces and readable mechanical interfaces do the work. A log bed, roller, wheel and attachment mount should explain their purpose before material noise is added. Keep a short shared material vocabulary; spend geometry on an engine's profile and working mechanisms. Do not infer that low polygon count makes layered translucency, huge shadow coverage or many independent draws inexpensive.

### A Short Hike and Sable: a consistent omission is part of style

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu describes low resolution shaping A Short Hike's flat shading and outline treatment alongside its compact explorable landscape. [Developer retrospective](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/). **[A]** Sable's art talk explicitly concerns small-team limitations, handmade appearance and gameplay readability; only its abstract was available. [Kythreotis's GDC session](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027721/The-Art-of-Sable-Imperfection).

**[I]** For Boogle, omit small surface noise consistently. Preserve the approved sunlit, rounded industrial toy language rather than importing Sable's ink or A Short Hike's pixel filter. An era can change roof profile, structure and machinery without changing the whole rendering language. Inspect a kit at the ordinary camera before spending time on a close-up that players rarely see.

### Nintendo and Fall Guys: many variants still need one recognizable family

**[V]** Nintendo's Ami Takai describes creating hundreds of Animal Crossing: New Horizons furniture objects with external modellers, written art guidance, recurring review of references and attention to each object's personality within the whole. The Japanese account also describes modelling for a placement grid and viewing objects around their sides/back. [Nintendo's production account](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/jobs/keyword/90.html), read in Japanese and paraphrased here. This is evidence for a coherent modular **object** catalogue, not evidence that Nintendo generates entire towns with the same kit algorithm.

**[V]** Fall Guys lead artist Rob Jackson describes maintaining the underlying bean identity when adapting Ratchet and Clank into costumes. [Developer art account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/07/22/ratchet-and-clank-blast-into-fall-guys-for-limited-time-events-and-unique-rewards/).

**[I]** Apply those production lessons to six-era architecture and gifts. Keep a common scale, edge treatment, palette roles and attachment envelope. A future train should still belong beside an old wood shelter. A gift can alter a train's silhouette without clipping the rider, feeder or other approved gifts. Retain Buddy's actual approved rig and appearance; the older characters report is not permission to redesign it.

### Valve and Sunset Overdrive: silhouette first; trim reuse where it helps

**[V]** Valve's paper describes testing class readability through silhouette and coordinating interior detail with large shapes. [Mitchell, Francke and Eng, NPAR 2007](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf). **[I]** Use the same diagnostic on stations, engine roles and era landmarks: black silhouettes and small monochrome views, not a similarity score against a painting.

**[V]** Morten Olsen's Sunset Overdrive slides describe a standardized trim layout and normal-map bevels; material maps can swap without moving the mapped edges. [The Ultimate Trim](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Olsen_Morten_TheUltimateTrim.pdf). **[I]** A trim sheet is attractive for repeated metal frames, panel seams and concrete ledges in later eras. It is not the first dependency for the wood station: actual one- or two-segment bevels plus colour work from all angles and preserve silhouette. Normal-map bevels cannot round an outline. Compare a small representative trim experiment only when repeated surface detail becomes expensive to author.

## Proposed kit contract, version 1

**[I/H] Proposed, not implemented.** A kit has three products: **render parts**, **headless physical/attachment metadata**, and **assembly recipes** referencing stable part IDs. Metadata is authoritative for joins and usable space; compressed render vertices are not. A public part ID survives LOD changes, mesh deduplication and palette revisions.

| Field                   | Proposed rule and concrete check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity                | Kit ID such as `wood-station`, integer `schemaVersion`, separate `geometryRevision`; part ID `frame_post`. Node names `frame_post__lod0__wood` and `frame_post__lod1__wood` are mapped explicitly, never guessed from traversal order. Reject duplicate names/automatic `.001` suffixes on public nodes. Preserve old prototype names until its adapter changes.                            |
| Units/axes              | Metres, right-handed game coordinates: +Y up, +Z along/forward, +X across. Retain place-kit's Blender mapping `(x, -z, y)`. Include a one-metre calibration part in authoring QA, not the shipped scene.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Pivots/transforms       | Base centre for posts/floors, axle centre for wheels/rollers, hinge/grip centre for moving mechanisms. Source part roots have identity rotation/scale and zero translation; vertices carry shape. Quantization may add child transforms: compare decoded output in the logical part frame, not raw accessor bounds. Never reset those decode transforms as if they were authoring mistakes. |
| Dimensions              | Explicit nominal span, footprint bounds, rendered overhang and usable-space envelope. Keep current station dimensions initially; do not round a 2.6 m frame or 3.05 m rail to a new global grid. Future building bays can use declared compatible increments, but joint frames win over an assumed grid.                                                                                    |
| Permitted variation     | Enumerated length/profile options; frozen seed for chips/lean away from joins. No random placement-time deformation of sockets. Reject negative scale; nonuniform stretch only on declared axes/ranges, with fixed-size end caps and material coordinates checked.                                                                                                                          |
| Bevels/normals          | Shared edge classes: start with 10–30 mm timber chamfers, 5–15 mm metal edges, 1–2 segments **[H]**; use larger silhouette cuts for cliffs. Treat broad rock planes as hard; smooth round boilers; preserve hard/smooth boundaries and valid tangents. Values are starting choices, not a universal bevel modifier on everything.                                                           |
| Material roles          | Stable `wood`, `paint`, `steel`, `stone`, `accent`, mapped to shared TSL node materials. Colour is linear in buffers; sRGB reference swatches convert once. Prefer one primitive per material role, then batch across parts. A new colour alone must not create another material program.                                                                                                   |
| Colour/contact channels | Declare whether `COLOR_0` is actual albedo/tint or neutral cavity multiplier; never silently reinterpret it. Optional `_BOOGLE_AO` is a proposed separate scalar attribute, with explicit exporter/loader/batch handling and a round-trip check. Alpha remains opacity, not a spare AO channel.                                                                                             |
| Connections             | Every socket has ID, kind, position and quaternion in the part/assembly frame, mating rule, allowed rotations, capacity and clearance envelope. Distinguish structural, delivery, rider, cosmetic and effect sockets. Matching a name is insufficient.                                                                                                                                      |
| Construction            | Recipe lists stable piece slots, prerequisites, final transform, incoming route/grip, phase, material allocation and activation event. Scaffold pieces are temporary support; walls/roof are permanent. Moving piece count and sweep bounds are declared.                                                                                                                                   |
| Collision               | Numeric primitives/convex hulls, or explicit static concave mesh data, named independently from render LOD. Include walkable faces, door/path clearance and activation phase. A debug `col_` mesh is only an authoring visualization; GLTFLoader never creates physics implicitly.                                                                                                          |
| LOD/proxy               | Named representations share pivot and exterior envelope. Mid/far versions remove detail deliberately; a town proxy is generated from its actual settled assembly state. Collision does not downgrade because the camera moved away.                                                                                                                                                         |
| Animation               | Named clips and affected hierarchy documented; default rest pose equals the final fitted pose. Timing/contact markers live in recipe data, not hidden clip callbacks. Prefer code trajectories for rigid pieces; use authored clips for mechanisms that need them.                                                                                                                          |
| Bounds/budgets          | Source and decoded vertices/triangles/bytes, primitives, material programs, instance count, shadow policy, resident textures, colliders, moving pieces and sweep bounds. Separate unique-library cost from placed-scene cost.                                                                                                                                                               |
| Build/evidence          | Source/dependency/output hashes; Blender/exporter/Node/package/encoder versions, settings and seed; validation result, review captures and accepted revision. A missing input or required part fails the build instead of silently using a stale output.                                                                                                                                    |

### Attachment frames that both construction and gifts can use

Use socket +Z as the outward normal and +Y as its up/tangent reference. Face-to-face structural mating reverses the normal; a mount/gift may use a different explicit mating transform. In presentation code:

```text
childWorld = parentWorld × parentSocket × matingTransform × inverse(childSocket)
```

The same rule works for a timber beam's end and a locomotive's cosmetic mount. Define `beam_end`, `post_top`, `feed_in`, `feed_out`, `grip`, `gift_mount`, `rider_clearance` separately. For an axle, declare the axis explicitly (+X for the current wheel); do not infer it from mesh extent. A gift declares its anchor, maximum envelope, compatible mount kinds and allowed orientation; reject pairs that overlap rider/carry space or a mechanism's swept path. This does not add a gift economy. **[I]**

For the current `frame_post`, the proposed metadata can describe a 0.34 × 1.25 × 0.34 m body, bottom at `[0,0,0]`, top joint at `[0,1.25,0]`, and a box centred at `[0,0.625,0]` with half-extents `[0.17,0.625,0.17]`. The top joint's outward frame points up; it is not the identity quaternion. Its cap is a separate part. Check fitted joint residuals after export/optimization: **≤1 mm translation and ≤0.1° rotation [H]** for station fittings. These tolerances are QA targets, not physics guarantees. Large cliff modules need their own justified tolerance; enlarging every asset's allowed error would conceal a bad station joint.

For game-critical placement, compile accepted transforms into the existing headless data/blueprint path. Render-side matrix composition can use three.js; shared simulation must use precomputed data or allowed deterministic math. A change to saved shape or operation meaning still follows [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)'s namespace/version policy; a new art revision is not permission to change physical state.

### Physical construction: preserve material identity through the stages

**[I]** Author a building as a small dependency graph of pieces, not one final mesh with a scale animation. The recipe owns a stable slot order and named contacts:

| Phase                   | What the player sees                                             | Physical/assembly obligation                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reserved footprint      | Plinth, stack location, delivery access                          | Streets, approaches and future exit stay clear. Show the boundary before walls arrive.                   |
| Scaffold and foundation | Poles, ties and floor supports placed at full size               | Temporary support has a declared lifetime; walkable platforms get matching colliders.                    |
| Frame                   | Posts, beams and braces seated in order                          | A beam cannot precede its supports; expose useful negative space and construction joints.                |
| Infill and roof         | Wall bays, door surround, roof spans and caps                    | Close exposed ends; preserve the doorway and safe standing surfaces in every partial state.              |
| Machinery and finish    | Rollers, chimney, engine fittings or signs attached              | Hinges, axles and working clearances agree with the actual mechanism.                                    |
| Settled                 | Scaffold visibly dismantles; finished parts enter static batches | Remove temporary support only under the declared occupancy policy; preserve permanent walkable surfaces. |

Material accounting follows [rail-slice's material-identity proposal](../plans/rail-slice.md): accepted cargo slots are assigned to one destination and transformed by explicit integer recipes. A bundle may become several visible planks, but document the conversion. Do not display the same accepted log simultaneously on the player's back, in the frame and in the machine. Visual plank count is not a new inventory currency.

Keep permanent progress and collision activation on accepted shared events/ticks when promoted. Local trajectories can be analytic falls or authored motions; they do not need a rigid body for every nail. A live collidable moving component uses the existing physics/authority model, never lockstep Rapier. `onApplied` starts presentation; a clip ending never consumes stock or completes the shared build. A late join reconstructs the settled/current phase instead of replaying old falling floors beneath a player. **This is a production seam to design under the approved shared implementation, not something the local place proof already guarantees.**

For occupied construction, make the plan physically possible: route walls/roofs around reserved access; defer a blocking phase on an authoritative occupancy decision or choose a nonblocking recipe. Never let each tab decide permanence from its own animation or view distance. The first art proof can exercise single-player occupied states; co-op correctness belongs to the later sync work. [Rapier's collider guidance](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders/) supports primitives and convex shapes for moving objects and concave meshes for appropriate static surfaces. Use the facade, not a collider per visible bevel.

## Authoring split and folder layout

### Put each task where it is easiest to revise

| Task                                                                                                | Default production method                                                     | Owner involvement                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Layout, routes, grade, construction recipes, sockets, collider numbers, runtime motion and batching | TypeScript data/tools and the existing runtime                                | Judge the place/action; no manual placement of every piece required.                                 |
| Timber, trestle sections, rail profiles, station frames, rollers, simple machines and engine shells | Headless Blender Python with explicit parameters, bmesh and bounded modifiers | Pick silhouette/proportion variants and identify what feels wrong.                                   |
| Repeated cliff/rim families, shaped roof profiles, controlled rock/tree variation                   | Python first; Geometry Nodes when a reusable graph makes edits clearer        | Select a few distinctive forms; inspect them beside the accepted style anchor.                       |
| One hero engine, unusual landmark, expressive organic form                                          | Agent-generated blockout, then focused Blender editing if needed              | Optional hand shaping/paintover where it changes the result; not a mandatory manual modelling queue. |
| Rigged characters and authored locomotion                                                           | Existing Buddy source and approved animation pipeline                         | Preserve approved silhouette/rig; specialist hand work only for an identified gap.                   |
| Colour, grain, roughness, contact and scene light                                                   | Shared TSL material roles, modest offline cavity bake                         | Judge one representative sheet/in-game comparison, not every numeric parameter.                      |
| Rare decal or trim image                                                                            | Existing image workflow after the object proves itself                        | Pick every shipped generated image as DESIGN requires. References stay references.                   |

**[V]** Blender's [Realize Instances](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/instances/realize_instances.html) documentation explains that realization converts shared instances to independent geometry and can greatly increase processing cost. Its [operator API](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.ops.html) explains context overrides. These sections were read from the bundled official docs. **[I]** Use Geometry Nodes to generate a bounded vocabulary, bake evaluated output per part, and retain instances during authoring where possible. Do not export a whole town after realizing every repeated beam. Version/pin the graph and Blender alongside its inputs; topology-changing modifiers can alter names, attributes and vertex counts.

Headless generation starts from factory state and writes only its declared outputs. Explicitly set object/mode/selection when using operators; prefer direct mesh/data operations for repeatable bulk work. A `.blend` authored by the owner is a source with its own generator/export entry; regeneration must not overwrite hand edits. Pick one canonical source per asset ID and record dependencies, rather than leaving both `<id>.py` and `<id>.blend` as competing top-level inputs. Python remains Blender's asset-authoring language under the existing `assets/src` convention; new runtime and `dev/` tooling is TypeScript.

## Materials, compression and normals

### Vertex colour and baked AO

**[V]** glTF defines `COLOR_0` as a linear base-colour multiplier, not a standardized ambient-occlusion channel. [glTF 2.0 specification](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/specification/2.0/Specification.adoc). Blender supports baking to an active colour attribute; read from its bundled [Cycles baking manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html). The exporter documents active colour export without materials, custom underscore attributes and evaluated modifiers. [Khronos exporter documentation](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/blob/main/docs/blender_docs/scene_gltf2.rst).

**[I/H3]** First use a neutral, restrained **self-cavity multiplier** in `COLOR_0` for tintable construction pieces; coloured props must explicitly declare their different albedo convention. Bake each reusable part without a ground plane or temporary neighbours, with fixed settings and enough vertices to represent the cavity. Otherwise an underside stays dirty while flying and a moved beam carries a shadow from a vanished wall. Do not subdivide every flat face solely to paint a soft shadow: a little strategic topology or a different AO representation can be cheaper.

If the multiplier makes direct sun look muddy, compare a separate `_BOOGLE_AO` consumed through `aoNode`. That path requires preservation through the exporter, optimizer, loader and batching helper; it is not already supported by `look-kit.bake()`. Keep tint, cavity, material IDs and gameplay surface tags distinct. Assembly contact darkening belongs to the settled arrangement or a small existing blob, not permanently in every piece. Check for double darkening from cavity, procedural contact and shadows. No new GTAO pass follows from adopting baked contact.

For normals, inspect the final optimized mesh under rotating directional light and the game sun. A weighted-normal modifier can make broad faces read well but cannot repair self-intersections, bad winding, zero-area triangles or a wrongly applied scale. Preserve intended hard edges and transform normals with the proper normal matrix when baking transforms. Flat faces/UV discontinuities split exported vertices; count the exported vertices, not just Blender's edit-mode vertices. Tangents need verification only for parts that use normal maps.

### Trim sheets and glTF delivery

**[I]** Keep the first station texture-free. Later try one common trim layout for metal rims/painted panel edges, with defined bands, padding, texel density, orientation and allowed UV stretching. Reuse that UV layout across material variants. Give end caps a deliberate UV rule; mirrored trims and discontinuous bevel normals can create seams. A single trim must not force every era to have the same ribbed silhouette. Shared texture reuse reduces authoring/storage, but separate materials or shader options can still increase draws.

**[V]** glTF Transform's [meshopt transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/modules/functions/functions/meshopt) performs reorder/quantization/compression; it does not by itself make a LOD hierarchy. Khronos's [KTX artist guide](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/blob/main/KTXArtistGuide.md) demonstrates ETC1S for many colour maps and UASTC for normal/packed data, with exceptions after visual inspection. Texture factors avoid a texture entirely.

**[I]** Retain the existing meshopt pipeline. Preserve logical roots/names and decode transforms; quantize after topology/LOD/attribute decisions. Validate sockets against decoded final geometry, since origin/scale errors can hide in the compressed file. Separate sharp seams deliberately rather than assuming weld repairs them. Record semantic rebuild equivalence as well as file hashes: tool upgrades can change bytes without changing the asset, while identical counts can hide a broken pivot.

For a future trim, use source PNG, generated mipmaps and current KTX2 tooling; compare ETC1S and UASTC on actual broad gradients and crisp boundaries. Store colour textures as sRGB and AO/roughness/normal data as linear. The build's current 256 px exception and 1024 px default remain the starting rules. KTX2 is a container/transcoding path, not a fixed GPU footprint: inspect the selected target format rather than promising ASTC on every device. A 1024² texture with a full mip chain is about **5.33 MiB at RGBA8**, versus **1.33 MiB at 8 bits/pixel**, before allocation overhead; this is arithmetic, not measured Safari memory. The current texture-free kit does not exercise the textured KTX2 path.

## Coherence across the six eras and different places

**[I/H2]** Keep a short accepted style anchor: one station, one machine, one cliff and Buddy at the real camera under the accepted place look. Record proportions, edge classes, material roles, saturation/value hierarchy, detail scale and permitted asymmetry. Do not change the follow camera or whole-game daylight to make a kit comparison win. Select three meaningful silhouette candidates when a new family needs judgment; the agent eliminates broken/unreadable candidates first.

The six era names are the owner's decision; the specific art below is a proposal:

| Era                 | Distinctive form addition                                                          | Reuse that keeps it Boogle                                                                              |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wood                | Chunky timber bents, pegged joints, short shelters, simple open machinery          | Shared span/socket families, warm wood and quiet ground; founder structures remain.                     |
| Steam               | Bulbous boilers, visible rods, broad chimney/cab masses                            | Same platform/rider envelopes and timber foundation grammar; brass is an accent.                        |
| Iron and harbour    | Braced gantries, riveted plate silhouettes, wide cranes and quay edges             | Common steel role and footings; stronger structural rhythm rather than more surface noise.              |
| Diesel and concrete | Low long engine hoods, thick slab canopies, broad vents                            | Human/door/rider scale and round edge language remain; stone/concrete separates by form and value.      |
| Electric            | Sleeker bullet train profile, pantograph, overhead support rhythm                  | Clear working attachments and established paint roles; do not assume this era clears all grime.         |
| Future              | One distinctive guideway/vehicle silhouette and a few surprising structural curves | Existing station/gift/access envelopes; no compulsory exact monorail and no entirely new shading style. |

Share structural schemas, not necessarily every visible mesh. A steam boiler should not be a stretched timber box. Use era overlays for details, a few new silhouette-bearing parts where needed, and place recipes for orientation, stepped foundations, roof rhythm and landmark context. A quarry and harbour must differ in massing and contact with terrain before colours change. For cliffs, follow [stylized-terrain](stylized-terrain.md): broad face, inside/outside corner, ledge, cap and foot families, not heightfield strips decorated with tiny rocks. Each place keeps recognizable old work rather than swapping all its buildings at an era boundary.

Reject a combinatorial catalogue. Test every declared compatible socket pair mechanically, then a small pairwise set of approved visual combinations plus worst-case extremes. New combinations enter through reviewed recipes; a validator cannot certify an arbitrary attractive town. Track exceptions: repeated site-specific patches are evidence that the base family is wrong, while one deliberately unique landmark is not a pipeline failure.

## Rendering and M4 iPad budgets

### Count instances, visibility and passes

**[V]** [InstancedMesh](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) shares geometry/material across transforms and requires bounds/update maintenance. [BatchedMesh](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BatchedMesh.html) requires compatible attribute layouts across its geometries. **[L]** The installed three.js r187 WebGPU backend loops over BatchedMesh's merged ranges and calls `drawIndexed`/`draw` for each; see [upstream r187 source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js). One BatchedMesh object therefore does not guarantee one WebGPU command.

**[I]** Instance repeated poles/rollers/sleepers by geometry, material role, LOD and spatial cell. Merge settled unique geometry by compatible material within a bounded cell where that reduces actual draws. Keep active pieces in a small moving pool, then transfer to settled batches without a visible duplicate. Start with **32–48 m cells [H]**, refining around station/terrain visibility. Recompute bounds when content changes; do not inherit `frustumCulled = false`. Stop updating settled matrices each frame. An entity owns lifetime and metadata while its many visual pieces share batches.

Near LOD retains accessible surfaces and useful mechanisms; middle LOD removes tiny fittings and groups structural masses; far proxies preserve landmark/roof shape and construction state. Try simple swaps with **10–15% hysteresis [H]** first, then measured crossfades only where needed. Use projected size and real route views to choose thresholds; distance alone misjudges a huge crane and a small crate. Far shadows, hidden bolts and tiny resident props are expendable; entrances and walkable collision are not. Streaming needs explicit shared-resource ownership and eviction, including cache/material references; disposing a shared geometry through one clone breaks the others.

### Initial envelope, explicitly unmeasured

Do not increase limits merely because agents can produce more art. Conversely, do not flatten good silhouettes to satisfy an invented hardware constant. These are allocations to test, not M4 specifications:

`npm run counts` currently measures a fixed normal-game pose, not an arbitrary station action. Use it for the whole-game baseline; obtain targeted counts through the existing look/perf/debug tooling at the named proof view. Use [asset priorities](../../src/render/asset-manifest.ts) to request the next kit early; a prefetch is only an HTTP fetch, not a completed decode/compile. ## How an agent verifies what it made

**[I]** Automate objective checks and capture generation, then have the building agent inspect the images and motion before the owner sees anything. Keep this in asset tools and the existing look harness; follow the test diet rather than adding UI unit tests or a scenario for each model.

1. **Freeze the brief and baseline.** Name the visible question, accepted style anchor, real camera, dimensions, action, source revision and render settings. Distinguish the painting's shape goals from its camera/light advantages. Make a matched engine capture before edits.
2. **Generate in isolation.** Record seed, dependency closure, Blender/exporter/tool versions and canonical source. Rebuild a second time in an empty temporary directory. Compare names, topology counts, attributes, bounds, joint frames and output hash where byte determinism is expected. Do not rewrite owner-edited sources.
3. **Validate raw and optimized files.** Run the [Khronos glTF Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator), then Boogle contract checks. Record unsupported-extension warnings rather than treating the validator as proof of meshopt/KTX2 correctness. Decode through the production loader too. Check finite vertices, valid indices/normals, zero-area triangles, winding, bounds, materials, attributes, names, socket fits, collider descriptions and LOD envelopes. Intentional open render surfaces can exist; declared collision hulls must meet their topology requirements.
4. **Make a contact sheet/turntable.** Eight azimuths plus top/underside, front/side orthographic views at the same scale, full-colour, neutral clay and black silhouette. Show Buddy and a metre reference. Inspect raw versus optimized versions under fixed and rotating light. This exposes one-camera modelling, backface holes, swollen bevels and lumpy normals.
5. **Compare in the game.** With [look](../../.claude/skills/look/SKILL.md), frame approach, delivery side, opposite side, beneath the trestle, aboard the train and a distant view. Put baseline, candidate and selected previz side by side at comparable framing. Read major shape, value grouping, material purpose and contact; do not call a pixel similarity score an art verdict. Existing approved previz files may live only in the main checkout; record the reference actually used.
6. **Exercise every relevant stage.** Empty/partial/full receiving frame, pieces travelling, half-built structure, finished structure, departure/return, both feeder orientations and attachments. Walk/stand/jump along the accessible surfaces with collider overlays. Open the artifacts and watch real-time `--video`; a turntable or sparse motion sheet cannot prove smooth gameplay. `level-check` is necessary for a supported static layout but does not cover every prototype/POI collider.
7. **Measure resource and runtime cost.** Report source-library and populated-scene cost separately, main/shadow passes, cold/warm load and sustained device behavior. Check bounds during motion, LOD transitions, shared-material mutations and lifecycle cleanup. Capture success requires WebGPU, advancing completed game frames and no graphics/page errors; visual acceptance is a separate judgment.
8. **Resolve and record.** Up to three visual correction rounds per AGENTS; unresolved issues stay behind the existing flag. Present one short owner comparison only after the builder has rejected technical failures. Record accepted revision, evidence, costs and remaining uncertainty; never mark plan implementation boxes complete for a research file.

## What is likely automatable, and how to price it honestly

**All percentages and time ranges below are H4 estimates, not measured agent productivity.** They mean the share of production steps expected to run without a human touching the modelling tool after a brief/anchor is accepted. They do not mean percentage of artistic quality, time saved, or guaranteed unattended success. Agent inspection/rework remains work even when owner time is low.

| Work                                                                        | Estimated automatable share | Remaining human judgment / likely exception                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Named hard-surface primitives, profiles, pivots, repeated dimensions        | 85–95%                      | Distinctive proportions and mechanical plausibility.                             |
| Assembly recipes, compatible sockets, exports, manifests and budget reports | 90–100%                     | Deciding whether the resulting place/action is worth keeping.                    |
| Contact-sheet/turntable/capture production and mechanical validation        | 90–100%                     | Image interpretation can be agent-led; owner style acceptance is not automated.  |
| Useful controlled variants and coherent era additions                       | 60–80%                      | Avoiding same-y silhouettes; selecting which contrast belongs in the world.      |
| Cliff/organic silhouettes and unusual hero engines                          | 40–70%                      | A focused owner edit or paintover may beat repeated procedural attempts.         |
| LOD/UV/trim/AO candidate generation                                         | 75–95%                      | Preserving silhouette, crisp joins and shading after simplification/compression. |
| Final taste decision                                                        | 0%                          | The owner decides; tools make that decision short and concrete.                  |

**Planning range [H]:** allow roughly **1–3 focused agent workdays** for the first contract/tooling/station proof and **1–4 agent hours** for the second placement if H1 holds, with **10–20 minutes total owner review** across shape and in-game comparisons. These are scheduling placeholders for an already working kit, not a delivery promise and not an estimate for all six eras. Hero modelling, new collision behavior or a failing iPad run can dominate; record them separately instead of hiding them inside “automated.”

Use an actual cost ledger: `setup + generation + integration + inspection + rework + owner review`, with tool wall time and agent effort distinguished. For later repeats, compare shared-kit marginal cost with a bespoke control of equal scope. The break-even count is `setup / (bespoke placement cost − kit placement cost)` when that difference is positive. If per-place exceptions keep growing or owner review exceeds the brief, revise the generator/contract before expanding the catalogue. Cheap art means a reusable accepted result, not merely cheap mesh emission.
