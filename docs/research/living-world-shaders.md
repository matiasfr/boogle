# Small effects that make Boogle feel alive

> **Verdict:** Few connected effects: shared visual clock, wind field, tree sway first.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Share one visual clock, wind field and sun/key-light handles across sky.ts, water-render.ts and the selected lab effects. 2. Start with anchored tree sway and restrained path-side grass from look-world.ts, replacing colour-inferred bend weights. 3. Make the track machine and chimneys exhale round, wind-driven puffs through an extension of particles.ts's existing pool.
> **Status:** open. No decision cites it yet.

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Environmental motion and material effects for a warm, simple, toy-like world in the spirit of Mario Sunshine, implemented with three.js WebGPU/TSL on an M4 iPad Pro. Live primary-source research and source inspection at `6d24bcd0`; proposals only, with no renderer changes, visual acceptance or device benchmarks._

## Brief, evidence and scope

The owner's prompt is the right scale: “we can make so many cool little shaders for things that breathe life into the game.” The strongest interpretation is **a few connected causes visible everywhere**: a breeze moves grass, crowns, cloth and smoke; water travels around a rock and leaves its foot wet; a machine exhales when it works. Moving every surface independently would create activity without making the place believable. **[H]**

This serves [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)'s **8. Designed for drop-in, drop-out** (being alone should be pleasant), **7. Telegraph everything** (working camps and material contacts read in the world), **10. Slapstick over precision** (chips, splashes and startled gulls), and **Small and readable**. All proposed animation is **Local**. It never changes terrain, collisions, production, weather rules or conserved quantities. Wear is appearance, not decay: **5. Nothing is the antagonist** still applies.

Authority is [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), [the decision log](../decisions.md), and [the render contract](../../src/render/README.md). This builds on [sunshine-look.md](sunshine-look.md), [stylized-rendering-on-a-budget.md](stylized-rendering-on-a-budget.md), [bang-for-buck-aesthetics.md](bang-for-buck-aesthetics.md), [webgpu-on-ipad.md](webgpu-on-ipad.md), and the current [look-pass to-dos](../plans/look-pass.md). Older WebGL snippets, the removed water simulation, character rims and previous era assumptions are not instructions for this work. The look pass's whole-game rollout still has an open time-of-day decision; this report does not settle it or complete a [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md) checkbox.

## The cost model on an M4 iPad

Apple explains three fragment categories: opaque, feedback (`discard`/shader depth), and blended; opaque work gives hidden-surface removal the most opportunity. It also documents attachment load/store traffic and inter-pass dependencies. These are architectural facts, **not** Boogle timings. [Apple, WWDC20: Optimize Metal Performance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/). **[V]**

For Boogle, count these separately **[H, engineering model]**:

- **Geometry:** visible and submitted vertices, tiny triangles, binning, instance data, plus every shadow pass that repeats them. Opaque occlusion does not make vertex work free.
- **Pixels:** covered pixels × overlapping layers × samples/ALU. One draw of large smoke cards can cost more than many small opaque birds. Low opacity does not make a fragment cheap.
- **Passes and dependencies:** colour/depth copies, resolves, target storage, blur/history textures, compute writes consumed by draws. A shared helper shares code; it does not cache results across fragments or materials.
- **CPU:** emitter decisions, terrain/neighbor queries, matrix uploads, material variants, graph construction and first-use compilation. GPU simulation leaves those costs intact.

At a fixed **1600×900 (1.44 MP)**, a nominal full-frame RGBA16F read plus write is `1,440,000 × 8 × 2 = 23.04 MB/frame`, or **1.38 GB/s at 60 Hz**, before depth, MSAA, repeated taps or cache effects. That arithmetic is a scale warning, not measured external bandwidth. Reusing the existing output pass can avoid an additional target; it still adds samples and work.

The bad stack is **sea + shoreline overlay + smoke + additive motes + haze cards + a scene-copy distortion**, all in the same view. Clipping cards to their visible silhouette helps, but `maskNode`/alpha testing is not equivalent to opaque triangles; alpha-to-coverage is also not a free cure. Double-sided transparent materials may add draw work, and intersecting instances do not automatically sort correctly. Prefer opaque geometry for silhouettes, bounded soft particles for dissipating material, and fog in surface shading.

Do not copy the older report's “every post effect is a new full-screen pass” literally: node operations can fuse into the output graph. Inspect the generated pass graph and actual counters. Keep stored MSAA; [the iPad field result](webgpu-on-ipad.md) takes precedence over a theoretical bandwidth saving. No subgroups, `shader-f16`, timestamp precision or browser memory ceiling is assumed without checking the device.

## Effect recipes

“Life/cost” is a qualitative **[H]** ranking, not an objective score. Costs below identify what to measure; the first-batch table assigns test allowances. Build node graphs once, update uniforms, and use [patchNode](../../src/render/material-patch.ts) when extending a material another module owns. Use `colorNode`, `normalNode`, `roughnessNode`, `positionNode`, `maskNode` and `opacityNode` rather than replacing `fragmentNode`; [three's NodeMaterial API](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/NodeMaterial.html) documents those hooks. **[V/L]**

### Grass: broad travelling gusts over quiet ground

**Technique and TSL.** Use opaque, tapered blades or small tufts grouped into spatial batches; keep roots fixed with a dedicated bend weight, and vary their static phase/height. Sample a shared world-space wind function at the **rest root**, then bend through `positionNode`. Use a quadratic root-to-tip weight and a slow gust plus weak flutter. Keep tip/root colours close to [place/land.ts](../../src/proto/place/land.ts)'s grass and vary by patch, not a fresh colour per frame. Start with roughly **4,000–8,000 visible blades, 3–5 triangles each**, concentrated beside paths and water; distant ground gets colour masses rather than individual blades. These are test caps, not a required density.

Sousa's weighted main/detail bending supplies the engineering precedent; Unreal's [SimpleGrassWind](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-position-offset-material-functions-in-unreal-engine) similarly exposes weights, strength and speed, although that stock function is non-directional. **[V]** Boogle's wind should be directional. A small bounded set of nearby player/ball positions can add temporary bending; do not loop over the whole world per blade.

**Cost and stacking.** Vertex count, per-vertex procedural noise, receiving/casting shadows, fragment coverage and batch bounds dominate. A zero-height blade still incurs submission/vertex work. Chunk culling and lower density precede compute-generated grass. Use explicit LOD for any vertex texture sample; fragment derivatives are unavailable there. Fade by shortening/thinning geometry, not dense alpha cards. Keep grass non-casting initially, and expand batch bounds for bending. **Life/cost: very high** when placed near the walking route; poor when it becomes an 80,000-blade carpet. **Compute: unnecessary** for this first scatter and analytic motion.

### Trees and palms: structural sway, then leaf detail

**Technique and TSL.** Keep trunks anchored, rotate/bend broad fronds or crown lobes around stored pivots, and add a smaller phase offset at tips. Start crown motion at **0.15–0.35 Hz**, tip displacement around **1–3% of crown width**, with lulls. Use a dedicated `aBend`/pivot attribute rather than inferring leaves from green colour; the current lab heuristic would animate green paint and miss autumn leaves. Broad three-tier crowns and a few opaque fronds fit [look-pass.md](../plans/look-pass.md).

Apply the deformation to `prev ?? positionLocal`; in installed r187, `NodeMaterial.setupPosition()` applies skinning/batching/instancing **before** `positionNode`. `positionGeometry` is useful for rest weights, not as a blind replacement for the transformed position. Convert world wind to the object's appropriate local basis, especially on rotated/scaled instances. For visibly rotating lobes, rotate normals too; translation-only flutter can retain a simplified normal. This uses Sousa's main/detail separation, not a skeletal or cloth simulation. **[V/L for precedent/API; H for recipe]**

**Cost and stacking.** Existing opaque meshes can gain motion with zero new draws; vertices, normal correction and shadow redraws still cost. Check moving crown shadows and culling at maximum bend. Small trees need less high-frequency detail at distance. **Life/cost: excellent**, often the first visible win. **Compute: unnecessary** unless a later field must store persistent interaction.

### Cloud shadows: one slow change across a hillside

**Technique and TSL.** A low-frequency repeatable cloud mask moves over world `xz`; project the surface point toward a cloud plane along the sun direction, guarding/fading near a horizontal sun. Use one filtered lookup or a few smooth analytic lobes, broad edges, **5–12% direct-light attenuation** and approximately **20–60 m patches**. Let it drift with wind, without forcing cloud altitude motion to match grass speed. Li Zihan's light function and [Unity's scrolling directional cookie](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/lighting-overview/lighting/light-component) are direct precedents. **[V]**

A small **material-only spike** can `patchNode(colorNode)` on terrain/props, but label its limitation: it darkens indirect light/albedo too. A better shared version attenuates the sun's direct contribution. Installed `AnalyticLightNode` reads `light.colorNode`, but that hook is not declared by the current `Light` types; validate one typed adapter before relying on it, supplying a live colour×intensity uniform because a custom node replaces the default light-colour path. Basic water must consume the same attenuation explicitly. Do not add a second directional light or an overhead shadow-casting cloud mesh.

**Cost and stacking.** A texture sample on every receiving fragment is not free; huge terrain/sea coverage dominates. Avoid extra transparent shadow planes and per-cloud loops. Sky/cloud visibility must agree enough that obvious dark patches do not pass under a completely clear noon sky. **Life/cost: high** at gentle contrast; distracting at fast speed. **Compute: unnecessary** for one moving field; only consider a cached low-resolution field after measurement.

### Sun glints: warm, stable light rather than flashing dots

**Technique and TSL.** Keep [crossing/sea.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sea.ts)'s world-anchored waves and widened specular lobe as the first control. Evaluate a half-vector lobe with consistent world-space normal/view/key direction, then combine it into Basic water's `colorNode`; setting `normalNode` alone does not give Basic material PBR lighting. Mute tiny details as their pixel footprint shrinks, redistributing energy into a broad sheen instead of simply deleting the whole highlight lane. The current exponent is controlled by [crossing/tuning.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/tuning.ts), not the older report's proposed exponent 96.

Sunshine's authored mip treatment demonstrates that distance-dependent highlights are an art tool **[R]**. [Toksvig's normal-filtering paper](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/whitepapers/2006/Mipmapping_Normal_Maps.pdf) explains why averaging normals and then renormalizing loses information needed to filter nonlinear specular highlights **[V]**. Boogle's current analytic variance estimate is not a verified Toksvig normal-map implementation. If switching to a texture, preserve variance/averaged normal length, and test that route separately.

**Cost and stacking.** Water screen coverage, wave layers, texture taps and specular arithmetic dominate. A few railhead glints can use existing steel shading; do not sprinkle additive quads over every shiny object. Compute does not help a small analytic lobe. MSAA does not solve shader aliasing; bloom can conceal overbright aliasing in a still while worsening motion. **Life/cost: high**, but only if it survives a **20 m/s ride** and slow yaw at fixed resolution.

### River flow and rock foam: show where water goes

**Technique and TSL.** Give a river ribbon along-stream distance/cross-stream UVs; simple bends can use these directly. For a winding channel with obstacles, bake a small **RG velocity field** from the authored river/rock footprints. Sample it once and advect two normal/colour patterns with half-cycle-offset phases, blending while each phase resets. Alex Vlachos' [Valve SIGGRAPH 2010 slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2010/siggraph2010_vlachos_waterflow.pdf) explain the two phases in “Smoothly Repeating Flow,” repetition/pulsing under “Two Major Problems,” and the modified distortion interval for flowing colour under “Flowing Debris.” **[V]** Use local phase variation to hide synchronized resets; check seams and pulsing.

Foam is a separate **contact/emission mask**: pale upstream collars around actual intersecting rocks and a short downstream trail carried by the same flow. For the first rock, use a local waterline footprint SDF; for many static rocks, bake a contact/direction tile at layout generation. Reuse the pattern samples where possible and mix foam into the river `colorNode`/`opacityNode`. [place/layout.ts](../../src/proto/place/layout.ts) and [place/land.ts](../../src/proto/place/land.ts) supply the gorge context, but there is no verified flow-map subsystem to call. Keep this as scenic water, not a new simulation.

**Cost and stacking.** Roughly one flow lookup plus two advected pattern lookups, river pixels, mesh extent and any extra foam ribbons. Avoid one SDF loop over every rock in every water pixel. Transparent rivers crossing transparent sea need clean mesh boundaries. A static flow/contact bake costs setup time, not a per-frame fluid solve. **Life/cost: excellent** at the gorge; **compute: no** for the first version. Rare's persistent foam field is a later capability with feedback-texture costs, not a prerequisite.

### Shoreline: a thin moving contact, not a second ocean

**Technique and TSL.** Start with the existing `seaDepth()` and `seaFoam()` and tune two soft advancing/retreating lines within the shallow band. Break them with one low-frequency phase field, preserving long quiet stretches. Vertical depth gives a wide band on a flat beach and a narrow one at a cliff; use horizontal shore distance only where constant artistic width is needed. Piers need **waterline footprints**, not ground depth or their top surfaces. Build one bounded local contact tile/strip with explicit validity, following [sunshine-look.md](sunshine-look.md)'s contact proposal.

Use surface colour/mask modulation within the existing water draw; a small local strip is the fallback where the bathymetry is unavailable. The height texture in [water-render.ts](../../src/render/water-render.ts) describes the main island, not every place or distant island. Outside valid terrain tiles, choose open water deliberately instead of clamping edge heights. Rare's calm-water contact foam is a useful precedent **[V]**; its blurred feedback system is not needed for this first shore.

**Cost and stacking.** Extra arithmetic/sample over the whole sea can cost more than one tightly bounded strip; measure both. Avoid a second full-size blended foam plane, z-fighting and widening distant foam into white noise. Match displaced water height and pier contacts. **Life/cost: excellent** near real contacts. **Compute: unnecessary** unless terrain/contact data changes, when rebuilding a dirty tile is enough.

### Steam and smoke: round puffs with a readable cause

**Technique and TSL.** Distinguish quick white steam from slower, warm-grey exhaust. Spawn a few growing lobes at a valve/chimney and carry them with wind; use machine state or existing chuff timing, with no new sound. A written [toon-smoke shader breakdown](https://blog.ldev.app/building-a-toon-smoke-particle-shader-in-shader-graph/) demonstrates normal-directed sphere deformation, two-tone shading and why deformed normals need attention. **[V]** For Boogle start simpler: soft two-colour shading on a low-poly lobe or a sphere-normal billboard, a small shape variation, and shrink/dissipate before the puff blocks the player.

Keep [particles.ts](../../src/render/particles.ts)'s **single `emit()` entry point and shared state**. A proposed `kind`/look field can select a puff or chip renderer; this API does not exist yet. For a billboard, derive a hemisphere normal from disk UV, transform the sun into its view basis, and compute shade in `colorNode`; use a tight disk `maskNode` and controlled `opacityNode`. For an opaque puff mesh, consume the same particle centre/size via attributes, deform in `positionNode`, and shrink at death. No per-puff lights or shadow casting. Start with **24 visible puffs**, roughly **1–3 s life**, and adapt the rate to distance.

**Cost and stacking.** Large overlapping cards, not the simulation's particle count, cause the worst fill. The existing pool is unlit and unfogged, so colour alone cannot guarantee rounded smoke or correct distance integration. Fix that inside the shared renderer with separate look batches rather than a pool per chimney. Compare **opaque lobes versus sphere-shaded cards** in the same camera; neither is universally cheaper. **Life/cost: excellent** for a working machine, poor for constant screen-filling fog. **Compute: already present**; add wind there, not another dispatch per emitter.

### Chips and dust: small consequences of contact

**Technique and TSL.** Emit chunky opaque wood/stone chips in a brief directional burst at confirmed contact, with a smaller soft dust pulse. Keep colour/material consistent with the struck object; chips rotate, dust grows then disappears. Start **8–16 chips plus 3–6 dust particles per event**, not a permanent cloud. A mesh look behind `emit()` can read centre, scale, stable seed and age for simple spin in `positionNode`; colour stays in `colorNode`. Existing dust can use today's `grow`, `fade`, `gravity` and `drag`.

The separation of simulation from sprite/mesh representation matches [Godot's particle shaders](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/particle_shader.html) and [Niagara's multiple-renderer guidance](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scalability-and-best-practices-for-niagara). **[V]** For Boogle, extend [place/chop.ts](../../src/proto/place/chop.ts), [place/rail.ts](../../src/proto/place/rail.ts) and the existing [track machine](../../src/features/track-machine.ts) emitters. A shared-state effect happens in the adapter's `onApplied`, never because `submitEdit` returned true.

**Cost and stacking.** Bursts stress the 256-spawn queue, total live slots, uploads and material/shadow variants. Use a single known floor or die on contact initially; the current pool's floor **kills**, it does not bounce or query Rapier. No per-chip physics bodies. Event feedback gets priority over ambient motes if the pool is crowded. **Life/cost: excellent** because the player sees a cause. **Compute: reuse the existing passes**; no GPU readback for collisions or emission.

### Heat haze: a late, local experiment

**Technique and TSL.** Real distortion samples already-rendered colour with displaced UVs. Prefer a small depth/distance-masked extension to the existing output graph over another scene render, initially **0.25–0.5 rendered pixels** over a hot machine or distant horizon. Sample scene colour/depth before divergent branches; clamp UVs and suppress offsets across near/depth discontinuities. Compose through `pipeline.outputNode` and keep exactly one final tone-map/sRGB conversion.

[Godot's screen-reading documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/screen-reading_shaders.html) explains the required copy and transparent-object limitations **[V]**; three's `backdropNode` is also a scene-reading route, not a free material trick. In Boogle an output extension can reuse the existing scene texture, but making a local mask or sampling depth can add work, and transparent sea/particles complicate which depth represents the pixel. The specific Sunshine heat-haze implementation remains unverified here.

**Cost and stacking.** Scene reads, mask/depth storage, extra composite passes if introduced, and displaced foreground edges. A tiny visual region can still pay full-screen output work. Screen copying after each heat source stacks badly; combine sources, or omit the effect. **Life/cost: low-to-medium**, because warm light and moving steam usually convey heat more clearly. **Compute: unnecessary**; leave out of the first batch.

### Birds and insects: sparse behaviour beats a swarm

**Technique and TSL.** Keep the seven existing gulls' peck/startle/circle/land behaviour and make habitat placement work for the new place. Add **12–24 insects** in a few waterside/flower patches, with stable phase and short loops. Tiny opaque wing triangles can flap in `positionNode`; body motion can remain on the CPU for these counts, or use analytic paths from seed/time. TSL should not decide real gameplay state. Far birds can use a very simple gliding silhouette, with no shadow and less animation.

[Craig Reynolds' original boids account](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/) establishes separation, alignment and cohesion within a neighborhood **[V]**. It is useful if flock interaction becomes the point; independent loops plus an authored group path are sufficient for decorative distance birds. Do not add an all-pairs neighbor simulation just to make six silhouettes cross the sky.

**Cost and stacking.** CPU ground/neighbor queries, instance uploads, draw batches and shadow casters matter before fill for small opaque birds. Tiny transparent insect cards waste coverage and flicker when subpixel. Spatially deactivate distant habitat groups; expand shader-motion bounds. **Life/cost: high** for occasional movement at a destination, low for constant screen noise. **Compute: only for a measured large swarm**, with spatial bins rather than quadratic neighbors.

### Fireflies: make the existing dusk accent belong to its habitat

**Technique and TSL.** Reuse [critter-fireflies.ts](../../src/features/critter-fireflies.ts), retargeting habitat anchors rather than adding another 60 lights. Keep soft blink envelopes, independent fixed phases and short wander paths. If moving this work to a vertex graph, use `positionNode`, `scaleNode`, `colorNode` and a compact radial alpha mask; the current instanced Sprite is already the correct sized-point representation. No actual `PointLight` per insect and no new bloom pass.

**Cost and stacking.** The present version rewrites positions/colours and samples ground height for 60 flies each active frame; cache habitat heights or retain that simple CPU work if it is insignificant. Additive blending still shades every covered pixel; keep halos small, fade with distance and hide during bright daylight. Spatially bounded groups should stop updating when inactive. **Life/cost: excellent at dusk**, very low at noon. **Compute: unlikely to repay a new dispatch at this count**; analytic GPU motion is a separate option. This is Boogle's implementation evidence **[L]**, not a claimed BotW firefly technique.

### Flags and cloth: a pinned edge and a shared breeze

**Technique and TSL.** Use a small subdivided opaque pennant, roughly **8×4 segments**, weighted from zero at the pole to one at the free edge. Combine a slow travelling bend and a restrained higher-frequency ripple; let wind direction change its mean shape. A loose cloth strip can have a static sag plus the same deformation. `positionNode` carries displacement; `normalNode` supplies analytically corrected normals in view space, or use a deliberately broad simplified normal on a distant pennant. This is an adaptation of weighted vertex wind, not a claim that [Unreal's WPO functions](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-position-offset-material-functions-in-unreal-engine) simulate cloth.

**Cost and stacking.** Subdivision, double-sided shading, normal correction and optional shadows. Model the triangular edge as geometry instead of a mostly empty transparent rectangle. Check pole attachment and both faces, including a no-wind pose. **Life/cost: excellent** for one or two readable silhouettes beside a static station. **Compute cloth constraints are unnecessary** until a player can pull or collide with it; no Rapier cloth approximation needed.

### Water drips: a few bright falls and a small landing ring

**Technique and TSL.** At a wet rock lip or pipe, grow a small bead, release one falling droplet, then trigger a tiny ripple at a known landing height. Start **1–3 drops/s at two nearby sources** with irregular fixed-seed intervals. Today's `emit()` can supply round spray; a later elongated droplet look uses shared particle velocity to orient/scale a tiny mesh or sprite. Use blue-white body/highlight colour, not refractive glass.

Compute the ballistic landing time on the CPU when emitting if the landing plane is known, and enqueue one cosmetic ripple then; no GPU readback. [splash.ts](../../src/render/splash.ts) already owns sea-ripple interaction. Respect its eight ripple slots: many tiny drips must not evict player splashes. Lagarde's [wetness/accumulation account](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/01/03/water-drop-2b-dynamic-rain-and-its-effects/) supports separating residual wetness from falling water **[V]**; this exact drip sequence is **[H]**.

**Cost and stacking.** At these counts, emitter scheduling and an extra representation can cost more than pixels. Full refraction per drop is disproportionate; keep source-local and disable beyond useful screen size. **Life/cost: high near a quiet stop**, almost invisible on a train. **Compute: existing pool only**. Defer until the shore/river contacts work.

### Day-tinted fog: continuous air, readable destinations

**Technique and TSL.** Extend [sky.ts](../../src/render/sky.ts)'s palette-driven fog and [look-world.ts](../../src/debug/look-world.ts)'s existing `scene.fogNode` experiment. Blend sky-horizon colour with a restrained sunward warmth, and combine range/height extinction as `1 - (1 - rangeFactor) * (1 - heightFactor)` rather than adding two unconstrained opacities. Maintain cave transitions and readable moonlit nights. Keep the light, sky, steam and water on the same palette handles; the fixed `place` light override must become an explicit preview mode before a general day-cycle rollout.

Campo Santo's [graphics-programmer sky breakdown](https://blog.camposanto.com/post/112703721804/this-blog-post-is-an-in-detail-explanation-of-part) documents artist-controlled procedural sky gradients **[V]**, not a fog implementation. Combining that palette with surface fog is the Boogle proposal. For long crossings keep the destination saturated enough to orient toward; avoid importing a short-island height density unchanged over kilometres.

**Cost and stacking.** Fragment ALU on affected surfaces, plus any unnecessary duplicated fog in custom particles. Use the existing material fog path; no stacks of translucent fog planes. A global colour uniform is cheap, but height/view evaluation still runs per pixel. **Life/cost: high** across time and distance; **compute: unnecessary**. Do not darken the whole world to make small emissive effects visible.

### Wet and worn surfaces: evidence of contact, without visual grit

**Technique and TSL.** Lagarde's [wet-surface research](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/03/19/water-drop-3a-physically-based-wet-surfaces/) distinguishes rough porous materials that darken and become more specular from smooth materials that change much less. **[V]** On Boogle's rock/wood use a bounded wet mask to mix `colorNode` and `roughnessNode`, starting around **0.9→0.45 roughness** and a modest **5–15% darkening**. Keep metalness unchanged. Shade waterline bands, drip trails and puddle margins where water actually reaches; no global wet multiplier.

For wear, bake broad edge/underside masks or named attributes at kit generation; tint exposed wood, softened stone corners and soot near a chimney. Do not compute curvature from every frame or infer all wear from screen-space edges. Reuse [place/look.ts](../../src/proto/place/look.ts)'s `paint()` and `bakeContact()` work, keeping contact/AO separate from wear so creases do not get darkened repeatedly. Store wear on object coordinates; wet zones use stable world/contact coordinates. This is our toy-material recipe, not a verified Tiny Glade damage shader.

**Cost and stacking.** Vertex attributes and a few lerps are cheap; multi-octave noise, triplanar textures, extra decal layers, sharp specular and clearcoat add cost or shimmer. A lower roughness alone is unconvincing without something to reflect; keep the existing simple sky response or measure an environment-light solution separately. Do not make concrete metallic to fake gloss. **Life/cost: high for grounding, medium for motion**. **Compute: unnecessary** for the first version; bake masks offline or on the CPU at generation, and update only if scenery changes. Appearance never implies structural decay.

## Shared helpers worth building once

These are proposed small modules under `src/render/`, not a new framework or a reason to reorganize prototypes now. Production code must not import `src/debug/` or `src/proto/` statically. Extract only the chosen pieces when they graduate, retaining entity-owned emitters and the existing service lifecycle.

### A visual clock and light handles

Provide a leaf module of uniform handles for `seconds`, wind direction/strength, sun direction, key-light direction/colour, sky colours and daylight. Let existing [sky.ts](../../src/render/sky.ts) and the existing frame service update the handles; consumers read them and never reach into materials. This avoids importing the whole sky/sea ownership chain into every effect.

Use **one clock epoch for new motion and event ages**. Boogle currently mixes TSL `time` with `seaU.uTime`/frame `elapsed`; the ripple start stamps use the latter. Do not replace one without updating its timestamps. Define behavior on pause, hidden-tab resume and preview scrubbing: clamp integrated particle `dt`, and avoid a gust or puff teleport on resume. For an eight-hour session, test float phase precision; reduce periodic phases without discontinuities rather than wrapping the whole timestamp used for particle age. Day/night remains derived from the existing world clock; decorative wind needs no new network field.

### One wind function, distinct responses

Define `windAt(worldRestPosition, visualSeconds)` as horizontal wind velocity in metres/second, with a gust envelope. Use stable low-frequency travelling waves or one small smooth texture, not independent 3D fBm per material. Each receiver converts velocity into its own response: a bend gain in seconds gives vegetation displacement in metres; smoke approaches the wind velocity through drag. Grass responds quickly; crowns lag and bend less; cloth ripples. If CPU emitters need wind, provide the same simple analytic expression on the CPU; exact peer/GPU bit agreement is unnecessary for Local effects.

An illustrative **uncompiled TSL sketch**, with argument types inferred from its callers:

```ts
import { Fn, float, sin, vec3 } from 'three/tsl';

// p: stable world rest/root position; t: shared visual seconds;
// dir: normalized horizontal vec2; strength: wind speed in metres/second.
const windAt = Fn(([p, t, dir, strength]) => {
  const phase = p.x.mul(0.035).add(p.z.mul(0.021)).sub(t.mul(0.7));
  const gust = sin(phase).mul(0.35).add(0.65);
  const ripple = sin(phase.mul(2.3).add(t.mul(0.4))).mul(0.08);
  const amount = gust.add(ripple).mul(strength);
  return vec3(dir.x.mul(amount), float(0), dir.y.mul(amount));
});
// In positionNode: prev/positionLocal + toObjectBasis(windAt(...)) * bendGainSeconds * bendWeight².
// toObjectBasis is a required adapter, not an existing exported TSL function.
```

Prefer explicit rest-root attributes for instanced plants; sampling wind at the already-bent tip makes the field feed back into its own deformation. A flat flag can use normalized distance from its pinned edge for the same weight. Centralized parameters produce coherence; identical phase/amplitude on every object produces synchronized rubber.

### Small material functions and contact data

Share only functions with multiple real consumers: `cloudAttenuation`, filtered water detail/glint, `shoreBand`, two-colour puff shading and `wetSurface`. A `Fn` deduplicates authoring, not runtime work. Use derivatives before `If`/`Discard`; keep `smoothstep` edges ordered; keep data masks in `NoColorSpace`, and linear colour values throughout. The final output handles tone mapping once.

A world contact tile should state origin, scale, valid extent and channel meaning. Bake river flow, static waterline distances and wet masks where their **resolution requirements agree**; unrelated fields need not be crammed into one texture. Pad edges, use mipmaps where valid, and make outside-coverage behavior explicit. A 256² tile over 64 m gives 0.25 m/texel; it cannot represent a precise tiny drip trail or a kilometre of narrow pier foam.

For repeated procedural noise, add a visual helper only when two accepted consumers need it. [render/noise.ts](../../src/render/noise.ts) is currently an **audio noise-buffer helper**, not a texture/noise field to import into shaders.

### Extend the particle service, not every emitter

Keep `emit()` as the shared API. Add only the chosen render looks, a stable seed/age and a small per-look wind response. Batch opaque chips/puffs separately from blended dust; both consume one simulation owner and one admission policy. Proposed caps should reserve slots for action feedback, reduce distant ambient emissions first, and expose dropped-spawn/overwrite counts alongside the existing lifetime estimate. Changing the pool is a concrete shared-state implementation task of its own, not done by this report.

The current five simulation vec4s plus two draw vec4s are `720 × 7 × 16 = 80,640` bytes; the queue is `256 × 6 × 16 = 24,576` bytes: about **103 KiB of nominal float payload** before allocation/staging overhead. Compute particles are already modest in storage. Their draw coverage, dispatches, synchronization and 720-slot updates matter more than squeezing these buffers prematurely. The update already binds seven storage buffers: pack new seed/kind fields carefully, inspect the adapter's limit, and retain the existing eight-buffer portability constraint rather than adding a buffer per property. Dead zero-sized slots still perform vertex work. Compact live lists or indirect draws only if measurements justify their extra passes and complexity.

## Compare them without fooling ourselves

### Keep the picture and workload comparable

Use `?proto=place&look=all` as the available look-pass control, and the existing crossing for the long-water view. A future per-effect switch is **proposed**, not an existing `fx=` API for `place`. The first useful comparison has three states: baseline, one effect, and the accepted set with that effect removed. The last comparison exposes redundant effects and costs that only appear in combination.

Freeze camera pose/FOV, render-target dimensions, exposure, sun phase, world seed, particle seed and emitter script. Use `?dpr=fixed` to disable adaptation, then **verify the actual target width/height**; that flag alone does not select 1600×900. Retain the same MSAA, shadows, post stack, material warm-up and room population. For timing an effect that can toggle via uniform, also test a genuinely absent graph/material: multiplying by zero may leave its shader work running. For first-use hitches, measure a separate cold run rather than mixing compile time into steady-state numbers.

### Use four deliberate views and real-time actions

1. **Path and crown:** walk beside grass, orbit a rotated tree, stop and watch a gust reach the pennant and smoke; check roots, shadows, culling and repeated phases.
2. **Working machine:** chop, carry, and lay a rail while close enough that steam could cover the player; check burst counts, no pre-confirmation rewards, and dark-smudge/ordering artifacts.
3. **Water:** look down at shore contact, walk around a rock/pier, then ride at **20 m/s** while yawing slowly; check foam location, glint flicker, flow resets, water seams and far destination contrast.
4. **Dusk return:** repeat the station view under the real day-cycle treatment, with gulls/insects winding down and fireflies visible; verify fogged puffs, cool open shadows, moon and amber accents without adding lights.

Use the existing `npm run look -- --question "..."` with a targeted eval/action and `--video` for real-time motion; open the stills/motion sheets and watch the action. `npm run perf -- --render` and `npm run counts` expose geometry/draw/pass changes; `npm run hitches` checks first-use and transition stalls. Use existing evals or a focused harness eval, not a new gameplay scenario for a visual change. The documentation task itself does not run these acceptance tests.

### Separate GPU categories and timing meanings

The installed renderer supports `resolveTimestampsAsync('render')` and `resolveTimestampsAsync('compute')`; [Renderer docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Renderer.html) and local `Renderer.js` confirm separate categories. **[V/L]** Extend debug measurement for a particle experiment to resolve both, with the same instrumentation enabled in A and B. Associate delayed samples with their configuration; do not record the same pending mapping promise repeatedly as independent frames.

Record CPU emission/update/submission, render GPU time, compute GPU time, main-thread frame time and actual frame intervals separately. Category totals are work estimates; overlapping pass times are not automatically elapsed GPU-frame duration. A CPU clock around `render()` is submission cost. Awaiting queue completion every frame changes pipelining. A flat 60 fps can hide a substantial regression; headless SwiftShader and a Mac cannot certify M4 headroom. If reliable GPU timestamps are unavailable, publish frame-pacing/CPU evidence and mark the 10 ms GPU target **unverified**.

For each fixed view run **A–B–B–A**, at least three repeated pairs with **30 s measured windows after warm-up**. Report median, p95, frame count, uncertainty/range across repetitions and frames over **33 ms**; distinguish a 0.04 ms budget allowance from a measurable 0.04 ms win. Sum neither isolated p95 values nor effect deltas to claim the combined p95. Run the full scene, including all relevant particles and shadows, and test multiple simultaneous action bursts without requiring the owner to arrange them.

Repeat on the actual **M4 iPad Pro in Safari after a 20-minute soak**, recording iPad model, OS/browser, power/charging state, viewport, render pixels and adapter features. Keep other rendering jobs off the shared Mac when collecting Mac timings; do not compare a cold iPad A with a thermally saturated B. Finish with adaptive resolution enabled and record where it settles: an unchanged frame rate at lower resolution is a quality regression, not a free effect.

### Judge life, not only busyness

**Hypotheses to test:** coherent wind reads better than independent sway; round steam fixes more of the “dead world” feeling than extra ambient particles; one moving cloud patch improves depth without flattening Sunshine's clear light; localized wetness/foam makes water more convincing than another wave octave. Compare matched clips with labels hidden, and retain “can't tell” as a useful result. A heatmap can show changed pixels, not whether the change is good.

For the owner, keep the kit to one new line and one gut question: **“The breeze now carries the trees, pennants and steam.” / “Does this feel like a place that's alive while you're standing still?”** Builders check clipping, long-session timing, tiny-feature shimmer, pool overload and GPU errors themselves. A still, a submitted capture or zero console errors alone is not visual acceptance.
