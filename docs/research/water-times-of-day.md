# Chosen times of day for the new sea and sky

_4 November 2026. Research and proposal at `906ddad4026e2131eacbb99190e1f6035edc6485`, three.js 0.187.0. Live primary-source research, focused source inspection and a Mac CPU measurement; no game-source changes. **[L]** inspected local code/decisions; **[P]** primary documentation or developer account; **[R]** public reconstruction, not verified against the shipped binary; **[M]** measurement in this session; **[H]** historical report; **[E]** estimate; **[D]** proposal. Not tested on a device: no physical iPad/iPhone test. The requested browser broker was unavailable; no new GPU, transition or visual acceptance is claimed._

**Recommendation: prove one prepared evening at a finished line, after the current replay/default-water work, using two authored lighting states and a brief fade. Keep late morning as the everyday look.** Start with both states prepared behind loading, share the meaningful milestone rather than a continuously advancing clock, and leave a visible sky blend for a second proof. A true night is a separate content-and-lighting task. This is a proposal for the owner, not approval to implement or extend water graduation.

## Direction and present implementation [L]

The [Look decisions](../proposals/boogle-2.md#look) say “change the time only intentionally, in parts of the game” and “bake each chosen time once, ahead of time.” The later 3 November call narrows today's implementation to one late-morning bake at `tod=0.24`; the 4 November call keeps Neutral. The [Design Table entry](../proposals/boogle-2.md#the-shape-of-the-game) still asks for day-only and night-only things and a brief northern-summer night. These are compatible: authored moments can provide those activities without reinstating the old cycle. “Ahead of time” does not yet choose build-time files versus preparation before a moment.

The [water plan](../plans/water-graduation.md#steps) owns steps 7–8, and the [round-4 tracker](../plans/round-4-notes.md) owns the replay. The [direction view](../direction.md) describes replay → durable production core → vertical slice; its reviewed commit is older than this checkout, so the cited decisions and tracker take precedence. New gameplay-dependent time work follows those gates. Independent research is authorized now; this report changes neither job nor their files.

## What a sky actually costs

### Allocation arithmetic, not measured browser residency [L/E]

For RGBA16F, each texel is 8 bytes. A complete cube mip chain costs `6 × N² × 8 × (1 + 1/4 + …)`, approximately `64 × N²` bytes. MiB below means 1,048,576 bytes. These allocations have the same nominal size on Mac, M4 iPad and iPhone; lower screen resolution does **not** shrink a fixed-size sky cube.

| Face width      | Raw sky + all ordinary mips | Raw + one same-size filtered environment | Two prepared raw/filtered pairs | Including one reusable same-size PMREM scratch cube |
| --------------- | --------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: | ------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------: |
| 1024 (current)  |                      64 MiB |                                 ~128 MiB |                        ~256 MiB |                  ~320 MiB for two; ~192 MiB for one |
| 512 (candidate) |                      16 MiB |                                  ~32 MiB |                         ~64 MiB |                                     ~80 MiB for two |
| 256 (candidate) |                       4 MiB |                                   ~8 MiB |                         ~16 MiB |                                     ~20 MiB for two |

The additional columns matter: inspected installed three.js `src/nodes/pmrem/PMREMNode.js` caches the filtered output per input texture, and `src/renderers/common/extras/PMREMGenerator.js` allocates a same-size half-float cube with roughness mips down to 8, plus a source cube with ordinary mips. Its minimum is 256. This is an allocation model, **not a claim that every target is currently resident at once**; renderer cache lifetime and actual use must be instrumented. See the upstream [PMREM contract](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html). Water directly samples the raw cube, so PBR's filtered environment cannot simply replace it without checking the water's different LOD convention.

Each atmosphere adds about **0.291 MiB GPU LUT payload** and **0.873 MiB retained CPU typed arrays** (256×64, 32×32, 192×108; float working data plus half-float upload data). Count alignment/driver overhead, compiled pipelines, readback buffers and uploaded source bytes separately. The existing shore atlas alone has a recorded **68.9 MiB** payload in [water step 1](../plans/water-graduation.md#step-1-evidence-3-november-2026), before FFT, scene/MSAA, depth and post targets. Three or four full-size looks therefore deserve scrutiny. None of these numbers establishes a safe Safari tab memory ceiling.

For scale, one extra unfiltered RGBA16F fetch across 1210×702 pixels is **6.48 MiB/frame** before filtering/cache effects; multiple consumers and trilinear filtering change actual bandwidth. The blend estimates are not a device rating. Target **16.7 ms total at 60 fps**, retaining margin, rather than spending “one millisecond” without a measured baseline. A prewarmed fade/cut should add **no sky scene pass**, with a proposed **<1 ms CPU activation budget and no new >33 ms frame gap** on either device; these are acceptance targets, not observed results.

Compression is a separate proof. [Khronos KTX tools](https://github.khronos.org/KTX-Software/ktxtools/ktx_encode.html) support HDR encoding, but that does not establish our current loader/browser transcode path, HDR GPU format support, achieved download size or preservation of the cube's cloud alpha. RGB-only HDR encodings need a separate transmittance mask or another representation. Ordinary LDR JPEG/PNG cannot preserve this linear HDR lighting input. A hypothetical 4 MiB package downloads in 1.68 s at 20 Mbit/s; **4 MiB is not a measured compression result**. Ship Boogle-generated assets with hashes/source parameters and explicit cube orientation, exposure units, alpha meaning and mip/filter version. Do not add an asset pipeline before the second look is accepted.

## Can the change be visible? [P/D]

**Yes, with prepared endpoints and one coordinated transition.** Start with a **0.3–0.5 s fade out, state swap while opaque, 0.3–0.5 s fade in** as an authored ellipsis; do not bake during that subsecond interval. A tunnel can conceal a swap for its occupants, but **does not conceal it from co-op friends outside**. If the time is global, use a cue/transition that works wherever each player stands. Do not pause the shared world, require everyone aboard, or black out active precision traversal without choosing a safe moment.

A later **4–8 s** visible transition is plausible, not yet visually proved. Prepare A/B background radiance, filtered environment and fill, then use a single progress value across:

1. linear-HDR background, water reflection, fog and environment lighting;
2. sun direction along a deliberate arc, key colour/intensity, shadow strength and caustic gain;
3. exposure target, lamp emissives, moon/stars and optional ambience.

Do not interpolate `.24` to `.8` and rebake every frame. Do not crossfade two full `background` nodes containing their analytic suns: that creates two suns. Interpolate one disc/key separately. Fixed cloud coverage/shape between states avoids ghosted clouds; even with that, bright silver linings and sunset scattering do not interpolate physically. Inspect the halfway frame, not only endpoints. Sun-path interpolation must handle opposing directions without a zero-length normalized vector.

PBR is the less trivial seam: `scene.environment = A/B` is a switch, not a blend API. A future TSL environment node must sample two **already filtered** maps consistently with material roughness, or a bounded compositing approach must blend corresponding filtered levels. Preserve raw-sky sampling for water and matching diffuse fill. Never invalidate PMREM each blend frame. Keep shader topology/bindings warm; at completion release the old state's resources only after pending GPU/readback use, and avoid paying for unused dual fetches forever. Precompile any single/blend variants before the moment.

## Co-op, saves, late joiners and mixed builds [L/D]

The [sync contract](../agents/sync.md), [room safety](../agents/room-safety.md), [mixed builds](../agents/mixed-builds.md) and [DESIGN's authority rules](../../DESIGN.md#pick-how-each-feature-shares-state) imply a split:

| Meaning                                                                 | Owner and persistence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dev preview, quality, exposure adaptation, GPU readiness                | **Local.** Never persist these as room state or let `skyNow.day` decide rewards/spawns. Different render resolutions must not create different available activities.                                                                                                                              |
| A later area's fixed evening with no time-dependent rules               | May be a **Local view derived from authored area identity**. Two separated players can have different scenic looks; players together should resolve the same area. Boundary priority/hysteresis and long sightlines still need design. This is not a personal time selector that unlocks content. |
| A completed-line evening or night activity that must survive departures | Prefer a **derived value of an existing durable shared milestone**; if that cannot express the choice, use a small registered **Lockstep state/op**, even though the state is small. Host presence is inappropriate because it resets on leadership change. No per-frame time broadcasts.         |

For a future saved transition, the minimal semantic record could be `lookId`, `fromLookId`, `startTick`, `durationTicks` and a revision; omit fields derivable from an existing milestone/tick. This is a shape proposal, not a reserved wire schema. Store semantic IDs/integers, not cubes, RGB, floating sun vectors or device quality. Use the server-stamped accepted tick for transition progress; all visual trig/interpolation stays Local. Validate the milestone/preconditions and make repeated completion idempotent.

`submitEdit` means queued, not accepted. Begin the visible cue through `onApplied` only after acceptance; `apply` changes deterministic state and returns results without effects. A reconnecting client must not optimistically switch the world and then discover its edit was rejected. Keep existing verified-snapshot persistence guards. The current Place protocol is still temporary; implementing a new durable milestone on top of it just to demonstrate a sunset would pre-empt the [production-core plan](../plans/production-core.md).

**Late join/reload:** load the shared snapshot, derive the target, prepare it while the normal loading screen is up, then show the current state. If a transition is still active, compute progress from the shared tick, not time since this tab opened. If over, show the endpoint; do not replay the reward or force a ceremonial pause. After background/resume, catch up to shared progress. A slow client's preparation must not hold the room clock: the first proof preloads both states. Later streaming needs a clear Local concealed fallback and must never hide a still-interactable night-only object until assets arrive.

**Mixed builds:** additive fields/ops do not automatically make new night gameplay safe for old clients. A purely cosmetic unknown look may fall back to familiar daylight while preserving all interactions. A new night-only rule cannot silently rely on that fallback: ensure the supported builds share semantics, or take the documented dev-phase clean break with `WORLD_NS` and its dated history. Keep rule registration append-only within a namespace; use state `version`/`migrate` when required. Validate unknown IDs and old saves without crashing; default missing historic look state to late morning. Do not repurpose an existing clock field or use a one-off message as the saved source of truth. Future sync implementation gets the contract-required replay/late-join checks; this research does not touch those tests.

**Scattered friends are the decisive design case.** One global evening is coherent and simple, but it changes the view for someone still hauling far away. Per-area times avoid that disruption but make adjoining horizons and shared day/night rules harder. Start with one global cosmetic evening, no availability change, no all-player readiness vote. Add a brief world cue and only stage a fade where it does not interrupt an unsafe action. The owner should decide whether this tradeoff feels right before any night activity depends on it.
