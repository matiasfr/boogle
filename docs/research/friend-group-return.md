# Research: why friend groups come back

*Research report, 1 November 2026. Why small friend groups (2–8) return to a shared world week after week without grind, FOMO or daily chores, and what kills a friend world. Precedents: Animal Crossing (real-time clock, letters, the "player 2" problem), Sea of Thieves and The Elder Scrolls Online (no level barriers between friends), Minecraft and Valheim friend servers, Big Walk, Peak, Lethal Company and R.E.P.O. (session games friends schedule), Sky: Children of the Light, Stardew Valley co-op, Deep Rock Galactic, Fortnite, Discord's play data, Wordle, West Marches tabletop campaigns, WoW's rested XP and Fortnite's Supercharged XP (catch-up), Pokémon GO gifts, and papers on social closeness and dark patterns. It feeds [docs/proposals/boogle-2.md](../proposals/boogle-2.md) (1–3 evenings per era, nothing decays, wary of material grinds) and builds on [communal-progression.md](communal-progression.md) (grow while away and never decay, traces with names, Lost Garden's reciprocation, Kojima's likes), [progression-models.md](progression-models.md) (DRG's seasons that come back, Sky's warning about compelling goals), [big-walk-and-communal-endings.md](big-walk-and-communal-endings.md) (traces of wanderers, the ritual goodbye) and [shared-moments-and-events.md](shared-moments-and-events.md) (gathering calls, aftermath, encores) without repeating them. Recommendations only: no design decisions.*

**Markers.** **[V]** means the claim or quote was checked this session on the linked page (an interview, a write-up of a talk, a paper, a primary help or blog page). **[S]** means secondary: a wiki, a forum, a search summary, or a page that could not be fetched. Treat [S] as likely, not certain.

## Summary

1. Friend groups don't return for the game's rewards; they return because the game is **where the friends are**. Discord measures a median 6× longer play on PC when one friend is present, 8× with three [V]; Fortnite is described as a place where teens talk "to their friends on Fortnite but not about Fortnite" [V].
2. Animal Crossing was built for a family that **played at different times**: letters and boards so the father who came home after bedtime could still share the village, and a rule that nobody is "punished for not playing" [V].
3. The worst thing a friend world can do is **split the group by progress**: Sea of Thieves and ESO removed every barrier between a 100-hour friend and a new one [V]; Animal Crossing: New Horizons made the second player "a glorified visitor" and was review-bombed for it [V].
4. Friend servers die in a few predictable ways: **one person finishes the game too fast**, bases drift apart until "multiplayer becomes a solo mode with a chat box", and a 24/7 world means nobody meets [V, a host's blog].
5. Session games that friends schedule (Peak, Lethal Company, R.E.P.O.) **assume the friends supply the comedy**, build in "you need a friend" friction, and keep the dead talking [V].
6. Big Walk shows the cost of a host-owned save and an all-together rule: reviewers liked it and still called setting up a session a board-game night with "the schedules of other working adults" [V].
7. West Marches solved tabletop scheduling with **no fixed schedule, a pool of 10–14, and whoever shows up plays** in one shared world, with players (not the GM) passing news between groups [V].
8. Catch-up works when it is framed as a **bonus for coming back, not a penalty for staying**: WoW's rested XP is the same numbers as a hated fatigue penalty, flipped [S]; Fortnite's Supercharged XP turns on after missed days and stops once you've caught up [S].
9. Respectful return hooks **don't want anything from you**: Wordle's one shared puzzle a day and no notifications [V]; the dark-pattern literature names "playing by appointment" and fake friend notifications [V].
10. Daily-chore social systems wear friends out even when they're kind: Sky's candle runs and Pokémon GO's gift limits are the common complaints [S].

## 1. Animal Crossing: a world for people on different clocks

- **The origin is asynchronous family play.** Eguchi wanted "to improve communication within my own family": he worked late, came home after his children were asleep, and wanted a shared activity "even if we weren't there at the same time", carried "through letters or message boards" ([Nintendo World Report interview](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/interview/11229/animal-crossing-interview)) [V]. The kids played after school, he played at night, and he "didn't really want people to feel like they were being punished for not playing" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crossing-into-the-mainstream-katsuya-eguchi-on-i-animal-crossing-i-)) [V]. He also says the team "consciously avoided any disaster type situations" [V].
- **Communication is the goal; the game is the excuse.** Eguchi and Nogami describe communication as what players should take away, and want players to talk about what the animals said ([shmuplations translation](https://shmuplations.com/animalcrossing/)) [V]. At GDC, Eguchi said visiting other towns "incites inspiration and healthy competition among friends", and that friction (the real-time clock making events "very elusive") is part of the pull ([Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/news/11224/is-that-a-franchise-in-your-pocket)) [V]. Boogle's owner is wary of this kind of scarcity; it's noted as Eguchi's view, not a recommendation.
- **Letters between human residents.** Every Animal Crossing lets players write to the other humans in their town, with an item attached; New Horizons caps it at 40 letters a day to other residents and sends them instantly ([Nookipedia: Letter](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Letter)) [V].
- **The counter-example: player 2.** In New Horizons only the first resident can advance the story, place villagers or order upgrades; everyone else is "a glorified visitor" with "a pretty passive existence", and couples and families sharing a Switch were unhappy ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/it-sucks-being-player-2-in-animal-crossing-1842477024)) [V]. Store reviews were bombed over the one-island limit ([Nintendo Soup](https://nintendosoup.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-is-getting-review-bombed-by-angry-players/)) [S].

## 2. No barriers between friends: Sea of Thieves and ESO

- **Sea of Thieves** made progression cosmetic so that a friend with 100 hours and a new player can sail together on day one. Joe Neate: "All of our design decisions and motivations have been about pulling down barriers and letting people play together." Mike Chapman names the awkward conversation they wanted to avoid: having to "play for 25 hours before you can play with them" ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/sea-of-thieves-return-to-form-for-rare-studi/)) [V].
- **ESO's One Tamriel** (2016) "removed leveling as a barrier to player grouping and exploration"; Matt Firor says "with One Tamriel, the game really took off", and that there is now "little separation between veteran and new players" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/the-elder-scrolls-online-interview/)) [V].
- **Deep Rock Galactic** scales difficulty by chosen hazard and team size, not by character level, so veterans regularly play with level-10 friends ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/548430/discussions/1/1755771676006012129/)) [S].

## 3. What kills a friend server: Minecraft and Valheim

- **A host's postmortem of friend servers** ([Mineando](https://www.mineando.net/en/blog/prevent-minecraft-server-dying-fast)) [V, opinion from a hosting company]:
  - "The biggest server killer is beating the game too fast": one player with endgame gear by day three ends the excitement for everyone. Its fix is a group rule to hold the End until week three.
  - Spread-out bases: "multiplayer becomes a solo mode with a chat box". Its fix is a shared spawn with homes within about 500 blocks.
  - A server that is always on means some play mornings, others nights, and nobody meets; its fix is set windows (weekends, one evening).
  - "Real life gets in the way": pause the world and restart it when people are free.
- **Valheim's world modifiers** (2023) let a group tune the world after the fact: a Casual preset, portals that carry ore, passive enemies, and **raids that follow each player's own progress rather than the world's** ([guide summary](https://low.ms/knowledgebase/valheim-world-modifiers-explained); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/update-server-modifiers)) [S]. Player-based raids are a small, direct answer to "the strongest friend sets the danger for everyone".
- **Stardew Valley** added an optional separate-money mode in 1.4 after players asked for it; the farm, community centre and mines stay shared ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/stardew-valley/stardew-valley-separate-money)) [S]. A shared wallet is one more place where one friend's pace overrides another's (Boogle already has no shared wallet).

## 4. Big Walk: the cost of "only together"

Big Walk needs at least two players, and the host keeps the save. Aftermath's reviewer liked that "having to always play together" gives it weight, but was rarely keen to "deal with the schedules of other working adults", and could only reach new areas "with the people I started the game with" ([Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/big-walk-impressions-review/)) [V]. Game Informer compares setting up a session to "setting up a board game night", and its crew of three finished in nine hours ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/big-walk/a-walk-to-remember)) [V]. Fan guides put a regular group at 12–20 hours across several sessions ([Pixel Twelve](https://pixeltwelve.com/articles/big-walk-how-long-to-beat-story-length-ending)) [S]. For Boogle, Big Walk's together-moments are worth copying (earlier reports); its scheduling rule is the part to avoid, and Boogle's server-hosted rooms already avoid the host-save problem.

## 5. Session games friends schedule: Peak, Lethal Company, R.E.P.O.

- **The friends are the content.** Aggro Crab's Caelan Pollock describes the genre as games that assume "you and your friends will make up the difference in comedy and interaction" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-was-meant-to-be-friendslop-game-from-the-start)) [V].
- **Friction that makes you talk.** Nick Kaman (GDC): "You need a friend to do it. It's just one more layer of friction that means you have to interact socially." Routes are argued over aloud; dead players keep talking as ghosts; a guidebook's "Rule Zero" is "Never abandon a friend in need", because the team feared strong players would leave friends behind ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/peak-co-developer-aggro-crab-shares-lessons-in-friendslop)) [V].
- **A shared, changing mountain.** Peak's map changes on a schedule and everyone gets the same one, so groups retry it and talk about it on Reddit and Discord (same source) [V]. This is a shared topic, not a chore: nothing is lost by skipping a day.
- **Zeekerss on R.E.P.O.:** he came round to it once his group was working on a hard task together, and called it the game for "me and my friends to play non-stop" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/horror/repo-is-fun-lethal-company-creator-recants-their-criticism-of-the-new-co-op-horror-game-after-trying-to-move-a-grand-piano-through-a-mansion/)) [S].
- **Late joiners arrive as an event.** Deep Rock Galactic drops a late joiner in a personal pod: Mission Control announces the reinforcement and a hologram marks where it will land ([DRG wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Personal_Drop_Pod)) [S].

## 6. Fortnite and Discord: the game as a hangout

- Matthew Ball describes Fortnite's role "as a social network or hangout rather than a game": teens talk "to their friends on Fortnite but not about Fortnite", like "loitering at a shopping centre"; Party Island removed guns to make room for that ([Matthew Ball](https://www.matthewball.co/all/epicprimer5)) [V]. Tim Sweeney has called it "a social experience that you play together with friends" ([PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/66845/epics-sweeney-fortnite-isnt-addictive-but-a-healthy-social-experience/)) [S].
- Discord's internal data (2021–2025): "When even one friend is present on Discord, players on PC spend a median of 6x longer in a game", 8× with three; players in voice channels play an estimated 66% more days ([Discord, GDC 2026](https://discord.com/blog/building-on-the-social-layer-of-games-whats-new-from-gdc-2026)) [V]. These are a platform's own correlational numbers, not independent research, but the direction is clear: a friend being around is the strongest return signal there is.
- A CHI PLAY survey from the pandemic found that people who already played multiplayer games played more and named social play as the reason, while non-players did not take up games to stay in touch: crises "mainly catalyze existing gaming habits" ([Cmentowski and Krüger 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00264)) [V]. Depping and Mandryk found that cooperation and interdependence each raise social closeness, even between strangers ([CHI PLAY 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cooperation-and-Interdependence:-How-Multiplayer-Depping-Mandryk/c5ac8e5133a14e681a388c191ed1a95e72314c14)) [S].

## 7. West Marches: whoever shows up plays

Ben Robbins ran a campaign for a pool of about 10–14 players with no fixed schedule. Players emailed the group to say when they wanted to play and where they wanted to go; whoever was free came. "If you can play once a week, that's fine. If you can only play once a month, that's fine too." Anything that could be player-initiated was, including passing maps and news between groups ([Ars Ludi](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/78/grand-experiments-west-marches/)) [V]. It is the tabletop version of a drop-in world: one persistent place, many small overlapping sessions, the group's own chat as the scheduler.

## 8. Friendship mechanics and drifting apart

Daniel Cook and colleagues (Lost Garden) list proximity, similarity, reciprocity and disclosure; earlier reports used reciprocity. The parts relevant here: "the hard part is getting people who played together once, to play together another time"; a published schedule of recurring events raises the odds of meeting; "lapsed friendships that lack negative exchanges are easier to restart than new friendships"; and progress or skill gaps mean that "If two friendly players want to play together, they may not be able to." They suggest watching for sudden drops in shared play as an attrition signal ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2017/01/27/game-design-patterns-for-building-friendships/)) [V].

## 9. Catch-up for the friend who missed a session

- **WoW's rested XP.** In beta, XP gains fell the longer you stayed logged in, and "people just hated it". Blizzard doubled the XP needed and flipped the curve: you start at 200% after a break and decay to 100%. The numbers are effectively the same; the framing changed from a penalty for playing to a bonus for coming back (Chris Remo relaying Rob Pardo, via [Psychology of Games](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2010/03/framing-and-world-of-warcrafts-rest-system/)) [S, second-hand]. Engadget lists the original tiers, down to 25% "exhausted", removed in beta patch 0.7 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-02-wow-archivist-beta-surprises.html)) [S].
- **Fortnite's Supercharged XP** switches on when you've missed daily or weekly quests and boosts XP until you've made up what you missed, then stops ([Epic help page via Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/fortnite/what-supercharged-xp-fortnite-explained)) [S]. It is self-limiting: it closes a gap, it can't open one.
- **Destiny 2** keeps players' power across a seasonal reset and offers a catch-up to a floor ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mmo/bungie-removes-seasonal-power-resets-introduces-catch-up-mechanics-destiny-2-addressing-player-feedback)) [S].

## 10. Hooks that respect people, and ones that don't

- **Wordle** gave everyone the same single puzzle a day, with no ads and no push notifications. Josh Wardle: "people have an appetite for things that transparently don't want anything from you"; sharing results is "a really comforting way of letting other people know that you're thinking about them"; one puzzle "everybody is solving" is why it spread ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/)) [V].
- **Dark patterns.** Zagal, Björk and Lewis's temporal dark patterns are grinding and "playing by appointment", which "forces players to return to a game daily in order to complete quests"; social ones pressure players "to recruit friends", and the catalogue lists friend spam and impersonation (notifications that look like they came from a friend). In a 2024 sample of 1,496 rated mobile games, only about 10% had no reported dark patterns ([Niknejad et al., MUM '24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05039)) [V].
- **Sky: Children of the Light** is kind by design, but players describe hours of daily candle runs, seasons and travelling spirits close together, and returning players unable to afford what they missed ([Sky wiki forum](https://sky-children-of-the-light.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000080204); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2325290/discussions/0/3881599433102252530)) [S, player opinion].
- **Pokémon GO gifts**: a friend can't send you another gift until you open the last one, and friendship rises once a day per friend ([Niantic help](https://niantic.helpshift.com/hc/faq/85-sending-receiving-gifts)) [S]. One-at-a-time keeps it bounded; daily caps across hundreds of friends turned it into a chore for big lists ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/pokemon/news/one-of-pokemon-gos-earliest-features-still-driving-players-wild-with-tedious-issue)) [S].

## What this means for Boogle

Today: rooms are server-hosted (no host owns the save), any player can start an opening day, earnings are personal and progress is the island's, an offline helper bank does up to one section's work while nobody plays ([src/sim/bank.ts](../../src/sim/bank.ts); [docs/game-design/haul.md](../game-design/haul.md)), and identity is a per-browser pid with a name and colour ([docs/game-design/multiplayer.md](../game-design/multiplayer.md)). The owner wants eras of 1–3 evenings, nothing that decays, no material grind, and solo-first prototypes. The biggest risk the sources point to, for that pace, is **one friend finishing an era alone** while the others were away.

1. **Watch for one friend outpacing the rest before designing against it.** Minecraft servers die when one player beats the game early; Lost Garden says to watch for drops in shared play. With 1–3 evenings an era, a keen player could open an era on a Tuesday that the others hear about on Friday. Measure first: per pid, the share of each era's sections laid, and whether each era's opening day had more than one player present.
   *Smallest prototype:* log `{ era, pid, sections }` and the opening-day attendee count into the existing telemetry, and read it after the first multi-person week.

2. **If outpacing shows up, slow the last step, not the work.** Options from the sources, softest first: the era's final ribbon waits for an opening day that rings louder with more people (already in shared-moments, rec 9); the last section stays unlaid until a second distinct player has helped this era, with a timeout of a few real days so a solo group is never stuck (the Minecraft "hold the End" rule, built in); or a room setting the group chooses (Valheim's world modifiers). Each bends DESIGN.md principle 8 a little; the timeout keeps solo play whole.
   *Smallest prototype:* a `?proto=` flag that holds the last section's ghost until two distinct pids appear in the era's contributors or N days pass; ask one solo tester whether the wait reads as a promise or a wall.

3. **No player 2: anything one friend can do, any friend can do.** Animal Crossing's resident representative is the clearest warning; Sea of Thieves and ESO the clearest fixes. Boogle already lets anyone start an opening day; keep that true for every new verb (placing, ordering, choosing a route, naming), and never key a decision to whoever created the room.
   *Smallest prototype:* a scenario where a brand-new pid joins a room mid-era and runs every Interact verb; it fails if any is blocked by role or seniority.

4. **Rested hauling for the friend who missed a session.** WoW's flip and Fortnite's Supercharged XP show that a self-limiting bonus on return feels like a welcome. A player who has been away for some days could carry a bigger bundle or lay faster for their first few trips, until they've done about one evening's share. It speeds shared progress only (no personal power, no knack skipping) and can't open a gap, only close one.
   *Smallest prototype:* `?proto=rested`: a local flag set when the pid's last-seen is over 3 days old, doubling bundle size for the next 10 trips, with a small sparkle so the player knows why; ask whether it reads as "welcome back" or as noise.

5. **Arrival as a small event.** DRG's drop pod gives a late joiner a moment and a place. When a friend joins, others could see where they arrive (a little boat bumping the jetty, a balloon) and hear a short honk-like cue, with their name; the joiner lands near the most people rather than at a fixed spawn. That answers "multiplayer becomes a solo mode with a chat box" from both sides.
   *Smallest prototype:* Local only: on a new remote presence, a 2 s arrival effect at their first position and a name toast; spawn the joiner near the densest cluster of players.

6. **A mailbox for whoever wasn't there.** Eguchi built letters and boards for family members who never overlapped; earlier reports cover traces and plaques for the whole group, but not a thing left for one friend. A mailbox per player where any friend can leave one item (a fruit, a shell, a hat) and a stamp; one slot per sender per recipient, like Pokémon GO's one-gift-at-a-time, so it can never become a daily chore; no expiry. The recipient opens it on their next visit; nothing is lost if they never do.
   *Smallest prototype:* Lockstep state `mail: { to, from, item }[]` capped at one per pair, a mailbox mesh at each player's spot (or one shared post box), and a "you've got something from Ana" bubble on join.

7. **Let the group's own chat be the notification.** Wordle spread through shared results with no push at all; Discord shows friends being around is what brings people back; the dark-pattern catalogue names fake friend messages and appointment play. So: no push notifications and no "the island misses you". Instead, a button that makes a **postcard** (a still of what changed plus the room link) for a player to paste into their group chat themselves. A human sends it, about something a human did.
   *Smallest prototype:* a "Copy postcard" entry in the pause view that renders the current view with a caption ("Era 2 opened · Ana, Leo") to a PNG and copies it with the room URL.

8. **Make one evening a whole session.** Peak, Lethal Company and Big Walk all give an evening a shape: a climb, a quota, a stretch of walk. With eras at 1–3 evenings, each evening could close on something visible (a stretch of line opened, a ride to its end, a site's scaffolding up), so a friend who only comes once still sees a finished beat. Measure how long a section takes with 1, 2 and 4 players and size each era so an evening holds two to four such beats.
   *Smallest prototype:* time the haul loop solo and with two players in the haul scenario; note how many sections fit in 90 minutes, and whether the last one of the night ends on a ride.

9. **Something shared to talk about that can't be missed.** Peak's same-for-everyone mountain and Wordle's one puzzle give friends a topic without making anyone log in on a given day. A Boogle version: one seeded curio per room per week (a wreck on the reef, a strange crate on the beach) that waits until someone finds it, then stays found with the finder's name. It never expires, so it's news, not FOMO.
   *Smallest prototype:* one weekly curio placed from the room seed and the ISO week number, persisted until picked up; a plaque or the postcard (rec 7) names who found it.

10. **Keep friends close by design.** Minecraft groups drift into separate bases; Peak argues routes aloud; Depping and Mandryk find cooperation and interdependence each raise closeness. Era work sites, stations and the home hub should sit close enough that friends doing different jobs still see each other, and some jobs (two-pusher crates, held ropes) should go faster with two but stay possible alone (solo first).
    *Smallest prototype:* log the median distance between players during two-player sessions; if it stays above voice range most of the time, move the next era's work sites closer together before adding anything else.

## Gaps

- No developer talk or postmortem was found on friend-group retention in Valheim, Minecraft Realms, Stardew co-op, Lethal Company or Sky; those findings come from wikis, press, a hosting company's blog and player forums.
- The Discord figures are the platform's own correlational data.
- WoW's rested-XP story is second-hand (a podcast recollection relayed in a blog), though widely repeated and consistent with the beta patch history.
- No source measured how often friend groups split when one player plays ahead in a shared progression game; recs 1 and 2 are written to measure it in Boogle first.
