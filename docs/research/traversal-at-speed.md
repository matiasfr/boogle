# Traversal at speed: flow, boost and the journey between places

> **Verdict:** Pleasant walking, expressive connected movement, chosen speed bursts.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Make ordinary movement pleasant, connected movement expressive and speed bursts a choice.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [The shape of the game](../decisions.md#the-shape-of-the-game).

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Live web research into Sunset Overdrive, Burnout, Breath of the Wild, Spider-Man, Tony Hawk, A Short Hike and Jusant; source audit of Boogle at `3280c6ff`. Builds on [movement-abilities.md](movement-abilities.md), with [third-person-camera.md](third-person-camera.md) and [vehicle-feel.md](vehicle-feel.md) as format and adjacent research. Recommendations only: no game was played, controller instrumented, route validated or performance measured for this report._

**Evidence:** **[V]** fetched primary source, including a developer speaking in an interview; **[P]** partial evidence, such as an official talk abstract or indexed guide text; **[C]** inspected repository fact; **[D]** calculation from stated assumptions; **[H]** proposed Boogle behavior or inference. All new tuning values are **[H]**, never purported constants from a reference game. Sources are inline. Spider-Man's published slides and speaker notes and Sunset Overdrive's audio slides were downloaded and read in the relevant sections; the recordings were not watched.

## Decision boundary and recommendation

Make **ordinary movement pleasant, connected movement expressive, and a short burst of speed something the player chooses**. Sunset Overdrive contributes transitions and a world that offers another move; Burnout contributes earning the means to continue by moving well. Boogle should combine those with Breath of the Wild's readable, altitude-funded glide and Jusant's forgiving recovery. The result should help a player enjoy a familiar route and arrive somewhere useful, without requiring a trick score, a dangerous near miss or a button sequence.

The owner's current instruction and the newest **Design Table** entry in [decisions.md](../decisions.md) take precedence: abilities are **ancient technology found in the world**, the glider should match **Breath of the Wild** closely, and faster travel belongs to becoming **hungry for efficiency**. At research time that entry was in fetched commit `275d7f8`, on `origin/ship/matias-franco-275d7f8`, while this checkout still had the older round-3 entry first. It was read without merging or editing that branch. Its new **Show, don't tell** principle also applies. The earlier movement report's automatic connection-to-ability awards and collectible route are therefore not the progression specification for this report [C].

Keep the handling work in [movement-abilities.md](movement-abilities.md): double jump, safe fixed-target hook, fast glide deployment, broad arrivals and real collision. This report adds the **speed envelope, transitions, boost economy, sensory cues and relationship to infrastructure**. It does not repeat that report's tower, eight secrets or control assignments. A proposed booster is an additional ancient device to discover, not an already-approved fourth ability or a mandatory task added to [round 4](../plans/round-4.md).

Serves [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) **1, Toys before chores; 4, Cooperation is automatic; 5, Nothing is the antagonist; 7, Telegraph everything; 10, Slapstick over precision; Small and readable**, plus the newer **11, Show, don't tell**. Ordinary walking must remain worthwhile. Trains remain transport and bulk logistics; the owner cut wagon stoke, snatch and grind, and round 4 explicitly leaves rail grinding out. Referencing Sunset Overdrive does not reverse those decisions.

## 1. What the references establish

### Sunset Overdrive: a chain of available actions

Drew Murray directly names **Tony Hawk and Jet Grind Radio** as traversal influences, and describes repeatedly changing surface attachment and input complexity to accommodate players who were also shooting and switching weapons. This is evidence of designing around divided attention, not proof of a particular snap radius or timing window. [Developer interview](https://www.gamerevolution.com/originals/9181-interview-sunset-overdrives-drew-murray-and-ted-price-talk-traversal-and-game-design) [V].

The official Prima guide describes air dash as a way to reach another grind, bounce, pole or wall run, and dodge-to-handspring as a continuation tool. The fetched page exposed its guide structure; these mechanics were available in indexed text, so treat them as **[P]**, not measured handling. [Controls Breakdown](https://primagames.com/eguides/sunset-overdrive-official-strategy-guide/sunset-city-essentials/controls-breakdown). Liz England's GDC abstract confirms that two city layouts were discarded as the role of traversal changed. It supplies no route dimensions. [The Worlds of Sunset Overdrive](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022114/Level-Design-in-a-Day) [P].

England's separate published workflow puts **whitebox spaces → traversal layer → split into blocks → handoff to combat, mission and multiplayer designers**. That makes traversal an early spatial constraint, rather than decoration added after missions. [Transitioning From Linear to Open World Design, PDF pages 86–91](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/England_Liz_TransitioningFromLinear.pdf) [V]. For Boogle, establish the enjoyable path and arrival before dressing the fast line [H].

**Boogle inference [H]:** the valuable unit is a **transition with a visible next opportunity**. A fast path can feed a jump, a jump a glide, and a glide a broad path that continues home. Put the next useful surface in view before commitment. A missed upper route should drop onto a lower route, not end the journey. Borrow this connectivity and forgiving acquisition; do not make the ground undesirable, turn every railway into a grind rail, or add wall-running before the existing abilities compose well.

### Burnout Paradise: distinguish a reserve from a full-bar chain

EA's **Remastered manual, printed pages 14–15**, distinguishes three boost types. Stunt favors jumps, rolls and drifts; Aggression favors hostile driving. Both allow simultaneous earning and spending. Speed requires a full bar to start; exhausting it earns a Burnout, partially refills the bar, and sufficient risky driving can refill it fully for another chain. Crashing or running out ends the chain. Gas stations provide refills. These are shipped rules, not an inference from a montage. [Official manual](https://eaassets-a.akamaihd.net/eahelp/manuals/bpr-pc-en.pdf) [V].

**Boogle inference [H]:** start with the **spendable reserve**, because a player should be able to accelerate out of a small success without first completing a performance. Compare a full-bar chain only as a later variant if that commitment is independently fun. Substitute route continuation for danger: no traffic attacks, proximity-to-friends bonus or collision farming. Keep charge when stopping to inspect, talk, build or wait for someone. The useful feedback loop is **move → gain charge → choose a burst → reach a new movement opportunity**. A multiplier that collapses when someone stops to help would fight co-op.

### Spider-Man: continuous presentation and deliberate departures from physics

Doug Sheahan's **Concrete Jungle Gym**, GDC 2019, provides unusually concrete primary evidence. The slide PDF's pages **172–175** describe replacing noisy velocity-facing camera behavior with predicted-position dragging; **186–201** describe speed-dependent FOV and distance, then replacing per-move cinematic framing with a unified model. Pages **216–225** explain how momentum-heavy air control made players overshoot and orbit destinations; stronger air steering and backward-stick drag improved arrivals. Page **85** gives a historical average-speed constraint of **30 m/s** due to streaming. That is Insomniac's production constraint, not a Boogle target. [Slides and speaker notes](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Sheahan_Doug_ConcreteJungleGym.pdf), [session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026084/Concrete-Jungle-Gym-Building-Traversal) [V].

**Boogle inference [H]:** preserve the feeling that speed was earned, while letting explicit braking and correction beat inertia. Keep one visual interpretation of speed across run, jump and landing. Boogle's owner already rejected trailing follow as perceived inertia: do not copy Spider-Man's entire camera. Use its continuity lesson inside the direct camera, and spend the complexity budget on arriving where intended. A dramatic dash is not successful if stopping at the work site requires three circles.

### Breath of the Wild: height buys reach, with a clear landing contract

Nintendo's controls guide describes opening the paraglider in the air, steering with the movement stick, closing and reopening it, and slowly spending stamina during flight. Closing stops consumption but does not recharge it; updrafts are explicit world features. Free camera inspection is separately documented. It publishes no forward-speed or turning constants. [Nintendo controls guide](https://www.guiasnintendo.com/2c-switch/zelda-breath-of-the-wild/guia-zelda-breath-of-the-wild/controles.html) [V].

The earlier [movement report](movement-abilities.md#glide-spend-height-choose-the-landing) already proposes **8 m/s horizontal, 2.5 m/s sink, comparing 3.2**, and explains the limits of the stationary-descent paper behind the comparison. Retain these as hypotheses, not a claim to have matched Nintendo. Match deployment, correction, neutral-stick behavior, looking and landing in a later side-by-side capture before deciding the glide feels right [H].

**Addition here [H]:** leave powered boost out of the base glide. Launching quickly may carry a brief overspeed into it, but deployment should settle into the familiar glide envelope. Do not turn a found canopy into a jetpack, or let opening/closing manufacture altitude or refill boost. Omitting BotW's stamina is an explicit Boogle proposal inherited from the movement report; it is not faithful reproduction of every BotW rule. The owner's request for close feel deserves a fresh comparison, not silent acceptance of all earlier omissions.

### Tony Hawk: connecting moves matter more than collecting moves

Activision's developer-backed primer describes **revert into manual** extending a vert combo, spine transfers linking opposing surfaces, acid drops supplying momentum and wall plants returning the skater into motion. These actions bridge situations that would otherwise stop a line. [Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 1 + 2 primer](https://blog.activision.com/tony-hawk/2020-08/THPS-Demo-Everything-You-Need-to-Know) [V]. Its balance meters and scoring are not requirements for flow.

**Boogle inference [H]:** prioritize the moments **after landing, after a hook, after a collision and after boost release**. Do not zero velocity because an animation changed. Keep the second jump available for correction, but do not reward jumping in place. A returning player can discover a better line across the same terrain without a combo counter, compulsory trick or another full movement system. A hook's authored safe stop can remain a useful punctuation mark; not every verb needs to preserve maximum velocity.

### A Short Hike: fast returns and space to stop

Adam Robinson-Yu describes summit respite, seeing the landscape differently and pacing that still works when players take an unexpected route. He also describes Mark Sparling's adaptive score: instruments vary by location, strings enter during flight and trumpets swell with motorboat speed. [Developer account](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/) [V].

**Boogle inference [H]:** speed should make revisits delightful without flattening the island into a corridor. Put a quiet overlook before a fast descent and a useful destination after it. Give the same journey a different rhythm outbound and homeward. A small procedural musical flourish can acknowledge sustained travel; it should not accelerate the entire soundtrack every time speed fluctuates. Let a player stop to look without losing a banked reward.

### Jusant: flow does not require punishment or constant speed

Co-creative directors Kevin Poupard and Mathieu Beaudelin describe a core climbing system built around route choice and combinations, with a safety rope and three pitons that create intermediate recovery points. Players cannot die; a mistake returns them to the last piton rather than necessarily the bottom. They explicitly distinguish the adventure from a climbing simulation. [Developer breakdown](https://dont-nod.com/en/scaling-new-heights-learn-more-about-jusants-innovative-climbing-system/) [V]. The later patch documents simplified climbing, jump assistance and optional removal of stamina. [Accessibility update](https://support.dont-nod.com/hc/en-gb/articles/14957643453853-Jusant-Patch-1-04-The-Accessibility-Update-November-2023) [V].

**Boogle inference [H]:** preserve the journey when a move fails. Broad lower paths, rest landings and short return ramps are cheaper and clearer than a new rope simulation. A slow tower ascent can make a fast return meaningful. Rapid traversal everywhere would remove that contrast. An optional assist should preserve destinations and rewards, not mark a less skilled player as ineligible.

### Celeste: help the intended transition

Maddy Thorson's own account documents coyote time, buffered jumps, apex leniency, corner correction and retained moving-platform momentum. [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html) [V]. Boogle already has several of these. The useful addition is to preserve them at boosted speed and audit the transition rules; a 2D corner nudge is not permission to teleport a 3D body through a wall [H].

## 3. A speed envelope that remains controllable

Start with a **ground booster**, discovered as ancient equipment after the player has experienced a useful walkable shortcut. Keep 5.6/9.5 ordinary movement. Trial **14 m/s**, then compare **12 and 16** on the same course; choose by read-ahead and arrival quality, not top-speed spectacle [H].

| Parameter / transition                       | Starting proposal [H]                                                                                                                                                                  | Why                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Boost acceleration above sprint speed        | 25 m/s²; retain ordinary acceleration below sprint                                                                                                                                     | 9.5 → 14 takes 0.18 s [D]: immediate, with a short audible onset.                                                                                                                            |
| Forward overspeed after release / empty tank | Bleed only excess above normal target at 3 m/s² while input continues forward                                                                                                          | 14 → 9.5 lasts 1.5 s [D]; release need not feel like hitting a wall. Never restore speed removed by a real impact.                                                                           |
| Ground neutral / opposite input              | Cancel boost; retain ordinary 70 / 115 m/s² response                                                                                                                                   | Stopping is a deliberate command. Do not apply the long forward coast when the stick rests.                                                                                                  |
| Steering during boosted ground travel        | Begin at 45 m/s² lateral authority; compare existing 70                                                                                                                                | Avoid both rail-like steering and a huge skid. Ordinary movement remains unchanged.                                                                                                          |
| Airborne overspeed                           | Preserve takeoff velocity, then bleed excess at 1.5 m/s²; stop powered acceleration and spending on takeoff; try 25 m/s² air correction and 35 opposing brake only for this fast state | A burst can launch a jump without becoming flight or preserving free overspeed through repeated hops. Clear provenance on landing/reset; ordinary air tuning belongs to the movement report. |
| Landing on a broad, walkable surface         | Keep collision-resolved horizontal velocity; no extra landing lock; coast only with forward intent                                                                                     | Never copy the previous frame's velocity back over Rapier's contact result.                                                                                                                  |
| Hook arrival                                 | Retain the safe arrival/braking envelope from the movement report; accept queued movement immediately when supported                                                                   | Do not fire a 16 m/s hook onto a 4 m platform without braking to sell continuity.                                                                                                            |
| Glide entry                                  | Keep 8 m/s target and 2.5/3.2 sink comparison; use the earlier 12 m/s² braking to settle incoming horizontal excess; no powered boost                                                  | A 14 → 8 transition takes about 0.5 s and adds 1.5 m beyond constant 8 m/s travel [D]. Handle inherited support once, not in a second carry term.                                            |

The numbers expose the layout requirement. With 45 m/s² lateral authority, `r = v²/a` gives an ideal constant-speed turn radius of **4.36 m at 14**, **5.69 m at 16**. These are flat-space estimates, not guaranteed controller behavior [D]. A path with a 2 m hairpin asks the player to brake. Preserve that choice instead of applying invisible speed zones. A large reverse input should stop and turn, not perform an unrequested U-turn drift.

At 14 m/s, neutral stopping under 70 m/s² is about **1.4 m**, before reaction time; a **0.35 s** reaction adds **4.9 m**, giving about **6.3 m** total [D]. Use **8–10 m clear runout** at a taught landing/work-site approach. A player should see a junction or destination at least **1.5 s** ahead: **21 m at 14**, **24 m at 16**. Read-ahead matters more than adding motion blur.

For a scrape, let contact remove inward velocity while tangential motion continues; smooth collision seams that catch the body. Start with no new stun or charge loss. For a frontal stop, cancel boost after **0.15 s below 2 m/s** with movement intent, play one short bonk, and permit steering/jumping immediately. Any pose over **0.2–0.3 s** must leave the route readable. Existing ball knockdown remains its own toy. A miss costs **3–6 s** of nearby recovery where practical, not a reload, dropped cargo or a repeated tower ascent [H].

## 4. Boost earned through travel, spent by choice

Everything here is a first comparison **[H]**. The meter is personal, transient movement state. Its charge buys no materials or cosmetics and is not a shared wallet.

| Rule                         | Starting value / behavior                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Capacity and first discovery | 100 units; first discovery starts full so the device can be tried immediately.                                                                                                        |
| Spend                        | 20 units/s while actively boosting on valid ground; start with at least 25 units; stop at zero, cancel, takeoff, water, seat, modal or loss of movement intent.                       |
| Basic earn                   | 4 units/s while moving unboosted on walkable ground at ≥7 m/s relative to support, with real displacement and forward intent. No earn merely from held input against a wall.          |
| Flow earn                    | +8 after a hook arrival or glide-to-run transition that covered ≥12 m; same transition kind pays at most once per 10 s and only after ≥12 m net displacement since its last award.    |
| What does not earn           | Jump/double-jump presses, repeated canopy toggles, time in the air, train carry, collisions, near misses, another player being close, or building more slowly.                        |
| Stopping                     | Charge remains; no decay, bank timer or multiplier to protect. Empty charge leaves ordinary sprint available.                                                                         |
| Chaining                     | Flow awards may enter the reserve immediately; no bonus merely for emptying it. Depletion requires a fresh activation before restarting, so tiny refills cannot cause pulsing.        |
| Limits                       | Clamp once to 0–100; one award per completed transition. Repeated travel can refill it: that is acceptable when the route is fun, not an economy exploit requiring a policing system. |

A full tank gives **5 s** of power, roughly **70 m** at top speed before acceleration/terrain. At 4 units/s the empty-to-full refill takes **25 s** of ordinary fast travel; a 25-unit starter takes **6.25 s**. Flow contributes meaningful small refills without demanding a trick chain [D]. Excluding ordinary jump rewards avoids making constant hopping the default way to haul; the 10 s per-kind limit constrains repeated hook/glide awards. Do not add arbitrary jump penalties to compensate for an overly generous reward.

Check the long-run balance before giving charge for more things. Ignoring turns, acceleration and coast, with unboosted earn rate `g`, powered fraction is `g/(20+g)`. At **g=4**, that is **16.7%**, so alternating 9.5/14 averages **10.25 m/s**. At an illustrative **g=6** including occasional flow awards, it is **23.1%**, averaging **10.54 m/s** [D]. Coast adds distance; route-changing abilities and waits change the result more. These are accounting examples, not playtest times or guarantees that a 12 m/s train wins.

Use a small visible device with four lit segments and a short charge sound; show a compact meter only when it changes, then fade after **1.5 s**. Let the device's exhaust and tone explain spending. Distinguish ready/empty with shape and sound as well as color. No permanent combo leaderboard or repeated tutorial text [H].

For agents, compare **A: ordinary sprint**, **B: reserve boost with travel earning only**, **C: B plus transition awards**. If C induces ritual hook detours or canopy spam, remove those awards before adding more conditions. A Burnout Speed-style variant—full charge required and continuation awarded at exhaustion—can be a separate later experiment; it is not the default because it makes short practical bursts harder to access.

## 5. Sell speed without losing the path

### Camera: one speed response, not a camera per action

Keep the selected **5.2 m** arm and zero X/Z follow lag. Extend the current 62→67° response with a small extra band: **67→70° vertical FOV over 9.5→14 m/s**. Start with **0.20 s widening / 0.40 s return time constants**, using `1 − exp(−dt/tau)`, not a frame-dependent increment. Give run/jump/landing the same filtered speed signal so an ability switch does not restart the effect [H].

Use movement speed relative to support for on-foot FOV. Riding has its own calm profile; a stationary passenger is not boosting. Carry the filtered signal briefly across takeoff, then converge to actual locomotion mode; avoid a lens jump from a support-frame change. Centralize the final lens write in the camera owner instead of adding another `camera.fov +=` contributor. On leaving a mode, restore the selected base plus the next mode's offset, including reset, seating and reduced-motion changes [H].

Only if players cannot see the next turn, compare a **0.10 s velocity look-ahead capped at 1 m**, filtered over **0.15 s**, applied to position and aim together. Keep the zero-look-ahead baseline: the owner's direct feel beats a theoretical improvement. No automatic yaw, roll, head bob or whole-view shake is needed for the first boost. A **+0.3 m arm pulse over 0.12 s, returning over 0.30 s** is a later isolated comparison, not something to combine with FOV in the first test [H].

Collision resolves after desired framing. Preserve the current arm's snap/hold/release. Test fast travel along a wall and around convex corners, because a radial arm sweep alone does not establish that the lens's inter-frame movement is safe. Keep the collision probe tied to actual FOV/aspect. [Godot's spring-arm documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/spring_arm.html) describes near-plane shape sweeps; [Unity Cinemachine Deoccluder](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineDeoccluder.html) separates radius, hold time and inward/outward damping [V]. Boogle already implements a smaller sphere-arm solution; it does not need either framework.

Offer **0–100% speed-FOV effect**, with 0 keeping the base lens and all mechanical speed. Retain useful jump-height stabilization when disabling effects. Keep blur, roll and shake off initially; disable extra streaks with reduced motion. [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) supports adjustable FOV, camera movement and distracting effects [V]. It does not establish one universally comfortable FOV. Compare 16:9 and 4:3, plus best-effort portrait: all proposed lens numbers are **vertical**.

Sunset Overdrive's audio team explicitly prioritizes amplifying gameplay without clutter. Kristen Quebe and Bryan Higa's slides describe audio changing with the Style Meter; their grinding system drives pitch and the relative gains of low/high material loops from horizontal speed, with animation yaw driving EQ. [All Style, All Substance, GDC 2015, PDF pages 14 and 22](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Quebe_Kristen_Higa_Bryan_All_Style_All.pdf) [V]. This supports continuous speed-responsive sound with a few distinct accents; Boogle can test the principle through shared noise and procedural chirps without reproducing Sunset's asset system [H].

Route continuous wind to a ducked ambience path or give it the existing dialogue duck, so speed cannot mask residents or future proximity voice. A Short Hike's movement-reactive orchestration above supports the idea of mode-sensitive sound, not these synthesis values. Neither source establishes the proposed frequencies, gains or timings [H].

Animate the **device and limbs**, with about **5–8° local spine lean**, not the whole avatar root pitching toward the ground. Keep its head and carried bundle readable; the owner already rejected the old whole-body lean. Broad surface landmarks, posts set safely outside the travel envelope and ground texture passing underfoot provide inexpensive peripheral motion. They are preferable to a full-screen blur pass that hides the landing [H].

## 6. Paths, abilities and trains should save different kinds of effort

Design each place at three overlapping scales [H]:

| Layer               | Job                                                                | Starting spatial test                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Walkable path       | First arrival, carrying, social movement, reliable return          | Continuous 3–4 m clear path where terrain allows; 5–6 m at shared landings and interactions. Corners reveal the next destination. No boost requirement. |
| Ability / fast line | Expressive revisits, shortcuts through height and familiar terrain | 20–40 m between readable decisions at 14 m/s; broad 6–10 m arrival areas with 8–10 m braking runout; alternate lower route on a miss.                   |
| Train               | Reliable long-distance travel and bulk goods; time to look or talk | Direct links over terrain/sea, useful boarding positions and low avoidable wait. Use measured door-to-door advantage, not just a top-speed comparison.  |

These are layout hypotheses, not a global widening order for existing paths. A slow ledge or a quiet village is useful contrast. Keep dense interaction spaces out of the fastest line, with the fast route visibly curving into a broad approach rather than silently disabling the player's equipment.

**Worked comparison [D/H]:** a 240 m winding foot route takes **42.9 s at 5.6**, **25.3 s at 9.5**, or roughly **22.8 s at 10.54** before corners. A 100 m direct train route at the current 12 m/s and 3.5 m/s² acceleration/deceleration takes about **11.8 s** from rest to rest. Add an illustrative **5 s walk/boarding plus 5 s wait**: **21.8 s**. That is barely better than skilled foot travel, so this distance alone does not make the train a strong personal efficiency reward. Cargo automation may still justify it; do not claim the ride is much faster.

For a **600 m foot / 240 m rail** trip, the same optimistic foot pace is **56.9 s**, while the train is about **23.4 + 10 = 33.4 s**, a more legible gain. The assumed 5 s wait is not today's guaranteed timetable wait; current dwell is 5 s, but a missed train can mean an entire cycle. Measure median and bad-case boarding/wait time from [place's rail code](../../src/proto/place/rail.ts) before deciding. Target roughly **30% or more door-to-door saving** when a train is presented as the next travel upgrade [H].

At the proposed 8/2.5 glide, a 30 m height difference buys **96 m** ideal horizontal reach, before deployment and landing reserve [D]. That can erase a local switchback while leaving a sea crossing a railway project. Keep the earlier report's conservative height and range margins. Audit **boosted launch + double jump + hook + glide** together against main-line gates; separate ability ranges underestimate their combined reach. Clever optional skips are welcome, but an accidental bypass of the first far-island railway needs a deliberate design decision.

Do not make carrying disable the new device by default or spill the owner's stable back bundle. The carried trip is the core use case. Let the improvement save travel effort while trains still move bulk goods and shorten the route. A friend who has not practiced the fast line should still be useful on the ordinary path. Discovery can be a shared persistent unlock, while each player gets a safe trial and personal charge; exact unlock ownership is still a proposal for the revised exploration plan [H].

## 7. Cheap implementation, with standard-engine comparisons

The transferable engine pattern is **one controller with explicit modes, a separate camera solver and presentation driven by state**:

| Engine evidence [V]                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | What Boogle should take [H]                                                        | Where Boogle differs [C/H]                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Unity Character Controller](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-CharacterController.html) gives scripted capsule movement, slopes/steps and collision; it does not automatically respond to forces. Its docs point to a Rigidbody when physics response is wanted. | Authored movement and forgiving contact rules are normal, not failures of realism. | Retain Boogle's dynamic Rapier body for existing toy and deck interactions; do not migrate controllers just to add boost.                |
| [Unreal Character Movement](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/understanding-networked-movement-in-the-character-movement-component-for-unreal-engine) dispatches movement modes and includes a specific prediction/replication framework.                        | Select the active movement rule once; make entry/exit behavior explicit.           | Boogle uses Owner physics and smoothed presence, not Unreal's predicted server character. No networking rewrite belongs in this feature. |
| [Godot CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html) provides script-driven movement, wall/slope detection and moving-platform options.                                                                                                      | Treat floor, wall and platform-relative movement as distinct cases.                | Boogle already has support-frame carry; boost must cooperate with it rather than add another support calculation.                        |

Implement eventually through [player state](../../src/entities/player/state.ts) and the [physics facade](../../src/physics/engine.ts), using the existing fixed-step schedule. **External takeover → hook → glide → boosted/ordinary locomotion** is a useful precedence; boost is a speed modifier to eligible ground locomotion, not another body, frame loop or teleport tween. Sounds, trails and the device are presentation entities; new reusable controller helpers stay outside `src/features/` so features do not import each other [H].

[Rapier's CCD documentation](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies/#continuous-collision-detection) explains motion sweeps and their cost [V]. At 14 m/s the player moves **0.233 m per 60 Hz step**, at 16 **0.267 m** [D]. Neither being below the sphere radius nor having CCD proves collision correctness. Thin seams, initial overlaps and moving decks remain cases to exercise. The current `sweepSphere` is **static-only**, separates thin geometry, skips initial penetration, and caches one shape radius; do not advertise it as a general player sweep. Alternating camera-sized and body-sized calls can also recreate its cached WASM shape. Extend/reuse queries deliberately if needed; do not add several casts per player per frame without measuring [C/H].

Keep collisions loaded along the **possible next route**, including jump and glide departures. A starting residency lead is `speed × measured load latency + braking distance + margin`: with assumed **0.5 s** latency, 14 m/s, 1.4 m braking and 5 m margin, about **13.4 m**; with an assumed 2 s latency it becomes **34.4 m** [D]. Those are illustrative assumptions, not browser measurements. Readable routes already want 21 m sight distance. Have a loading failure stop admission to a new area safely; do not discover missing collision beneath a boosted player. Do not build a new streamer for the first resident greybox.

**State split [H]:** charge and controller are Owner/local-authority; device visuals, wind, camera and particles are Local; a permanent shared discovery uses the existing persistent-world rule recipe when promoted. The prototype stays isolated. Use one registry-checked descriptive presence field for boost presentation, returning `null` when inactive. Reuse existing 20 Hz pose and **120 ms** interpolation; at 14 m/s that delay represents **1.68 m** [C/D]. Sample trail origins and mode at the pose's interpolation time, not one fresh mode against an old position. Never send particles or another position stream. Clear views on missing fields, leave and teardown; validate all wire values.

**Controls [H]:** boost needs an explicit registered action, usable while steering and looking, with hold/toggle equivalents and a touch target around **56 CSS px**. Run the registry at implementation time; the previous ability report already proposed using both shoulders, so do not quietly claim one again. If no pad slot is free, resolve sprint/boost as one intentional registered control or revise the layout before building. Touch's existing automatic sprint is not permission to spend charge automatically. Use `wrapAction` for an existing action and clear active boost on focus loss, `pointercancel` and modal ownership. This report does not commit an unverified spare button.

**First budget [H]:** no new post pass, light, texture or dependency; reuse the existing emitter and audio context, share materials and keep transient vectors. Aim for **≤0.2 ms additional CPU and ≤0.3 ms GPU** at the M1 Max test resolution, then measure rather than report these as achieved. Start with fewer remote particles/audio layers on the M4 iPad Pro; do not reduce input responsiveness. The existing four-step catch-up cap makes hitch recovery worth testing even at a moderate speed. A new physics rate or global CCD-policy change would be a separate architecture decision.

## 9. The smallest honest comparison and evidence gaps

The owner gets one line: **“You found a speed device; moving through the island charges short bursts for the familiar trip home.”** One gut question: **“Did getting there feel so good that you wanted to take the trip again?”**

Agents first compare controller-only variants, then add FOV, then sound/trails, so a sensory effect cannot hide poor handling. Include a full back bundle, a narrow path with a visible slow option, a missed landing, reverse input, an empty tank, repeated jump/glide toggles, a wall scrape, a moving-deck departure, two arrivals and keyboard/pad/two-thumb touch. Check that charged travel remains optional and that neither train carry nor held input against a wall fills the meter. With charge banking, waiting for a friend should change nothing.

Use the existing headless course/replay for changes to player physics, within its documented limits: it does not reproduce every POI collider, browser input path or remote player. Open the `look` stills and watch a real-time recording of the same action. Verify advancing WebGPU frames and no graphics/page errors. Use existing performance tools only with the shared GPU free when comparing timings; record device, resolution and effects. Run normal required checks when implementing, and update the game-as-built docs then. This research alone finishes none of round 4's research-and-pictures or exploring checkboxes.
