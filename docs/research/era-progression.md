# Era progression: making technological progress readable at a glance

*Research report, 1 November 2026. How city builders, strategy games, factory games and live-service worlds show that a world has moved into a new era, and what that means for Boogle's eight eras (wood age → steam wagons → iron and coal → steel and harbour → diesel and concrete → electric → monorail city → maglev). Sources: Anno 1800 and 117, Civilization VI and VII, Humankind, Age of Empires II and IV, Factorio, Satisfactory, Frostpunk, Valheim, The Settlers, Rise of Industry, Timberborn, Cities: Skylines, SimCity 4, Transport Fever, Kingdom Two Crowns, Minecraft, Fortnite, Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Spore, RollerCoaster Tycoon. Builds on [progression-models.md](progression-models.md) (what moves an era forward) and [communal-progression.md](communal-progression.md) (progress as world state); this report is about how an era looks, sounds and paces, not what unlocks it. A gap-fill pass on the same day added The Settlers, Rise of Industry and Valheim (see the update note below).*

> **Update, 1 November 2026 (gap-fill pass).** Added sections on Valheim (Iron Gate's per-biome template, and its CEO's admission that early biomes were "a new coat of paint"), The Settlers (busy, animated workers as the series' signature) and Rise of Industry (a timed research tree reviewers found forced). Civilization VI's per-era music is now backed by co-composer Roland Rizzo's own description, with a note that Civilization VII dropped evolving themes. Recommendations 1, 7 and 12 gained one line each from this; none was reversed.

**Markers.** *Verified*: the page was fetched and the point read there. *Secondary*: seen only in a search summary, wiki or guide, or the page refused the fetch. Quotes are only given where verified.

## Summary

1. **An era reads by shape first, colour second.** Civilization VI made each district identifiable "not just through color but through shape"; Anno 1800 says palette and material define a tier as much as its models. Boogle eras need one signature silhouette each, visible from the court.
2. **One landmark per era is the strongest single cue.** Age of Empires IV ages up by building a landmark; Satisfactory's space elevator and Frostpunk's generator are the city's visible clock. Opening day can build that landmark.
3. **Six channels, change them together:** palette (sky, fog, ground), silhouette, material, sound, vehicles and pace. A swap that changes only blocks (today's greybox) uses one of six.
4. **Sound evolves by re-arranging one theme, not replacing it.** Civilization VI's music keeps each civ's theme and adds an ensemble per era (verified, from a co-composer).
5. **Heritage is the norm in persistent worlds.** Civ VII keeps wonders and "ageless" buildings, Humankind keeps old emblematic districts, Minecraft never regenerates a chunk someone built in, Transport Fever leaves existing buildings alone. Age of Empires swaps everything, but its matches last under an hour.
6. **The reveal works when it was telegraphed.** Fortnite's meteor was long predicted before it cratered a town; Animal Crossing finishes construction overnight; Civ VII moves everyone through an age at once.
7. **Don't make eight games.** Spore's five stages had different verbs and controls and each came out shallow (Soren Johnson). Each Boogle era should add at most one verb or vehicle to haul, lay and ride.
8. **Avoid tech-tree grind by unlocking through doing.** Factorio 2.0 replaced early lab research with trigger technologies (craft or mine the thing), and Cities: Skylines 2 moved from population gates to XP with a free unlock order.
9. **Pace: fast early, scale up later, never longer.** Satisfactory's phases escalate quantities, not new rules; 1–3 evenings per era fits if later eras grow in scale, not in steps.
10. **Smallest test:** extend `?proto=era` so the swap changes sky and fog, adds one landmark and crossfades one ambience bed, then ask whether a thumbnail of the court is enough to name the era.

## Findings by source

### Anno 1800 and Anno 117 (Ubisoft Mainz)

- **Tiers are visible states of one place.** In the residential-tiers dev blog, lead artist Manuel Reinher describes the goal as watching a small farming village grow into a city with higher-tier districts, factories and cultural buildings. Game designer Christian Schneider frames tiers as the game's level-ups: each unlocks more advanced production chains and asks for more. ([Anno Union DevBlog: Residential Tiers](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-residential-tiers/), verified)
- **Palette and material count as much as models:** "the right palette of color and material also defines look and feeling of a tier" (Reinher, verified). Lower tiers keep personal, less standardised houses; progress shows most in the production buildings.
- **One footprint, many looks.** All residences were made 3×3, so a house upgrades in place and the street plan survives the tier change (verified). Upgrading needs a full house, met needs and building materials (secondary, [Anno 1800 wiki](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Population)).
- **Anno 117 branches the look.** In Albion, players choose whether a Celtic population stays Celtic or Romanises, and the choice changes buildings, production and the landscape; Celtic towns are said to read clearly differently from Latium's grids (secondary: [Anno 117 wiki, Albion](https://anno117.fandom.com/wiki/Albion), [city-layout guide](https://allthings.how/anno-117-pax-romana-city-layouts-that-actually-work/)).
- **Lesson:** upgrade in place on a fixed footprint, so the same street changes character; spend the visual budget on what the era *makes* (factories, cranes), not on houses.

### Civilization VI and VII (Firaxis)

- **Shape beats colour.** Lead producer Dennis Shirk: districts had to be "identifiable not just through color but through shape". Districts are built from pieces that come online in different eras, so bases, clutter and decals all update by era and must fit every building; Shirk calls this component pipeline the biggest technical challenge. ([Game Developer, Designing Civ VI's districts](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-civilization-vi-i-s-distinctive-districts-system), verified)
- **The map as a record.** Art director Brian Busatti wanted a cartographer's map that gets redrawn as wonders go up or tiles are pillaged; his example is a Sphinx later shown headless after a war. ([Pixel Judge, The Art of Civ VI](https://pixeljudge.com/news/the-art-of-civilization-vi/), verified)
- **Music per era.** Co-composer Roland Rizzo: in Civ VI a civ's theme was first "presented in its most basic form — usually three instruments or fewer", then "more instruments were added to the ensembles and the styles changed" ([Laced Records, the Civ VII music team](https://www.lacedrecords.co/blogs/blog/how-the-civilization-vii-music-team-created-a-world-music-masterpiece), verified). Composer Geoff Knorr adds the detail: the medieval arrangement brings a fuller civ ensemble with a viol consort; the industrial one brings in the symphony orchestra with the civ's instruments as soloists; the atomic one adds electronic and percussive elements (secondary, via search summary of an [interview with Knorr](https://harryalexandermusic.org/geoff-knorr-questions-answered/); the site no longer resolves).
- **Civ VII dropped it.** Each civ now belongs to one Age, so its theme no longer evolves; Knorr says this led to "an increased use of full ensembles specific to each tradition" (same Laced Records piece, verified). The evolving theme only works when the thing it belongs to persists through the eras, which Boogle's island does.
- **Civ VII cuts the game into Ages** because late game "is just not that fun", and more than half of Civ VI players never finished a campaign. The dev diary frames it as "history is built in layers". Everyone transitions at once; leaders, wonders and unique quarters carry over. ([Civ VII dev diary on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=steam_community_announcements&enddate=1726173950), verified)
- **Old buildings go obsolete, not away.** Earlier-age buildings keep base yields, lose adjacency, and can be overbuilt; "ageless" buildings and wonders are exempt. (Secondary: [CivFanatics, Civ VII Ages](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/).) Critics say swapping civs at each age breaks the sense of one continuous people (secondary: [Sullla](https://sullla.com/Civ7/WH.html)).
- **Lesson:** an era change is a good moment to reset pacing, but players notice when identity resets with it. Keep the place and its landmarks; change the layer on top.

### Humankind (Amplitude)

- Each culture has an emblematic district that only it can build, in its era, and which keeps working after the game moves on; a city can hold several from different cultures, so a city becomes a visible record of who built it. Destroyed ones are gone for good. (Secondary: [Humankind wiki, District](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/District).)
- Creative director Romain de Waubert counts 60 cultures over 6 eras, each with unique buildings (secondary: [Gaming Trend interview](https://gamingtrend.com/interviews/it-takes-more-than-a-village-in-depth-interview-with-studio-head-creative-director-of-humankind-romain-de-waubert/)). I found no Amplitude source on how era transitions look.
- **Lesson:** layered heritage (one signature piece per era that stays) is a stronger record of time than a whole-town swap.

### Age of Empires II and IV

- **AoE II swaps the whole building set** on reaching the Feudal and Castle Ages; a few buildings (town centre, market, university) change once more in the Imperial Age. Each civilisation's architecture set draws on its region, such as thatch roofs in the Feudal Age of the Northern European set. (Secondary: [AoE wiki, architecture set](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Architecture_set_(Age_of_Empires_II)); [The Last Chieftains art post](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/from-concept-to-final-polish-the-art-of-the-last-chieftains/).)
- **AoE IV** keeps the rule that ageing up updates the building set, and creative director Adam Isgreen promised new age-up presentation ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/age-of-empires-4/ages), verified). Most civilisations age up by building one of two landmarks, which stay on the map (secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Empires_IV)).
- **Lesson:** a full swap is right for a 40-minute match nobody returns to. The landmark that *is* the age-up is the part that transfers to a persistent world.

### Factorio (Wube)

- Space Age added **trigger technologies**: research completed by mining, crafting or launching something, instead of feeding science to labs. Early ones need only a token amount, so players earn their first unlocks without a delay, and triggers stop players researching far past what their factory can make. ([FFF #376](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-376), verified)
- The tree has been restructured several times, from a linear path to branching choices (secondary: [FFF #275](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-275)).
- **Lesson:** "you made the thing, so you've unlocked it" keeps progress inside play and out of a menu.

### Satisfactory (Coffee Stain)

- Tiers 0–2 are open from the start; every later pair of tiers is gated by a Space Elevator phase, a delivery whose quantities climb steeply (dozens, then thousands). Inside an open tier, milestones go in any order. (Secondary: [Satisfactory wiki, Space Elevator](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Space_Elevator).)
- The elevator is a building in the world, visible from kilometres away, and its Project Assembly visibly builds up as phases complete; players read it as a progress meter, but I found no developer statement saying so (secondary: wiki and community posts).
- **Lesson:** one huge, central, physical goal that grows is a clock everybody can see. Later phases ask for more of the same rather than new rules.

### Frostpunk (11 bit studios)

- The city is built in rings around one coal-fired generator, whose heat radius grows as you upgrade it; the circular layout is what the team says sets it apart (secondary: [TechRaptor feature](https://techraptor.net/gaming/features/how-11-bit-studios-created-society-survival-simulator-frostpunk), fetch refused). Snow melts to slush around it and windows glow warm against the cold (secondary: [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-industrial-apocalypse-of-frostpunk-is-more-truth-than-fiction)).
- **Lesson:** a smoky industrial era stays readable through warm light against a cold, grey ground, and through one central machine.

### Valheim (Iron Gate)

- **One template per biome.** CEO Richard Svensson: each biome gets "a boss, key items, more difficult enemies and better gear for the players", and the team tries to "create some kind of unique gameplay for each biome" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/indie-game-works-chapter), verified).
- **Reskins wore thin.** Svensson told GamesIndustry.biz that the early biomes "were basically the same thing with a new coat of paint", and that the team gave later ones (Mistlands, Ashlands) their own identity, at a production scale he calls far bigger (secondary: [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/valheim-was-released-in-early-access-because-the-studio-couldnt-afford-to-keep-working-on-it) and [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/survival/valheim-released-into-steam-early-access-because-the-survival-game-started-as-a-solo-project-and-the-dev-couldnt-afford-to-keep-working-on-it/) reporting the interview; the original wasn't fetched).
- **Gates are soft.** Senior developer Jonathan Smars: beating Yagluth "basically lets you start unlocking the Mistlands", though a player could go earlier; lead artist Robin Eyre says the team starts from "what is the feeling that we want to achieve" and wanted Mistlands to "hide beauty inside the mist, but also danger" ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/valheim-interview-the-mistlands-creation-design-process/), verified). Bosses are summoned at altars with an offering ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valheim), verified); each boss's drop opens the next tier of tools (secondary: fan guides).
- **Lesson:** a fixed per-era template (what rises, what's new to ride, what's new to make) keeps eight eras affordable, but an era that only repaints the last one reads as filler even to the developer. Each era needs its own feeling first and one thing to do that the last didn't.

### The Settlers (Blue Byte; Volker Wertich)

- **Watching workers is the point.** The remake of The Settlers II has, per tribe, "25 different types of Settlers which are fully animated in their jobs, 31 different animated buildings" (producer Thomas Friedmann, [Hooked Gamers](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2006/09/20/thomas_friedmann_on_settlers_ii_-_tng-3.html), verified). In Wertich's later Pioneers of Pagonia, "the player gives superior commands, which the various and numerous Pagonians implement, creating chain reactions"; art director Timm Schwank: "thousands of Pagonians hustling and bustling through daily life give the world a pulse" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/pioneers-of-pagonia-volker-wertich-timm-schwank-interview/), verified). The German word for it, *Wuselfaktor* ("bustle factor"), is the series' signature (the writer's gloss).
- Settlers 7 upgrades residences in place, twice, once the right perk is unlocked (secondary: [Steam forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/48210/discussions/0/1741105170031732189/)).
- **Lesson:** the "pace" channel can be literal bustle: more figures, wagons and crates moving in later eras reads as progress without any new building. Crates riding trains (decided) is Boogle's version.

### Rise of Industry (Dapper Penguin)

- Progress runs through a research tree that costs money and time; you start with three free unlocks, and a chosen specialism earns more research points (secondary: [Indie Game Website guide](https://www.indiegamewebsite.com/2019/05/02/rise-of-industry-guide-beginners-tips-tricks/), [Player.One](https://www.player.one/rise-industry-review-industrialization-its-finest-125959) and [OnRPG](https://www.onrpg.com/news/editorial/rise-of-industry-review/) reviews, via search summaries). Reviewers say the tree still pushes one order, and research times vary oddly (one cites beef stew at 540 in-game days against 60 for a train depot). I found no developer statement on how the game shows time passing.
- **Lesson:** a counter-example. A timed tree in a menu is the version of progress the Factorio and Cities: Skylines 2 changes moved away from, and the one rec 9 avoids.

### Timberborn (Mechanistry)

- "Lumberpunk": timber becomes ever more elaborate machinery, from water wheels and lumber mills to engines and pumps, with metal only for the most advanced structures (secondary: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1062090/Timberborn/)). The 1.0 visual pass reduced the number of materials on some buildings (secondary: [1.0 patch notes](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/22280927/)).
- **Lesson:** a wood age can carry "technology" convincingly through moving parts (wheels, gears, belts) before any metal appears.

### Cities: Skylines, SimCity 4, Transport Fever

- **Cities: Skylines** gated 13 milestones on population; the sequel moved to 20 XP-driven milestones and lets players choose their unlock order, so they don't need a huge city or an "unlock all" mod (secondary: [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/cities-skylines-2-doesnt-solely-rely-on-population-numbers-for-progression/)).
- **SimCity 4** has building styles modelled on 1890s Chicago, 1940s New York, 1990s Houston and a contemporary European set; which appears depends on wealth and road type, not a clock (secondary: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2013/03/14/antique_code_show_simcity_4/), [EA forum](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/simcity-franchise-discussion-en/how-do-i-set-the-building-style/10367469)).
- **Transport Fever** grows towns in era sets (wood and stone, then concrete and render, then modern), and a change of era affects only new buildings, so old quarters survive (secondary: [era filter mod](https://transportfever2mods.com/town-building-filter-era-mixed-buildings-mod/)).
- **Lesson:** real-looking cities mix eras. A town where only new construction follows the era looks older and richer than one that swaps wholesale.

### Kingdom Two Crowns (noio, Raw Fury)

- Walls go dirt mound → barricade → wood → stone → iron; upgrading the town centre to stone or iron upgrades the innermost walls for free (secondary: [Kingdom wiki, Walls](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Walls?oldid=8079)).
- **Lesson:** the material ladder (wood, stone, iron) is legible with no text, and a free upgrade of what you already built makes the era change feel like a gift, not a chore.

### Minecraft (Mojang)

- When Caves & Cliffs changed world generation, only new chunks and chunks players had seen but not built in were affected; chunks with player building were left alone and the terrain at the border was blended to fit (secondary: [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/minecraft-worlds-will-blend-when-new-world-generation-arrives/1100-6492413/), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/minecraft-java-edition-21w43a-snapshot)).
- **Lesson:** a world update must never overwrite something a player made.

### Fortnite (Epic)

- The Season 4 meteor was "long-predicted", then cratered Dusty Depot into Dusty Divot, which kept two of the original hangars ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/article/44363-fortnite-season-4-dusty-divot-meteor-impact-superheroes), verified). The Kevin cube rolled across the map for weeks, changing places as it went (secondary: [Upcomer](https://upcomer.com/fortnites-cube-kevin-wrecks-part-of-tilted-towers-transforms-loot-lake)). Chapter 1 ended with a black hole and roughly 37 hours of downtime before a new map (secondary: [VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/news/fortnite-black-hole-event/)).
- Former director Donald Mustard on live events: "This is actually the magic." They faded because they took so many people and so much time. ([Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/fortnite-donald-mustard-interview/), verified)
- **Lesson:** weeks of visible build-up make a change land; keeping a recognisable piece of the old place (the hangars) makes it read as the same place changed. Hand-made events are expensive, so a small team needs a template.

### Animal Crossing: New Horizons (Nintendo)

- Director Aya Kyogoku: players start on a blank deserted island, new residents and facilities arrive as they progress, and "you can enjoy the phase before the place becomes a village" ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-devs-on-the-decision-to-start-on-an-island-new-features-multiplayer-more/), verified).
- Nook's Cranny upgrades after roughly a month of play, closing for a day of renovation first (secondary: [Nintendo Wire guide](https://nintendowire.com/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons/unlock-and-upgrade-nooks-cranny/)).
- **Lesson:** the before-state (tent, camp) is its own era worth enjoying, and a short closure followed by a reveal is a cheap, beloved before/after moment.

### Spore (Maxis)

- Soren Johnson, who worked on it: each of the five stages had different controls, interfaces, verbs and goals, and "making five different games at once is a bad idea" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/spore-my-view-of-the-elephant), verified).
- **Lesson:** the strongest warning for an eight-era game. Change what the world looks like, not how the player plays.

### RollerCoaster Tycoon (Chris Sawyer)

- Each scenario starts with a few ride types; more rides and scenery arrive as funded research completes, and players can steer research by category (secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RollerCoaster_Tycoon_(video_game))). I found no design rationale from Sawyer.
- **Lesson:** new things arriving one at a time, each placed and watched, keeps a long progression feeling fresh without eras at all.

## What this means for Boogle

These are recommendations, not decisions; the design record ([boogle-2.md](../proposals/boogle-2.md)) owns the picks. The greybox era swap (`?proto=era`, `src/pois/era/`) is the place to test most of them.

1. **Give every era a kit across six channels, not just new blocks.** Palette (sky, fog, ground tint), silhouette, material, sound, vehicle and pace. A first cut for Boogle:

   | Era | Silhouette | Material | Sound | Pace |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Wood age | low huts, A-frames | timber, rope, thatch | hand tools, creaks, gulls | slow, hand-pushed |
   | Steam wagons | first chimney, water tower | timber with iron bands | chuff, whistle, oompah brass | chugging |
   | Iron and coal | headframe, tall stacks | riveted iron, brick, soot | anvils, coal chutes | busy, smoky |
   | Steel and harbour | gantry crane, ship funnels | steel girders | foghorn, crane winch | heavy, rhythmic |
   | Diesel and concrete | silo, flat roofs | concrete, corrugated iron | diesel rattle, horns | faster, louder |
   | Electric | pylons, lit windows | glass, enamel signs | hum, tram bell, clean air | bright, quick |
   | Monorail city | pillars, tall blocks | glass, white panels | swoosh, chimes | flowing |
   | Maglev | thin spans | chrome, light | near silence, whoosh | very fast, quiet |

   Valheim is the warning case: its CEO calls the early biomes "a new coat of paint". Each row needs a feeling and a new thing to do, not only a new palette.

   *Smallest prototype:* add a per-era sky and fog preset and one ambience bed to the greybox swap; take a court screenshot of each era at thumbnail size and check whether the eras are told apart without the blocks' colours.

2. **One landmark per era, seen from the court, and opening day builds it.** This is the AoE IV landmark, Satisfactory's elevator and Frostpunk's generator in one. The landmark should also be the tallest new silhouette, so a returning player sees the era change before they reach it. *Smallest prototype:* in `blocks.ts`, give era 2 one tall landmark (a water tower or clock tower) and check the default spawn camera frames it.

3. **Shape first, then colour.** Following Civ VI, each era's pieces should be told apart in silhouette alone (roof pitch, chimneys, cranes, pylons). Industrial smoke turns colour to grey, so shape has to carry eras 3–5. *Smallest prototype:* render the court in flat grey (no colour) for eras 1 and 2 and see if they still read.

4. **Keep heritage: upgrade the generic layer, keep the signature layer.** Generic fill (huts, fences, lamps) can upgrade in place, as Anno houses and Kingdom walls do. Anything a player built and every past landmark stays, as Civ VII wonders, Humankind districts and Minecraft's built chunks do. The heritage loco (already decided) is the moving version of the same idea. Note: today the greybox swap sinks all era-1 huts. *Smallest prototype:* leave one era-1 hut and the helper hut from `?proto=build` standing amid era 2, and ask whether they read as history or as leftovers.

5. **Change new building more than old.** Transport Fever and SimCity look lived-in because eras mix. Consider applying a new era's style first to what is built in that era and to a ring around the new landmark, while older streets catch up later (or never). This also spreads the art budget. *Smallest prototype:* swap only the blocks within a radius of the landmark and compare with the full swap in one sitting.

6. **Make the reveal a before/after you can see, then remember.** Three beats from the sources: build-up you can watch over the era (Fortnite's meteor, the next island visible across the water, the archipelago decision), a short closure or countdown (Animal Crossing's renovation day, the bell), and the change spreading from a centre (the ripple already in the prototype). Then keep the before: Civ VI's map shows history, so a "before" postcard next to the "after" makes the change land for people who missed it. *Smallest prototype:* when the bell starts, grab a still of the court from a fixed camera; after the ripple, show it beside a new still on a paper card.

7. **Sound changes by arrangement, not replacement.** Follow Civ VI (Rizzo, Knorr): one island motif, re-orchestrated per era (whistled and plucked, then brass and chuff, then anvil and foghorn, then clean electric). The wagon's clack and the train's rhythm can quicken by era, so pace is heard as well as seen. *Smallest prototype:* two ambience beds (wood, steam) that crossfade at the swap; judge with the screen off.

8. **Don't make eight games.** Spore's lesson. Keep haul, lay and ride as the verbs throughout; each era adds at most one new verb or vehicle, and the new vehicle is the era's most visible upgrade. *Smallest prototype:* none needed; a review rule for each era's spec ("what does the player do here that they couldn't before? one thing").

9. **Unlock by doing, inside the world, never in a tree menu.** Factorio's trigger technologies and Cities: Skylines 2's free order both point to the same rule: making or delivering the thing is the unlock, and within an era the order is the player's. This matches [progression-models.md](progression-models.md)'s "any K of M" wants, so it is a constraint on that design rather than a new one. *Smallest prototype:* a debug list of what the current era wants, generated from world state, with no tree UI, to check it reads as a to-do list and not a grind.

10. **Pace: grow quantity and scale, not steps.** For 1–3 evenings per era, keep the number of distinct asks per era flat and let later eras grow in scale (longer lines, bigger builds, a new island), as Satisfactory's phases do. Make era 1 quick, so the first opening day happens in the first evening (Factorio starts with token-cost unlocks for the same reason). *Smallest prototype:* time a solo player from a fresh room to the era-2 opening day in the greybox; under one evening is the target.

11. **Goofy, smoky, still readable.** Frostpunk keeps grime readable with warm windows against cold ground. For eras 3–5: chunky, cartoon smoke puffs (particles, Local), saturated accent colours on signs and bunting, warm light at dusk; then the electric era's "clear air" as one of the biggest reveals of the game (fog and haze drop, sky turns blue). *Smallest prototype:* a haze-density and sky-tint slider pair in `?proto=look`, set for "iron and coal" and "electric", and A/B them on the same view.

12. **Ceremonies from a template, not hand-made.** Mustard's point that live events are the magic but cost too much applies to eight opening days. Keep the ceremony as data (who, where, what rises, which sound) so each era fills in a table, as `blocks.ts` already does for blocks. Valheim's per-biome template (a boss, key items, harder enemies, better gear) is the same idea for content. *Smallest prototype:* move the bell, ripple speed and sound cue for era 2 into the same data table as its blocks.
