# Beautiful corner craft: a small, polished part that predicts the game

_Research report, 2 November 2026. How to take one already promising Boogle job through composition, lighting, art, motion, sound, play and production validation quickly. Studies Nintendo, A Short Hike, Journey, Tiny Glade, Firewatch, Hollow Knight, Astro Bot, Uncharted, Valve and vertical-slice practice. Ends with numbered recommendations and a pass plan for one gorge station and one track-laying machine. Recommendations only; this report authorizes no implementation or change to the pending rail-slice plan._

**Evidence.** **[V]** means developer text, interview, paper or slides fetched and read. **[A]** means only the official talk abstract was accessible; the recording was not watched. **[S]** means secondary reporting. **[D]** is a deduction for Boogle. **[H]** is an untested hypothesis, including proposed timeboxes and acceptance thresholds. Firewatch's original slides were downloaded, their text inspected, and slides 58–61 rendered and viewed. Other linked videos were not watched. Repository observations are from `6d24bcd0`; no game, device benchmark or new playtest was run for this report.

## The answer and the starting point

**Build a quality target around one complete, repeatable action, then prove its production method on a second arrangement.** The best early returns should come from a clear purpose, convincing contact, broad composition and coherent light. Materials and details reinforce those decisions. Motion and sound start as rough feedback immediately and receive their final pass together. More effects cannot compensate for an unreadable delivery, a slippery deck or an attractive scene that is exhausting to traverse. This ordering is a Boogle hypothesis, not a universal ranking established by the sources.

The binding principles are [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md): **Toys before chores**, **Telegraph everything**, **Cooperation is automatic**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, **Leave something for the next visitor**, and **Small and readable**. The owner's [decision log](../proposals/boogle-2.md) already selects the sunlit, toy-like Mario Sunshine direction, the crossing references and the place look without GTAO. Keep the approved camera and Buddy; a polish pass is not permission to reopen character design, introduce a new progression system or replace the art direction.

The inputs already contain unusually useful evidence:

- [Finding the fun](finding-the-fun.md) and [the current prototype plan](../plans/find-the-fun.md) put the enjoyable verb before expansion. Place's recorded material-to-track verdict is a reason to polish that action; it is not evidence for hours of repeated hauling.
- [Look pass](../plans/look-pass.md) records owner approval of the look experiment. Its light/kit/material/ground percentages are the builder's subjective attribution, not measured causal contributions. Its Mac figures are historical measurements of that experiment, not an iPad budget. Some comparison shots froze the machine and restored logs: useful art comparisons, insufficient play evidence.
- [Place as built](../game-design/place.md) explicitly describes a **local** prototype. [`index.ts`](../../src/proto/place/index.ts) saves its progression locally. Two tabs seeing similar machinery would not prove shared contributions or persistence. The documented cradle collision exception also remains a concrete acceptance risk.
- [Bang-for-buck aesthetics](bang-for-buck-aesthetics.md) supplies techniques, but its older direction proposals do not override the newer owner verdict. [Visual validation](visual-validation.md) demonstrates why a successful screenshot command or an independent rAF counter cannot establish a working, smooth game.
- [Rail slice](../plans/rail-slice.md) is a draft awaiting approval. This report can inform its art and production gates; it does not approve or silently implement its shared-state work.

## What the precedents actually establish

### Nintendo: test interactions cheaply, then make a compact place interdependent

**[V]** In the Skyward Sword Iwata Asks discussion, Aonuma describes concentrating development in four broad areas instead of expanding the map. Leaders including sound met regularly; changing one element could require reconsidering terrain and surrounding encounters. Density came from coordinated relationships within those places. This is evidence about **Skyward Sword**, not a claim that Breath of the Wild had a small world. [Nintendo: The Secret to Extreme Density](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Skyward-Sword/Vol-5-The-Dense-Sky-and-Town/4-The-Secret-to-Extreme-Density/4-The-Secret-to-Extreme-Density-204520.html).

**[A/S]** Nintendo's GDC session covers breaking Zelda conventions; contemporary reporting and reprinted Fujibayashi interview answers describe an early top-down, 2D-looking prototype and building the Great Plateau first. The original IGN interview was inaccessible here, so those particular details remain secondary evidence, not independently inspected footage. [Official GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024562/Change-and-Constant-Breaking-Conventions), [reprinted developer answers](https://nintendoeverything.com/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-devs-on-starting-out-with-10-people-showing-miyamoto-the-2d-prototype-great-plateau-made-first-more/).

**[D]** For Boogle, make one relationship richer: a grove supplies a frame, the frame feeds a machine, the machine makes a crossing, and the crossing changes travel. Density is how often the same objects become meaningful from a new position or state. Extra bushes and collectibles are not a substitute. The early experiment and the polished place answer different questions; keep their evidence separate.

### A Short Hike: a coherent constraint makes finishing possible

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu explains designing a small island that rewards wandering off its main route. Its low-resolution presentation informed simplified shading and outlines; Mark Sparling's music changes with location and movement. The constraint shaped the entire experience rather than being applied as a filter afterward. [Developer retrospective](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/). **[A]** The GDC abstract identifies a four-month deadline for the initial release; it is not a measure of all later development or a forecast for Boogle. [Crafting a Tiny Open World](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026613/).

**[D]** Bound one self-directed loop with a useful overlook and a return path. Use Boogle's approved chunky forms and material families consistently. Do not add pixelation or a mountain of optional content because those belonged to another game's successful constraint.

### Journey: polish serves an emotion and a social contract

**[V]** In a pre-release interview, Jenova Chen describes how removing the lobby implied joining at any time and allowing solo completion; competitive resource behavior conflicted with the intended relationship between players. Kellee Santiago's retrospective emphasizes movement, proximity and spatial rhythm as communication. These support emotional and social design choices, not specific final control details from the pre-release interview. [Chen interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2011/01/11/jenova-chen-explains-journey-social-relevance-and-artistic-inspirations/), [Santiago retrospective](https://www.kelleesantiago.com/selectedworks/journey).

**[A]** The Journey design and sand-rendering abstracts describe years of iteration. A short, visually restrained game can still contain expensive bespoke work. [Designing Journey](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018751/Designing), [Sand Rendering in Journey](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018864/Sand-Rendering-in).

**[D]** The target feeling here is shared pride in an understandable machine making a beautiful crossing. Preserve control and room for a friend throughout the reveal. Do not use a solo cinematic, resource race or camera takeover to manufacture the feeling the actual co-op interaction must deliver.

### Tiny Glade: responsiveness is authored, and procedural does not mean effortless

**[V]** Anastasia Opara and Tomasz Stachowiak describe repeated interaction-design iterations, authored procedural rules and learning from players repurposing tools. Their examples include users seeking openings or supports through tools designed for something else, prompting changes to the interaction. The visual craft depends on rules that handle combinations gracefully, not one general algorithm automatically creating beauty. [Developer interview](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games).

**[D]** A station kit should resolve joins, supports and material travel predictably. Test its awkward edges: half-full, both players delivering, slopes, reverse views and a person standing where a piece lands. Use code to assemble a deliberately limited vocabulary. A universal building generator or a new global illumination renderer would expand the research problem rather than finish this corner.

### Firewatch: composition and an artist-friendly palette precede fine detail

**[V]** Jane Ng's original GDC slides show the production division of work, sky as a large share of the image, versatile modular rocks, and the importance of settling the player experience before expensive environment work. Slides 58–60 compare a rough lit scene, a paintover and the in-game result; the large foreground framing and depth relationships carry the image through the stages. Slide 61 compares tree silhouettes. The rendered pages make the shape and value changes visible; they do not establish the time each stage took. [The Art of Firewatch, especially slides 31, 53, 58–61 and 69–72](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Ng_Jane_The_Art_Of.pdf).

**[V]** Campo Santo graphics programmer Paolo's sky article describes choosing direct artistic color controls after observing Olly Moss paint, rather than making artists derive their palette through physical atmospheric parameters. [Campo Santo's procedural sky](https://blog.camposanto.com/post/112703721804/this-blog-post-is-an-in-detail-explanation-of-part).

**[D]** First control Boogle's sky, distance, cliff masses and the machine's contrast against them. Make a few meaningful palette and shape parameters easy to adjust. A paintover may diagnose a composition; acceptance still happens inside the normal camera. Preserve the difference between a reference painting and the rendered result.

### Hollow Knight: rough navigation, cohesive assets and cuts beat endless preplanning

**[V]** Team Cherry describe building spaces with simple tiles, playing them, adding art and interactive detail, and adjusting as those layers expose issues. They also discuss cutting a large area and concentrating on useful connections. Their recalled room-production timings are anecdotes, not comparable productivity benchmarks. [Developer map-design interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map/). Unity's case study describes a deliberately straightforward asset and lighting approach supporting the hand-drawn work. [Hollow Knight production case study](https://unity.com/made-with-unity/hollow-knight).

**[D]** Do not perfect every greybox dimension before seeing the actual silhouettes and contact. Resolve traversal first, then allow bounded geometry revisions during the art pass. A consistent kit can make a short route feel authored without making every rock unique. A missing useful connection is a larger defect than a missing decorative layer.

### Astro Bot: production starts after a toy works, but play keeps changing the art

**[V]** Nicolas Doucet describes focused brainstorming, storyboards, then gameplay prototypes taking days or weeks without finished art. Production brings multiple disciplines together; a prototype dragon boss became a gorilla as the theme developed, while gameplay continued to be adjusted through production. Art, animation, effects and sound contribute to the final interaction. [Japan Studio's production account](https://blog.playstation.com/2019/05/16/how-japan-studio-assembled-astro-bot-rescue-mission/).

**[D]** Keep a playable machine throughout the passes. Start with rough mechanical poses and contact sounds, improve the shell around its working envelope, and retest the action after the change. A finished model delivered before anyone checks how the feeder and the rider fit is a poor handoff.

### Uncharted: the blockmesh carries a spatial contract across specialists

**[V]** Anthony Vaccaro's Madagascar breakdown explicitly follows a section from designer blockmesh to shipped environment and credits collaborators in design, lighting, shaders and effects. Andres Rodriguez's Tower Bottom breakdown similarly distinguishes world building, lighting and final set-dressing polish. These are developer portfolio descriptions; the linked time-lapse recordings were not watched here, and no studio-wide pass duration is inferred. [Madagascar breakdown](https://autocon.artstation.com/projects/l4wLo), [Tower Bottom breakdown](https://arodz3d.artstation.com/projects/XDwny).

**[D]** Keep dimensions, travel lines, standing surfaces, sightlines and interactions as an explicit contract between Boogle's code and Blender kit. Art may improve them, but an altered platform edge requires collision and movement review. Attractive geometry is not evidence that the gameplay envelope survived.

### Valve: small multidisciplinary groups need shared judgment and real observation

**[V]** Ken Birdwell's cabal account describes an early Half-Life with impressive individual pieces that failed as a coherent game. A small group combined promising working ideas into one level, cut what failed and used the result to establish a target. They emphasized density paced by player movement, rather than a relentless timed sequence, and acknowledgment that left visible evidence of the player's action. The later cabal brought disciplines together around particular areas and recorded both design and layout. [The Cabal](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-cabal-valve-s-design-process-for-creating-i-half-life-i-?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).

**[V]** Valve's Half-Life 2 design slides describe chapter teams within global constraints, cross-team work on shared problems, and late attention to overall pacing and quality. Playtests put creators in front of players without explaining the intended solution. [GDC 2006 slides, especially 9 and 20–26](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2006/GDC2006_HL2DesignProcess.pdf). Mike Ambinder's slides frame design ideas as hypotheses, warn about observer bias and verbal interference, and distinguish aggregate statistics from individual behavior. [Valve's Approach to Playtesting](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf).

**[D]** For coding agents, borrow the integrated judgment, not the meeting count. One integrator owns the playable composition and accepts combined changes. A beautiful lighting branch and a beautiful material branch can produce an overbright game together. Owner observation supplies taste and feel; builders supply edge-case coverage. Neither substitutes for the other.

### Polish talks and visual research: hierarchy, timing and restraint

**[A/V]** The official _Juice It or Lose It_ abstract describes adding response to a simple game; its authors' public demo repository makes the layered experiment inspectable. That is evidence for comparative iteration, not a requirement to add every available effect. [GDC session](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose-It), [authors' demo](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout). **[A]** Joonas Turner's sound-design session connects audio decisions with gameplay and production; no precise timing prescription is attributed to an unviewed talk. [Oh My! That Sound Made the Game Feel Better!](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022808/Oh-My-That-Sound-Made).

**[V]** Valve's Team Fortress 2 paper emphasizes recognizable silhouettes, controlled detail frequency and separation of characters from environments. [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf). **[D]** Apply the hierarchy, not its specific shading: the station's receiving mouth and the moving material deserve more contrast than background cliff grain. Buddy's rejected rim light stays rejected.

The local [game-feel skill](../../.claude/skills/game-feel/SKILL.md) is useful for discrete response, importance tiers and returning to rest. Its generic hit-stop examples do **not** authorize stopping Boogle's world, network ticks or physics. For this machine, settle and recoil belong on cosmetic parts. Steady travel should remain steady; universal easing can make an apparently heavy machine surge and drift.

## What “polished” should mean in this corner

**[D]** Polish is consistent agreement between what a player sees, expects, does, hears and receives. Use observable criteria rather than an effects score. A failed contact or lost contribution cannot be averaged away by excellent lighting.

| Dimension           | A player can name the quality                                  | Builder's acceptance check                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purpose             | “I can see what needs my logs and what it will make.”          | Approach in the normal camera; receiving frame, parked machine and unfinished crossing read without debug labels or explanation.                                           |
| Composition         | “My eye goes to the machine and across the gorge.”             | Inspect approach, feeding side, aboard, opposite bank and view back; background detail does not compete with the work.                                                     |
| Shape and scale     | “It looks like a chunky machine I can stand on.”               | Silhouette works small on the iPad; deck, feeder, rails and Buddy have a coherent size relationship. No camera change used to hide scale trouble.                          |
| Material identity   | “Those are the logs I delivered becoming sleepers.”            | Empty → partial → full → feeding → laying → finished remains legible. No unexplained whole-pile disappearance or material teleport during the key handoff.                 |
| Contact             | “It sits on the ground; I stand where the picture says I can.” | Walk, jump, ride and leave each solid-looking working surface; inspect supports, wheel/deck contact and rail joins in motion.                                              |
| Response            | “The action happens when I do it.”                             | Input, accepted delivery, visible movement and contact sound agree. Rejected/blocked action gives truthful feedback; idle parts settle.                                    |
| Temporal continuity | “I can follow the work without losing my place.”               | Uncut ordinary play shows no pose snaps, popping kit, disappearing rails, texture swimming or camera obstruction; turning away and back preserves the state.               |
| Sound hierarchy     | “I hear wood, steel and the machine finishing.”                | Contact beats are distinct from ambience; repeated deliveries are tolerable, concurrent players do not produce a wall of sounds. Muted play remains understandable.        |
| Freedom and comfort | “I can watch, help, ride or walk away.”                        | Touch and desktop controls work; free camera and input remain available; no mandatory shake, cutscene or precision landing.                                                |
| Co-op truth         | “My friend's work is here, and mine stays.”                    | Two actual clients contribute, join mid-build, leave and return without lost/duplicated material or a restarted completed stretch. Local-only target cannot pass this row. |
| Technical finish    | “It stays clear and responsive on my device.”                  | Real target-device play, cold entry, worst working state and sustained run; completed frames advance, WebGPU works, no page/console graphics errors.                       |
| Production finish   | “We know how to make the next place.”                          | Rebuild the kit from source and fit it to a second arrangement; record time, rework, custom parts and cost changes.                                                        |

Decorative motion comes after the action reads. One tree's breeze, a small exhaust beat or a worker reacting to a real delivery can help; three unrelated effects drawing attention away from the feeder are regression. Remove a layer as a comparison before adding another.

## Bound the slice by a complete job and representative risks

Use **timber line Station 2**: [`LINKS` in layout.ts](../../src/proto/place/layout.ts) currently runs from chainage 116 to 62, a **54 m stretch**, asks for 24 logs and lies across the gorge. `TUNE.laySpeed` is 5 m/s, so travel alone is **10.8 s**, derived from those numbers; feeding, startup and finishing are additional. Keep these existing values for the first comparison. They are not newly validated pacing targets.

The polished footprint includes the last approach bend, the nearby rim grove, the receiving frame and feeder, the machine, the complete working stretch, and enough of both banks to judge landing and the return view. Include the visible gorge walls beneath it: an attractive rim with broken side faces would fail normal play. Define the boundary in a top-down capture before work; do not commit to an arbitrary square area before seeing the actual sightlines.

Keep surrounding routes functional but visibly unfinished where necessary. Ordinary movement must reach and leave the corner; a player may look behind the camera or go under the bridge. No locked beauty camera, disabled turning, invisible wall disguising unfinished geometry, or compulsory cinematic. One iteration preset may start with Station 1 complete and Station 2 empty; label that fixture. Final acceptance also includes arriving through the real upstream path without skip actions.

The immediate payoff is a traversable span and the machine reaching its next stop. Keep the existing downstream line/cargo/forge connection functional and verify it once when completing the line; there is no need to art-pass the entire village to prove this station. Do not make Station 2 launch a cargo train early merely to give the demo a finale.

There are two honest milestones:

1. **Quality target:** one real, freely playable corner reaches the desired look and feel inside the local prototype. It answers whether this craft direction is worth extending and exposes costs. It may be valuable on its own.
2. **Production vertical slice:** the same action uses the approved production route for shared material, build progress, collision, join/return and asset delivery. It also passes the repeatability experiment below. This answers whether Boogle can produce and operate more of it.

The first does not imply the second. Keep this boundary visible in the review card and completion report. Adding the required shared path is real work, subject to the approved scope of [rail slice](../plans/rail-slice.md), not a last-minute polish checkbox.

Common traps and the countermeasure:

| Trap                                                                  | Countermeasure for Boogle                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A marketing scene with exceptional one-off assets                     | Count every custom part and exception; reuse the station kit in a second arrangement.                         |
| A perfect first loop hides the grind                                  | Replay deliveries and the return trip without presentation skips; record waiting and carrying separately.     |
| Parallel craft changes silently break each other                      | One palette/camera/state baseline and one integrated playable checkpoint per pass.                            |
| Expensive setup is mistaken for per-corner cost, or excluded entirely | Report setup, reusable production, integration, rework and owner time separately.                             |
| The prettiest case becomes the performance budget                     | Exercise feeding, track growth, riders, particles, shadows and first asset use together on the target device. |
| Prototype shortcuts become permanent architecture                     | Keep the experiment isolated; promote only the required behavior through existing entity and sync contracts.  |

**[V]** Rami Ismail distinguishes prototypes that answer whether to make a game from vertical slices that test whether it can be produced. He recommends making a second representative thing after the first to expose the production rate once tools and setup exist. [Prototypes and Vertical Slice](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/prototypes-and-vertical-slice/). **[D]** The proposed second arrangement is a cost experiment, not authorization to expand the world or add a second polished machine permanently.

## Fast craft with agents: constrain the handoffs, inspect the combined game

**[H]** Agents should be effective at bounded geometry scripts, material variants, repetitive kit placement, deterministic bookkeeping, collecting captures and checking explicit edge cases. There is no controlled evidence in the sources that agents autonomously judge taste or game feel as well as a director, or that code generation makes total production proportional to generation time. Log the actual results rather than claiming that premise as established.

Give each pass a compact contract: intended feeling, unchanged interaction dimensions, owned files, representative states/views, and an observable rejection condition. Keep one integrator responsible for the integrated result. If later work is delegated, separate genuinely independent outputs, such as kit geometry and a source audit; serialize changes to layout, palette, shared material helpers and machine timing. Git merging successfully does not mean two changes compose visually.

Use the existing division: [`assets/src/place-kit.py`](../../assets/src/place-kit.py) owns reusable shapes; [`look-kit.ts`](../../src/proto/place/look-kit.ts) assembles the kit; [`look.ts`](../../src/proto/place/look.ts) controls the experiment's material/light treatment; [`rail.ts`](../../src/proto/place/rail.ts), [`stations.ts`](../../src/proto/place/stations.ts) and [`pieces.ts`](../../src/proto/place/pieces.ts) connect shapes to the action. Preserve meaningful names, metres, origins and attachment points. Rebuilding through the [asset pipeline](../plans/asset-pipeline.md) must reproduce the checked output.

This division has standard-engine precedents: **[V]** [Unity prefabs](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/working-with-gameobjects/prefabs) package reusable object/component hierarchies; [Unreal level instances](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/level-instancing-in-unreal-engine) reuse actor arrangements, with packed variants for static setups; [Godot PackedScene instantiation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/nodes_and_scene_instances.html) creates reusable node hierarchies. **[D]** Boogle's small kit plus placement data serves the same reuse need. Its differences are code-owned gameplay/colliders and headless shared state. Keep those boundaries; neither a new general scene editor nor Blender-authored game authority is needed for this experiment. Promotion follows [`entity.ts`](../../src/entity/entity.ts) and [world rule contracts](../../src/world/rules/README.md), while the temporary prototype keeps its permitted structure.

Agents can fool themselves in specific ways:

- **A still hides the broken action.** Inspect the unloading and laying sequence at wall time, then play it. A contact sheet checks coarse poses, not smoothness or input feel.
- **The fixture fixes the thing being tested.** Record any teleports, replenished materials, forced times of day, frozen machines or debug completions. Do not use them in the uncut acceptance run.
- **The camera improves the painting but worsens the game.** Keep the normal approved follow camera for acceptance; use an extra inspection camera only to diagnose geometry.
- **An added effect wins its isolated comparison and loses in the mix.** Compare layers integrated, then remove the least useful one. Check sound during repeated and overlapping events.
- **A counter measures its own loop.** [`dev/look/hitches.ts`](../../dev/look/hitches.ts) samples rAF timing; correlate timing evidence with completed game frames and actual play. [`counts.ts`](../../dev/look/counts.ts)'s normal-game capture is not proof of the lazy corner's asset or render cost.
- **An agent asserts that a viewer felt something.** Report observable behavior and defects; only the owner supplies the owner verdict. “No errors” and “looked better to the agent” are separate statements.

Follow [AGENTS.md, See it before you ship](../../AGENTS.md): use `npm run look` with a named question, corner setup/action and real-time `--video`; open the PNGs and observe the action. [`dev/look/look.ts`](../../dev/look/look.ts) supports these inputs already. Preserve health output separately from the visual judgment. Fix and look again for up to three rounds; an unresolved result remains an explicitly uncertain flagged experiment. Do not invent visual unit tests or a permanent scenario for every pass. A new shared-state contract can warrant a scenario under the repository's test diet.

## Spend owner attention at three decisions

**[D/H]** Offer a short iPad play at three integrated checkpoints. They can all happen in one day if the builds are ready; otherwise batch routine status into one daily update and request a review when its checkpoint is ready. Do not interrupt for individual tweaks. **Two to four minutes** is an initial review budget, not a time limit imposed on play. Builders handle setup, collisions, cold loads and long-run checks beforehand. Each review supplies exactly one line about what changed and one gut question:

| Checkpoint                          | What's new                                                                                  | Owner's question                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Composition and rough action        | “The gorge approach, receiving frame and machine now read together.”                        | “Does this feel like a place you want to walk into and work on?” |
| Complete material-to-track sequence | “Your logs stay visible through feeding and laying, with the finished motion and sound.”    | “Does this feel like your work becoming a satisfying crossing?”  |
| Integrated target                   | “This is the whole corner at the proposed quality, with the previous rough edges resolved.” | “Is this the quality you want us to reproduce elsewhere?”        |

Record the answer and the one thing that most weakened it. Do not turn the owner into the QA operator or ask for separate approval of ten shaders. Reopen an earlier decision only when a later pass contradicts it. If several qualities changed, the comparison can establish a preferred package but cannot identify which change caused the preference; isolate variants when that distinction matters.

Watch the first attempt without coaching, then ask. A quick negative judgment is useful evidence; never answer it with more spectacle before understanding the obstruction. For a genuinely unfamiliar player's understanding, seek one short fresh-player observation when available. The owner knows the project, so their successful play does not establish newcomer onboarding. If unavailable, mark that claim unverified rather than turning the owner into a pretend novice.

## Measure whether the corner predicts the cost of the world

Record a small ledger per pass, with commit, build URL, device/browser/OS, camera, lighting state, resolution/DPR and quality switches. **Separate measured values from proposed budgets.** A prettier result delivered through lower adaptive resolution is a tradeoff to show, not a free performance win.

| Measure                    | Why it matters                                  | Boogle capture or experiment                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Production and review time | Finds the real bottleneck                       | Active agent work, elapsed work, asset export/build time, integration/rework time, owner minutes, rejected variants; include unsuccessful work.                                                 |
| Repeat cost                | Distinguishes reusable craft from a one-off     | Fit the same kit to a second bank/station orientation; include collision, all action states and QA. Record new custom parts and edits to shared helpers.                                        |
| Iteration latency          | Predicts how quickly the owner can steer        | Time one shape or material change from source edit to checked in-game result, including rebuild and capture.                                                                                    |
| Frame distribution         | Exposes short stalls hidden by averages         | Real iPad and desktop, before/after, same route and settings: CPU/GPU where available, p50/p95/p99 and counts over 33/50 ms, correlated with completed frames. Record instrumentation overhead. |
| Cost by state              | Separates idle scenery from active construction | Empty, full, feeding, laying, finished; solo, two contributors, then room-cap occupancy. Draws, shadows, triangles, material variants, particles and memory trend.                              |
| First use and return       | Finds asset, compilation and lifecycle costs    | Cold load to usable controls, first kit appearance, first sound/action, leave/re-enter and tab background/resume; actual lazy asset transfer/decode, not only JS gzip.                          |
| Interaction quality        | Finds polish that makes work slower             | Input-to-visible-response observations, complete job time, carrying/waiting share, mis-targets, lost footing, camera corrections, retries and repeated-run annoyance.                           |
| Shared-state correctness   | Makes this a Boogle production test             | Accepted material conserved, simultaneous contributions, late join during feed/lay, disconnect/rejoin and saved completion; actual clients and existing sync tooling.                           |

Use [`perf.ts`](../../dev/look/perf.ts) before/after substantial mesh or pass changes, [`hitches.ts`](../../dev/look/hitches.ts) for investigations and [render instrumentation](../../src/render/README.md) for device data. The default island tour and pose do not automatically cover this remote prototype: set up the corner and its actions explicitly. Do not collect performance timings while another agent is rendering on the same Mac. A 30 fps recording is evidence of the observed action, not a 60 fps device benchmark. Safari/iPad measurements cannot be replaced by Chromium allocation traces or Mac timings.

**[H]** Start by testing a 60 Hz target (16.7 ms frame interval) on the owner's M4 iPad, with roughly 20% steady-state headroom for the rest of the game. Report tail behavior and actual resolution, not just this target. These are proposed budgets, not a promise or existing measured pass. Run a representative 10–15 minute device session to expose warming and lifecycle problems; that duration is a starting sample, not proof against all thermal or memory failures. If the target fails, measure and cut optional work before proposing a lower target to the owner. The [look-pass](../plans/look-pass.md) GTAO result already argues against adding it speculatively.

For planning, separate **one-time setup + repeated known work + new content families + integration/regression**. Estimate repeated known work from the second arrangement, with a range reflecting rework; a single sample still has large uncertainty. GPU cost scales with visible/shadowed objects, pixels, active work and material variety, not total map square metres. Build size scales with unique assets and variants. CPU/network cost depends on concurrent active systems and players. The gorge can price another gorge-like station; it cannot price a town, underground scene, new biome, all six eras or eight hours of pacing without further representative samples.

## Concrete pass plan: one gorge station, one track-laying machine

**Quality-target done:** the owner accepts the final target on the iPad; an ordinary uncut approach → supply → feed → lay → ride/exit works; each representative visual state passes the checklist; the solid-surface issue is resolved; real-time motion and device checks support the claim; source assets rebuild; evidence identifies commit, settings and remaining limits. A target accepted in selected daylight proves selected daylight only. Record any remaining production or lighting-policy gap explicitly.

**Production-slice done additionally:** the approved production implementation conserves shared materials, handles actual concurrent clients and late joins, preserves progress through return, and uses maintainable kit/entity/state boundaries; the second arrangement has measured cost and no hidden one-off dependency; worst representative occupancy and action state have device evidence; the functional downstream payoff still works. Record required check results, actual deployment state if later shipped, and the accepted owner verdict separately. No staged screenshot, local save, successful build or research document satisfies those gates by itself.
