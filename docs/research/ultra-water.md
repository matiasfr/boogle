# Ultra Water: what to take for Boogle's sea and gorge

> **Verdict:** Take filtering, shared fields and accounting; no whole-renderer port.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Build a selective TSL treatment inside the existing sea, with provenance from the start. 2. Measure completed work and the actual pixel/pass budget before adding a simulation. 3. Fix local bathymetry and contacts before adding wave complexity.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [plan water-graduation](../plans/water-graduation.md), [Look](../decisions.md#look).

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Source audit of Ultra Water, selective WebGPU/TSL adaptations, scene-performance lessons, wave queries and attribution. Target: Super Mario Sunshine's warmth and toy-like readability, an M4 iPad Pro with a controller, and train views at 20–50 m/s. Recommendations only; no implementation or performance measurements._

## Brief, evidence and scope

**Take the filtering, shared fields and disciplined accounting; build a small water treatment inside Boogle's existing renderer. Do not port the whole renderer.** The likely visible improvement is turquoise water meeting warm banks and piers, a longer, stable sun lane, broken creamy foam and readable river flow. A four-cascade ocean, realistic atmospheric scattering and an underwater optical pipeline are not prerequisites for that look. This is a design and engineering recommendation, not an observed result. **[H]**

The owner asked what [Ultra Water](https://github.com/Humpalumps/Ultra-Water) could look like in Boogle, and what it teaches about efficient three.js scenes even if its water is not used. The binding constraints are [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), the [Boogle 2 decisions](../decisions.md) and the [render contract](../../src/render/README.md): TypeScript, WebGPU only, TSL node materials, one linear HDR `RenderPipeline`, Neutral tone mapping, and physics outside Lockstep. This serves **Small and readable** and the established Sunshine direction; it proposes no change to a design principle.

## The techniques, their value and a selective port

Cost ranks below are **relative hypotheses**, not iPad timings. “Cheap” means a small field bake or a few samples in the existing material; a full-screen shader can still dominate at high resolution. Implementation complexity and steady GPU cost are separate.

### FFT: efficient for its scope, still the wrong starting scope

[OceanSim.js][uw-sim] uses four 256² cascades with physical lengths **600, 85, 12 and 2.1 m**, packed into a 256×1024 atlas for the transform stages. A seeded PCG/Gaussian initial spectrum combines JONSWAP wind and swell with directional spreading. Frequency cutoffs prevent the cascades from simply counting the same energy repeatedly. Dispersion includes gravity and a capillary term; angular frequency is quantized to a 300-second loop to keep phase precision bounded. The shortest cascade contributes slopes rather than geometric displacement to the surface. Its capillary strength is deliberately reduced for the desired appearance. This is already an art-tuned model, not unmodified physics. **[C]**

The steady simulation is **one spectrum-evolution MRT draw + sixteen Stockham inverse-FFT MRT draws + four assembly MRT draws = 21 draws**, not sixteen transforms for each of four independently rendered cascades. Dirty spectrum initialization adds a draw. Two floating-point attachments carry packed fields; half-float ping-pong intermediates reduce traffic. Assembly produces displacement/Jacobian-history and slope/moment textures, with mip generation beyond those 21 draws. Foam history recovers over `dt`, so the whole simulation output is not solely a function of time. **[C, D]**

**TSL/WebGPU option [H]:** keep spectral initialization infrequent, evolve complex fields into storage, then perform row and column transforms using workgroup memory, followed by assembly. Combining butterfly stages within a workgroup could remove many global texture round trips. That is a reason to write a native compute algorithm, not translate every full-screen GLSL pass into a TSL full-screen pass. A row and a column stage still need ordered dispatches; workgroup barriers do not synchronize arbitrary workgroups. Validate workgroup storage and invocation limits, precision and occupancy on the actual device. Neither subgroups nor `shader-f16` should be a hidden requirement. Half-float texture storage and half-float arithmetic are separate choices. [WGSL synchronization](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#synchronization-builtin-functions), [WebGPU limits](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#limits). **[V]**

three.js exposes [TSL storage textures](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/StorageTextureNode.html) and a [compute ping-pong example](https://threejs.org/examples/webgpu_compute_texture_pingpong.html). Those establish an implementation route, not an ocean library or a measured speedup. **[V]** Boogle's installed r187 `Renderer.compute()` can accept a group of nodes; its WebGPU backend begins and submits compute work per compute group. Sixteen separate calls can retain sixteen scheduling boundaries. Inspect the actual generated dispatch/pass/submission sequence before claiming a port is cheaper. **[C, H]**

If simpler waves eventually fail the brief, start with **one 128² band and no foam history**, then a second band only if the comparison demands it. Check displacement, slopes, energy and seams before adding any beach solver. No FFT shader or readback result belongs in `src/sim/`. **[H]**

For the first version, use a few low-amplitude analytic waves for geometry and the existing two slope-map scales for detail. Mark Finch's shipped **Uru** technique separates geometric undulation from finer normal-map waves; his chapter explains why fine shading can supply much of the impression without resolving every ripple in geometry. That supports a selective approach, not a present-day performance claim. [GPU Gems: Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models). **[V]**

### `shoreMap` is two different ideas; take the simpler one first

[shoreMap.js][uw-shore] first bakes a **512² field over 1,800 m**, storing `(bed height, signed coast distance, gradient x, gradient z)` in RGBA32F. It uses a separable Euclidean distance transform and smoothing for the coast field. The height source also constructs the visible island, so water and terrain share a definition. The distance-transform family is documented by its authors in [Distance Transforms of Sampled Functions](https://cs.brown.edu/people/pfelzens/dt/); the algorithm is useful independently of the ocean. **[C, V]**

That resolution is about **3.52 m per texel**. It can describe broad beaches, not a 0.2 m foam fringe at a pier. A global distance texture is also not enough for bridge pilings in deep water. Ultra Water's contact foam uses screen depth with a shallow-water restriction, and its hero rock has a special skirt treatment. “Port `shoreMap`” therefore does not mean “all contacts are solved.” **[C, D]**

**Small Boogle port [H]:** a TypeScript CPU bake for a bounded shore tile, then TSL texture sampling in the existing material. Store bed/depth and shore distance with an explicit world-to-tile transform and validity mask. Use an analytic cylinder/box distance or a separate small contact field for pier supports. Start with a 64 m, 256² tile, giving 0.25 m sampling; use analytic contacts for narrower edges rather than raising the resolution of an entire archipelago. Interpolate tile borders and retain an explicit open-water fallback. A surface function must report what is valid; clamping UVs to the last bank texel would extend a false shore across the sea.

For the gorge, bake from `H()` in [place/land.ts](../../src/proto/place/land.ts), using [layout.ts](../../src/proto/place/layout.ts)'s `ORIGIN` and river branches. Select one 32–64 m section first. Depth is the difference between the water level and **bed**, not the closest roof, rail deck or arbitrary collider. Separate shoreline foam from object-contact foam. A field should invalidate when its terrain or static contacts change, not rebuild every animation frame. Keep the prototype provider inside its scope; promote a shared provider only after both crossing and gorge prove the same need. **[H]**

Compute is unnecessary for this first static bake. It becomes interesting for frequently edited terrain or many tiles: a parallel approximate distance transform is possible, but it changes error characteristics. Do not replace a small deterministic CPU preprocessing job with a global GPU system merely because compute is available. **[H]**

### Eikonal refraction: a convincing beach model, not a fluid simulation

The other part of [shoreMap.js][uw-shore] solves a travel-time field with a first-order Godunov update and four-direction fast sweeps, up to twelve sweep rounds with settled-cell skipping. Its local wave speed is based on `sqrt(g * depth)` with bounded depth. Sub-texel coastal seeds, smoothing and gradient coherence suppress discontinuities where fronts meet. A second texture stores travel time, shoreward direction and shoaling; a third stores shelter derived from shallower barriers along the approach. Small islands can be removed from the propagation mask to avoid concentric artifacts. The optional rock dome affects wave propagation without rewriting the actual bed field. **[C]**

[OceanSurface.js][uw-surface] combines two shore-wave trains with this field, depth-limited breaking, set modulation, asymmetric crest shaping, sideways peeling and constrained horizontal chop. These are procedural surface shapes; they do not simulate overturning water, collisions between fluids, flooding or a navigable current. The source's shock suppression and special cases are significant parts of the effect. **[C]**

**Port [H]:** retain a CPU-precomputed travel-time texture for one static beach. Use TSL phase, direction, shoal and coverage functions; share them between position, normal and foam evaluation. A naive compute translation of sequential Gauss–Seidel sweeps loses their update ordering. Parallel relaxation or jump-flood-style approximations need convergence/error comparisons, extra dispatches and explicit boundaries. They are only attractive if field rebuilds become a demonstrated bottleneck.

A gorge has opposing banks, forks and a preferred downstream direction. Opposing banks create competing travel-time gradients and a meeting boundary; smoothing that boundary does not produce river flow. Use a branch-aware flow field there, not the eikonal field. A later beach experiment should have a calm comparison and no gameplay dependency. **[H]**

### Glints and filtering: preserve the bright band as detail disappears

Ultra Water's slope texture stores **`sx, sz, sx², sz²`**, so filtered samples retain variance `E[s²] − E[s]²`. The surface adds lost fine-scale variance and derivative-based geometric roughness to the specular response. Its unresolved wind-driven roughness estimates what is below the simulated band. This is a **LEAN-style moment treatment**, not a full LEAN covariance representation: the cross moment is absent. [OceanSim.js][uw-sim], [OceanSurface.js][uw-surface]. **[C]** Olano and Baker's [LEAN Mapping](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/papers/lean/) explains the essential benefit: filter a distribution of slopes so unresolved bumps broaden a highlight instead of vanishing or aliasing. **[V]**

The source also implements sparse glints by sampling Poisson-like facet counts in world-space cells. It blends adjacent cell scales according to pixel footprint, varies them with smoothly blended time samples, and transitions toward a mean GGX response when facets become dense. The rare-event multiplier is capped. This is more deliberate than random bright pixels each frame, but motion stability at Boogle's train speeds still needs observation. **[C, H]**

**First port [H]:** extend the generated textures in [crossing/sea.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sea.ts) with second moments, retaining average slope in RG and moments in BA. Choose an encoding that filters linearly; RGBA16F is a straightforward starting option, not a requirement. Do not normalize each mip as if it were an ordinary normal map and thereby discard the distribution. For the two 128² maps, full mip chains are approximately 171 KiB in RGBA8 or 341 KiB in RGBA16F, excluding allocation overhead. **[D]** Combine the bands' variances consistently with their amplitudes; maintain a broad art-directed sun lane as the tiny facets fade.

Use world-space sampling and a conservative footprint at grazing angles. The current geometric mean of derivative lengths can underrepresent the long axis of a highly elongated pixel footprint. Compare an anisotropic or conservative maximum-axis estimate before adding more noise. Keep derivative and implicit-LOD evaluation in uniform control flow; use explicit LOD where appropriate in vertex/compute code. Translate reversed-edge `smoothstep` expressions into the ordered-edge form required by Boogle's render contract. **[C, H]**

Only then try a restrained statistical glint layer, with a minimum visible feature size and smooth scale/time blending. More bright events are not automatically better: continuous high-frequency sparkle can erase the toy-like forms and become uncomfortable during a 50 m/s pan. Judge a real-time video, not just a beauty still. **[H]**

### Polar grid: separate geometry sampling from shading sampling

[OceanSurface.js][uw-surface] centres a polar grid on the camera: 384 angular segments, geometric radial spacing based on angular width, extra dense inner rings, main rings to 3 km and a flat skirt to 60 km. At altitude it switches to 192 angular segments with hysteresis and increases grid scale. The README describes roughly 190,000 vertices. Displacement is filtered by the physical size of each cell; short shore waves and interaction waves fade when the mesh cannot represent them. A final bed-clearance clamp prevents troughs exposing the seabed, with a distance allowance for depth precision. **[C, A]**

**Take now [H]:** constrain any new vertex wave to several samples per wavelength and fade it before that condition fails; shade shorter waves in the normal field. Keep a flat far sea. More vertices alone do not solve specular aliasing, and shading LOD alone does not stop geometry folding. Preserve stable world phase as a mesh follows the camera.

**Conditional port [H]:** if a near-water camera needs a moving silhouette, compare a much smaller camera-following ring mesh or clipmap against the existing plane, with a budget derived from the measured view. TSL `positionNode` samples explicit mips; normals use the intended shading band. Check join continuity, grazing depth, shadows and bounds. A 60 km skirt is not a reason to expand Boogle's camera far plane. The [vast-world report](vast-world-scale.md) and [crossing sky](../../src/proto/crossing/sky.ts) already separate visible extent, fog and camera depth precision. The narrow gorge can keep simple local strips; a full ocean radial grid there would spend geometry where land hides it.

### Foam and river flow: reuse a pattern, distinguish the masks

Ultra Water has four mechanisms: temporal Jacobian whitecaps from choppy FFT waves; breaking-crest and residual shore foam; screen-depth contact foam restricted to shallow water; and special rock foam. [bakeTextures.js][uw-bake] makes a 512² texture containing lace, billow height and gradients. [OceanSurface.js][uw-surface] combines coverage, age, flow, bump shading and thin-versus-dense lighting. Residual patterns move through two offset flow phases and crossfade to conceal resets. Far foam fades; that is deliberate band-limiting, not simply “everything visible everywhere.” **[C]**

**Port [H]:** one small original procedural pattern shared by all shore/contact consumers. Separate three quantities: where foam is allowed, how much exists, and how its interior moves. Start with a broken 0.2–0.45 m contact fringe and a wider, sparse shore wash, using warm off-white below the sun-lane peak. The widths are proposed art values; they must not be hardcoded as physical truth. Put the mask and colour in the current water node graph. Use the shared particle emitter only for a few local impact accents if the look demands them.

For the gorge, derive downstream vectors from the river branches, blend junctions, slow near banks and perturb around a single rock. Advect normal detail and sparse foam through two resetting phases. Valve's **Left 4 Dead 2 / Portal 2** water presentation uses low-resolution flow vectors, phase-offset layers and noise to avoid stretched textures, repetition and pulsing. It changes shading, not geometry or simulated fluid transport. The lesson is especially applicable to Boogle's river. [Alex Vlachos, Water Flow in Games, SIGGRAPH 2010](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2010/siggraph2010_vlachos_waterflow.pdf). **[V]**

A static flow map remains Local visual data. It does not establish a shared current that pushes cargo. That would be a separate Owner-physics design decision, using a simple CPU current field. No per-frame compute is needed for the first flow shader. A persistent foam-advection texture would add history, reset and memory costs; defer it until the two-phase treatment visibly fails. **[H]**

### Caustics: attractive close up, easy to overbuild

[Caustics.js][uw-caustics] refracts a grid using the 12 m cascade's slopes, projecting onto depths of **1.5, 4 and 10 m**. There are three mesh draws into two targets: a 512² near R16F map and a 256² deep RG16F map. They use **4× MSAA even when the main mobile scene requests 2×**. Bicubic B-spline slope reconstruction avoids point-like artifacts; source-area/projected-area ratios focus light, with additive blending, capped intensity and bounds handling. Depth slices, mips and blur are shared by terrain, props and underwater lighting. Ideal area conservation motivates it, but the clamps mean exact unit mean should not be assumed. **[C]**

**Small port [H]:** after the local depth field makes the bed visible through the existing shallow-water alpha, generate an original low-resolution caustic pattern once, animate two low-amplitude phases, and project it in world space onto that bed and one rock through [patchNode](../../src/render/material-patch.ts). Mask by water coverage, depth, receiving orientation and sunlight/shadow state; do not illuminate the underside of a bridge through solid ground. Keep it quiet enough to leave stones and player feet readable. No extra full-screen pass is necessary for this version; deep opaque water cannot display receiver caustics merely by patching the hidden bed.

Compute can generate a procedural pattern, but refracted splatting is a many-to-one accumulation problem. Raster additive blending is already a natural solution; a compute rewrite needs a gather formulation or accumulation strategy. Core WGSL atomic types are integer types, so ordinary floating-point scatter-add is not a portable assumption. [WGSL atomic types](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#atomic-types). **[V]** Do not call a port faster merely because it uses compute. The full refracted solution is only justified if a close-water shot makes its coupling to wave motion visibly important. **[H]**

### Reflections, refraction and underwater optics are frame-graph work

[PlanarReflection.js][uw-reflection] renders selected opaque objects from a reflected camera into a reduced-resolution target, with mips; sky is filled separately. It skips that work underwater. The water samples resolved opaque colour/depth for refraction, absorption and contact tests, and then writes into the retained scene target. [UnderwaterPass.js][uw-underwater] adds waterline classification, a small lens-depth target, conditional shafts and compositing. Other source files add marine snow, spray and camera droplets. The alpha channel also carries classification values; it is not conventional opacity throughout. **[C]**

These cannot be pasted into Boogle's material. A TSL port needs explicit pre-water colour/depth resources, legal read/write lifetimes, a correct water composite order and a decision about transparent objects. Reflection clipping must use WebGPU depth conventions rather than copied WebGL oblique-projection maths. Preserve the single `RenderPipeline`; additional effects extend its graph, not a second renderer or animation loop. **[H]**

The desktop image has about **ten times the pixels** of the low-resolution phone image; the phone desktop-profile image has about **2.8 times** its mobile-profile pixels. Pixel count alone still cannot scale the timings because FFT, fixed-size caustics, geometry, shadows, CPU work and thermal behaviour scale differently. There is no defensible “the M4 is faster, therefore this fits” conclusion. No tablet result, sustained Boogle load, controller session, or 20–50 m/s visual-stability result is established by those numbers. **[D, H]**

### Why the fence matters, and what is ANGLE-specific

The README says older results were **2–4× too optimistic** because its `readPixels`/`finish` timing approach did not wait for the intended work under that Chrome/ANGLE configuration. Treat this as the author's diagnosis, not a statement that `gl.finish()` is generally specified to return before completion, nor evidence of a corresponding WebGPU bug. **[A]**

In [main.js][uw-main], `runBenchmark()` pauses the ordinary animation path, renders twenty warm-up frames, waits on a `fenceSync` inserted after submitted work, then times 300 frames and waits on another fence before dividing elapsed time by frame count. `flush()` submits, `clientWaitSync(..., 0)` polls, and a timer yields between polls. Simulation advances by `1/200` per rendered benchmark frame. [gpuTimer.js][uw-timer] separately uses disjoint timer queries for labelled GPU regions. **[C]**

The useful general rule is **measure completed work, with warm-up and known configuration**. The batch measurement includes CPU submission, driver/GPU throughput and fence-poll overhead; it is not a per-frame GPU timestamp or display/input latency. Rendering hundreds of frames back-to-back also changes clocks and overlaps CPU/GPU differently from a 60 Hz game. Its separate uncapped mode limits outstanding fences to three; that is a diagnostic, not a reason to uncap Boogle's game loop. An equivalent harness should handle errors, device loss and unavailable/disjoint samples explicitly. **[H]**

For WebGPU, `GPUQueue.onSubmittedWorkDone()` resolves after previously submitted queue work completes; use a queue drain before and after a bounded benchmark batch, **not an awaited drain on every gameplay frame**. Timestamp queries are optional and should be reported separately from wall-clock throughput. [WebGPU queue completion](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#dom-gpuqueue-onsubmittedworkdone). **[V]** The installed three.js r187 `waitForGPU()` is a removed-API stub, so do not propose calling it. A backend-aware debug harness may use the underlying queue, isolated from game code. **[C, H]**

### Count the work behind “one water mesh”

This ledger comes from source control flow, not a GPU capture. A draw, a render pass, a compute dispatch, a resolve and a queue submission are different units. **[C, D]**

| Work                   | Source-visible steady work and hidden qualifications                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| FFT                    | 21 full-screen MRT draws; dirty initialization adds one. Two attachments do not mean two draws. Output mip generation is additional work.                                                    |
| Caustics               | Three mesh draws in two target renders, plus multisample resolves and mip generation.                                                                                                        |
| Wave queries           | One tiny render when the readback slots are available, then asynchronous transfer.                                                                                                           |
| Planar reflection      | Another scene render above water; many geometry draws, target clear/resolve/mips. It is not one draw just because it is one method call.                                                     |
| Opaque scene           | Terrain/rocks/props plus applicable shadow work. Static and moving-object shadow paths differ.                                                                                               |
| Water scene            | Surface plus enabled spray/underwater particle draws; early shader rejection does not erase submitted vertices.                                                                              |
| Bloom and composite    | `LEVELS = 6`: one prefilter + five downsamples + five upsamples = **11 extra bloom draws**. Final composite is another draw, also needed with bloom off.                                     |
| Auto exposure          | Two more draws. Therefore PostFX with auto exposure is **14 draws with bloom, 3 without**. The README's “12 bloom passes” wording includes a composite that disabling bloom does not remove. |
| Underwater             | Conditional lens-depth/shaft/composite work, not a constant overhead in every above-water shot.                                                                                              |
| Atmosphere/environment | LUTs, six cube faces, mip generation and PMREM on changes; excluded from steady unchanged-sun accounting, not free at startup or update.                                                     |

### Mobile choices: useful priorities, not a profile to copy

Ultra Water detects a coarse pointer and starts at `0.6 × min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`, with 2× main MSAA, a 0.35 reflection scale and bloom disabled. Desktop uses full selected resolution, 4× main MSAA and 0.5 reflection scale. The mobile profile does **not** reduce FFT resolution or replace the entire polar mesh with a low-poly version. Altitude controls the mesh tier separately. [main.js][uw-main]. **[C]**

Its adaptive controller skips startup frames, evaluates 120-frame windows, counts intervals over 20 ms as misses, lowers resolution in 0.05 steps to 0.5, and cautiously raises it to 0.6 after clean windows. Severe misses at the floor select 30 fps; this path does not automatically recover to 60. These are demo policies built around a 60 Hz assumption. Boogle should detect the presentation cadence, distinguish a browser/low-power cap from GPU overload, retain hysteresis, and log the actual internal target dimensions. A pointer or attached controller is an input property, not a GPU class. **[C, H]**

WebGPU's portable multisample counts are **1 or 4**. In Boogle's installed r187 `WebGPUUtils.getSampleCount()`, a requested value below four becomes one: copying `samples = 2` would silently give no MSAA, not the reference's 2× mode. [WebGPU multisample validation](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#validating-gpumultisamplestate). **[C, V]** Compare actual 1× and 4× explicitly, at matched pixel dimensions, with thin rails and glitter in motion. Preserve the stored MSAA setting in [scene.ts](../../src/render/scene.ts); the existing iPad regression is stronger evidence than a theoretical memory saving. **[H]**

Apple's [WWDC25 WebGPU session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/236/?time=0) recommends minimizing command buffers, passes and resource creation, and using smaller data representations. It explains the on-chip/unified-memory cost of command-buffer boundaries and the bandwidth consumed by passes, and discusses reusing render commands and bind groups. This supports reducing repeated transfers, full-screen work and per-frame allocations on iPad; it does not promise that every draw merger or compute rewrite is faster. The recommendation applies to Boogle's WebGPU/Metal path independently of the reference's ANGLE timing problem. **[V, H]**

## Wave queries and buoyancy under Boogle's sync rules

[WaterQuery.js][uw-query] runs the surface equations into a small float target: one query point per texel, returning height and horizontal displacement. It iterates four times to invert choppy horizontal displacement, then samples again. It reads results asynchronously through three.js's PBO/fence path, allows at most two requests in flight and skips dispatch when busy. Tags and sample times accompany results. The README describes roughly 1–3 frames of latency; that is not a device guarantee. [floaters.js][uw-floaters] consumes the samples for volume-based buoyancy, relative-water drag and beach contact in its own small rigid-body integrator. **[C, A]**

Two qualifications matter:

- The query shares the wave function, **not the exact visible surface**. It samples LOD zero, while the rendered polar grid band-limits waves; it also omits the final seabed-clearance clamp and triangle interpolation. Query/display agreement must be defined, not assumed. **[C]**
- The reference floaters are not Rapier bodies, do not establish multiplayer authority, and do not provide a general boat/cargo system. Water velocity inferred from successive samples is an approximation, especially when sample positions move. Copying the integrator would introduce a second physics system. **[C, H]**

| Boogle state                                             | Appropriate treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Surface shading, caustics, foam, non-interactive bobbing | **Local.** A shared seed/time can make views broadly coherent; no need to sync a texture or every crest.                                                                                                                                              |
| A player-driven floating toy                             | **Owner.** Apply bounded buoyancy/drag to Rapier on the owning tab; broadcast pose/velocity through presence and interpolate remotes with `createSnaps()`. Do not run competing forces on each peer.                                                  |
| A shared floating object that changes hands              | **Owner with transfer.** Use [createOwnerSync](../../src/netplay/owner-sync.ts), following the [ball](../../src/entities/ball/state.ts). Clear pending-query ownership epochs and stale forces on transfer; preserve the existing claim/orphan rules. |
| A small shared weather descriptor                        | **Host** if it should reset on host change; otherwise a deliberately designed persistent state. Send parameters/epoch, not evolving wave samples. No weather system is needed for the first visual prototype.                                         |
| Persistent tide schedules or water-related grid rules    | **Lockstep only for the deterministic schedule/state.** Use shared ticks, supported math and explicit validation; never GPU samples, wall time, trigonometry or Rapier results in apply/tick logic.                                                   |
| One-off splash/honk-like cues                            | Local from observed motion when sufficient; otherwise a validated, rate-limited room message, not persistent wave state.                                                                                                                              |

This follows [DESIGN.md's sync choices](../../DESIGN.md), [netplay](../../src/netplay/net.ts), [snaps](../../src/netplay/snaps.ts) and the [world contract](../../src/world/README.md). **[C, H]**

**Can waves be a pure function of shared time?** A finite analytic height function can be evaluated from `(world position, seed, phase epoch)` without storing a history, and a spectral displacement with fixed parameters can also be evaluated at an absolute time. That does not make GPU floating-point sine/FFT results deterministic across peers, or legal Lockstep inputs. Ultra Water's temporal foam history additionally depends on its update sequence. Keep visual phase coherent using a bounded phase and a shared tick-derived epoch; let the object's owner decide physical forces. A later Lockstep tide must have its own allowed deterministic representation, rather than borrowing shader results. **[H]**

**Preferred first query [H]:** a small CPU analytic wave function or the existing mean sea level, outside `src/sim/`, composed through the existing [water-field surface hook](../../src/physics/water-field.ts). Preserve cave-specific behaviour. Sample a few buoyancy points on one Rapier toy, clamp force and drag, and compare the rendered surface at those points. Epic's [buoyancy component](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/water-buoyancy-component-in-unreal-engine) similarly uses a small set of spherical pontoons as a volume approximation, rather than full fluid simulation. **[V]** Boogle differs by having one network owner simulate those forces.

If a later FFT genuinely requires GPU queries, a TSL compute kernel can write packed results to a storage buffer; r187 exposes `getArrayBufferAsync()` for asynchronous readback. Use a bounded ring of pending requests, stable object IDs, ownership epoch, position layout, sample time and monotonic completion sequence. Reject old/out-of-order answers, bound accepted age and blend or fall back to mean water when late. Never await readback in the frame/physics loop, never apply a former owner's answer, and never let a delayed sample decide immediate camera waterline state. GPU querying improves batch throughput, not latency or determinism. **[C, H]**

## What not to take

- **No WebGL2 backend, GLSL strings, `ShaderMaterial`/`RawShaderMaterial`, `onBeforeCompile`, or second renderer/loop.** Translate selected algorithms into TypeScript/TSL within Boogle's contracts.
- **No full four-cascade ocean as the first task.** It does not supply the missing pier bathymetry, and its complexity is not the desired Sunshine look.
- **No eikonal beach field as a river current or a fluid simulation.** Use branch-aware downstream flow for the gorge.
- **No photoreal optics bundle by default:** atmosphere LUT stack, planar/SSR reflections, physical underwater volume/shafts, marine snow, camera droplets, automatic exposure or AgX. Preserve Neutral and the current sky direction.
- **No global bloom chain just to brighten water**, no high-contrast foam carpet, no unlimited point glints, and no large spray pool. Reuse Boogle's particles for any later local accents.
- **No 60 km geometry/far-plane expansion, 4096 shadows, 1024 sky cube, 512² global shoreline map or 190k-vertex grid as unexplained defaults.** Their domains and visibility differ from Boogle's.
- **No coarse-pointer performance classifier, literal 2× WebGPU MSAA, irreversible 30 fps adaptation or copied ANGLE fence calls.** Preserve the documented iPad storage workaround until a device test disproves its need.
- **No GPU-result-driven Lockstep or copied floater physics engine.** Shared visual time is not deterministic physics authority.
- **No performance prediction from RTX/Android numbers, screenshots or draw counts alone.** M4 timing, sustained heat, pixel count and moving-view quality remain unknown.

## Recommended path and smallest prototypes

**Recommended path [H]:** instrument the relevant views, then improve the existing TSL sea with local depth/contact data, variance-preserving glints and sparse foam/flow. Stop and judge that result before optional caustics. Keep geometry simple and gameplay on the existing water level. Treat FFT, beach refraction and interactive buoyancy as separate, conditional experiments.

The first visual comparison should stay behind the existing prototype flags: `?proto=crossing` for one pier and a short train segment, and `?proto=place` for one gorge bend. One owner question: **“Does this feel like inviting Sunshine water from the train and the bank?”** One line on what is new: **“Shallows meet the piers, sunlight stays readable while moving, and the river has a clear flow.”** The agent checks seams, occlusion, reverse views, startup, 20/50 m/s motion, graphics errors and device costs itself using [the look workflow](../../.claude/skills/look/SKILL.md). This report has not run those checks.

The recommendations below are ordered by implementation sequence, not a claim that every optional experiment should ship. Each names the smallest crossing and gorge comparison.

1. **Build a selective TSL treatment inside the existing sea, with provenance from the start.** Touch [crossing/sea.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sea.ts) and the [look-pass plan](../plans/look-pass.md); keep [scene.ts](../../src/render/scene.ts)'s pipeline, Neutral and MSAA storage. **Crossing:** an A/B of the current pier view and the combined simplified treatment. **Gorge:** the same material treatment on one bend through `dressSea()`. No new water dependency or renderer. If source is adapted, include the scoped `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` entry and verify its presence in the production artifact for both routes. This is the recommended overall path.

2. **Measure completed work and the actual pixel/pass budget before adding a simulation.** Extend [perf](../../dev/look/perf.ts), [counts](../../dev/look/counts.ts) and [the profiler](../../src/debug/prof-overlay.ts) with the missing configuration/resource data and a separate hardware timing mode. **Crossing:** baseline and variant on the same 20/50 m/s segment, then sustained M4 playback. **Gorge:** one low bank camera and one bridge pan at the same internal resolution. Report render versus compute, timestamp availability, frame delivery and rebuild hitches separately; never label the RTX timings as an iPad budget.

3. **Fix local bathymetry and contacts before adding wave complexity.** Prototype a bounded field provider for [water-render](../../src/render/water-render.ts)/[crossing sea](../../src/proto/crossing/sea.ts), using actual final terrain and separate support distances. **Crossing:** one 64 m shore tile plus one analytic pier contact, with a valid open-water fallback. **Gorge:** one 32–64 m tile from [place/land.ts](../../src/proto/place/land.ts)'s `H()`, excluding the bridge deck. The acceptance question is whether shallows and edges belong to the geometry, without seams or false coast beyond the tile.

4. **Preserve distant highlight energy and band-limit each kind of detail.** Refine the existing slope-map bake and sun lane in [crossing/sea.ts](../../src/proto/crossing/sea.ts); use second moments before statistical microfacets. **Crossing:** a fixed-sun 20/50 m/s pass through the current glitter fade, preserving the broad lane as fine sparkle resolves away. **Gorge:** a grazing bank pan with small ripples and a stable highlight. Keep the current mesh unless silhouettes fail; only then compare a small filtered near mesh with the flat far sea.

5. **Share one sparse foam pattern and give the gorge a downstream flow field.** Use the depth/contact masks above, with an original baked pattern and two-phase advection in the same TSL material. **Crossing:** one broken warm-white pier fringe and a short shore wash. **Gorge:** one branch junction and one rock, deriving flow from [place/layout.ts](../../src/proto/place/layout.ts). Judge readability and reset/pulsing artifacts in real time. Do not add persistent foam simulation or treat visual flow as physical current.

6. **Try cheap receiver caustics only after the surface reads well.** Patch selected materials through [material-patch.ts](../../src/render/material-patch.ts); retain depth, orientation and sunlight masks. **Crossing:** a single visible shallow shelf beside the pier, A/B against none. **Gorge:** one bed patch and rock with the bridge overhead to expose light leaks. Keep the effect only if it adds sunny clarity at normal play distance without needing the reference's three-depth refracted splat system.

7. **Apply the general performance lessons to shared resources and invalidation.** Audit bakes, repeated prop geometry/materials, shadow updates and target lifetimes around [crossing sky](../../src/proto/crossing/sky.ts), [place land](../../src/proto/place/land.ts) and the [render boundary](../../src/render/README.md). **Crossing:** log that one shore bake and shared texture set stay reused during the ride and a sun transition. **Gorge:** the same audit while crossing the tile boundary and rebuilding one local field. Confirm no per-frame rebake, duplicated material cache or leaked target. Optimize a measured cost; do not install the demo's atmosphere or shadow sizes.

8. **Keep buoyancy owner-driven and start with CPU wave queries if a floating toy is wanted.** Extend the [water-field hook](../../src/physics/water-field.ts) without breaking cave handling; follow [owner-sync](../../src/netplay/owner-sync.ts) for one Rapier entity. **Crossing:** one floating crate beside the pier with a few bounded buoyancy samples. **Gorge:** the same crate near a bank, with a separate simple current only if the toy needs it. Check two peers, ownership transfer and stale-query fallback. A GPU query path is conditional on a later wave model, not required for visual water.

9. **Defer FFT and eikonal work until a specific shot defeats the simpler version.** Keep any research spike in [crossing](../../src/proto/crossing/index.ts), with no new Lockstep state or core pipeline reshaping. **Crossing:** compare one 128² FFT band against a few analytic swells; independently test a 32–64 m eikonal beach patch only if wrapping breakers matter. **Gorge:** use the existing flow bend as the control; at most compare reduced ripple shading, with no coast-launched breaker solver. Promote neither unless it visibly improves the brief and fits measured M4 costs; an attractive ocean demo alone does not establish that need.
