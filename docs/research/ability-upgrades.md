# Ability upgrades: make familiar places worth another visit

_Note added 6 November 2026: the owner moved the main-game target to roughly six hours, a rough target rather than a rule, with solo and groups of two or three close together ([decision log](../decisions.md#the-shape-of-the-game)). Read eight-hour figures below as the earlier target._

_Research report, 5 November 2026. Repository baseline `68ee082d`. Live primary-source research, focused inspection of Place's current moves and a proposed eight-hour ladder for double jump, glider and target-pull hook. Research and proposals only: no gameplay changes, reference-game replay, Boogle playtest or campaign simulation._

**Evidence:** **[V]** = fetched primary text or inspected repository; **[R]** = recorded playtest, not reproduced; **[G]** = shipped-game rule used for comparison, not independently replayed here; **[P]** = inference, proposed placement or tuning hypothesis. ## Answer first

**[P] Give each ability a short, distinct ladder: double jump → triple jump; glide → faster cloth → authored updraft riding; target pull → longer cable → fixed-anchor relay.** That is three base acquisitions and **five subsequent upgrades**, distributed through eight hours, with the final hour spent enjoying the complete kit. The jump gets one upgrade after acquisition; hook and glider get two each. A further tier must earn its place through a new route or decision, not a percentage label.

Keep **range, speed and count** when the result is legible in the world. Add **new behavior** when more of the same would erase the challenge: relay between fixed anchors instead of extending the hook forever; rise in visible, bounded air columns instead of granting universal flight. The hook always pulls toward a target. There is no swinging, pendulum release or momentum-pumping upgrade.

This serves [DESIGN](../../DESIGN.md)'s **Toys before chores**, **Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated**, **Telegraph everything**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, **Slapstick over precision**, and **Show, don't tell**. Disposition: **design proposal**, with a bounded proof after the existing production/replay prerequisites. Nothing here approves a new mechanic or changes the current opening.

## What the reference games contribute

### Zelda: a longer line and a different line are different rewards

**[V]** Nintendo's original _Ocarina of Time_ Player's Guide, pp. 82–83 ([public OCR mirror](https://www.scribd.com/document/492995769/Nintendo-Players-Guide-N64-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-Text)), describes the Longshot's chain as twice the Hookshot's length and uses it to continue through previously obstructed Water Temple routes. This is **one flat range upgrade**, with a large spatial consequence rather than a new control. The guide is primary publisher material hosted by a third party; this is not a measurement of game units.

**[V]** _Twilight Princess_ makes the qualitative distinction explicit: a second Clawshot lets Link hold one eligible surface, aim at another and traverse without landing. The acquisition room immediately uses ceiling medallions and a vine exit. The licensed guide then sends the player back through **Lanayru Spirit Cave and Kakariko Gorge** for newly reachable chests. [Acquisition and first use](https://primagames.com/eguides/the-legend-of-zelda-twilight-princess-hd-eguide/dungeon-7-city-in-the-sky/task-5-battle-the-aeralfos); [return routes](https://primagames.com/eguides/the-legend-of-zelda-twilight-princess-hd-eguide/overworld-assembling-the-mirror-of-twilight/task-1-obtain-all-remaining-chests-using-double-clawshot).

**[P] Boogle:** one longer-cable fitting can reopen distant familiar rings; a later relay head can change how an overhead route is traversed. These are separate design axes. The relay can hold the avatar at an authored fixed clamp, with unlimited aiming time and an explicit exit. That retains target-pull identity without importing a swinging rope. Do not infer that Ocarina's 2× multiplier suits Boogle's already compact 24 m layout.

### Metroid: quantitative growth can eventually change the movement rule

**[V]** Nintendo's [_Super Metroid_ manual, special items](https://www.nintendo.com/es-es/games/oms/snes-classic/manuals/super-metroid/manual.pdf) distinguishes Hi-Jump's increased height, Space Jump's repeated airborne jumps, Speed Booster and Morph Ball. It also describes upgrades granting access to hidden areas. Nintendo's [_Dread_ report 5](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2021/August/Metroid-Dread-Report-Vol-5-The-powers-of-Samus-Aran-2026540.html) documents Spin Boost adding one aerial jump and explains the team's emphasis on retaining momentum between actions. These are examples across games, not one claimed acquisition order.

**[P] Boogle:** count is easy to understand and combines with glide, but unlimited jumping would dissolve altitude, hook and railway approach design. Cap the proposed jump ladder at two air charges. Use an already-seen high shelf for the first return payoff, and retain optional alternate routes. Teach the finite count on broad landings; never make remembering an invisible charge the challenge.

### Hollow Knight: the ability order and the map must be designed together

**[V]** In [Team Cherry's map-design interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map/), William Pellen describes establishing powers and their order alongside the world. Ari Gibson explains that they later relaxed hard gates and welcomed routes that omitted powers. The interview also records moving the double-jump placement when the map changed.

**[G]** The shipped comparison is mostly qualitative: dash, wall jump and Monarch Wings expand movement; Shade Cloak changes what a dash passes through, including shade barriers. High ledges and prior crossings acquire new routes. This is not an endless sequence of dash-speed purchases. Those individual obstacle rules were not replayed or exhaustively checked against a primary item catalogue in this pass; the verified interview supports the broader map/process lesson.

**[P] Boogle:** author the obstruction and its return route with the upgrade. Distinguish a required material connection from an optional high balcony. A clever early cosmetic pickup is acceptable; accidentally bypassing the only explanation of a railway service is not. Do not turn every opening into a differently colored ability lock.

### Super Mario Odyssey: knowledge and local tools can provide progression

**[V]** Motokura and Koizumi's [developer interview](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/06/17/marios-developers-on-hat-capture-and-peachs-love-for-mario) explains building the hat mechanic from a familiar part of Mario and changing kingdoms as their main objectives advance. **[G]** Cappy combinations are largely a stable kit to learn; captures provide contextual movement such as extending or launching, rather than permanent “jump level 6.” Revisits also gain value through changed kingdom state and new objectives, not only greater jump numbers.

**[P] Boogle:** some small stops should teach a new combination with the equipment already owned. A hook-to-glide return can be satisfying without another unlock toast. The proposed final hour therefore introduces no new power. Keep expert shortcuts optional and the main route readable without demanding cap-bounce-like precision.

### Astro Bot: spend novelty on a strong local sequence

**[V]** Team Asobi's [development interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/09/09/astro-bot-how-team-asobi-created-a-unified-vision-for-fun/) describes primitive gameplay prototypes before art, building settings around scenarios, and developing the climbing monkey from the action it needed to support. **[G]** Astro's stage-specific powers offer distinct local behaviors rather than a campaign-wide numerical ladder for each gadget. Returning to a stage is predominantly mastery/secret discovery; carrying an ever-stronger gadget back is not the model.

**[P] Boogle:** borrow the concentrated teaching sequence, not disposable powers. Each small stop should establish one behavior, vary it once, then let it pay off outside the lesson. A new gadget head without enough distinct places to use it is content noise, even if its first room is charming.

### Ratchet & Clank: do not confuse weapon upgrades with traversal gadgets

**[V]** Insomniac's [_Rift Apart_ introduction](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/05/18/welcome-to-zurkon-jr-s-almost-launch-party-for-ratchet-clank-rift-apart/) distinguishes Rift Tether's relocation from Phantom Dash's evasive phasing. The [official game guide](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart/) separately describes Hoverboots for fast travel, wall running for access and Raritanium upgrades for **weapons**. That is evidence for several traversal functions, not for an equivalent multi-level gadget skill tree.

**[P] Boogle:** give each hook head a clear destination contract. A faster reel can improve convenience but offers weak gating value alone; a new supported attachment changes route topology. Old paths can become quicker while specific new surfaces open another route. Keep travel fittings visibly attached to Buddy, and show what a destination accepts before the player launches. Reuse the familiar hook input.

### Just Cause: interaction variety can matter more than numerical reach

**[V]** Avalanche's Hamish Young and Ben Jaekle [describe extending wingsuit play through wind and valuing unexpected uses of grapple tools](https://www.shacknews.com/article/108504/just-cause-4-developer-interview-discusses-tornadoes-vehicles-secondary-fire-and-crazy-stunts). Their concrete example is a player using configurable Air Lifter balloons as protection. **[G]** Across _Just Cause 3/4_, faster reeling and stronger/more tethers are quantitative changes; retractors, balloons and boosters change interactions. Exact unlock prices and mod counts were not verified here.

**[P] Boogle:** the useful transfer is “one tool acquires a new use in familiar space.” These games mostly increase freedom and efficiency in an open sandbox; do not portray every mod as a key that opens a new region. A pullable lever or fixed relay could suit Boogle. Free-form tethering of freight, players and trains would instead create a new physics/construction system and is outside this ladder. Neither Just Cause nor Spider-Man is evidence to reverse the rejected swing.

### Spider-Man: preserve transitions when increasing speed

**[V]** PlayStation's [_Spider-Man 2_ developer-backed overview](https://www.playstation.com/en-sg/games/marvels-spider-man-2/whats-new-in-marvels-spider-man-2/) explains Web Wings complementing swinging across lower-rise Queens, environmental wind tunnels/slingshots, traversal suit-tech improvements and a skill strand shared by both protagonists. Geography creates different uses for the new traversal. It is primarily increased flow and route choice, not a Metroid-style hard gate at every district.

**[P] Boogle:** hook arrival, jump and glide need to remain one understandable sequence after any upgrade. Increased speed can make an old route fun again, but must not make its landing unmanageable. The two Spider-Men share upgrades inside a **single-player** game; that is not evidence that its progression solves multiplayer fairness. Boogle's room-wide policy follows its own drop-in requirements.

### Breath of the Wild: stamina upgrades reach without replacing the paraglider

**[V]** The [official Piggyback guide, Navigation and Character Progression](https://www.piggyback.com/online-guide/the-legend-of-zelda/en/) documents receiving the paraglider after the Plateau objectives, flight distance constrained by stamina, stamina vessels, deliberate closing/reopening, and updrafts. The flat reserve increase affects climbing as well as glide; updrafts add a qualitative environmental interaction without being a paraglider upgrade tier. Familiar cliffs become better launch routes, while distant crossings become feasible through sufficient height and reserve. Nintendo's [GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024562/Change-and-Constant-Breaking-Conventions) is relevant context only; its full talk was not inspected.

**[P] Boogle:** preserve the baseline's immediate control and height economy. Do not add stamina depletion just to have something to sell. Faster cloth supplies a visible range improvement; an optional later lift-sail can use deliberately authored vents. Making vent riding a later upgrade is **our departure**, not a claimed BotW rule. Its additional content and feedback cost must be justified in the proof.

### Celeste: more expressive movement does not require many purchased powers

**[V]** Maddy Thorson's [_Celeste & Forgiveness_](https://www.mattmakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html) explains coyote time, buffered jumps, corner correction and other concessions that help intended actions succeed. The [GDC level-design abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024307/Level-Design-Workshop-Designing-Celeste) concerns arranging hundreds of stages; it does not establish a numeric rule for upgrade frequency. **[G]** Most campaign variety comes from room mechanics and growing mastery of a small kit; the later second dash is a major count change. Celeste is not a model of mandatory Metroidvania backtracking: revisits mainly serve optional mastery and collecting.

**[P] Boogle:** forgiveness belongs in the starting kit. Do not charge for reliable targeting, jump buffering or safe hook arrivals. One extra jump can be a large payoff; it does not justify Celeste's precision difficulty. Air-charge feedback, generous landing space and a short failure return are prerequisites, not premium upgrades.

### Ori: a new behavior can multiply the value of an existing jump

**[V]** Xbox's [developer demonstration of the Ginso Tree](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2014/08/14/gamescom-ori-preview/) explains acquiring Bash, using it to redirect projectiles and traverse, then combining it with double jumps and wall movement. It records immediate puzzle use and a later escape sequence. **[G]** The series also contrasts double/triple-jump count gains with qualitative Bash, glide and grapple interactions. High passages and redirectable objects make previously visited terrain newly useful; exact skill-tree prices are not part of this report.

**[P] Boogle:** the earned jump's use during glide is already a successful combination to preserve. Test a third jump with the upgraded canopy, not only in isolation. Do not copy Bash's world-freezing aiming into a live shared room. A stationary hook clamp can offer time to aim while the rest of the world continues, with no precision countdown.

### A Short Hike: repeated flat upgrades work when terrain expresses thresholds

**[V]** Adam Robinson-Yu's [first-person development post](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/11/13/the-award-winning-a-short-hike-hits-ps4-november-16/) says movement and terrain developed together. He divided the world by elevation, with golden-feather stamina governing access; the summit rewards more exploration while remaining possible with very few feathers for a skilled player. The [official game page](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/a-short-hike/) describes feathers helping flight and access to heights. The [GDC postmortem listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1028679/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A) establishes the small-scope development context, not feather counts or tuning.

**[P] Boogle:** this is a strong counterexample to “flat upgrades are boring.” The same small addition can pay off repeatedly when it buys another rest ledge or a new descent line. But Boogle has three movement abilities competing for attention and a railway economy. Use a few large, recognizable thresholds rather than a feather hunt feeding a fourth economy. No exact feather total is needed to make that distinction.

### Sable: leaving the kit alone can protect the appeal of a place

**[V]** Creative director Gregorios Kythreotis [describes opening the world after departure and making quests optional](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/11/29/awaken-your-wanderlust-with-open-world-adventure-sable-out-today-on-ps5/). His [WIRED interview](https://www.wired.com/story/sable-game-interview-shedworks-creative-director/) supplies context for the exploration-first design; its account describes the protective gliding bubble and vertical routes. **[G]** The glider is primarily a stable freedom/safe-descent tool; climbing stamina progression changes access to launch heights rather than providing a long series of wing-speed levels. Exact stamina rewards were not verified.

**[P] Boogle:** do not assume every ability needs an upgrade in every region. A more interesting tower can improve glide play without altering the canopy. Revisit value can come from a newly opened lift, a view or a route home. This is also the fallback if the proposed lift-sail does not justify a new target/vent grammar: cut that tier, retain the launch site as a world improvement, and reduce the ladder to seven acquisition moments.

## Synthesis: count consequences, not skill-tree nodes

### Flat and qualitative are descriptions, not quality rankings

**[P]** Use the [progression brief's kinds](../brainstorms/progression.md#best-suggestions): **F** for flat gains, **A** for new personal movement behavior, **R** for a lasting route. Classify by the principal effect. A third jump is F/count even though it can unlock a qualitatively different route; a relay hook is A/behavior. A bridge opened using either is an R payoff, not another personal ability purchase.

| Upgrade question                              | Good case                                                                            | Noise or failure case                                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Does a flat gain cross a readable threshold?  | One longer pull reaches the old opposite-bank ring.                                  | Five +5% upgrades on indistinguishable gaps.                |
| Does a new behavior work in several contexts? | A fixed relay helps a roof shortcut, an optional cavern and a later machinery route. | A special head used on one special door, then forgotten.    |
| Is backtracking voluntary and rewarding?      | A train brings you back to a memorable near-miss and a short route now opens.        | Search every old wall after every unlock.                   |
| Does it preserve the toy?                     | Extra jump extends a move players already enjoy.                                     | Baseline steering is made poor to sell the fix.             |
| Is the count affordable in authored content?  | One lesson, two earlier-space uses and one forward application per tier.             | A large tree whose nodes have no distinct places to matter. |

There is **no researched universal maximum**. The proposed **1/2/2 post-acquisition budget** follows Boogle's eight-hour scope, three existing abilities and construction/exploration split. Eight acquisitions across 480 minutes average 60 minutes apart, but the actual schedule is deliberately uneven. A mechanic can develop between awards through mastery, construction and combinations. The report does not propose an hourly vending machine.

Before accepting a tier, name its first-use space, at least **two earlier-route applications**, and one later use. This is an authoring hypothesis, not a published industry rule. A modest speed tier may reopen efficiency rather than access; label that honestly. If testers cannot distinguish a proposed setting or remember any changed route, merge it into a larger step or cut it. Do not increase the purchase price to manufacture significance.

### Make backtracking a return trip, not an errand list

**[P]** Reveal future possibilities from the railway: a ring across a gap, a rooftop flag above a known landing, or a vent with a visible top exit. Use consistent shapes and moving air cues, not color alone. At acquisition, orient the small stop's exit toward one of those remembered places. Let the return coincide with exporting the new material or checking the town's growth.

Every return needs both an arrival and an exit at the lowest eligible tier. It should yield a view, optional personal find, permanent shortcut or useful interaction. Do not move an already-earned prize behind the new requirement. Preserve the [finders' bay](../game-design/place.md#ancient-technology-round-4-exploring) recovery policy rather than making missed attendance cost a reward. Later players may still explore the original route for its experience.

Limit new **main-line** ability gates to two proposed campaign beats: a forgiving double-jump route to a latch in Split Shell, and a relay route to a latch in Exhibition. They are part of the main expedition, never prizes for a compulsory mini-game. Both latch permanently. Speed cloth, long cable and triple jump retain baseline/alternate access to required work; the lift-sail is optional. Main-line gating is therefore not proportional to the number of upgrades.

### Teach at small stops, including upgrades purchased in town

**[P]** An acquisition's first action should be available immediately, within sight of the equipment. Use a short sequence:

1. **Show the change:** a reachable target now answers, or one additional jump visibly remains.
2. **Safe action:** broad dry floor below, no freight at risk, no timed pursuer, no precision edge.
3. **One variation:** change height, direction or landing—not all three.
4. **Transfer:** use the upgrade toward a familiar landmark outside the lesson.

Proposed lesson budget: **30–90 seconds**, with an optional **2–4-minute** side course. These are upper planning allowances, not waits or completion conditions. The purchase bench needs a nearby trial lane too. A late joiner can enter any lesson without resetting it or holding the group. Use brief device-correct prompts when necessary; never a long modal upgrade explanation.

## Tuning and feel: the combinations are the real risk

**[V/P]** The starting constants below come from `MOVE`; candidate changes are hypotheses. The proposed [tunables interface](tunables.md#one-small-typed-registry--proposed) is the intended authoring direction, not an interface this report implements. Keep one owner for each value. New identifiers below are descriptive requirements, not existing exported names.

| Risk                                | Why it matters                                                                                   | Parameters and proof required before a build is accepted                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Extra jumps become flight           | Jump gains height; glide converts it into distance. A refilling clamp could make that unlimited. | Air-charge count, impulse floor, grounded refill rule, jump buffer, steering, ceiling clearance. No refill merely from opening a canopy or clamping a hook. Test two nearby anchors and repeated open/close. |
| Faster glide misses old landings    | Increasing 10 to 12.5 m/s raises ideal braking distance at 12 m/s² from 4.17 to 6.51 m.          | Speed, acceleration, braking, angular speed/response, overspeed decay, camera response, minimum landing dimensions. Keep low-stick control. Compare brake unchanged versus 15 m/s² (5.21 m ideal stop).      |
| Lower sink silently bypasses gates  | Range is coupled to speed, height, deployment and jump count.                                    | Sink, launch height, arrival margin and every reachable route at every supported tier combination. Do not simultaneously buff speed, sink and jump impulse in the first comparison.                          |
| Longer hook selects the wrong ring  | More eligible rings share the same view cone; tiny distant silhouettes invite errors.            | Range, cone, target priority/hysteresis, visibility, body-clear path, destination clearance. Hold the 25° cone and 16 m/s cruise for the first 24→32 m comparison.                                           |
| Fast reel feels like lost control   | Speed without enough braking can obscure the arrival or collide with a ledge.                    | Acceleration, brake distance, cruise, cancel exit caps, stall timeout and camera framing. Keep the current speed while testing range.                                                                        |
| Relay feels like the rejected swing | Dangling motion or elastic release would change the toy's identity.                              | Fixed clamp pose, zero pendulum motion, valid next target, explicit cancel, no auto-fire, no charge refill. Unlimited aiming time; the world continues.                                                      |
| Updraft feels arbitrary             | An invisible lift volume reads like an exception to gravity.                                     | Visible plume footprint, lift speed, vertical cap, entry/exit blend and safe side exits. A visible upgraded canopy must explain the new interaction. No moving weather simulation needed.                    |
| Friends cannot use the same lesson  | Occupied anchors, tiny pads or a camera seizure create waiting.                                  | Nonexclusive anchors, room for arrivals, no required partner timing, local prompts/reveals. Two simultaneous arrivals, one idle learner, one departing friend.                                               |

**Simple arithmetic, not reachability proof [P].** At steady speed with no acceleration or obstacles, `range = height × forwardSpeed / sinkSpeed`. From a 20 m height budget, today's 10/3.2 ratio gives **62.5 m**, while 12.5/3.2 gives **78.1 m**, a 25% increase. The game's launch, jump apex, controller transitions and collision reduce or alter that envelope. Author landings well inside the measured usable range; do not put a required target at the formula's limit. The ideal braking calculations use `distance = speed² / (2 × brake)` and ignore current turn/controller interactions.

For every tier, measure early/late jump, neutral-stick fall, full-stick approach, fast-fall deployment, hook cancellation, crowded targets, wall-adjacent arrivals, water recovery and departure from a moving train. Include keyboard, controller and two-thumb touch before accepting control changes. Keep forgiving control available at tier zero. Preserve the glider's existing stop/reopen and unused-jump behavior through all upgrades.

**Protect the railway's role [P].** Personal travel can become much freer without material export becoming free. Let traversal reveal the next resource or machinery solution early; railway connection still supplies its goods. Do not add arbitrary movement penalties to cargo as a patch without a separate design decision. Compare an actual loaded hand-trip with the automated network; if movement upgrades erase the network's benefit, change service/layout design before inflating bills or imposing invisible barriers.

## Co-op fairness and arriving late

The requested reference set is overwhelmingly single-player. It supports movement and teaching patterns, **not empirical claims about multiplayer fairness**. The policy below follows Boogle's principles, [upgrade economy's co-op contract](upgrade-economy.md#co-op-sharing-contract), and [production core's state and authority](../plans/production-core.md#state-and-authority). No new wire shape, save schema or authority protocol is specified here.

| Situation                                                    | Proposed player-facing result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One friend discovers an ability or installs a fitting        | All current and future room members gain that capability. The discoverer gets the immediate physical moment; others get a small cue and their own optional practice/reveal.                                                                                                       |
| A friend arrives at hour six with no personal upgrades       | They inherit the room's functional kit immediately. A safe nearby practice loop is available; repeating the first five hours is not required. Personal finds/cosmetics remain theirs to discover.                                                                                 |
| Friends choose purchases in a different order                | Purchases are shared installations funded by explicit material contributions. Nobody spends another player's personal wallet. Required base routes remain usable before optional fittings.                                                                                        |
| Friends have different visual equipment or learning progress | Appearance and tutorials may differ; permanent reach/speed/count within the same room do not depend on attendance. Never confuse an unseen reveal with an unowned ability.                                                                                                        |
| A future system permits a weaker optional loadout            | Design the required route for the room's guaranteed capability, offer a free way to restore that kit, and keep a usable lower-tier return path. Do not balance compulsory co-op around someone carrying the “right” hook. This is contingency guidance, not approval of loadouts. |
| One joins from a more advanced room                          | Use the destination room's progression; do not import campaign-breaking power. Cross-room accounts/imports remain outside scope.                                                                                                                                                  |
| The expert opens a route while others are elsewhere          | The gate stays open. The original puzzle and nearby practice can still be tried personally, without closing the gate for everyone.                                                                                                                                                |
| Two players arrive at one hook target                        | Both can use it; no last-hit award or exclusive anchor reservation. Broad destinations and safe separation need a physical proof.                                                                                                                                                 |
| A friend leaves during a puzzle                              | Any required latch remains reachable and solvable solo. No held two-person switch or absent inventory key can block progression.                                                                                                                                                  |
