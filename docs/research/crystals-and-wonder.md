# Rare materials and wonder on the railway

> **Verdict:** One extraordinary crystal property inside familiar construction; lift first.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Test one impossible property inside familiar railway work. 2. Make rarity geographical and discovery generous. 3. Keep the material visible from source to installed part.
> **Status:** partly adopted. See [The shape of the game](../decisions.md#the-shape-of-the-game).

_Research report, 2 November 2026. Rare materials, a possible fantastic element, distant promises across a roughly eight-hour rail journey, and discovering today's small island as an already inhabited place. Live web research and repository inspection; options for the owner and narrative collaborators, not approved fiction, a build plan or implemented mechanics._

**Recommendation:** try one extraordinary property inside familiar construction. A crystal should make a particular piece of railway do something players can see, and its source should be a place worth reaching. Keep the source beautiful after collecting from it. First compare this with a mostly natural mineral treatment; the owner's delight in a floating crystal is permission to investigate, not a decision that Boogle needs magic everywhere.

**Verification, following [maximal-rewards](maximal-rewards.md) and [compounding-infrastructure](compounding-infrastructure.md).** **[V]** means fetched primary-source text, with its kind stated; **[C]** means inspected repository code or documents; **[S]** means secondary or search-extract evidence; **[H]** means hypothesis, interpretation or proposed Boogle design. All candidate material properties, timings, quantities and player reactions are **[H]**. No reference game was played, film rewatched, talk video watched, Boogle prototype run or eight-hour campaign measured for this report. Alex Beachum's 2013 thesis was downloaded and its relevant sections read; it describes the student version, not the finished 2019 game's exact rules. GDC session abstracts and Sky's extracted slide text have narrower evidential scope than a watched talk.

## The decisions this must fit

**[C]** [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) makes rail the core loop: connect camps, bring goods closer, carry materials for each track segment, watch towns grow and gain reach. This report serves **Toys before chores**, **Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated**, **Telegraph everything**, **Designed for drop-in, drop-out**, and **Leave something for the next visitor**. No principle change is proposed. Nothing decays, no hazards threaten progress, and optional toys or mini-games cannot become access requirements.

The [2 November audit and later entries](../decisions.md) take precedence over earlier research:

- Six eras: wood; steam; iron and harbour; diesel and concrete; electric with pantographs and wires; future trains, without a mandatory monorail or levitation choice. A key connection ends an era.
- The first far island is reached on rail built outward over the sea. No material on that shore can be required to reach it. Preset links offer an order of work; the final Home–port link closes the ring. Home becomes a busy town, while the mainland is an existing metropolis.
- Keep the physicality that landed in `?proto=place`: delivered objects become sleepers, rails and buildings. The network reduces repeat fetching; it does not remove the player's contribution to each new segment.
- The owner read the beacon as “a cool giant floating crystal” and said “it made me feel like there might be a fantasy element in the future, which is cool.” Earlier leanings included crystals or gems used in the rail and advanced materials worth exploring for. The decision log keeps that possibility alive with the far shard; it does not settle the material, its origin or its powers.
- A roughly minute-long crossing at 1.2 km and 20 m/s is the owner's starting point. Eight hours is the larger experience, not permission to stretch every ride or repeat a collection job.
- Generous, distinctive rewards are wanted. Their system remains undesigned. Story details, the ending, the mayor role and opening-day gates remain parked; existing Boogler lines stay intact.
- The old island as the first far island is a **suggestion under open question 17**, not a settled map change. The Boogler appearing in different places is **open question 18**, not an established itinerary.

**What the earlier reports contribute.** [Discovery progression](discovery-progression.md) separates learning, collecting and building; retain that distinction, not its old three-lead gate, required group chord or water examples. [Maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md) supplies generous personal finds and source provenance; its 30–40 finds/hour is a proposal, not a crystal demand. [Era progression](era-progression.md) supplies silhouette and retained heritage, not its eight-era schedule or ceremonies. [Compounding infrastructure](compounding-infrastructure.md) supplies the return benefit and bootstrap rule; its earlier hauling opt-outs are superseded. The directions here are alternatives to react to, not silent amendments to that proposal.

## What the references establish

### Death Stranding: give the impossible material an infrastructural job

**[V, developer guide]** Kojima Productions documents chiral crystals among the racetrack's construction inputs and chiral crystal energy driving the Director's Cut jump ramp. Its chiral bridge opens terrain where a foundation-based bridge cannot be placed. These are concrete uses of fantastic matter beside ordinary cargo and machinery ([beginner's guide, parts 3–4](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/en/death-stranding-directors-cut-beginners-guide)). **[S]** Indexed community documentation describes floating carriers consuming crystals to float; a fetched guide confirms auto-pavers use crystals alongside metals and ceramics. Exact consumption and yields are not adopted here ([carrier, search extract](https://deathstranding.fandom.com/wiki/Floating_Carrier), [road guide](https://www.pushsquare.com/guides/death-stranding-how-to-build-roads)). **[V, developer interview]** Kojima connects DS2's monorails to the appeal of building infrastructure ([PlayStation interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/05/08/death-stranding-2-an-interview-with-hideo-kojima/)).

**[H] For Boogle:** a material is strongest when it changes the construction possibility, not just the recipe colour. Put an unusual mineral in a visible lifting shoe, bridge bearing or optical signal. Leave ordinary timber and steel doing recognizable work. Do not inherit crystal fuel consumption, repairs, hostile harvesting or an exchange terminal that turns the crystal into universal purchasing power. Boogle can make the installed part permanent.

### Tears of the Kingdom: understandable magic and restrained sky silhouettes

**[V, Nintendo developer interview]** The team describes Zonai devices as supernatural powers made legible through familiar forms, including an electric fan and a battery. It also explains why visible glue and a consistent attachment sound make assembly understandable and satisfying. Magical origin does not excuse unreadable operation ([Ask the Developer, part 4](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-4/)).

**[V, same interview series]** Too many sky islands cluttered the view; small, correctly scaled islands could read as debris from below. The team also describes cave entrances changing how players inspect familiar cliffs ([part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-3/)).

**[H] For Boogle:** a lift crystal belongs in a clamp that visibly holds a load. One suspended mass above a conventional coast can carry the fantasy promise; a sky full of tiny shards weakens it. Finding one seam should teach players a recognizable geological sign they might notice elsewhere. Borrow the causal readability of Zonai construction, not a complete freeform vehicle editor or a second crafting economy.

### Outer Wilds: wonder is a question with evidence, not merely a strange shape

**[V, creator's thesis, pp. 4, 7–11 and 18–22]** Beachum describes worn expedition equipment and familiar geography supporting alien exploration; distant curiosities connected by clues; and Wind Waker's Lenzo gallery inspiring voluntary journeys. An early introduction failed because an anomaly appeared before players knew the village: everything was equally unfamiliar. The revised village established context and encouraged exploration. These are documented development lessons from the student game ([thesis PDF, public mirror](https://sbekumod.github.io/Assets/OWtesi.pdf)). **[V, abstract only]** Kelsey Beachum's later GDC session frames narrative as support for curiosity-led progression ([GDC 2021](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027368/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity)).

**[H] For Boogle:** establish a working railway before asking a floating stone to be extraordinary. Give the landmark a question, an accessible piece of evidence and an answer players can use. Unlike Outer Wilds, Boogle has tangible construction progression; a friend sharing an answer should help rather than destroy the game. Do not transplant its dangerous world, reset loop or knowledge-only finale.

### Subnautica: new materials make the next expedition possible

**[V, developer's game description]** Exploring deeper and farther yields rarer resources for more advanced equipment; habitats become operating bases. The world also asks who made its strange structures ([Unknown Worlds' Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/264710/Subnautica/)). **[V, abstract only]** Charlie Cleveland's GDC session explicitly covers crafting, mysterious tooltips and radio signals giving a sandbox structure without excessive direction ([The Design of Subnautica](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025745/The-Design-of-Subnauticann)).

**[H] For Boogle:** combine a place-based material lead with a practical frontier benefit. An outcrop on an already reached island can supply the longer-span kit for the next link. Teach its use near the find, so the player does not wander with an unexplained inventory icon. The equivalent of a deeper dive is a new railway reach, not oxygen pressure, predation or survival preparation. A clear small objective and voluntary investigation can coexist.

### Astroneer: the railway carries the means to explore

**[V, developer release notes, version 1.25.147.0]** Rails support autonomous trains, automatic station loading/unloading, oxygen and power along the line, and missions investigating different planetary environments and a larger mystery ([Rails Update](https://blog.astroneer.space/p/rails-update/)).

**[H] For Boogle:** make connecting the mineral camp the moment exploratory scarcity becomes dependable supply. The source's identity stays visible in a cradled cargo piece and its destination fitting. Adopt the support relationship, not oxygen, wiring puzzles or the update's separate large-quantity reward event. The railway should release the explorer from revisiting the same vein for every project.

### Palworld: material identity can give an island a reason to exist

**[V, developer release notes, Feybreak v0.4.11]** Hexolite Quartz is described as exclusive to Feybreak; Chromite is underground and found through detection. The update also adds production and research features ([Pocketpair announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1623730/announcements/detail/518574472406499343); [fetched Steam text](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?enddate=1734919898&feed=steam_community_announcements)). This establishes geographical specificity, not proof that its costs are pleasant or its latest economy unchanged.

**[H] For Boogle:** the owner-liked material fantasy can be “that coast has something our home does not.” First discovery can be guaranteed at an authored source rather than a rare random drop. A new colour of ore with the same old job is insufficient. Give a later material a different visible use, or keep using the first mineral in a better machine. No required combat, detection sweep or long ore-processing ladder follows from the reference.

### Horizon: extraordinary machinery can belong to an ordinary landscape

**[V, developer statement]** Guerrilla describes combining natural materials with machine parts in a world of reclaimed ruins ([Hermen Hulst's introduction](https://blog.playstation.com/2015/06/15/announcing-horizon-zero-dawn/)). **[V, developer interview]** Machine designs were tested as crude models; animalistic forms and detachable components evolved together with their gameplay ([making the machines](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/24/the-making-of-horizon-zero-dawns-machines/)).

**[H] For Boogle:** show the same strange material fitted to ordinary wood, rivets and concrete across eras. That continuity makes the future train feel descended from earlier work. Ancient machinery is one possible explanation, not an obligatory lost civilization. Boogle needs neither machine hunting nor a catastrophe to make an unexplained old fitting interesting.

### Wind Waker's Great Sea: the gaps make the places distinct

**[V, Nintendo developer interview]** The sea setting supported distinct islands and eccentric inhabitants, while the team also acknowledges the original's later pacing drag ([Iwata Asks, part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/The-Legend-of-Zelda-The-Wind-Waker-HD/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But-/3-The-First-Part-Is-Perfect-But--807230.html)). Beachum's Lenzo example above provides a concrete route from a story about a place to wanting to visit it.

**[H] For Boogle:** water separates identities and makes arrival count. A cave mineral, a peculiar loading cradle or a view from a window can point across the bay without adding a quest giver. Each reached shore needs local pleasures and an inhabited scale. More sea is not more mystery; repeat rides should benefit from the improving network and hub travel, without compulsory onboard activities.

### Laputa and Nausicaä: two different fantastic directions

**[V, official distributor synopses]** In Castle in the Sky, an engineer's apprentice encounters a girl held aloft by a glowing pendant, and the crystal's mystery leads toward Laputa ([GKIDS](https://gkids.com/films/castle-in-the-sky/)). Nausicaä places a peaceful valley beside a strange forest and frames its protagonist through care for living things amid conflict ([GKIDS](https://gkids.com/films/nausicaa-of-the-valley-of-the-wind/)). These establish story premises, not Miyazaki's design intentions or detailed engineering rules.

**[H] Laputa direction:** one small object's impossible property makes a huge destination imaginable; practical engineering gives that property a place in daily life. **[H] Nausicaä direction:** a strange landscape becomes understandable through attention and care, rather than simply being a mine. In Boogle, either could support a friendly railway world. Living mineral gardens and natural shed fragments are an original option, not a mechanism claimed for Nausicaä. Keep war, poison, destruction and compulsory ecological maintenance out of this rail brief.

### Journey and Sky: a destination, a mood and company

**[V, developer game description]** Journey establishes the distant mountain as the destination and possible companionship along the way ([thatgamecompany](https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/)). **[V, extracted GDC slide text, PDF pp. 13, 17, 25 and 34]** Yuichiro Tanabe's Sky presentation links humanity, wonder and community, and organizes environmental progression through time of day, life stages and civilization ([Art of Sky](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcsummer2020/presentations/Tanabe-Yuichiro-Art%20of%20Sky_%20Children%20of%20the%20Light.pdf)). Those slides are not evidence of a particular player-retention effect.

**[H] For Boogle:** use the port as the persistent long-range destination, with the shard or island as the current unanswered question. Vary the emotional context of an approach through scale, inhabited detail and light. Give friends room to stand together and look. No need to copy Journey's adversity or Sky's economy, timed events or party-dependent doors.

### Myst and familiar-place returns: revelation can change the viewer

**[V, developer description]** Myst connects an island to distinct Ages through books, devices and observation ([Cyan](https://cyan.com/games/myst/)). **[V, Nintendo interview]** TotK's team explicitly wanted new surprises without erasing familiar Hyrule, while supporting newcomers too; Dohta recalls the idea of a location becoming a recurring character ([Ask the Developer, part 1](https://www.nintendo.com/ph/interview/totk/index.html)).

**[H] For Boogle:** return with a new way to understand or use something, while retaining its recognizable setting. An old cave's mineral becomes meaningful after seeing its installed counterpart. The island should make sense to a new player even if a veteran recognizes every corner. Borrow Myst's feeling that objects and places belong to a larger whole; keep its opaque puzzle locks out of the compulsory railway. A compact, welcoming place can be a reward without becoming a museum of references.

## How wonder fits a grounded toy world

**[H] Use a familiar action, one unfamiliar consequence, then a consistent explanation through use.** Carry a clamped stone as you carry logs. Install it with the same readable assembly. Let the installed shoe lift a heavy beam a little before the crane guides it into place. A goofy wobble can occur in the decorative load; it must not knock a rider off or consume progress. The property is extraordinary, while the player's intention remains obvious.

Leave the **origin** open; make the **next action and benefit** clear. “A lighter span can cross that gap” satisfies Telegraph everything without explaining who formed the mineral. If the player cannot tell what the object does, lore will not fix the mechanical ambiguity. Conversely, if every specimen explains the whole world's history, there is nothing left to wonder about.

## One material: from seeing it to building with it

**[H] Separate four meanings of rarity.** The source can be geographically uncommon; the first encounter can be surprising; a personal specimen can have an unusual appearance; routine construction supply can be reliable. These need not share one drop probability. Call the test material **liftstone** only as a working label, with its origin left blank.

The smallest complete chain is:

1. **Notice:** on an already reachable headland, a stone rests above its cracked bed and the distant shard has a similar silhouette. An ordinary tool and carrying rack establish scale.
2. **Try:** one short, forgiving harvest action releases a guaranteed clamped sample. The main formation stays. A nearby workshop test load demonstrates the property; experimenting is optional, not a skill test or mini-game gate.
3. **Use:** carry that sample to an obvious empty shoe on the construction rig. The crystal remains visible behind its retaining cage as a beam rises. A new longer-span kit becomes intelligible through the action.
4. **Connect:** extend the ordinary line to this local camp. Camp connection makes future fittings available through freight. It does not require repeatedly collecting more samples to keep the camp alive.
5. **Build again:** a crystal-bearing fitting arrives beside logs and rails at the frontier. The player carries the segment's mixed supply and sees every fitting installed. Supply may refill, but the next project waits for players; the existing one-project absence cap still applies.
6. **Return:** see the same crystal shape doing a bigger job in a familiar workshop or bridge. Understanding grows without introducing another arbitrary ore tier.

The sample does **not** pay for the first crossing to its own island. Reach the shore with existing timber/metal technology; its mineral helps the next connection. The exceptional horizon formation stays intact and publicly accessible. No player can take the last key and strand the room.

**[H] Visible construction grammar:** outcrop → recognizable clamped fragment → train cradle → exposed fitting → visible lifting effect. Preserve at least the fragment's profile and its cage through processing. A factory can cut or polish it in view; a generic sack disappearing into a progress ring loses the material's identity. [`place/pieces.ts`](../../src/proto/place/pieces.ts), [`stations.ts`](../../src/proto/place/stations.ts), [`forge.ts`](../../src/proto/place/forge.ts) and [`rail.ts`](../../src/proto/place/rail.ts) are the inspected presentation references. `PieceKind` currently supports only logs and rails; the existing system is not already a mixed-mineral implementation.

### What crystals should never become

**[H] Keep the discovery generous and the repeated job short.** Reject a universal gem wallet, a price on every interaction, a fuel meter, a daily respawn circuit, a random critical-path drop, a rare blueprint needed to advance, and a final demand for hundreds of the original specimen. A shiny tier that only raises yield and price together has not added exploration. Removing the landmark to fund progress also destroys the thing the next visitor came to see.

This does not diminish the maximal-reward direction. [Personal rewards](maximal-rewards.md) can arrive generously with source-themed crystalline hats, carriage fittings or specimens, including optional useful traits if that system is chosen. A personal rarity roll neither consumes communal construction material nor determines whether the normal railway can be completed. Give each friend a claimable memento; do not make them race for the shared world's only pretty rock. Keep origin labels truthful and unranked.

For the **first experiment [H]**, use one source, one guaranteed sample, one installed socket and one subsequent freight-supported build. Record manual source revisits and fetching time; do not inflate bills to create eight hours. If the player describes it only as “blue wood,” revise its function before multiplying materials. If mining feels good but they resent the second identical supply trip, improve the depot handoff rather than raising rewards to mask it.

## Keeping a distant promise alive across eight hours

**[H] A mystery needs intermediate answers.** Repeatedly showing the same unreachable icon for eight hours is deferral. Offer three scales of promise: the work visible ahead, the next island, and the mainland beyond the network. The current question should change after an expedition: what is that → what can it do → how does it belong here → what can we make with it? Its eventual origin can remain a collaborator's choice.

The table uses [six-eras](../proposals/six-eras.md)'s **hypothetical** 45/60/90/100/95/90-minute budget. Events advance with connections, not wall-clock gates. This is a storyboard, not an additional quest schedule or a claim that the campaign is built.

**Pay the visual promise literally.** If the player was drawn by a suspended crystal, arrival should allow seeing its suspension from below, approaching its surface or standing beside a smaller related piece, and using the learned property. A sealed pedestal, chest of unrelated coins or distant backdrop behind an invisible wall is a weak answer. Preserve the silhouette in its near version; don't swap the shard for an unrelated building at arrival. A quiet local encounter can follow the grand approach.

**Separate the first-island reveal from the campaign-scale landmark.** Today's island is compact and friendly; the current far ring implies a huge, unusual place. They need not be one destination. Prefer testing the old island as an early inhabited landing, with the shard still visible beyond it. Alternatively, put one related sample in its existing cave while keeping the giant formation on another shore. Neither option requires retconning the whole island into an ancient installation.

**Revisits should change interpretation or usefulness, not reset work.** The same shore seen from a faster train, an old cave mineral recognized in a new machine, a new connection seen from the lighthouse, or a safe overlook reached with later movement can each justify returning. Keep the thing previously loved intact. Repeated approaches may use new views or ordinary changes in light, but no event waits for a specific real-world hour. Allow quiet; a succession of mandatory gags would reinstate the cut wagon show.

## Finding the small island, already lived in

**[H] The reveal should say “we joined somewhere,” not “we generated another worksite.”** Approach from a new angle. First recognize its low coast and lighthouse; then notice the paths, windmills and objects left ready for use; finally walk off the train into a place that has a life independent of the arriving crew. Make the platform a small addition at the edge, not a replacement of the familiar centre.

The [as-built island](../game-design/island.md), [POIs](../game-design/pois.md) and [dino/guest](../game-design/dino-and-guest.md) already supply the material for that feeling: a lighthouse frisbee, ball, windmill sails, cave pond and cellar, and the guest Easter egg. Preserve their availability and the existing copy. Do not turn finding the guest, playing a toy or talking to The Boogler into the mineral key. No new nostalgic plaque or explanation is necessary for the first comparison.

There are **two audiences**. Existing testers can recognize the old Boogle island and feel a return. New players have never lived there; their reward is discovering a compact, welcoming place, not remembering it. For them, the later revisit creates familiarity: they remember the cave, see their completed connection from its lighthouse, and understand what has changed. Test both readings; a veteran's recognition does not establish a newcomer's desire to stay.

An inhabited place needs continuity, not an obligation to restore it. Keep recognizable landmark relationships, ordinary objects and accessible routes. Add one useful railway service at the landing if progression needs one; don't demand rebuilding the whole island before its pleasures work. Any future relocation needs a dependency and collision audit, including the cave entrances and guest setup. [`caves/site.ts`](../../src/pois/caves/site.ts) currently uses an identity site frame with world-space stamps, so moving a root mesh is not enough to move the whole place correctly.

**The Boogler stays a narrative option.** Possible roles: familiar resident on this island; travelling acquaintance at several stops; or someone encountered later whose previous traces become recognizable. Each can support the same mechanical sequence. His presence cannot be a missable construction permission. Preserve every existing line in [`boogler/lines.ts`](../../src/boogler/lines.ts) and surrounding dialogue modules; do not draft a new origin, prophecy, mayor ceremony or explanation of the shard here. [`boogler/boogler.ts`](../../src/boogler/boogler.ts) has Host-driven wandering, not a persistent cross-island story itinerary. If one is chosen later, it needs its own design and state contract.

## Production and evidence boundaries

**[H] Keep the first comparison small:** one source silhouette, one carried piece, one receiving machine, one usable outcome. Work in a flag beside the existing place/crossing experiments. This report does not require an asset pipeline rewrite, a new world simulation, a general crafting system or production streaming. A tiny test can prove comprehension; it cannot prove eight-hour pacing.

Use the current [render contract](../../src/render/README.md), [entity contract](../../src/entity/entity.ts) and [world-rule recipe](../../src/world/rules/README.md) when a direction graduates. Cosmetic crystal light and dust are Local; permanent source connections, accepted material delivery and installed capabilities must survive a late join. Never grant material from a visual landing callback, treat a queued edit as accepted, or introduce shared Rapier physics into Lockstep. Those are existing repository constraints, not new architecture recommendations. [`sim/materials.ts`](../../src/sim/materials.ts)'s old age-indexed catalogue is not authority for a new one-material-per-era economy.

Implementers check source exhaustion, simultaneous delivery, disconnects, late joins, safe solo access and geometry themselves. New lockstep rules get determinism/conservation tests; a new sync contract may need a scenario. Visual experiments get [look captures and real-time observation](../../.claude/skills/look/SKILL.md), with the mineral, its assembly and its effect framed together; static additions also need `level-check`. These tests are future work, not claims made by this document. Do not tick [find-the-fun](../plans/find-the-fun.md)'s playtest boxes for writing research.

**Evidence limits:** the external sources establish mechanisms, intentions and production lessons, not an ideal Boogle reward rate or eight-hour curiosity curve. The detailed Death Stranding carrier/road mechanics have secondary support; no exact costs are imported. Palworld evidence is explicitly versioned. The inaccessible Outer Wilds narrative slides and Subnautica postmortem PDF are not used as if read; their accessible session abstracts are identified as such. Ghibli deductions are interpretations of the cited premises. The strongest direct match is Nintendo's account of understandable supernatural devices, paired with Beachum's warning to establish the ordinary world before introducing the anomaly.

## Numbered recommendations for Boogle

All are **[H]**, ranked in the order worth testing; each points to the code or proposal it would affect.

1. **Test one impossible property inside familiar railway work.** Prefer lift as the first comparison, with a mostly natural control; keep the function understandable and the origin undecided. Touches [`place/forge.ts`](../../src/proto/place/forge.ts), [`pieces.ts`](../../src/proto/place/pieces.ts) and [the fantasy reading](../decisions.md).
2. **Make rarity geographical and discovery generous.** One authored source guarantees the first useful sample; connecting its camp turns repeated supply into freight. Touches [six eras](../proposals/six-eras.md), [find-the-fun steps 6 and 9](../plans/find-the-fun.md) and [`place/stations.ts`](../../src/proto/place/stations.ts).
3. **Keep the material visible from source to installed part.** Carry, cradle, clamp and assembly should preserve its identity; its effect must change a build capability. 4. **Keep crystals out of currency, fuel and mandatory random-drop economies.** Permanent fittings and guaranteed construction supply can coexist with abundant personal specimen rewards. Touches [maximal rewards](maximal-rewards.md), [the audit picks](../decisions.md#the-audit-picks-2-november) and [`sim/materials.ts`](../../src/sim/materials.ts).

Touches [`place/land.ts`](../../src/proto/place/land.ts), [`layout.ts`](../../src/proto/place/layout.ts), [six eras](../proposals/six-eras.md) and [vast world scale](vast-world-scale.md).
6. **Preserve ordinary construction across all six eras.** Let a rare fitting improve selected machines while wood, metal, concrete and overhead wires retain clear jobs; no obligatory late exotic-ore bill. Touches [six eras](../proposals/six-eras.md) and the [electric/future decisions](../decisions.md).
7. **Compare the old island as an early inhabited discovery.** Keep its toys and Easter eggs usable on arrival, and test new-player appeal separately from veterans' recognition. 8. **Reward returning with recognition, new understanding and easier access.** Preserve landmarks and let a new route, viewpoint or familiar mineral use reveal change without repeating old costs. ## Four distinct directions to choose between

These are alternatives, not four systems to combine. Names are working labels. Each prototype is solo first, with one line of what's new and one gut question for the owner. The budgets below are hypotheses, not production estimates.

### A. Extraordinary geology — mostly non-fantasy

**Direction [H].** Mineral cliffs, geodes, coloured glass and old industrial workings provide wonder through scale and craft. A naturally striking crystal is cut into a signal lens; a denser ore becomes a visibly stronger fitting. Nothing floats without support. The beacon stays a guidance abstraction, and the horizon landmark could become a dramatic mineral spire with an industrial structure around it, subject to the owner's choice. The future train can remain wheel-on-rail. Closest references: Wind Waker's island identity, Myst's intriguing devices and Horizon's mixture of familiar materials.

**Smallest prototype [H], 3–5 minutes:** one walkable mineral alcove, one sample, a lens press beside a signal and a short piece of track. Carry the sample, watch it seat behind glass, then see the signal cast a distinctive pattern down the new line. No ore ladder, sorting or new reward ledger. In a second view, preserve the same skyline distance as the fantasy variant so brightness alone does not win the comparison.

**What's new:** “A mineral from that cliff becomes the signal beside your railway.”

**Gut question:** “Is that enough wonder to make you want to see the next island?”

**Agent observation:** can the player identify the source in the finished object, and do they look toward another place after using it? Risk: handsome decoration without a reason to explore; strengthen destination identity rather than secretly adding magic.

### B. Buoyant mineral — one impossible property, practical engineering

**Direction [H], preferred first fantasy test.** A few stones lift nearby loads when held in a fitting. People use ropes, cages, winches and rivets to do practical work with them. Start with a lifting shoe, later a lighter span-launching rig. A floating formation is the large natural counterpart. This draws directly on Laputa's small crystal/large destination relationship, Death Stranding's material use and the Zonai devices' readable function. It does not decide a magical civilization or require a hovering train.

**Smallest prototype [H], 4–6 minutes:** one low hovering source on a reachable shore, one sample, one rig and one short rail span. Install the sample; watch it lift a beam that the rig guides into position; walk or ride the completed span. A preconnected test depot supplies the fitting for a second identical span to show the eventual freight handoff, explicitly labelled in the test brief as a shortcut, not a finished camp simulation.

**What's new:** “The stone you found lifts the beam your railway is built from.”

**Gut question:** “Did that feel like discovering something this railway could really use?”

**Agent observation:** the material's cause is readable without explanation, no thrown object can remove the installed benefit, and the second build needs no source revisit. Risk: magic makes all structural work seem unnecessary; bound the effect to the visible rig, with clamps and normal foundations still doing work.

### C. The railway has predecessors — mechanical archaeology

**Direction [H].** The strange ring is associated with older infrastructure whose builders and history are undecided. Finds are recognizable components rather than mined fuel: a guide bearing, lens or levelling assembly fits a modern workshop cradle. The discovery is learning what something was for and putting it to use. Closest references: Myst's machinery, Outer Wilds' distributed evidence and Horizon's reused parts. This direction can remain ambiguous between ingenious engineering and fantasy.

**Smallest prototype [H], 4–6 minutes:** one worn component at a reachable site, a small intact demonstration mechanism beside it, and a new rail rig with a matching empty socket. Generous snap placement makes the fitting obvious; no symbol cipher or multi-room puzzle. Use a source model and an installed model with the same recognizable profile. A matching large outline across the bay supplies the next question.

**What's new:** “A part from an older line makes your new construction rig work.”

**Gut question:** “Did fitting that part make you curious about the place it came from?”

**Agent observation:** the player anticipates a function from the example and recognizes the distant match. Risk: a compulsory scavenger hunt or a fictional history decided by asset design. Guarantee the part, keep spare fittings available after connection, and leave inscriptions, factions and chronology blank for collaborators.

### D. Living geology — the landscape is a neighbour

**Direction [H].** Crystalline gardens or reef-like formations quietly respond to their surroundings and provide loose shed pieces. Light can travel through a fitted fragment, making station signals or a sheltered platform come alive. The fantasy is a relationship with a living landscape, not anti-gravity or abandoned technology. Nausicaä supplies the attention-to-ecology reference; Sky supplies social wonder. No crop care, population damage, pollution score or obligation to maintain the source.

**Smallest prototype [H], 3–5 minutes:** one luminous formation with an always-available shed piece, a short path and a station-light fitting. Carry the piece into the fitting; the light response now links station and formation. The source stays recognizable and complete after collection. No growth timer or ecology simulation; one authored response suffices to test the reading.

**What's new:** “A piece of the living shore lights the station without consuming its source.”

**Gut question:** “Did this feel like connecting to a living place?”

**Agent observation:** the player reads a relationship instead of a plant to farm; a second visitor can repeat the encounter. Risk: the loop becomes mandatory gardening, or the material is too abstract to fit railway construction. Keep harvesting simple and its installed use literal; if the feeling needs a care schedule, this direction is a poor fit.

**First comparison [H]: A and B on the same small route, with equal effort and comparable presentation.** Then, whichever material direction is preferred, separately test a minute-long approach to the old island followed by free exploration: “Did arriving here feel like discovering somewhere worth coming back to?” Keep that arrival test distinct from the material test so familiarity cannot conceal a weak crystal mechanic. C and D remain real alternatives if the owner prefers mystery through history or ecology. Narrative collaborators choose the explanation after the physical experience earns it.
