# Colour sources: put Neutral back in front

_3 November 2026. Live-source companion to the offline colour-and-post report; recommendations only, no renderer changes. Serves **Telegraph everything**, **Show, don't tell**, and **Small and readable**._

**Metered Neutral should lead the next comparison. Yes: the tested AgX configuration is costing Boogle some toy-colour immediacy.** Its deliberate bright-colour compression is a real trade-off, not necessarily a material mistake. The original board, reopened here, supports its own verdict that Neutral is the liveliest: stronger greens, warmer paths and a richer purple player. That does not establish AgX as the sole cause of dullness: the board lowered AgX about **0.375 EV** from production, and every curve retained dark tree faces and muddy stars. The proposed “AgX sunny toys” finish was never demonstrated. Keep the approved sky and AgX production control; change the ranking of hypotheses, not the owner's decision history.

## Recommendations, in order

1. **Compare three named looks:** unchanged production AgX; **metered Neutral / clean toys** (leading candidate); calibrated AgX / sunny toys (challenger, with selective midtone colour rather than another global 30% saturation lift). Use the same late-morning sky, scene radiance and bloom state in [water/post.ts](../../src/render/water/post.ts), replacing its actual output transform; changing `renderer.toneMapping` alone will not do it. First lock exposure to isolate the transform, then allow a recorded exposure trim per look to match subject brightness. Keep an untrimmed production reference. Judge Buddy, cream walls, foliage shade, cyan water, RGB/CMY ramps and bright glints together. **Which keeps the toys inviting and the sea luminous?**
2. **Fix shade and feedback alongside the curve.** In [frozen-sky.ts](../../src/render/water/frozen-sky.ts) and [place/look.ts](../../src/proto/place/look.ts), tune warm sun against readable cool fill with exposure locked; retain broad palette families. Keep the original report's separate star-radiance repair in [impact.ts](../../src/features/impact.ts), but its 8× probe is not an accepted value. No source below supplies a transferable numeric sun:shade ratio. Measure Boogle's lit/shaded reference patches; do not invent a Nintendo ratio or brighten every albedo.
3. **Keep one output owner and explicit colour domains.** Reuse [water-tuning.ts](../../src/debug/water-tuning.ts) and the [existing post owner](../../src/render/water/post.ts). Put exposure/white balance and any future portable HDR grade before display rendering; an SDR finishing grade after the curve remains legitimate, but is not Unreal's HDR grading workflow. Existing bloom now lives in [water/bloom.ts](../../src/render/water/bloom.ts), off by default here: the companion should not commission another implementation.
4. **Finish the SDR look before adding another transform or HDR output.** GT7 is now a credible later experiment if Neutral loses sky/glint gradation, not an automatic third implementation. Tony McMapface is another colour-preserving reference, not an instant saturation enhancer. Keep the [water-graduation plan](../plans/water-graduation.md) and [owner's look decisions](../proposals/boogle-2.md#look) as authority; this report approves no switch.

## HDR and wide gamut are real; Boogle is still SDR

**Chrome:** HDR canvas shipped in [129](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-129), using `rgba16float` and `toneMapping: {mode: 'extended'}`. [155–156 documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-155-156) also describes linear sRGB/P3 canvas spaces. **Safari:** [26.0 release notes](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/#hdr-images) explicitly include HDR in WebGPU canvas; [WebKit's implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebKit/WebKit/main/Source/WebCore/html/canvas/GPUCanvasContextCocoa.mm) maps P3 and extended P3 into its compositor. These are implemented capabilities, not merely proposed dictionaries. Actual brightness/gamut still depend on browser build, OS, display and available headroom.

The original caution about Boogle holds: inspected Three r187 requests extended output for HalfFloat but supplies no canvas P3 `colorSpace`; Boogle clamps its AgX result and manually encodes sRGB. HDR needs an intentional end-to-end output path, not just a canvas flag. No HDR/device test was run. A recent [Chrome macOS regression lead](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/567321057) was search-visible but its issue body required sign-in; no fix/version claim is relied on here.
