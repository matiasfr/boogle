# Smooth, sculpted and textured terrain

> **Verdict:** Continuous authored normals, banded strata, grass/rock/sand/path families.
> **Top recommendations:** 1. Fix normal continuity before adding triangles. 2. Keep the heightfield-plus-kit architecture and round only visible major turns. 3. Make painted textures part of the first material proof.
> **Status:** adopted. See [plan terrain-pilot](../plans/terrain-pilot.md), [Look](../decisions.md#look).

_Research report — 2026-11-05. Live primary-source research, close study of publisher/artist images, current-source inspection at `736e6066`, and the separate terrain-pilot and lookdev-loop branches. Recommendations only: no game changes, generated textures, fresh game captures or performance measurements. Not tested on a device._

## Answer and evidence

**Keep Place's heightfield and small cliff kit; give them continuous authored normals, rounded silhouettes, painted repeating surface colour and explicit grass/rock/sand/path coverage.** Use the current rough PBR lighting with restrained detail. Sunshine and Wind Waker supply the inviting shape and colour direction; Odyssey's Seaside supplies the closest textured coastal reference. Boogle's existing water remains the quality anchor, as requested by the owner on 5 November. Reducing its quality to match older games is not part of this proposal.

Texturing is a first-class part of this experiment. This revises the **texture-free-first / one tiny packed pattern** recommendation in [terrain-art](terrain-art.md#materials-broad-forms-first-quiet-detail-last) and [stylized-terrain](stylized-terrain.md#grass-rock-sand-and-paths-in-tsl). Retain an untextured control to diagnose shape, but compare authored painted tiles immediately after the normals comparison. A 128² noise field cannot by itself supply the intentional rock marks and turf character now requested. Nor will a detailed texture repair a triangular silhouette.

Evidence labels: **C** = inspected source; **P** = primary publication/developer statement; **V** = visual observation of an identified published image; **R** = earlier report, not rerun; **H** = proposed Boogle technique, budget or interpretation. Screenshots establish appearance, not proprietary topology, shader graphs or texture resolution. GDC abstracts below were read; their full talks were not watched. No reference-game executable, ROM or extracted asset was used.

The proposal serves [Show, don't tell](../../DESIGN.md#11-show-dont-tell) and [Small and readable](../../DESIGN.md#small-and-readable). The [owner calls](../agents/owner-calls.md#owners-standing-calls) and [Look decisions](../decisions.md#look) still govern adoption: coordinate terrain with level design, preserve the current route during the proof, and stop for owner review before extending it. The latest [iPhone note](../playtests.md#2026-11-04-owner-solo-on-iphone-the-default-url) is not the two-player replay. This research does not resolve that gate.

## Technique stack for Boogle

### 1. Geometry and shading normals solve different problems

Keep the heightfield for hills, beaches, paths and rail grades. Use the existing cove mesh family for vertical faces, projecting ledges and cap thickness. A heightfield has one height per x/z; adding samples cannot create an overhang. [GPU Gems 3, Geiss](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu) explains that representational limit and triplanar projection. Its old geometry-shader terrain generator is not a WebGPU implementation recipe. **P/H**

First compare **unchanged positions with corrected shading normals**. For the kit, preserve original adjacency before `facetColours()` splits colours; accumulate area-weighted face normals within authored smoothing groups, then copy those normals to duplicates. Share normals across intended continuous segment sockets. Split at actual ledge corners and rear closures. Three's [BufferGeometry documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BufferGeometry.html#computeVertexNormals) distinguishes indexed averaging from unshared triangle normals. Do not average the cliff front through the buried rear face. **C/P/H**

If the silhouette still looks cut from cardboard, add a few rows around the crest and large face turns, or author a low-resolution rounded profile in Blender and export its normals. Bevel width and silhouette curvature matter more than uniformly subdividing flat triangles. Weighted normals can preserve a large plane beside a bevel; they do not create a visible bevel by themselves. A stylized normal should agree with the broad surface, not point everywhere upward and erase cliff illumination. **H**

Here “tessellation” means **offline/load-time subdivision where silhouette curvature warrants it**. WebGPU's [render pipeline](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#render-pipeline) exposes vertex/fragment stages, not a drop-in hull/domain tessellation stage. Compute-generated geometry is possible but unnecessary for this bounded proof. No runtime subdivision, displacement, parallax occlusion, global remesh or smaller world grid is proposed. If positions eventually change at a foot, rim or waterline, update and recheck collision and shore inputs together. **P/H**

### 2. Semantic coverage, then pigment, then detail

Separate four signals:

- **Coverage:** rock, grass, sand and worn path weights. Keep `pathMask` authoritative for the visible path; use authored cliff/cap tags and broad slope/elevation rules for defaults. Do not infer path identity from the red/green balance of a newly painted texture.
- **Macro tint:** low-frequency warm/cool or dry/damp variation in vertex colour, independent of material identity. Treat existing baked crease shading cautiously so it does not double-darken real shadows.
- **Repeating pigment:** painted tiles at metre scale. Use top-down island-local XZ mapping for turf, sand and soil; triplanar rock for steep faces. Share position origin, scale, palette and masks between land and kit.
- **Surface response:** high roughness, small optional normal perturbation, and the existing wet receiver. Avoid baking strong sunlight, shadow direction or wet highlights into colour tiles.

Use normalized vertex weights for broad regions; introduce one **256² RGBA coverage map over a 64 × 64 m corner** only where the 2 m grid cannot represent the desired edge. That is **0.25 m per texel**, arithmetic, not proof of sufficient edge quality. Clamp it to its local footprint and fall back to normal terrain coverage outside; do not stretch it over the island. A metre-scale soft path shoulder can remain vertex-driven. Fine grass-edge breakup should come from a small repeating mask or cap geometry, not a giant unique map. **H**

Unity's [Terrain Layers](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-TerrainLayer.html) separate repeating layer maps, tint and mask data. Unreal's [Landscape Materials](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-materials-in-unreal-engine) use named painted weights and height/alpha blends. Godot's [StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html#triplanar-mapping) supplies local/world triplanar projection. Boogle needs these few material concepts, not an editor, virtual-texture system or a second terrain representation. Its existing typed terrain data can author the coverage, while TSL evaluates it. **P/H**

### 3. Restrict triplanar to rock; preserve bedding direction

Installed `three/src/nodes/utils/TriplanarTextures.js` samples three projections and blends normalized absolute normal components. Its defaults are **local position and local normal**, and its built-in weights have no sharpness exponent. Supply the shared island frame explicitly; otherwise independently placed kit pieces restart the pattern. A small custom helper is needed only if the proof benefits from sharper weights such as `abs(n)^k / sum(abs(n)^k)`. **C**

Slope-based texturing means slope helps select rock coverage; triplanar means choosing texture coordinates. They are different operations. Keep the classification normal/mask independent of the perturbed lighting normal, so changing normal strength cannot grow grass on a cliff. Artist tags override slope at the cap, beach and path. **H**

Directional sandstone needs a convention: the two vertical projections must share **Y as the texture's vertical coordinate**. The top projection can use a quieter nondirectional version or the same low-contrast mottling. Blindly applying a directional tile through the stock `yz/zx/xy` swizzles can rotate bedding on one projection. No random 90-degree rotations for bedded rock. World/island-space alignment removes UV seams, but cannot hide intersecting silhouettes, mismatched normals or an open mesh seam. Use the pilot's buried opaque joins. **C/H**

Normal mapping comes later. Decode each tangent-space normal, apply the correct signed basis for its projection, bring it into one coordinate space, combine and normalize with a geometric fallback. Do not average RGB normal-map colours as if they were albedo. Barré-Brisebois and Hill's [Blending in Detail](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/blending-in-detail/) explains why normal layering needs special treatment; its RNM technique does not itself solve triplanar basis conversion. The first proof can omit normal maps entirely and still test painted character. **P/H**

### 4. Height-blend small transitions, not the world

Material height means a small relief value inside a tile, not world elevation and not vertex displacement. Use it to let sand settle between rock marks and turf break into soil; keep the broad authored coverage in control. Start with a small strength and retain the [safe weighted scheme](stylized-terrain.md#height-blending-means-microrelief-not-world-elevation): exclude zero-coverage layers from the maximum, multiply by coverage, normalize, and fall back to original weights if the sum approaches zero. Unreal explicitly documents black spots and invalid zero normals when height-blend layers all contribute zero. **P/H**

Do not apply a single hard height contest across all four layers. Preserve the path's broad soft shoulder; reserve the stronger breakup for rock/turf and sand/rock. A texture cannot extend the silhouette of grass over a lip: build a narrow opaque rounded cap with a shallow irregular edge and a restrained underside tint. Add sparse opaque tuft meshes only where a close silhouette needs them. Avoid a continuous transparent grass skirt, which adds overdraw and shadow/aliasing work. **H**

### 5. Painted appearance inside the current PBR pipeline

Keep `MeshStandardNodeMaterial`, metallic zero and a proposed dry roughness range **0.85–0.98**. Broad pigment marks, smooth normals and high roughness produce a painted, sculpted surface while preserving scene shadows and the water's light context. Toon and PBR are not mutually exclusive art directions, but replacing the terrain BRDF with hard two-band lighting would introduce another variable and is not needed here. No new post pass, outline, bloom or exposure compensation. **H**

Use `colorNode`, `roughnessNode` and eventually `normalNode`; preserve existing water receiver patches through [material composition](../../src/render/reference/materials.md). Albedo files are `SRGBColorSpace`; coverage, height, roughness and normal data are `NoColorSpace`; vertex tint is linear. Blend colour in linear space and let Boogle's single output path handle display conversion. These input rules follow [three.js colour management](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html); its WebGL output examples are not instructions to add another output pass to Boogle. **P/C/H**

Textures and derivative-based samples must stay in uniform control flow under the [TSL contract](../../src/render/reference/tsl.md). Multiplying a layer by zero does not guarantee its texture fetch disappears. Reduce actual graph/material variants for lower-cost modes; do not promise savings from a per-pixel `If` around implicit-derivative sampling. **C/H**

## Texture, geometry and sampling budgets

All numbers below are **proposed caps or arithmetic**, not measured capacity. Preserve the pilot's **2–4 colour/spatial draws** and **2,000–8,000 added render-triangle envelope**; smoothing alone adds no triangles. Count shadow submissions separately. Do not spend the unused triangle allowance automatically or subdivide the entire 210,600-triangle base grid. **H**

| Smallest cove proof          | Two 512² RGBA8 albedos: sandstone and turf; one 512² RGBA8 linear data tile; one 256² RGBA8 local coverage map. Retain current sand/path palette initially. |                                **4.33 MiB** |

Arithmetic: RGBA8 texels × four bytes × approximately 4/3 for a complete mip chain. One 512² map is 1.33 MiB; one 256² map is 0.33 MiB. These totals exclude alignment, upload staging, retained decoded CPU images, transcoder memory and all existing scene textures. A PNG/WebP download size is **not** GPU residency. A second 64 m coverage patch adds another 0.33 MiB before compression; do not budget per-file while forgetting every resident patch. **H**

For the two-surface proof, the linear tile can hold rock microheight, turf microheight, a broad breakup field and a spare channel. For the four-surface expansion, explicitly repack RGBA as the four heights; keep roughness as per-class constants first. Do not silently reinterpret channels between assets. Normals stay separate. Author masters at 1024² if helpful; ship 512² only where it beats 256² in the actual camera. No 2K/4K runtime terrain maps initially. **H**

Start rock at **4–8 m per repeat**, turf/sand/soil at **2–4 m**. A 512² tile spanning 4 m gives 128 texels/m; this is a scale control, not a demand to resolve every texel on screen. Use vertex macro tint to hide repetition at tens of metres. Avoid unique conspicuous cracks in a tile, since those advertise repetition. Keep mips, use bounded anisotropy after checking renderer support, and fade normal/fine contrast at distance. Do not sharpen the texture to compensate for a low rendering resolution. **H**

**Fragment work matters independently of bytes.** For the cove pair, triplanar rock albedo costs three samples, turf one, triplanar rock height three and planar turf height one, plus one coverage fetch: **nine source-level samples** at a blended pixel. A rock normal adds three, reaching twelve. For four layers, four planar height channels can share one fetch, but rock's projected height still needs three: six albedo + four height + one coverage = eleven; with rock normals, fourteen. These are conservative graph counts, not GPU instruction or time measurements. The first cheap comparison should also test flat blending without the height tile: five samples for the cove pair including coverage. If vertex coverage suffices, remove that fetch too. **H**

Use regular small image files for the first art comparison; introduce GPU compression after a candidate survives it. [KTX2Loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html) and [Khronos KTX](https://www.khronos.org/ktx/) support transcoding Basis textures into supported GPU formats. Verify the actual selected format and colour/normal quality; KTX2 is a container, not a promise of one bitrate or ASTC support everywhere. ETC1S/UASTC selection and transcoder startup need a measured asset-pipeline decision. Preserve [DESIGN's](../../DESIGN.md#code-first-images-by-approval) aim of under 100 KB per shipped image where visually adequate; record actual transfer bytes rather than assuming all maps meet it. **P/H**

## Authoring painted tiles, including image generation

Use one **original Boogle coastal material family** with a shared palette and scale sheet. Keep source files, exports, channel definitions, metre repeat, colour spaces, generator/version or brush workflow, prompts and hashes together. Example **future generation prompt**, with identical framing/style constraints reused for turf, sand and soil:

> Square, orthographic, straight-on material swatch covering four metres of original warm coastal sandstone. Soft painted pigment, broad cream and muted ochre patches, a few shallow irregular sediment lines, rounded weathering, quiet fine grain. Even diffuse colour, no directional illumination, cast shadows, perspective, vignette, objects, grass, water, lettering or border. Repeat seamlessly left/right and top/bottom. Make the pattern broadly even with no isolated focal crack. This is a flat base-colour texture for a playful sculpted 3D landscape, not a rendered rock or a finished scene.

For bedded sandstone specify the vertical axis and request irregular thickness; keep most geology in the cliff geometry/macro treatment, since a repeating black stripe cannot produce the larger uneven ledges seen in Seaside. For turf, request grouped low-contrast leaves/brush marks instead of thousands of individually sharp blades. Sand should be quieter still. Generate several candidates once, compare their 3×3 repeats and then their **256²/512² game renders**; do not repeatedly reroll until an unreviewed texture happens to look plausible. **H**
