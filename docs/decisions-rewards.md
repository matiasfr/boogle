- <a id="d-2026-11-10-boogles-build-houses-and-upgrade-sources"></a>**2026-11-10, Boogles build houses on town plots and upgrade sources; anything on rails costs materials, the passenger train and seats included** (owner, on the decisions page, later on 10 November; "[group] question: answer"). **Changed from the recommendation:** [What Boogles buy] What a town upgrade is: "Boogles build the house itself; unbought plots stay empty ground". [What Boogles buy] Where "train parts" end: "Anything on rails costs materials, including the passenger train and seats". **As recommended:** [What Boogles buy] A faster loader arm at a source: "Boogles (a source upgrade)". [What Boogles buy] First prices: "Fine as stand-ins; tune them on the 2D model page". His notes, in full: "faster loader arm would theoretically increase resource gain in the system so it's a good candidate fro upgrades. train parts take as light suggestion as it relates to boogles. I like the idea that buying plots of land both helps a town economically but also introduced new characters and a building to discover. Maybe these are intentional upgrades instead of naturally happening from connecting a place. it's also a good opportunity for construction animation. for strongbox, im wondering what the separation should be between personal money and not? should depositing your own money to help with communal upgrades be the way it's incentivized to be merged? Maybe money doesn't buy anything for individuals, it can just be pooled into the shared deposit, and otherwise it's used comedically ." **Changed:** this replaces the "passenger cars" part of the same day's "Boogles buy town upgrades, source upgrades and passenger cars; never engine or cargo parts, never materials" (below): passenger cars and seats are now bought with materials, like every other train part. What Boogles buy is therefore town plots (a house built on each) and other town upgrades, and source upgrades, the faster loader arm included; the rest of that entry stands (never engine or cargo parts, never materials, no upgrade with two prices, no route step with a Boogle price). It also moves [engine-garage](brainstorms/engine-garage.md)'s placeholder "Loader upgrades (faster jib, gantry)" from steel to Boogles; that price was the brief's stand-in, never a decision. **A leaning, not a hard rule:** he marks the train-parts answer "a light suggestion", so seats on materials is recorded as his leaning, which the 2D model step may revisit. **Ideas he is leaning to, not decisions:** (1) "Maybe these are intentional upgrades instead of naturally happening from connecting a place": town buildings as deliberate buys. This would bend 2 November's "the rail builds the world" ("towns grow from what arrives") and "trains growing the town from cargo is the part that lands" ([rail](decisions-rail.md#d-2026-11-02-the-rail-builds-the-world-the)); neither entry is changed by this one. (2) "Maybe money doesn't buy anything for individuals, it can just be pooled into the shared deposit, and otherwise it's used comedically": see the money entry below. Orchestrator's reading, beside his words: a town upgrade is buying a plot; Boogles build the house, a new character moves in and there is a new building to discover, with a construction animation of little guys building; an unbought plot is empty ground with a sign or stakes, never a sealed box, and a built house is walk-in, so "every building can be walked into" and its opening order hold ([core loop](decisions-core-loop.md#d-2026-11-10-every-building-can-be-walked-into)); "on rails" means what rolls (engines, cars, seats, the express's passenger set, more trains), so whether a station's fit-out and train looks and whistles follow it is not settled here; the crowd a train leaves behind no longer prompts a Boogle buy, and seats come from hauled materials. The [passenger-trade brief](brainstorms/passenger-trade.md) is reworked for this the same day: its buy list, the design of both ends (what a town has on arrival against what is bought), who might move in (roles only; characters are the owner's and his collaborators' to write) and its open questions are proposals, not decisions. Not built.

### Progress and rewards

- <a id="d-2026-11-06-economy-principles-amended"></a>**2026-11-06, economy principles amended** (owner approved the [principles audit](research/economy-principles-audit.md)'s wording, and added: "let's make progression completely shared. If there's a shared currency, the upgrades should apply for everyone"; "really tuned to 1 to 2 to 3, maybe 4 people, unlikely more than that ... The simplest is 1 to 3 people"; "we can still have The Boogler and the NPCs treat money in a comical way, but have real use cases for resources"; "the cosmetics can still be shared. Or maybe certain cosmetics and secret crates can be personal, which makes sense"). DESIGN.md 1, 6, 8, 9 and Server hosts every room changed. **One shared wallet** of town sales, anyone may spend it. **All progression is shared:** every upgrade and ability bought or earned (carry sling, powered pick, double jump) applies to everyone in the world. **Cosmetics:** most can be shared and are bought from the wallet (train looks, town and station decorations); what you wear from secret crates, and certain wearables, stay personal. **Money in dialogue** stays a joke; resources and the wallet do real work; NPC copy is unchanged ([conflicting lines](brainstorms/ring-and-economy.md#npc-lines-for-the-owner)). **Group size:** design and tune for 1–3 players, tolerate 4, don't design for more. **Paused world:** an empty room doesn't simulate; the next join applies one capped away credit to producers' yards and finishes the funded job. Supersedes "no shared wallets", "three things you earn", money as only a score, both "while nobody plays" entries and production core's offline contract; code follows after R1b ([orchestrator state](agents/orchestrator-state.md)).
