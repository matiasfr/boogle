Apply these standing calls whenever their subject arises; the root summaries do not weaken the full calls.

## Owner's standing calls
- **Review motion in the asset environment** (owner, 4 November). Anything with motion gets a motion review in the asset environment by the agent before the owner's final check; inspect consecutive frames, the full cycle, real-time video and stage-owned numeric curves, and report frame-numbered defects without automatic approval.
- **Keep The Boogler's copy.** Cleanups and refactors never trim or reword his lines (dialogue, small talk, gift `line`s, HUD lines), even ones that look unreachable or mention removed systems. List those for the owner instead.
- **Nintendo names are fine; ROMs are not.** Never rename or reword something to remove a Nintendo reference. The hard line: no ROM data, or anything cut from it, in the repo, on any branch or in the build.
- **Use references by name.** When the owner points at a game or character, work out the features they're responding to (proportions, clothes, a mechanic's feel) and use those, naming the reference in briefs and docs: "the stubby proportions of Castle Story's Bricktrons, as our own character", not a vague paraphrase. Don't recreate the thing itself.
- **Research before deep facets.** A facet with real depth (level design, vehicle handling, rendering, construction, pipelines) starts with research into how games solve it: web sources, GDC talks, postmortems, in `docs/research/<topic>.md` with sources and numbered recommendations. Builders cite it.
- **Compare to standard engines.** Justify an architecture call by what Unity, Unreal and Godot actually do (their docs), and say where Boogle differs; don't defend a pattern because the code already uses it.
- **Fold copies into one helper.** When the same small thing has been written several times (like the white-noise buffer, now `src/render/noise.ts`), simplify it into one shared helper when you notice it, as long as it's cheap and doesn't change behaviour. After each batch of tracks, not on a timer, Codex runs a simplify pass over the batch: no behaviour change, checked against a playthrough.
- **Prototypes are temporary, but don't clear them out for tidiness.** Don't over-index on their decisions: no design debates about a prototype's internals, and no reshaping core code around one. A prototype still doesn't change fundamental code outside its own scope without saying so. While it's being tested, or other work depends on it, it stays on `main` behind its flag. Once answered with nothing depending on it, it's archived, not thrown away: tag the last commit that has it `archive/<name>`, remove it from `main`, and set `archived` on its card in `protos/protos.ts`. When graduating, the prototype version goes. Clean up what a prototype leaves in core code (a `protoOn` check, a seam, a setting only it uses), and remove code a decision has replaced so nobody takes it for the real thing. A tool for testing real code (`?proto=lay`) isn't a finished prototype.
- **Research is fair game.** Learning how existing games solve a problem isn't copying any one of them. No copyright caveats or avoidance guidance in research docs, briefs or repo files.
- **The owner playtests fast.** An experienced player with little time reads feel within minutes: one gut question and one line on what's new, for prototypes, playtest kits and briefs alike. Agents check edge cases themselves.
- **Call-first playtest feedback** (owner, 4 November). Prefer a Granola call while playing over a long survey. On a call, say where you are when the feedback is visual and share a screen when possible. Decisions aren't announced on the call: find them by reading the whole transcript, and surface anything that might have been a call in the follow-up questions, with the suggested answer. Flag moments without typing. Afterwards, combine the call with session data and ask at most five questions the call did not answer, each with a recommended answer first and a free field for anything else. Recommendations are not owner answers until confirmed. Keep private transcripts in scratch files, not the repo; log the findings in `docs/playtests.md` and the active notes tracker.
- **Codex first** (owner, 3 November: Claude usage is scarce, and Codex has much more room, though not unlimited). All implementation, research, review, tooling and bot work goes to Codex by default, including visible work, since `task` jobs can see the game: GPT-6.1-Terra at low effort for a clear shape, GPT-6-Astra for harder work. Claude directs, makes design calls, spot-checks results and judges real-time feel only where Codex can't. This changes who does the work, not how well it's verified. The owner can cap spending on a specific check, and that cap binds. If Codex hits a usage limit, don't move the work to Claude: pause it and tell the owner, who will upgrade the plan.
- **Stop at the owner's playtest.** When a round is ready to play, or what's left needs the owner, stop developing and stop any check-ins or loops, and tell them. Nothing past that point starts until they've played and given notes. No usage goes on gameplay the playtest could change. Systems and graphics that make the world look better without affecting the flow under test may go ahead. This is a default rather than a strict rule, and collaborators may ship meanwhile (owner, 3 November).
- **The Mac and mobile are the targets.** An M1 Max is the average; judge looks and budgets on the Mac the agents run on, and let mobile scale down, though it still has to run there. The owner tests on several devices, so don't check every device each time. In a report, one short line is enough: "not tested on a device" (owner, 2 and 3 November).
- **Stay on the latest versions.** Default to upgrading frameworks, tools and runtimes for their features and performance, as long as nothing breaks. A change in feel or download size is something to measure and report, not a reason to stay behind; hold back only for a real break, and say what would unblock it. Prefer a complete upgrade over keeping an old version alongside; a partial step is a stop on the way, with the rest of the path named (owner, 3 November).
- **Start animation simple.** Not a general rule against complex motion: agents are less good at its nuance, and the character adapted from the mannequin warps easily. Begin with simple motion, and refine later with iteration (owner, 5 November).
- **Brainstorm new gameplay first.** A gameplay feature whose design isn't already settled goes through `/brainstorm` before it's built, so it gets the multi-agent panel. Technical work, small fixes, art with unchanged behaviour, and ports of accepted behaviour don't need one (owner, 5 November).
- **Image references before asset work, as an experiment** (owner, 5 November). Previz from a real game screenshot before modelling; try generated motion references for simple animation, starting with an axe swing. Keep real Buddy proportions and compare with the current rig/trial. Judge a reference-built recovery rack against a text-only control before claiming improvement. This remains an experiment until the results are judged; fun and quality stay the owner's call. Keep the simple-animation call above. [Evidence and limits](../research/motion-references.md).
- **Early motion-reference exception** (owner, 5 November). This experiment may start ahead of the round-4 replay, assuming it goes well. Stay on its worktree branch; do not push or ship before the replay. This does not establish that the replay passed or authorize changes to the separately owned chop code.
- **Machines show their work physically** (owner, 5 November). Track laying, resources filling up, scaffolding and construction should look physical, even if unrealistic: the gantry picks a rail and lays it, a magazine empties as rails go down, a stack grows as logs arrive. Anything the game counts gets a visible physical form that changes, not just a counter. Build machines from rigid parts with pivots, driven by real game state, and keep the motion readable over realistic. For a machine, plan the motion first with a storyboard of the action's beats and a parts sheet of every moving part, its pivot and its range. Use beauty previz only for the static shell, so a render never fuses parts or freezes a pose. Judge the motion from real-time capture, not stills.
- **Some lo-fi is character** (owner, 5 November). Abstract or lo-fi parts that read well, such as the little-guy workers, stay as they are. Improvement passes target things that read poorly, not everything that's simple.
- **Previz before modelling** (owner, 5 November). For new assets, previz from a real game screenshot before modelling. Steer it to fit the game: the screenshot and real Buddy stills as the anchor, simple chunky shapes, the game's materials and camera, and no extra detail, restyled characters or changed scale. Every model also gets detail references beyond the in-game silhouette: a front, side and back sheet, plus close-ups of the parts that carry its character (joints, trims, handles, faces), so agents can model the details properly. Treat these as design intent, not measurements. Chosen after the recovery rack: the reference-guided build beat a text-only one ([comparison](../research/rack-model-compare.md)). Generated motion references for simple animation remain an experiment until the owner judges them; keep the simple-animation call above. [Evidence and limits](../research/motion-references.md).
- **Decisions live in the repo.** An owner decision or working preference binds every agent unless it's specific to one tool, so it goes in this section or the decision log, not only in one tool's private memory.

## Deferring to direction

Agents follow recorded owner direction and approved scope. For an ordering conflict, use the [game-direction skill](../../.claude/skills/game-direction/SKILL.md) and give its source-backed recommendation to the planner; pause only dependent work needing an owner decision. It cannot approve new scope, overrule the owner, or require a review for routine work already covered by a plan. [direction.md](../direction.md) is a derived view, not authority.
- **Contact sheets are for specific beats or sequences being evaluated, not the full game** (owner, 4 November).
