Before changing materials, lights, shaders or the render pipeline, read this and only the affected sections of src/render/README.md.

## Rendering
`WebGPURenderer`, WebGPU only (no WebGL 2 fallback), one `RenderPipeline` (linear HDR, Neutral tone mapping), all custom shading in TSL node materials. Contract and pitfalls: [src/render/README.md](../../src/render/README.md).
- Custom looks go in node slots (`colorNode`, `positionNode`, `maskNode`, ...) of a `*NodeMaterial` from `three/webgpu`; extend someone else's material with `patchNode` (`src/render/material-patch.ts`). No GLSL, `ShaderMaterial`, `onBeforeCompile` or `THREE.Points` (lint fails them).
- Colours are linear; draw through `renderFrame()`; post effects extend `pipeline.outputNode`. Compute shaders, storage buffers and the rest of WebGPU are fair game, with no fallback path.
- Prefer `material()` in `src/render/materials.ts` for the six named presets when opting an asset in. Cached materials and maps are shared: never mutate or dispose them. Explicitly `.clone()` before per-player colour, previews or `patchNode()` extensions. Preserve existing values, authored glTF materials and special terrain/water graphs; a preset migration is not permission to retune the look.

## Terrain and large static meshes: the pattern
Follow established terrain practice rather than a home-grown scheme (owner, 11 November 2026). The ground is **Chunked LOD** (Ulrich 2002; Unity Terrain patches, Unreal Landscape components): square chunks, each with its own bounds so the frustum culls it, drawn at one of a few power-of-two resolutions chosen by distance to its bounds, static index buffers over the one vertex set (`src/place/far-ground.ts`, pure part `far-ground-index.ts`, test `test/far-ground-index.test.ts`). Cracks are closed by **stitching** (a chunk keeps full resolution along its own border, coarse interiors fan to it), not skirts: no geometry hangs under the ground and any level pair meets on shared vertices. Rejected for this island: geometry clipmaps and CDLOD (nested rings with vertex morphing: for much larger worlds; the morph is the cure if a LOD switch ever pops), Nanite-style virtualised geometry and GPU-driven culling (not in three.js WebGPU), an impostor proxy of the main island seen from Ring Island (far tiles already draw 4 m and 8 m cells; an HLOD proxy is the next step only if a measurement still shows the main island's tiles). Any new big ground (Ring Island's 199,960-triangle `ring-island-ground`, later areas) should follow the same pattern: tiles with real bounds, 3 levels, stitched borders. Never set `frustumCulled = false` on a mesh just because it is large: it is the reason one 242 k-triangle draw was submitted from a kilometre away. The shadow camera covers ±36 m (`src/render/scene.ts`); a caster with `frustumCulled = false` is submitted to the shadow pass whatever its distance.
