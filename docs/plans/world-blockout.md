# World blockout

The whole map, rough, so the connections between areas work before each area is built in depth.

**The call** ([decisions](../decisions.md#decided), 10 November): do both. Block out the whole map now as a greybox,
then build areas to full depth one at a time on top of it, Ring Island first. The owner, same day: the blockout goes
into the real game behind `?blockout=world`; the far silhouettes of future areas stay on by default; performance is
measured, not a gate; and the future lines become real server lines from line 1 and line 2's components later.

**Sources.** The geography is the Sickle Isles layout the owner chose and greyboxed (6–7 November), in the `#map-data`
JSON of [map-prototypes.html](../world/map-prototypes.html), which the 2D page, the dev greybox, the live horizon and now
this plan all read. The order and hours come from [ring-and-economy](../brainstorms/ring-and-economy.md) (6.1 solo hours
to the ending), moved to Sickle's order. Ring Island's arrival is [ring-island](../brainstorms/ring-island.md) (decided
10 November). Today's geography is Place's layout (`src/proto/place/layout.ts`: Home, Far Head, line 2 to the pier) and
the crossing's six bents (`src/sim/railway-works-layout.ts`).

**Pages.** The top-down map with every table below, computed from the data: [world-blockout.html](../world/world-blockout.html)
(generated by `node --import ./dev/sim/hooks.ts dev/level/world-blockout.ts`, with the aerial stills under
`docs/world/world-blockout/`). The 3D greybox: `?greybox=sickle` on the dev server (rides, views, labels); the live

## The branch choice

At Ring Island, about 1.8 hours in: **west along the cliff ledge to High Gorge** (the stack line, 1.7 km out to the
split stack: copper, the powered pick and a longer hook reel) **or south-west across the bay by Bell Rock to Canopy
Town** (the bay trestle, 1.4 km, then on: fibre, the carrying sling and glider cloth). Both work with steel and
footings. Either way the bay shortcut follows (stage 4), so the second branch is short. If the Gorge is first, the
powered pick opens the sea cave only after both branches anyway (High Port takes all four materials), so the order
changes which tool you hold longest, not whether a lock waits. The upgrade-order pick at the first Roundhouse upgrade
is the second choice (ring-and-economy, open question 1).

## Open conflicts with the live geography

1. **Ring Island's position matches; its size doesn't.** The live cones and the map both centre on (330, −1120), 751 m
   from the pier. The map's `ring-land` outline is 480 × 400 m; the live main cone is 680 m across and the brief's
   waterline about 640. The greybox uses the live cones, so nothing in 3D disagrees, but the 2D pages draw the island
   small.
2. **The crossing's deck: 4 m in the map, 24 m in the game, about 6 m in the brief.** The map ran `ringx` at 4 m from
   20 m past the pier, which the live 24.3 m pier and its six built bents can't meet. This change redraws it as the
   decided bow: 24.3 m at the pier and the first bent, down to 6 m at the railhead 260 m out (7%), low to the island
   halt by the shore. The live bents still stand at 24 m until Ring Island build 1 redraws them, so in 3D the bow's deck
   runs under them.
3. **The Ring top junction is on the cone's summit, at 63 m.** Every line out of Ring Island climbs or drops about 57 m
   in 265–280 m there (about 21%), which no train in the game climbs. Recommended: put the junction at the island halt
   by the shore (6 m) and run the stack line and bay trestle out along the shore; or tunnel under the cone, which also
   gives build 2's "thread the ring" a reason.
4. **The island halt isn't a map place.** The brief's island halt (railhead plus about 180 m, at the shore) is in the
   line catalog at (414, −820), 6.2 m, as line 2's next stop; the map's Ring Island place stays at the cone's centre.
5. **High Gorge's halt is at the waterline, its town 82 m above.** The station stands in the split at 3 m, the stacks'
   tops are 85 m, and the incline and the White Wall line come down from 86 m to 3 m in their last 40 m. Either a lift
   or stair in the split (the falls cave is already there), or the station moves up onto the bridge with the stack line
   climbing its last 600 m.
6. **Home's seam.** The bay and cave lines start at the West landing, 63 m apart from each other and 100–130 m west of
   the Village halts, through the town. No rail joins them to lines 1 and 2, so a loop ride has a walk across the village.
   Recommended: one West landing halt for both, and decide whether the walk is the hub (Home as Firelink) or line 1's
   village end runs on to it. `?home=b` moves the turntable, so check there too.
7. **The order.** The brief puts High Port between the branches; Sickle (the chosen map) puts it after both, which is
   what this plan follows. Map-prototypes open question 2 ("Does High Port come after both branches?") is still open.
8. **Bell Terraces is Bell Rock.** Same role (crystal seeds by hand, on the first return spoke). The terraces can be
   its dressing.
9. **The sea's coast texture.** One texture, at most 2048 texels a side, covers every registered shore; Home's alone
   is about 1 m a texel. The blockout registers no shores, so nothing breaks today. But every island past Ring Island
   lies outside a 2 km box round Home (see the dashed box on the map), and all of them together span about 3.5 ×
   3.5 km, so once an area's shore is real it needs its own texture (or a coarser far cell). Ring Island alone already
   stretches it to about 2 km (the brief's own risk).
10. **The bay trestle's hump.** As drawn it climbs to 84 m mid-bay; nothing there needs clearing. Lower it if it stays.
11. **The crossing's built bents carry one track.** The six bents (Split-rock J5) are one 3.6 m deck wide; double track
    needs a 7.6 m deck, so Ring Island build 1's bow redraw should widen them (or stand a second row 4 m east), and line
    2's single track meets the double crossing at the pier head with a turnout.

## Double track

Owner direction, 10 November: every rail corridor is double track, existing and future (decided in
[docs/decisions.md](../decisions.md), "2026-11-10, the railway is double track": one direction per track, both tracks
laid together, today's lines widened in one planned `WORLD_NS` bump after v25).

- **Formation.** Two tracks on **4 m centres**. The gauge is Place's own (rails 0.5 m either side of a track's centre,
  `src/proto/place/rail.ts`, on a 3.6 m deck; `src/sim/railway-line-layout.ts` has no gauge, only centimetres along the
  line), so the double formation is **7.6 m** wide, on bridges, trestles, cuttings, tunnels and the pier crossing alike.
  4 m is generous for a 1 m gauge; it leaves room for a person between passing trains.
- **Stations.** A 40 m platform outside each track (centres 5.8 m off the corridor's middle), so the track centres stay
  4 m through every halt. An island platform would need the tracks spread to about 9 m at each station.
- **Turnbacks.** Every terminus turns a train back without reversing it. Where the line ends on a small skerry, a
  slope or a junction, a **scissors crossover** 35 m inside the terminus halt: Ring top (both lines), High Gorge, Bell
  Rock (three lines), the West landing (both lines), High Port (the White Wall line). Where the island has room, a
  **40 m-radius turning loop** past the end: Canopy Town (bay line), High Port (cave line), Lantern Notch and the
  Original Island. In the data: `FORMATION` and each line's `ends`.
- **Today's lines stay single track.** Lines 1 and 2 are not moved. The blockout draws a pale, non-solid second-track
  bed 4 m off each on the side with fewer clashes (line 1 and line 2 both on the left of their drawn direction), with an
  orange post wherever it meets something. The spots, from `LIVE_SECOND_TRACK` (sampled every 2 m):

  | Line | Along the line | Meets | Other side free? |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Line 1 | 0–4 m | The turntable (both lines start on it) | No: the table takes one track; the second track ends at a turnout before it |
  | Line 1 | 6–32 m | The Village platform | No: line 2's Q1 frame stands 4 m off the other side at 6–8 m. The second track needs the platform moved out 4 m, or an island platform |
  | Line 1 | 112–114 m | A drop over 6 m and a bank (the ridge climb) | Yes: the right side has room there |
  | Line 2 | 0–4 m | The turntable | No, as line 1 |
  | Line 2 | 732–734 m | The pier rack | Yes there, but the pier frame stands as close on the other side 8 m on: the pier head needs its rack or frame moved either way |

  The other sides, for comparison: line 1's right meets the Q1 frame, the old train at the camp and the Camp platform;
  line 2's right meets all four of its platforms (Village, Quarry, Far Head) and the pier frame, which is why both go
  left.

  Everywhere else a second track fits beside today's lines, the sea trestle and the pier included (they only need the
  deck widened). This is a note for the plan, not a change to the live lines.

## Future lines as data

Every future line is in [src/sickle/world-lines.ts](../../src/sickle/world-lines.ts), generated from the map JSON by the
script above and checked current by `test/world-lines.test.ts`. Its shape follows the sim's own lines:

- `pts`: track vertices, `[x, z, y]` world centimetres, each pair one straight span (like Place's sampled rails, but
  integers);
- `stops`: centimetres along the line, like `TRAIN_STOPS`; Ring Island's line `extends: 1` and counts on from line 2's
  rail end (`fromCm` 74300), so its stops append to line 2's;
- `halts`: `{ name, stop, x, z, y, reach }`, like `LINE_HALTS`;
- `speed` and `accel`: `TRAIN.speed` (200 cm a tick, 20 m/s) and `TRAIN.accel`;
- `legs`: which map link each stretch runs over and the stage it is built at, for each branch order;
- `ends`: each terminus's turnback, a crossover at `sCm` or a loop centred at `(x, z)`; `FORMATION` holds the double
  track's centres, gauge, deck, platform offset and loop radius;
- `LIVE_SECOND_TRACK`: the side and the clashes of a second track beside lines 1 and 2, in centimetres along them.

It is pure integers with no imports, written to src/sim's rules, so the follow-up moves it into `src/sim/` unchanged
(`git mv`). Today only the client's blockout reads it, and the rails it describes are visual, not sim state.

### What a generic data-driven line needs from the sim (the follow-up's brief)

The follow-up comes after the v25 rules land, because it touches the same files (`railway-train.ts`,
`railway-systems.ts`, `src/world/rules/`, the train adapter).

1. **Line ids.** Today a line is an index, 0 or 1, in `TRAIN_STOPS`, `TRAIN_HOME`, `LINE_HALTS`, `trainStopsOf` and
   the `sweep: line ? 1 : -1` default. Make the line a row of a catalog (lines 1 and 2 plus `WORLD_LINES`), with its
   index fixed by catalog order so registration stays deterministic.
2. **Train state per line.** One `RailTrain` row per line that has a train, sized from the catalog, registered in a
   fixed order. New lines start parked at their first stop and never run until laid.
3. **Laid and open per line.** `trainStopsOf(line, laid)` generalised: each stop opens when its leg's stage is reached
   (the catalog's `legs[].stage`, by branch order), as the pier stop opens on Q6 today. Line 2's extension appends
   stops, which changes the meaning of index 3+ on line 2.
4. **Stops and request bits.** Per-line `call` state for stop-on-request (10 November): one request bit per stop
   (a mask per line), the halts' request levers and aboard requests addressed by `(line, stop)`.
5. **Speed per line.** The speed ladder (20 → 25 → 30 → 40 m/s) per train or per network, with `trainLegTicks` taking
   the line's speed and acceleration instead of the one `TRAIN`.
6. **Junctions and double track.** Walk-between platforms first: no through-running and no shared track between
   lines. With two tracks a line can run one train each way, each on its own track, meeting only at the crossovers and
   loops at its ends, so a line needs at most a reservation of its terminus turnback (one bit), not signalling. Line 1
   and line 2 stay single track (one train each) until their second tracks are laid.
7. **Views.** The client's train view follows a line's `pts` rather than Place's `LINES` (it already does for line 2 via
   the layout); the halt boards' island map gains the new lines.
8. **WORLD_NS.** New state rows and a changed stop catalog are a clean break: **one `WORLD_NS` bump**, owner-called and
   never mid-session ([mixed builds](../agents/mixed-builds.md)). Bundle it with the next area's bump (Ring Island's
   v26 if it hasn't shipped, else its own), installing every catalog line at once with nothing laid, so later areas
   only change accepted state, not the shape.
