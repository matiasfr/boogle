## Goal

Make the first two eras a playable chain of visible causes: wake a stranded track layer, chop and carry materials into
fixed stations, ride the machine as those pieces become track, then follow cargo into a forge that supplies the next line.
The railway should progressively do more of the work, while home becomes a solid, inhabited town worth returning to.

Serves DESIGN.md **1, Toys before chores; 4, Cooperation is automatic; 6, Communal world, personal rewards;
7, Telegraph everything; 8, Designed for drop-in, drop-out; and 9, Leave something for the next visitor**.
No principle change is proposed. Six eras and roughly eight hours remain the campaign direction; this plan builds only
wood and steam, and does not pad either to hit a clock.

### The opening job and the next reason to work

Use Death Stranding's auto-pavers as the named reference for fixed receiving stations, with Boogle's more direct
objective and a visible, rideable machine turning the delivered pieces into track.

1. Arrive with home, its unfinished forge, the rail stub and a strong path in view. One small objective says to find and wake
   the stranded track layer. The beacon supports it. No large instruction sign, survey stake, tutorial menu or mandatory talk.
2. Walk down under the future trestle and up to the timber camp. This establishes what the railway will shorten. Interact
   wakes the machine: a readable mechanical sequence, its loaded rail rack and the first receiving frame answer together.
3. Chop in a nearby grove. Keep the quick axe contact and automatic pull to the compact back bundle. Walk to the fixed
   auto-paver frame; accepted deliveries stack as recognizable logs. No tipping balance, spill or precision harvesting.
4. The full frame feeds the machine visibly. Logs become sleepers; rails leave its rack; bents, caps, stringers and deck
   land in order. Stand on the machine or the track it has just made. Never hide the build behind a progress bar or scale-up.
5. Completing the line sends its train home with visible cargo. Make this first completion unmistakable without taking
   the camera/input away. Cargo unloads where it can be followed into the forge's scaffold, frame, walls, roof and chimney.
6. The lit forge produces rails onto a rack. The turntable aligns the layer for line 2. Take rails and timber, feed the next
   stations and connect the quarry. The initial home visit introduces a useful place; repeated compulsory home errands are
   the first thing to test and shorten, not a justification for personal capacity upgrades.
7. A connected line supplies the next station's nearby unloading rack. Players still carry and feed **each segment**;
   the network moves the bulk distance. Later materials add geography and visible transformations, not more identical trips.

**First risk test:** compare the round-3 result with the same later station supplied near the frontier, keeping recipes,
gifts and machine speed fixed. Measure first and repeat return trips, including a missed train. If the last station still
drags, shorten the carry or remove a redundant segment before adding costs, stats or more rewards. Keep the first forge
visit meaningful; automatic onward freight can remove its repetition. Two negative rounds stop expansion of that loop.

The earlier approved roughly **14 s quick laying run** remains a pacing reference, not a fixed duration for every
length. Keep place's physical cadence and continuous endpoint; do not stretch short segments to fill time.

**Material identity is a production requirement.** Save material kinds/counts and ordered piece slots, not a rigid body
per log. The same slot range travels back → frame → cradle → built assembly; no simultaneous full frame and full machine,
and no animation callback grants material. A timber crate may visibly open into a fixed number of logs; the forge visibly
processes inputs into rails. Write these integer conversions in the recipe data. The prototype's 12 starter rails versus
its many drawn rail pieces is a visual abstraction, not a production conservation model: settle bundle/piece units in the
first shared station, with honest rack labels. Do not promise literal one-to-one pieces while silently duplicating them.

A focused pair or solo player may finish far sooner. Record discovery, chop, carry, waiting, repeat travel and voluntary

segment ceiling as a content sanity check, not a demand to build that much now. If home runs out of new reasons to work,
move the remaining opening budget to a meaningful new place or accept a shorter chapter; the owner chooses that scope.

**What multiplayer changes:** anyone can wake, chop, carry, stock and ride; there is no assigned chopper/porter/operator.
Two people can work one tree, with only accepted strokes consuming its remaining yield. They can carry separate loads to
the same station; the last valid deposit starts one shared run, once. Others may ride while a friend stocks the next eligible
station, but its frame stays full until the shared machine reaches it. The machine has no player lease and continues if its
starter leaves. Cap costs stay fixed. Give contributors equal acknowledgement without numerical ranking.

The solo Local prototypes prove none of those races, online latency or late joining. In the first shared test, start the
swing immediately as reversible Local anticipation; material gain, tree depletion and stock arcs wait for acceptance.
Measure the online feel against the Local chop (the current sync pipeline can add several ticks). If it fails, solve a
bounded reconciliation design before more content; do not silently mint predicted logs or compensate with larger yields.

Install projects → supply → service → optional rewards. Later rules can call earlier APIs; earlier rules cannot import later
ones. Supply reads project eligibility, service calls supply for loading/unloading, and UI derives town numbers from accepted
supply counts. Use `src/sim/rail-home.ts` for the static graph/recipes and `src/island/rail-home.ts` for rendered geometry;
a pure `src/sim/rail-town.ts` derives district structure. Avoid a second town timetable or generic job framework.

The current `stacks` API is one kind per stack, whereas place carries logs and rails together. Proposed rail carries therefore
live in the new supply state with a total capacity and per-kind integers, rather than quietly changing the old stack wire
shape. Keep old stacks for existing systems during isolated testing; decide their remaining callers at cutover. Raw harvested
stock belongs to its player and survives a visit; **network freight claims** belong to one project and return there exactly
once on disconnect. Neither can be stolen. Returning claims cannot be spent again under stale IDs. Reserve no more than the
project's remaining requirement, counting stored, in-flight and claimed goods; cancellation never deletes paid work.

`rail.plan` selects an eligible unfunded project by revision; once supplied it cannot be switched out from under carriers.
Start with one active supply project, not a crew-wide negotiation screen. Known town construction can be the next project
when the current one finishes **with players present**. A project's forge inputs are dependencies inside its single bounded
allowance, not extra projects or unlimited stock. Manifest identity `(service, version, lap, stop)` makes arrival exactly once;
changing schedule must preserve already credited calls. Explicit production/conversion is the only mint/consume boundary.

**Late join / absence:** reconstruct current stacks, frame/cradle contents, built slabs, forge and district phases from the
snapshot and ticks, quietly. Restore active construction at its current phase; do not replay historical pours, rewards or
collider transitions. A new friend gets the shared routes/capabilities and a clear current objective without redoing wood.
Existing cosmetics stay personal; proposed catch-up gives a welcome cosmetic once, never invented contribution credit.

When the last player leaves, server membership stamps `rail.attendance`: freeze **one project's remaining allowance**, counting
in-flight cargo and active construction. An already-running build takes that slot; otherwise use the selected unfinished
project. Never grant a running build and a new banked bill separate allowances. If it is a town build, it may finish; if it is a rail station bill, network supply
can bank that bill, but no absent player is invented to carry it or select the next line. An already-started lay may finish.
No second project is commissioned, and no automatic town chain spends another allowance. On restart, `rail.catchup` advances
an absolute credited watermark against the same cap, so empty-room ticks and restart catch-up cannot both pay. Server wall
time enters as a stamped integer fact; never `Date.now()` in rules. Rejoining resumes bounded active demand, not an absence
multiplier. This precise cap interpretation is a recommendation for owner approval.

Reach checks use proposed `railActorPosition` env from validated presence on the server and the matching local-host source;
missing/stale facts reject. Membership/profile/wall-time facts originate on the host, never the requesting tab. `ctx.env`
is validate-only; apply rechecks inventory/caps. No new WebSocket. Preserve snapshot-load/save failure guards.

Export valid and `JUNK` `edits` for every op, and `invariants` for integer/nonnegative counts, recipe/carry/absence caps,
unique claims/manifests/receipts, monotonic completions and conservation including explicit production/conversion. Pure tests
cover simultaneous last cuts/deposits, departure with freight, duplicate arrivals/wakes, reordered admissible district cargo,
bad IDs, partially built snapshots and reload before/after transitions. Fixed tick/order; seeded `mulberry32` only if needed;
cm samples and integer units; floats only allowed arithmetic/`Math.sqrt`. No trig, wall clock, per-peer counters, three.js
or Rapier in shared rules. Yaw, wheels and physical motion stay on the presentation side.

## Risks

- **Unproven motivation and online feel lead.** Solo station success does not settle the last carry, first-line celebration,
  repeated rail fetching or multiplayer edit latency. Gifts and a roundhouse cannot stand in for those answers.
- **An apparently physical build can lie.** Counted slots, crane/forge conversions and shared phase collision must agree;
  snapshots cannot replay a build under a standing player. A solid-looking cradle/town with no collision blocks promotion.
- **Scope can expand through nice ideas.** Districts, inhabitants, gifts, a roundhouse and crystals do not authorize five
  new economies. Keep cosmetic life simple, reward results conditional and the sea landing bounded. No required camp choice.
- **Authority and persistence:** queued edits may reject/drop; no speculative goods, effects in apply, animation-based
  arrivals or double offline credit. Reject malformed/mixed-build data; preserve failed-save guards and named versions.
- **Geometry/performance:** heightfields do not solve cliffs/underpasses, abstract timetable reservations do not solve actual
  passing yards, and historical Mac look timings do not prove an M4 iPad result. Budget resident collision and far proxies.
- **Coordination/evidence limits:** this draft was written before round 3 shipped or was played, without seeing the running
  game or checking in-flight branches. Recheck before building, especially place, entity, terrain, render and reward
  overlap, and run `npm run inflight`.
