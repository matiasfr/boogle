# Six eras: a railway that keeps changing what you can build

> **Proposal for the owner to react to. Not approved, not an implementation plan, and not the game as built.**
> Follows the [audit picks and later owner entries](../decisions.md). Every gameplay number below is a hypothesis,
> marked **[H]**, including the requested crossing and speed anchors. None is a measured playtime or new tuning decision.

The arc is **make a local shortcut → establish a foothold overseas → join productive places → build above a town →
electrify the railway → close the ring to the port**. It serves [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)'s **Toys before chores**,
**Cooperation is automatic, not negotiated**, **Telegraph everything**, and **Leave something for the next visitor**.
No principle change is proposed. The mainland is visible from the start, walkable at the end, and built in its final
state; Home grows only into a busy town.

Each era has a single new construction job, using the same generous harvest, bundle, delivery and build interactions.
Choose the order of available preset links; nobody routes cargo, pays upkeep or drives the train. Camps can send
several goods automatically. The network brings those goods to the frontier, but players haul **every track segment**.
Sea works advance from the supplied shore, with a moving depot beside the workfront; no far-bank resource is needed
to reach that bank. Friends can ride another player's work; solo progress comes from building. Absence banks only the
next committed project's supply and leaves its next step to players.
Proposed construction tools and movement unlocks are available to late arrivals in an eligible world, so friends can
keep exploring together. Costs do not grow to cancel tool improvements or because another player joins.
Old-line upgrades arrive with freight and visible assembly, preserving the route without repeating its original haul.

**Working geography.** Use Two-Bay Weave's placeholder names: **Home → Puffspring → Canopy Run → Whistle Dunes →
Bottlewater → Rattlebank → Port Boogle**. Home uses Threefold Reach. The source is the local previz board's
“Shot 4 — Two-Bay Weave” section (`.look/previz/network-ring-chain/board.md`, gitignored in the owner's main checkout).
Its drawn geometry has Home–Reef Junction–Canopy Run and Reef Junction–Whistle Dunes cross-links; this proposal
reschedules them below. Play Bay and Discovery Bay retain shores, reef landings and a wreck to explore, with no venue
plan assumed. The board's old era schedule, characters and activities do not bind this proposal. Home–Port is last.

**Reading the numbers.** The comparison crossing is **1.2 km [H]** of rail. Passing time means distance divided by
cruise speed: acceleration, approach speed, platform access and waiting add time. Early examples are comparisons,
not a demand for a long wood-age commute. Era budgets include walking, harvesting, building, rides, revisits and
unhurried exploration, for a solo-led world with no required side activities. Friends need not take equally long.

## Wood — “We made that shortcut”

**Feeling and new job.** Intimate, sunny and slightly improvised: chunky Buddy figures hauling oversized bundles
through a place worth wandering. **Connect a camp across Home's gorge.** Begin with light rails, not a plank road.
Walk the folded route first; choose whether to connect the wooded hollow or the rocky shoulder first. The railway
then takes the direct chord across terrain you understand. Big Walk supplies the folds and changing views; the
construction stays our own.

**Construction.** Each delivered bundle visibly advances scaffold, trestle, sleepers and rails. Repeated sections
assemble briskly; the completed gorge span earns the longer look. Place its work ring so the structure and Home's
town are in the same view. No precision placement or hold-to-watch requirement.

**Train and passing time.** A stubby timber flatbed with exposed wheels and a simple canopy, ride-only and timetabled:
**12 m/s [H]**, or **100 s [H]** across the comparison crossing. Its real first job is the shorter local shortcut.
Riders can sit on the deck using Interact; the camera remains theirs.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Chop wood and break exposed stone; carry compact log bundles or stone sacks. A hand axe
takes **3 strokes per familiar tree [H]**, yielding **12 logs total [H]**; tap or hold gives the same forgiving action.
The tool visibly contacts the source, which releases material into the prompt magnet pickup. The grove camp sends
logs and planks; the stone camp sends rubble and foundation blocks. Neither needs a recipe menu.

**Connection, town and access.** The era ends when the gorge line joins the ridge depot to Home's hub. The first
cargo unload raises the workshop frame within sight of the platform: the town answers the player's work. The
workshop supplies the steam-era span kit and improved axe. Home's ridge and launch shore become convenient; no
offshore island opens yet. Walking and the starting jump suffice, and the original foot loop stays enjoyable.

**Old lines and silhouette.** The earliest rails gain timber bracing and a loading rack as supply reaches them.
Keep that little line as the future heritage route. Kit: low pitched roofs, rope lashings, crooked sign poles,
A-frame trestles and a workshop gable. **Playtime: 45 min [H]; cumulative 45 min [H].**

## Steam — “The line goes beyond home”

**Feeling and new job.** A cheerful machine is suddenly doing work bigger than the players. **Extend a supplied
workfront over the sea.** Choose among the near-side camp branches before committing to the crossing. Look back
while riding the newly laid length: Home shrinks, but its workshop remains recognisable. The next shore has a clear
silhouette throughout the journey.

**Construction.** A steam winch lifts bundled trestle frames into place ahead of the machine. Frames, deck and rails
remain separate visible phases. Players carry the next segment's bundle from the advancing depot, rather than
returning to Home for each load. A construction segment is a meaningful span, not every sleeper; passenger stops
do not appear at every construction joint. Bigger reach must not multiply identical carrying laps.

**Train and passing time.** A squat saddle-tank locomotive, round boiler and open cargo deck: **16 m/s [H]**, giving
**75 s [H]** on the comparison crossing. The view, sitting pose and arrival carry the journey; there is no stoking,
catching cargo or mandatory activity aboard.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Harvest timber and soft stone, then haul planks, bracing and block sacks. The workshop's
spring-return axe reduces the old tree to **2 strokes [H]**, still **12 logs total [H]**. The pick has a broader,
more forgiving contact; no stamina or durability. Existing grove and stone camps stay productive. The new island's
clay-and-stone camp sends both brick and blocks, while its grove also supplies timber.

**Connection, town and access.** Joining the last span of **Home–Puffspring** ends the era; connecting Puffspring's
camps afterwards is the next expedition. The first ride is the moment: the new shore becomes ground beneath your feet.
Home gains a water tower and brick workshop wing; the new landing settlement
can grow from return deliveries. Its rounded cone stays a landmark, with no volcanic hazard. Its depot brings the
next haul close to where it is needed.

**Movement and old lines.** Double jump becomes available with the connection, including for late arrivals. It opens
optional shelves beside Home's familiar paths, with ordinary foot access to core sites. Home's working line gains
iron straps and sturdier platforms while the timber heritage spur remains legible. Kit: boiler cylinders, water
tower, brick chimney, winch and timber frame. **Playtime: 60 min [H]; cumulative 105 min [H].**

## Iron and harbour — “Our places are working together”

**Feeling and new job.** Grimy, busy and generous: cargo you recognise moves past work you remember doing.
**Join previously separate branches through a harbour junction.** Reach **Canopy Run** via Puffspring or the new
Home–Reef Junction–Canopy Run route, choosing the order of those preset links. Then extend to **Whistle Dunes**.
Reef Junction is a ride-only stop, not a fast-travel hub. The giant tree and sea arch orient the expedition;
the choice is between useful destinations, not signal or freight-routing puzzles.

**Construction.** Riveted trusses replace the steam kit's dense trestles on new wide gaps. Supply the segment,
watch a crane lift its frame, then see deck panels and rails settle across it. The crane performs the alignment.
Reach Canopy Run with the existing steam kit, then connect its foundry to supply trusses toward Whistle Dunes.
The finished junction draws on connected camps automatically, so a known line becomes the staging route for the
next expedition. Keep its loading yard reachable without climbing machinery.

**Train and passing time.** A heavier steam locomotive with broad driving wheels and a short, visibly loaded freight
consist: **20 m/s [H]**, or **60 s [H]** across the comparison crossing. This is the baseline vast crossing; the
destination grows steadily through the view rather than appearing at the platform.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Break ore-bearing rock and quarry stone; timber remains useful. Carry ore chunks,
brick sacks and compact bundled girders from output racks. The steel axe takes **1 stroke per old tree [H]**, still
**12 logs [H]**; the heavier pick similarly removes repeated work on familiar rock. Canopy Run's small foundry below
the wooded bluff sends rail sections and girders; Whistle Dunes' harbour works send fittings and deck panels.
Clay, timber and stone goods continue alongside them. Manufactured bundles release from racks; nobody chops a girder
or manually feeds a furnace.

**Connection, town and access.** The era ends on **Canopy Run–Whistle Dunes**; completing the alternate approach also
closes the Play Bay loop. The first through cargo train crosses the new truss while the opposite platform is visible:
separate expeditions now belong to the same network. Whistle Dunes' settlement gains a working quay and crane; Home gains a
covered goods hall and heavier construction workshop. The harbour becomes a convenient frontier supply point.

**Movement and old lines.** Glide opens here, turning known high stations into launches toward broad shore clearings,
in the spirit of A Short Hike. It does not reach an unopened island across the sea. Old working trestles visibly gain
truss bracing and masonry feet; existing paths and landmarks remain. Kit: rivets, brick arches, iron headframe,
gantry crane, warm windows and chunky smoke. **Playtime: 90 min [H]; cumulative 195 min [H].**

## Diesel and concrete — “We can build through a real town”

**Feeling and new job.** Confident weight: large pieces arrive ready to do something useful. **Build an elevated
connection over an occupied place.** **Bottlewater** has a stilt settlement between its landing and far shore.
Choose the preset shore approach or inland approach, then bring the route above the streets; neither requires
demolishing someone's work. Local walks weave underneath and return to the same station.

**Construction.** The diesel-era machine handles prefabricated piers and deck slabs. Players haul each segment's
compact supply bundle to its approachable loading apron; the machine raises the heavy pieces. Watch feet, columns,
deck and parapets assemble in that order. Wider modules cover more ground per familiar haul. There is no concrete
curing timer, crane-driving test or extra fuel job.

**Train and passing time.** A blunt diesel cab, broad windows and chunky bogies: **30 m/s [H]**, giving **40 s [H]**
across the comparison crossing. Ride the old iron crossing again to feel the gain on the same distance. Approaches
stay calm enough to board and see the town, rather than carrying cruise speed through every bend.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Break stone along Bottlewater's rocky shore and harvest timber for forms. Carry aggregate sacks,
reinforcement bundles and compact slab kits. A powered cutter/pick works **2 nearby sources per held gesture [H]**;
each keeps its normal yield, and an old tree still needs only **1 contact [H]**. This changes where you stand, not
how quickly you mash. Bottlewater's concrete works send aggregate and precast pieces; the existing foundry sends reinforcement
and rails. Output racks and the back bundle remain the same interface.

**Connection, town and access.** Reach **Bottlewater from Whistle Dunes** with the iron kit; connecting its concrete
works supplies the new elevated route. The era ends when that line joins the landing yard to the far-shore depot.
A loaded train passes above an already busy street, then the player walks underneath it.
This is the tangible before/after. The local town gains sheltered workshops; Home gains a freight canopy and raised
promenade while staying town-sized. The new depot makes the far side a practical starting point for the next bay.

**Movement and old lines.** No new avatar move: the high station makes double jump and glide useful in a fresh place.
Old working bridges acquire concrete feet and deck edging in visible build passes; the original steel frames remain
readable. Kit: rounded diesel nose, silo, broad piers, slab edges, flat canopies and corrugated roofs.
**Playtime: 100 min [H]; cumulative 295 min [H].**

## Electric — “The railway flows across the whole place”

**Feeling and new job.** Long, smooth movement through a network that has acquired a skyline. **Build a wired
corridor to Rattlebank.** Carry wire reels and mast kits with each new segment's supply; the construction rig threads
the overhead line. Connected older lines receive their retrofit automatically with visible freight deliveries.
No wiring diagram, power balancing or second round of hauling over completed track.

**Construction.** Existing spans receive mast bases, uprights and suspended wire in a travelling build pass.
New sections assemble the deck and overhead equipment together. Announce the work in-world before it passes through,
keep the route usable, and change service at a stop so people aboard can keep riding. The distinctive construction
motion is the wire unspooling continuously between the poles, rather than another building rising from the ground.

**Train and passing time.** A recognisable bullet train with a long nose, visible pantographs touching overhead wires
and a companion freight deck: **40 m/s [H]**, or **30 s [H]** across the comparison crossing. Keep near bridge structure,
middle-distance islands and a steady far skyline readable. Electric need not erase industrial smoke or clear the air.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Harvest exposed copper-bearing rock and mineral stone when scouting; collect manufactured
reels and insulator packs at camp racks. A smooth electric cutter keeps the broad harvest action, while its pickup
harness draws scattered pieces into the bundle sooner and from a more forgiving area. The old tree stays at
**1 contact [H]**, with unchanged yield. Bottlewater's new cable works supply wire and fittings before the crossing;
Rattlebank's quarry camps add copper and stone once reached. Earlier camps keep supplying timber, metal and concrete.

**Connection, town and access.** The era ends on the electrified **Bottlewater–Rattlebank** crossing.
The final wire settles, the pantograph meets it, and the first bullet train carries you into the next bay. Return
cargo adds a cable workshop and lit platform canopy at Home; Rattlebank gains a station court and supplied works yard
below its red rock needle. The optional **Reef Junction–Whistle Dunes** link also opens in this era, closing another
useful inner loop. Rattlebank's high route exposes the port approach beyond the unbuilt final stretch.

**Movement and old lines.** No extra movement ability: ridge stations provide new glide starts, and improved service
makes revisits easier. Fast travel remains at hubs only. Older mainline platforms gain masts, wire and lamps; the
heritage loco keeps its branch and its own pace. Kit: pantograph diamonds, slender poles, wire curves, long train nose,
enamel signs and luminous canopies. **Playtime: 95 min [H]; cumulative 390 min [H].**

## Future bullet train — “That faraway skyline is a place we can walk”

**Feeling and new job.** Familiar labour now reaches an extraordinary destination. **Close the ring.** Build the
remaining **Rattlebank–Port Boogle** chain approach first, then the last direct **Home–Port Boogle** link.
The order preserves the stepping-stone journey and makes the final connection a shortcut through the history of
what everyone built, rather than a bypass available from the start.

**Construction.** Thin, long span modules launch outward from the supplied shore. Their visible sequence is support,
slender deck, track and finish; familiar work sites still require a player-carried bundle for every segment. A larger
kit buys more reach from that action. The opposite port structure is already there waiting to be joined. Both
approaches use goods available on the island network, so an unreachable city never funds its own access.

**Train and passing time.** A future bullet train with a low, continuous body and tucked running gear:
**50 m/s [H]**, giving **24 s [H]** across the same comparison crossing. It can stay on a paired-rail structure;
neither an exact monorail nor levitation is required. The port approach gets a slower arrival so the skyline resolves
into streets, rather than making the whole route longer to stage a scene.

**Hands, camps and goods.** Continue harvesting familiar wood and mineral sources when useful. The final collector
extends the powered tool to a selected cluster of **3 nearby sources [H]**, each conserving its normal yield; it
never vacuums other players' bundles. Haul light panel packs, rail modules and fittings from the network's upgraded
assembly yards. Those yards combine already connected goods automatically. No exotic endgame ore, new extraction
minigame or exponential final bill is needed.

**Connection, town and access.** First reach and explore **Port Boogle** by the chain, then complete Home–Port Boogle to
end the era. The final ride frames the old branch across the inner bay and lands at a place visible since the
beginning. Home gains a compact interchange and completed waterfront; the metropolis gains the player connection,
not staged city growth. The moment is being able to ride the whole ring and walk its destination. Narrative finale,
mayor and ceremony remain parked.

**Movement and old lines.** The ring and express service are the movement gain; no flight ability replaces the railway.
Old trunk spans gain slim caps and platform extensions through visible assembly, retaining their characteristic
supports. The heritage narrow-gauge loco keeps running. Kit: continuous train profile, thin spans, smooth panel joints
and a distinctive port terminal. **Playtime: 90 min [H]; cumulative 480 min / about 8 hours [H].**

**Shared to limit assets.** Reuse Buddy, tool grips/strike clips, bundle, output rack and construction staging. Wood,
stone and ore share rules, with distinct contact/fracture treatments. Trains share rider decks, boarding, sitting and
timetables; noses, wheels and roof gear change. Bridges reuse foundations and decks with era attachments. Towns reuse
footprints, house shells and workshop additions around retained landmarks. The mainland needs only its final state.
Vegetation and rock kits recur in different landforms. Spend bespoke work on the era silhouettes, not complete replacements.

**Reward-system slot — intentionally undesigned.** The owner's maximal, generous, distinctive reward direction
remains in the [audit picks](../decisions.md#the-audit-picks-2-november). Refer to the
[rarity, incremental and gacha research](https://github.com/matiasfr/boogle/blob/97aed0dd/docs/research/rewards-rarity-gacha.md)
(read from local Git history) and [personal-rewards.md](../research/personal-rewards.md). No reward systems are designed
here; som, knacks and helpers stay parked or undecided. [Story and finale](story-and-finale.md) remains parked.

**Questions only the owner can answer**

- Should the last era's train remain visibly wheel-on-rail, or should its running gear suggest hovering?
- Does glide belong at the harbour connection as proposed, or should that feeling wait until the concrete high route?
- Which silhouette should dominate grown Home: the goods hall, water tower or compact interchange?
- Should camp helpers be part of the world's character at all? This proposal works without deciding their role.

**Unverified:** sustained haul/harvest fun, return-ride timing, geography, kit readability and device cost. No running

exists; this proposal implements nothing.
