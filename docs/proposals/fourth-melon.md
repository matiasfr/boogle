# The fourth melon: a brief for the writers

*Proposal, 1 November 2026. Written for the owner and collaborators who might write the Boogle story. It covers where the gag comes from, what it's for, and three drafts of the reveal. Nothing here is decided, and the melon itself is open to change.*

## 1. Where it comes from: it's already in the game

The fourth melon isn't new. It's one line in The Boogler's family chapter ([src/boogler/lines.js](../../src/boogler/lines.js), chapter `mom0`), which players unlock at friendship level 1:

> **Boogler:** Family? I have Oyijon. My mother. She is small and loud and she can carry four melons at once.
> **You:** Four melons?!
> **Boogler:** Four! Two in her arms, one under her chin, and the fourth... I never figured out where the fourth one goes. That is her secret.

Melons run through more of his story than that one joke. They're his origin story:

- **How he got here.** He started a melon export business from Uzbekistan ("doubly landlocked") with "four trucks, two trains, one very small boat, and seven hundred golden melons". Then a storm hit. He held on to one melon, and ate it the first night.
- **His idle line:** "Did I tell you about the melons? Seven hundred. I still dream about them."
- **His homesickness:** golden melons at the bazaar, "stacked like cannonballs".
- **His promise:** he told Oyijon he'd come back "a big success". She said "Come back fed." He's ashamed to go home as "a blob who lost some melons".

So melons are The Boogler's failure and his longing. The fourth melon is the one melon-related thing that *isn't* sad. It's a small, impossible feat of his mother's that he has never figured out.

## 2. What the gag is for in Boogle 2.0

The [story-and-finale](story-and-finale.md) proposal makes it one of five running gags that escalate across the eight eras:

- **One clue per era**, hidden in the world: a sketch, a strap, an ad for a "melon harness", a museum display that's mysteriously empty.
- **The reveal at the finale.** In "the last train to the mainland", Oyijon is waiting on the platform with four melons.

That gives it three jobs:

1. **A question every player hears early.** It sits in the first family chapter, so most players hear it.
2. **A reason to look around in every era.** The clues are small rewards for exploring, and they're stories, not stats.
3. **A soft landing for the ending.** The Boogler's arc is about going home. Meeting Oyijon and finally seeing the trick ties his story off with a laugh, not a speech.

**The brief's one rule** is "make it as stupid as possible". The reveal should be funny on the spot and still make sense of the clues looking back.

## 3. Three drafts of the reveal

Each draft gives the reveal, the clues that point to it, and its tone.

### Draft A: "There is no trick"

- **The reveal.** At the finale, Oyijon picks up four melons: two in her arms, one under her chin, and the fourth... she just holds it. It's balanced on top of the third, plainly, in full view. The Boogler stares. "That's it?" Oyijon: "You never asked, bolam. You only wondered." Then she hands him all four.
- **The clues.** Every clue is a red herring the island invents: the melon harness ad (a scam by Gary), a patent drawing of a "melon hat", an empty museum case labelled "The Fourth Melon Technique (on loan)". The group's theories become the joke.
- **Tone.** Anticlimax as the payoff, warm. It fits the mayor's speech gag, which also ends in "Thank you." and nothing more.
- **The risk.** Two anticlimaxes at one finale might be one too many.

### Draft B: "The fourth melon is The Boogler"

- **The reveal.** Oyijon hugs her son, lifts him up (he is round, green-gold in the right light), and carries him off the platform with the other three. She has always carried four melons. The fourth was him, as a baby. "Two in my arms, one under my chin, and you on my back, bolam. Every day, to the bazaar."
- **The clues.** An old photo with a blurry round thing on Oyijon's back. A baby sling in a suzani pattern. A coconut letter: "carry them the usual way" (already in the slice's gag step 1). He has never noticed, because it was him.
- **Tone.** The silliest and the sweetest at once. It turns his origin story ("I lost seven hundred melons") around: she never lost hers.
- **The risk.** Being the sincere one, it needs the silly setup to hold up. And a blob being mistaken for a melon has to read clearly in the art.

### Draft C: "The fourth melon was the friends all along"

- **The reveal.** Oyijon can only carry three. The fourth was always carried by whoever was standing next to her. She says it like it's obvious: "Nobody carries four melons alone." Then she hands the fourth melon to the nearest player, then the next, and the whole group ends up carrying melons off the train together.
- **The clues.** Every clue shows someone nearby: a cropped photo with a hand at the edge, two sets of footprints, a letter signed "and the neighbours".
- **Tone.** Communal. It's the theme of the game (friends carrying things together on the haul stack) said as a joke.
- **The risk.** The closest to a moral. It works only if it's played completely deadpan.

## 4. If it isn't melons

The owner is open to moving off melons. Two sizes of change:

- **Change only the fourth-melon gag.** This is cheap: rewrite two lines in `mom0` and the story-and-finale table. Keep the shape: Oyijon has one small impossible feat that her son never figured out. Some examples to react to:
  - "she can pour tea for twelve without looking";
  - "she can fold a suzani in the wind";
  - "she can win any haggle without speaking".
- **Change melons everywhere.** This is a bigger rewrite. About 20 lines of The Boogler's backstory, the melon raft and regatta, the melon launch finale and the golden melon pattern would all need a new cargo. Worth doing only if the new cargo is funnier and still carries his story: something precious, plentiful, lost in a storm, and missed.

4. Melons: keep, change only the gag, or change everywhere?
