## How the owner works

These are standing preferences from the thread.

- **Use Codex wherever it makes sense** (2 November; owner: "I have weekly resets for Codex that I don't have for Claude. So let's continue leveraging Codex where it's most sensible"). Previz, research, plans and self-contained code go to Codex; work that needs eyes on the running game goes to Claude, which also reviews what Codex makes.
- **Feel first.** "Game feel is tantamount." Every interactive thing gets a feel pass before it gets rewards.
- **Mechanics before narrative.** Find the fun in the core loop and its feel first (1 November). Story, characters and gags come later and are easy to fill in. Don't commit to them before the loop works.
- **Think ambitiously.** Early mocks were "too simplistic". The owner wants clear technological stratification between eras, a bigger island, and a city.
- **Likes:** vehicles, exploration, construction you can watch, goofiness, Palworld's take on materials.
- **Wary of:** material grinds.
- **Collaborators may write the narrative.** Don't lock story details in alone. Draft options for them to react to.
- **Decides through HTML pages with pick buttons.** The concept page ends with a round of questions and a copyable summary. Keep its localStorage key `boogle-2-v5`: changing it wipes the owner's picks. Playtest feedback works the same way: tap answers on a page Claude can read, as in the [Round 4 Replay](https://claude.ai/artifact/TAb4PhNz2E9g1DiZD5bHoF) (private to the owner).
- **Solo first** (1 November). Every prototype must work and be fun for one person playing alone; multiplayer builds on top.
- **Fast iteration over test coverage** (1 November). Agents run many tasks in parallel, see their own results and ship by themselves; tests only where lockstep needs them.
- **Research before deep facets** (1 November). A facet with real depth (level design, vehicle feel, construction, rendering, asset pipelines) starts with deep research into industry practice, written to `docs/research/`, before its options are drafted.
- **Painted concepts** come from `npm run concepts` (the `concept-art` skill). They run through Codex CLI on the owner's Mac, using a session started on that Mac. They're references, not game art.

## Decided

- **The hotbar is cut: one thing in your hands** (2 November, owner, after playing `?proto=onehand`: "No I didn't [miss it] at all. I think in that case we cut it. We can always bring it back if a real gameplay element demands it."). Picking something up replaces what you hold, which lands by you; no slots, no LB, RB or 1-5. What you hold isn't saved.
- **The ball bump is cut** (2 November): the owner was already unsure of it, and it made the main action conditional. RT and left click use or throw what you hold; with empty hands they do nothing.

### The audit picks (2 November)

The owner's answers to the standing design audit ([design-audit.md](design-audit.md)). **Where an older entry below disagrees, these win.**

- **Skipping the rail:** friends can ride what others built; alone, you build to progress. This replaces "every system is ignorable" for the rail loop.
- **Materials:** "playing still produces what builds the island", through hands-on work with "really great game feel, but very simple harvest mechanics". Mini-games don't produce building materials; they can reward other things.
- **Rewards, a direction not yet designed:** "I remember we did some exploration on having different rarities, and like, I'm definitely interested in incremental game and gacha mechanics". Mini-games may feed this. Open question 15.
- **Rewards should be maximal** (2 November, after [rewards-rarity-gacha.md](../research/rewards-rarity-gacha.md)): "something a little more, you know, just maximal dopamine to get people motivated to keep playing the game and have that sort of uniqueness element of it. It doesn't have to be super grindy. Like, I like just handing out a lot of rewards that feel good. But we just got to figure out how to integrate it with the game mechanics well and the world". So many rewards, each feeling good, with items that feel unique, woven into the loop. That report's small, gentle candidates aren't the target.
- **Hauling:** you haul each track segment, and the network brings materials closer.
- **Helpers:** not decided. Owner: "I'm not sure if in the same form, but I kind of feel like it'd be fun to have little helpers running around and giving life to the world, but maybe they can work as part of the camps".
- **Goods:** not one material per age; camps can make different goods at once.
- **Eras:** six: wood, steam, iron and harbour, diesel and concrete, electric (bullet trains with pantographs and overhead wires), and a future train that "doesn't have to be actually an exact monorail".
- **What ends an era:** reaching a key connection.
- **The mayor and opening days:** parked until the loop is fun, and never a gate. His existing lines stay.
- **Som and knacks:** parked until the loop is fun.
- **First reaching a far island:** the line builds out over the sea, and you ride it.
- **The network:** preset links, your choice of order, and a last link from Home to the port. So the chain closes into a ring. Which links appear when is still open.
- **Home:** a busy town at most.
- **Danger:** open. Was "no hazards at all, nothing decays", see Changed.
- **While nobody plays:** the network banks one project, and the next step waits for players.
- **Thanks:** credit people with no order or ranking.
- **Mini-games pass:** after the rail loop has its answer.
- **Phones:** best effort, at lower quality. The M4 iPad Pro stays the lowest target.
- **The dino and the guest:** optional fun for now.
- **Documents:** this log and [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) count. PLAN.md, SETTLED.md, the vertical slice and older proposals are marked historical, and the story briefs stay parked.

### The shape of the game

- **2026-11-04, Place gets the top-level room** (owner, including follow-up). Place is the game for now: the bare URL joins `boogle`, its invite is `https://boogle.club/`, and the pause title reads `boogle`. Classic and headless tools retain `beach` and its saved world. Explicit rooms win, explicit prototype links keep their isolated rooms, and old `?room=proto-place-coop` invites still work. **Follow-up: no progress migration; the owner will start fresh in `boogle`.** The old co-op save is simply unused there, not deleted. Keep `reopenQuery` so an installed app remembering the old room-only default still reopens in `boogle`. Start over and Start fresh must work without allowing public-room tuning or changing rules/world namespace. This supersedes the previous default room name, not the entry mode or the later architecture work.

- **2026-11-03, bring the default switch forward** (owner). The bare main URL now opens round 4, equivalent to `?proto=place&fresh=1&coop=1`, in the existing shared `proto-place-coop` room. Explicit URL flags and rooms win; `?classic=1` opens the old island, which stays for later. Solo remains fully playable. This is the entry switch only: `src/proto/place` stays where it is; retiring prototype code and the architecture pass still follow after the round.

- **2026-11-03, scaling is a tool, used with care** (owner: the old rule "was only referring to a specific kind of scaling where buildings would appear", which wasn't physical enough; scaling "can still be a useful tool for animation and making things feel good"). Buildings and big structures still don't scale into existence: they're built from pieces. Scale is fine for animation and juice, like squash and stretch, a pop or a log growing in flight, when it reads well.
- **2026-11-02, the call on the Design Table** (owner and Grant, spoken while going through the table; Granola note of 2 November; the table's own database stayed empty, so this entry is its record). About 17 minutes of the call didn't transcribe, and they recapped it quickly, so this may be short of what was said.
  - **Show, don't tell** is a principle now: DESIGN.md 11. In-game helper text is usually a sign the world is unclear, and the fix goes in the world.
  - **Where it ends:** back on the original island, meeting The Boogler. The old island gets redesigned into a proper endgame hangout worth reaching, with little games to play with friends. Later in the game you can build your own track from certain places instead of following one set route, and that may be part of how you get there.
  - **The Boogler** is an entrepreneur. You were doing his project all along, and he starts charging you when you reach him. Rumours around the world call him a prophet or a mystic, and the joke is that none of it is true. The JRPG-style relationship system gets refactored into plenty of fun dialogue that isn't gated on it.
  - **The strange thing in this world** is extraordinary geology plus ancient technology, which both gates places and helps build things. Abilities are ancient technology you find, in the style of Tears of the Kingdom.
  - **Abilities replace curios.** Grant doesn't like collectibles for collecting's sake, so the things you find are Metroidvania unlocks that open new paths: hook shot, bombs, a sledgehammer for walls, double jump and the glider. **The glider** matches Breath of the Wild as closely as we can, done our own way.
  - **Materials replace eras.** The world doesn't turn into a steel era. You find better materials like cement and steel, and your engines are better because you upgraded them.
  - **Length and sessions:** about 8 hours for the main game, leaving room for more mechanics. Each area is one or two hours aimed at something that feels complete, and it ends in a sense of closure such as a new ability, as Big Walk did it, so you can walk away feeling good.
  - **Places:** all five on the table, and many more. They should be cool to reach.
  - **Water:** the ultra-realistic water from the iPad demo as the starting point, made stylized to sit with toon characters.
  - **Time of day has a purpose:** some things only by day and some only by night, with a brief night. In the lore these are northern islands in summer.
  - **The roundhouse** is where you unlock, upgrade, customize and send out trains, with a light management feel. You start with one slow train, so each one joining the network is exciting.
  - **Cosmetics have swappable slots.** Character: a hat, an accessory (headphones, glasses, a scarf) and a full-body outfit, with no separate pants and shirts. Train: a flag, a side accessory and a chimney accessory.
  - **Boxes have rarity.** Common ones first and rarer ones later, with some cosmetics only in certain boxes. They're cosmetic for now; something functional may come later, and the last box on an island could hold a new ability. **The pet** stays small and hops on your shoulder when you board a train.
  - **Mini-games** are co-op only, in the spirit of Human Fall Flat, and give cosmetic boxes.
  - **What brings friends back:** splitting up jobs so progress goes faster, and rough proximity voice chat, possibly with mechanics that use it. **What's next in multiplayer** is finding the friction and complementary tasks that let two people split off.
  - **While nobody plays,** whatever you kicked off finishes, as a small incremental touch.
  - **Sound and music** are procedural.
  - **After round 4:** real multiplayer gets priority. The main URL starts on this build, prototype code goes, and we take a deliberate look at the architecture as it becomes the main game.
  - **Research next:** incremental game maths, goofy physics co-op, and traversal at speed (Sunset Overdrive, Burnout's boost); cosmetic collecting got a soft yes. **Not yet:** funny-resident writing research (though the first resident arrives in round 4), maps and fast travel, a level-building tool, a style guide, names, and getting friends in. The goal now is a vertical slice that's really fun, with the right frictions and the sense of building towards something.
  - **The speed device waits until after round 4** (owner, after reading [traversal-at-speed.md](../research/traversal-at-speed.md)). It's a candidate for later, as ancient technology found after a useful walkable shortcut, giving short bursts you choose.
  - **Unresolved: where your axe comes from.** You walk up to a tree and suddenly have one. If the screen shows what's true and there's no inventory, tools should show somehow, in a lo-fi way given complex animation is a worry. It can wait for the systems to lock, but Grant invited Claude to try an idea.

- **2026-11-02, the direction after round 3** (owner and Grant, "we concur completely"; answers to the director's questions, playtests.md). The hour-to-hour game is **all three equally**: building the railway, exploring with abilities, and an incremental climb. **Path first, train later:** a new path you can walk is its own reward, and the train is its own unlock once distances are long ("too much too soon"). **A good slog** is more chopping and carrying that feels good until you want a better way, helped by longer routes; the point is getting "hungry for efficiency". **The efficiency ladder:** better tool, bigger carry, materials brought closer, then a faster way to travel, with trains at the top. **Ability gates:** a few key gates on the main line, the rest optional. **Two players:** splitting up helps, like one chopping while the other rides cargo, with optional co-op-specific things for certain rewards; chains aren't required. **The Boogler's story** stays mostly for later, and we plant rumours now. **Money is a comic score**, with The Boogler's prices as a gag ("money as a joke is a compelling concept"). **Towns:** test a house interior with a funny resident, a shop and a museum, all three; buildings have roles and you can go inside and talk to little guys. **Next test:** path-before-train and movement abilities with gated secrets together, on one island.

  3. Iron and coal
  4. Steel and harbour
  5. Diesel and concrete
  6. Electric
  7. Monorail city
  8. Maglev
