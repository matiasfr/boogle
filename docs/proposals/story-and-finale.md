# Story and finale for Boogle 2.0

*Proposal, 30 October 2026. A goofy story told across the eight eras of the Boogle 2.0 concept, and three communal finales for 1–6 friends who are not always online together. It builds on [big-walk-and-communal-endings.md](../research/big-walk-and-communal-endings.md); the concept itself is the Boogle 2.0 artifact (the eight eras: castaway, steam wagons, iron and coal, steel and harbour, diesel, electric, monorail, maglev). Ideas marked "inference" go beyond what the sources say. Nothing here is built.*

## 1. How the story is told

### The rule: mundane stakes, absurd causes

House House kept Untitled Goose Game's stakes small on purpose ("Anything out of the ordinary is the result of the goose"). Boogle's version:

- **The world stays ordinary.** The island builds a railway, a town gets bigger, a mayor wants a ribbon cut. Nobody saves the world.
- **The absurdity comes from two sources: the players' slapstick and The Boogler's ambition.** He is the self-elected mayor ("one to zero") who takes every small thing with enormous seriousness. He is the straight man to the chaos the players make, and the fool to the island's ordinary progress.
- **Moments first, systems second** (Goose). For each era, write the funny moments we want, such as the ribbon snapping on the train before the speech ends. Then add the system that makes them happen, like ribbons as physics objects that trains can hit. Keep a running list of slapstick clichés and pull from it when an era needs more.

### What carries the story: opening days

Each era ends with an **opening day** for its big project. The mayor's ceremony is the story beat:
- a speech;
- a ribbon;
- a band that sounds like the era (an accordion in era 1, brass and steam whistles in era 3, a synthesiser in era 7);
- a goofy accident caused by physics, not a script.

The ceremony also unlocks the era's new places. Lines are short and react to state ("the game is paying attention", from the earlier Hades finding).

**Music as narrator** (Goose's adaptive score): each era has a theme with stems keyed to coarse states: idle, a train arriving, a ceremony, a mishap. That makes it cheap to sound funny without scripting each moment.

### Running gags across the eras

Each gag escalates one step per era and pays off in the finale. The callback structure is inference; no verified source covers it.

| Gag | How it escalates | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The mayor's speech** | It gets longer every era. In era 2 it's one sentence. By era 7 it has slides and an intermission. A honk chord from three or more friends cuts it short (the existing honk detector), and he pretends he meant to stop there. | At the finale he says only "Thank you." Everyone waits. That's it. |
| **Gary** | You always just miss Gary. In era 1 there's a soggy business card ("Gary. Boats."). In era 4 his steamship is pulling away as you arrive. In era 6 you find his yard, with his coffee still warm. Era 7 has a billboard; era 8, his voice on the station announcements. | At the finale he is there, finally, and he is completely normal. Or he's in disguise in the crowd; the group decides. |
| **The fourth melon** | Oyijon can carry four melons and nobody knows how the fourth one goes. Every era hides one clue: a sketch, a strap, an ad for a "melon harness", a museum display that's mysteriously empty. | Revealed at the finale. Make it as stupid as possible. |
| **Coconut mail** | Letters to Oyijon get "faster" each era: coconut → bottle → telegraph → pneumatic tube → email → maglev express post. Her replies stay exactly the same length and always mention eating. | Her last letter arrives in person (see finale 2). |
| **The town hall** | Era 2's is a crate with a top hat on it. Then a wooden hall, a brick hall with a clock that is always wrong, a concrete block, then the top of Central's blue dome. | The hat from the first crate is still there, on the highest shelf. |

### Communication as play (borrowing from Big Walk)

Big Walk turns talking into play through a short voice range and tools to reconnect. Boogle has no voice chat, but it has the honk, the ping and the island's size. Some of this is inference.

- **Signal tools by era:**
  - era 1: a horn;
  - era 3: the steam whistle from any loco you ride;
  - era 5: a flare gun at the docks;
  - era 6: radio stations at every station;
  - era 8: maglev chimes.

  They're useful because the world gets bigger (Big Walk's megaphones and flares).
- **Folk games, not aha puzzles.** Some mini-games are charades-like: guess the crate's colour from a friend's mime, or shout directions to a friend driving a blindfolded handcar using honks only. Big Walk draws from Keep Talking And Nobody Explodes and We Were Here.
- **Traces.** Friends who wander off leave signs: a lit lamp, a dropped item, a ping marker that fades slowly, a train livery they unlocked. You can tell where they've been (Big Walk's "detective-style" tracking).

## 2. Three communal finales

Each finale is a ritual that "met your accomplishment" (Big Walk), so friends don't just log off. Each one is non-competitive (Journey). Each has to work for 1–6 friends who may not all be online.

**Shared rules for every finale:**
- It is announced days ahead on the departures board and by the mayor (principle 7, telegraph everything).
- The group picks a time with a calendar-style vote at the town hall.
- Anyone absent appears through their traces, and can replay the finale later as an echo.

### Finale 1: The Grand Parade

Every train from every era runs the Grand Loop at once, in order. The heritage narrow-gauge loco leads, sail wagon and steam wagon behind it, then the rest. The maglev comes last, floating, apologetic.

- **The trains carry your history.** The crates are the colours you actually delivered over the whole game, so every friend's play is visibly in the parade.
- **Each player has a role:** driver of one era's train, flare-launcher, band, or ribbon-holder. With fewer players, the mayor takes the leftover roles badly.
- **It ends with a goodbye:** the trains park around Central. One by one, each player honks their own note from the honk pitch table, and together the notes make a chord. Then the lights go down.
- **If someone is absent:** their train still runs in the parade with their name and their colours. They can replay the parade afterwards from any station's departures board, and their honk joins the chord the next time anyone plays it.

### Finale 2: The last train to the mainland

- **The trip:** everyone boards the maglev to the mainland port city, finally reachable. Oyijon is on the platform with four melons. The fourth melon is revealed.
- **The welcome party in the port city:** a dinner (osh) where each friend's knacks and hats are on display. Oyijon approves of exactly one thing each friend did, read from their save.
- **The goodbye:** you wave from the pier as the mayor tries to give a speech and Oyijon makes him sit down and eat.
- **If someone is absent:** their seat on the train holds their hat. Anyone can take "the next train" later and find the party still going, with Oyijon's line for them waiting.

### Finale 3: The melon launch (the silliest)

- **The build:** the maglev's last project is a launch track. Everyone helps load a single golden melon. It takes a mix of cargo colours, so every kind of play is in it.
- **The countdown:** the mayor's speech, interrupted by the launch.
- **The payoff:** the melon goes into orbit and becomes a new moon, visible every night afterwards, a permanent mark on the shared sky.
- **If someone is absent:** the melon carries every friend's name, and anyone who logs in later sees the new moon and a postcard from the mayor explaining what happened, badly.

**A suggestion, not a decision:** finale 1 has the strongest ritual and every friend's history in it; finale 2 has the strongest story payoff. They can be combined: the parade ends at the mainland terminal. Finale 3 works as an optional epilogue gag.

## 3. After the finale: a toy box

Big Walk has a second, 100%-completion ending (a hidden theatre). Boogle's version:
- once the finale has happened, the archipelago stays open as a toy box, with every era's train still running on the timetable;
- a hidden "museum of us" opens for groups that finish every project, showing each friend's best moments as plaques with no rankings.

## 4. Open questions

- Which finale, or which combination?
- What does the fourth melon actually turn out to be? This is a writing session for the group.
- Should the finale wait for the whole group, or happen with whoever is there plus the absent friends' echoes?
- How much reacts to each friend's actual history? It needs a small per-player record in their save: their colours delivered, their best knack, their hats.
